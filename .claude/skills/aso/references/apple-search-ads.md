# Apple Search Ads (as an ASO lever)

Apple Search Ads (ASA) is paid promotion in App Store search results. Most playbooks treat it as a separate paid-acquisition topic. For ASO purposes, ASA matters as a **research lever** — you can pay to learn what your organic strategy should be — and as a halo-effect amplifier of organic ranking.

## The March 2026 expansion

Through Q1 2026, ASA only filled the #1 search result slot. As of March 2026, Apple opened positions #2-5 to ads as well. Practical impact:
- Search results pages are more ad-saturated; the organic top slot is rarer
- Competitors with budget can sit ahead of you on YOUR brand-keyword unless you bid defensively
- Brand defense bidding (a small ASA campaign on your own app name) is now table stakes for any app with measurable revenue

This DOESN'T mean organic ASO matters less — it means you have less margin for error in the top slots. Strong keyword + screenshot strategy still wins long-term; ASA fills the short-term gap and creates the data you'd otherwise have to guess at.

## ASA as research, not just acquisition

The most underused ASA pattern for indie devs:

1. Run a small Search Ads campaign ($5-20/day) targeting the keywords you're considering as primary
2. Watch which keywords produce the lowest cost-per-tap AND highest tap-through-rate
3. Those are the keywords your CONVERSION matches — your screenshots and copy already speak to that searcher's intent
4. Promote those keywords into your name + subtitle + keyword field for organic
5. Keywords with high CPT and low TTR are where your listing FAILS to convert — fix the listing OR drop the keyword from your organic strategy

This is a $50-200 experiment that replaces months of organic guessing.

## The halo effect

Active ASA campaigns produce measurable organic uplift even on the keywords NOT being bid. Mechanism: install velocity from paid traffic feeds the same ranking model that decides organic position. A short, intense ASA push around a launch can compound into organic ranking that outlives the campaign.

The reverse is also true — pulling ASA spend after a launch can drop organic ranking until the engagement signals catch up. Plan ASA pulls deliberately, not as an end-of-month cost-cut.

## Campaign types

ASA offers several campaign types; for ASO research the relevant ones are:

| Type | Use for |
|---|---|
| Search Tab | Brand awareness in the Search tab itself (not search results) |
| Search Results | The main keyword-bid campaigns — the research lever |
| Today Tab | Premium placement on the Today tab (high CPM, low ROAS for indie) |
| Product Pages | Ads that appear on competitors' product pages |

Indie default: Search Results campaigns only, on a tight keyword list, with brand defense as a separate small campaign.

## Relevance over bid

Apple decides which ad shows based on a combination of bid AND relevance. A high bid alone does not guarantee impressions. Relevance is judged from your name + subtitle + keyword field — meaning your ASO and ASA share infrastructure. A weak organic listing handicaps your paid campaigns too.

This is the strongest argument against treating ASO and ASA as separate workstreams.

## Practical research workflow

```
1. Pick 10 candidate keywords from your ASO brainstorm
2. Set up a Search Results campaign with each as exact-match
3. Set $1-3 max CPT, $20-50/day total budget
4. Run for 2-4 weeks
5. Pull the report:
   - Sort by TTR (tap-through-rate) descending
   - Sort by CR (conversion-to-install) descending
   - Top of both = your organic primary candidates
6. Update name + subtitle + keyword field accordingly
7. Optionally: build a CPP for each top keyword (Jul 2025 organic CPP eligibility)
```

## Anti-patterns

- Bidding on broad-match keywords as a beginner — too noisy to learn from
- Outbidding category-leader giants on their primary brand keyword — Apple's relevance scoring will burn your budget without serving impressions
- Setting up ASA without a clean attribution setup — install data without per-keyword breakdown is just a bill
- Treating ASA as "set and forget" — keyword quality scoring requires ongoing pruning of underperformers
