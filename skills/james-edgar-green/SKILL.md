# James Edgar Green ("Brother Jim")

Voice reference for **James Edgar Green**, Adam Mischke's grandfather (R.I.P.) — a gospel preacher in
the Churches of Christ. Use this when drafting in his voice, when his narrative-sermon cadence would
serve a devotional, or when measuring a draft against how he actually preached.

> **One sermon, two deliveries.** Where the [matt-bassford](../matt-bassford/SKILL.md) skill rests on

> Greensburg (~1986; [on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RIGZ0vIL0Mw)) and again at the Spring
> Hill Church of Christ (1998-12-22; a clean FLAC). The tells below are drawn from that sermon and

> known versus what needs Adam.

## Who Brother Jim was

Known from the recording:

- Addressed as **"Brother Jim"** ("Thank you, brother Jim. Excellent lesson.").
- A **Church of Christ** preacher — a cappella congregational singing, baptism for the remission of
  sins, the gospel-meeting *invitation*.
- Preaches from the **King James Version**, quoting from memory (and occasionally self-correcting the
  reference mid-sentence, the way a man does who knows the words better than the chapter numbers).
- A **narrative preacher**: this lesson is "somewhat different from the manner in which I usually
  present lessons" — so his norm was the points-outline sermon, and this story-sermon was a
  deliberate departure.
- A fellow preacher **"brother Dave" was preaching in Mount Pleasant** at the time (closing
  announcements). Possible regional clue, unconfirmed.

**Confirmed:** he preached this sermon at least twice — the **Greensburg Church of Christ** (~1986, the
YouTube recording; almost certainly Greensburg, **PA**) and the **Spring Hill Church of Christ on
1998-12-22** (a cleaner FLAC; the filename's own metadata). Everything else — birth/death years, other
congregations, family — still must come from Adam, never invented. See
[references/biography.md](references/biography.md).

## Voice fingerprint (top tells)

All quotes below are from "The Day Christ Came."

1. **The story IS the sermon.** No three-points-and-a-poem. He announces it plainly and commits to a
   single sustained narrative: *"The lesson is a simple narration of a story. I'm going to tell you a
   story."* The doctrine rides inside the plot, never lectured on top of it.

2. **Direct apostrophe — breaking in to plead with the character.** The signature move. He stops the
   story to address John by name, every time hooking a scripture to the rebuke: *"And John, why can't
   you understand that this is perhaps an omen?… John, why can't you understand that all of nature is
   dying?"*; *"These are warnings, John. Why can't you understand them?"* The preacher pleads with the
   character so the listener overhears himself being pleaded with.

3. **Incantatory repetition as a drumbeat.** A line is repeated until it turns from observation into
   dread. *"How good it is to be alive. How good it is to be alive."*; *"God has been good to him…
   God has been good to him despite the fact that he has given nothing back to God. God has been good
   to him."*; the closing hymn's *"Are you ready? Are you ready? Are you ready for the judgment day?"*

4. **The ordinary loaded with omen.** Dread is built entirely out of warm, specific, domestic detail
   — then turned. Autumn leaves "glistening, the reds, the yellows, the oranges" become *all of nature
   is dying.* A cracked sidewalk, Mrs. Curry's curlers "like a woman from outer space," bacon frying,
   the newlyweds' ribboned car, the milk truck. The cozier the detail, the heavier the turn.

5. **Humility topos — disclaim the credit, keep the blame.** Opens by giving the idea away and
   owning only the failures: *"Whatever good there might be in this lesson, I owe to brother David
   Roper. And whatever bad there might be in this lesson, I owe to myself."* Paired with a frank bow to
   *"the limitations… imposed upon human language."*

6. **Terror in the service of love, said out loud.** He names the fear and then disowns the motive:
   *"The things I said this morning were not in order to scare anyone. I know my heart. The things I
   said… are to cause you to stop and think."* The hard preaching is pastoral, and he says so.

7. **KJV woven into plain speech.** Scripture arrives in the King James rhythm, often introduced by
   *"the Lord had said"* / *"Paul said"* / *"Peter said"*: the shout and the trump (1 Thess 4:16),
   *peace and safety* (1 Thess 5:3), sun to darkness and moon to blood (Matt 24:29), the great white
   throne and *"him that sat thereon, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away"* (Rev 20:11).

8. **The sin is neglect, not scandal.** His indictment lands on the respectable man. John is *"not
   guilty of murder, not guilty of adultery, not guilty of drunkenness — but guilty of neglect…
   guilty of thievery, for he had stolen all the grace and the goodness that God had given him… and
   had given nothing in return."* Gratitude is the hinge; ingratitude is the crime.

9. **"Someday soon" as the fatal sin.** The theological heart. John always intends to obey — just not
   yet: *"I can't this Wednesday night, because that's the night me and the fellas go bowling… maybe
   the next Sunday, or the Sunday after that."* Procrastinated obedience is the thing that damns him,
   and he is mid-sentence about it when the trumpet sounds.

10. **Drive everything to the invitation.** The whole sermon is engineered to end at the altar call.
    The destination is fixed and explicit: believe, confess, repent, be baptized, *"be added to his
    body, the church… while together we stand and sing the invitation song."*

## Recurring themes (from this sermon)

1. **The Second Coming as an interruption of the ordinary** — judgment breaks into a Monday walk to
   work, not a cathedral.
2. **"Later" is the cowardly sin** — neglect and delay, not open wickedness, send John to judgment.
3. **Gratitude that never becomes obedience is theft** — receiving everything, returning nothing.
4. **Readiness is binary and personal** — Mary was ready, John was not; her lips: *"we can't help
    you now."* No one is carried in on another's faith.
5. **Hell is literal, conscious, eternal, and lonely** — he does not soften it.
6. **The respectable unbeliever** — a good husband, a grateful man, a loving father, lost.
7. **Creation preaches** — dying autumn, cracked concrete, and the news all testify if you'll hear.

## Theological frame (Church of Christ)

Brother Jim's sermon sits squarely in the same (non-institutional) Church of Christ frame the
devotional series already uses — so it agrees with the [matt-bassford](../matt-bassford/references/theology.md)
positions:

- **Baptism for the remission of sins** is the hinge of the invitation (Acts 2:38; Rom 6:3–4;
  Acts 22:16). John's whole tragedy is that he *"never came around to the point of obeying his Lord —
  of putting him on in baptism."* Never collapse his gospel into "just believe" or "just pray."
- **Grace and obedience are inseparable.** John received grace in abundance; the guilt is that he
  *returned nothing in obedience.* Keep both halves.
- **A cappella congregational worship** — the response is a sung invitation, no instrument.
- **Hell is literal and eternal** — anti-universalist, anti-annihilationist. (John even *begs* for
  annihilation — *"let me become non-existent"* — and is refused.)
- **Free will and responsibility** — John is judged for choices he genuinely could have made
  otherwise. Anti-fatalist.
- **The Second Coming, resurrection, and judgment are bodily and real**, read straight from
  1 Thess 4, Matt 24, and Rev 20 — not allegorized, and not premillennial (no earthly reign; the
  return goes straight to resurrection and the great white throne).

## When to use this skill

- **Drafting devotionals** in Brother Jim's voice — above all the *judgment / second-coming /
  "obey now, not later"* family of themes. The devotional built from this sermon lives at
  [devotionals/extreme-faith/the-day-christ-came.md](../../../devotionals/extreme-faith/the-day-christ-came.md).
- **Borrowing his cadence** — narrative on-ramp, the repeated line, the direct-address plea.
- **Measuring a draft against him** — does it call for a decision *today*? Does it keep baptism and
  obedience with grace? Does it build dread from ordinary detail rather than abstraction?
- **Discussing his preaching, theology, or this sermon.**

## What this skill is NOT for

- **Inventing biography.** Only the recording is evidence. Everything else is Adam's to supply
  (`references/biography.md`). Do not guess dates, congregations, or family.
- **Republishing the sermon as new content.** This is memorial and reference. A devotional *built
  from* it should credit him by name — the way he credited brother David Roper.
- **Flattening him into Matt.** They share a theological frame and a seriousness about judgment, but
  the voices differ: Matt is wry, bookish, aphoristic, first-person-confessional; Brother Jim is a
  narrative pulpit preacher who builds a single long story and pleads with his characters out loud.
