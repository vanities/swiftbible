# Matt Bassford — Voice analysis

Concrete rhetorical and syntactic patterns observed across ~30 sampled posts. Each tell has 2–4 examples with filenames so you can verify and find more.

## 1. Flat single-line confession

A bald admission of fault dropped as a complete paragraph. No qualifier. Then he builds his argument from there.

- *"I hold grudges. It is one of my biggest spiritual failings."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
- *"I am. I know it. ALS robs its victims of dignity along with everything else."* — `2022-12-07-disgusting.md`
- *"I'm not eager to die."* — `2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`
- *"By nature, I'm a planner."* — `2016-09-28-fixing-our-hope.md`

**Why it works:** lowers the reader's defenses before he asks them to look at themselves. Matt rarely indicts the reader before indicting himself first.

## 2. Inverted/paradox aphorism

Pivoting on a cliche, contradicting an expectation, or holding two opposing truths in a single line. He almost never wrote a meditation without one.

- *"Hell isn't other people. Heaven is."* — `2015-02-02-why-i-want-to-go-to-heaven.md`
- *"This world isn't wonderful, and it never will be. Our Savior is wonderful, and He never will be anything else."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`
- *"To live is Christ and to die is Christ."* — `2017-01-09-philippians-part-1.md`
- *"From a selfish perspective, heaven wins every time."* — `2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`

**How to write one:** find a cultural/cliché phrase ("hell is other people," "what a wonderful world," "to die is gain"). Either invert it ("hell isn't other people. Heaven is.") or own it harder than expected ("the fatal flaw of life under the sun is that it's fatal").

## 3. Concrete domestic detail as on-ramp

Posts open in laundry rooms, hair salons, with Boomer the chihuahua, with Marky on the couch. He resists abstract opens.

- *"My in-laws own a Chihuahua named Boomer."* — `2022-04-25-loyalty.md`
- *"Yesterday, I got my hair cut."* — `2022-04-05-passing-for-normal.md`
- *"So apparently I have pneumonia and a partially collapsed lung."* — `2023-01-23-white-noise.md`
- *"Of late, it seems like I've been collecting a lot of compliments about how well I have been handling the changes that ALS has brought."* — `2022-08-16-uselessness.md`

**Why it works:** the universal-second-person voice ("we," "you") feels more earned when it grows out of a specific, ordinary moment.

## 4. Verdict-style closing imperatives

Three to five short clauses, command voice, no flourish. His late closings are gavel-bangs, not prayers.

- *"Fear them. Hate them. Fling them away from you as you would a viper or scorpion. Now is the time, not tomorrow or the next new year."* — `2023-01-23-white-noise.md`
- *"Create. Give. Lose. The world will think you're an idiot, but God won't."* — `2022-05-04-create-more-than-you-consume.md`
- *"There is no other sensible way to live our lives than to go all-in on Jesus."* — `2016-09-28-fixing-our-hope.md`
- *"May I have the courage to take it."* — `2022-08-16-uselessness.md`
- *"This world isn't wonderful, and it never will be. Our Savior is wonderful, and He never will be anything else."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`

**Recipe:** state the verdict, then a single short imperative or a parallel triplet. Don't pivot into a prayer; let the verdict be the last word.

## 5. Bible-geek precision

Quotes "the back half of Phil 1:21," names paper weights and editors, distinguishes textual variants. He's a working preacher *and* a translation nerd, and he doesn't hide it.

- *"the back half of Philippians 1:21"* — `2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`
- *"the 38-gsm Tervakowski ThinOpaque in my Cambridge Wide-Margin"* — `2017-01-13-crossway-single-column-journaling-bible-preaching-teaching-and-study.md`
- *"Look here at Philippians 1:1-2."* — `2017-01-09-philippians-part-1.md`
- *"Consider Jesus' description of it in Mark 9:42-48."* — `2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md`

## 6. Self-aware aside in parentheses

Wry footnote on himself mid-argument. The parenthetical never advances the argument; it acknowledges that he's aware of how he sounds.

- *"(my poor wife has discovered far too often)"* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
- *"(though I certainly would have been thankful if they had!)"* — `2022-07-06-thoughts-and-prayers.md`
- *"(Really! I do!)"* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`

## 7. Specific congregation/place anchor

Names Westley Pollard at Dowlen Rd., Clay's rule of thumb, Jackson Heights, Joliet. He never writes "the Church" when he can write "the Joliet church" or "Jackson Heights."

- *"The most successful personal worker I've ever known is Westley Pollard, elder of the Dowlen Rd. church in Beaumont."* — `2022-04-05-passing-for-normal.md`
- *"I like Clay's rule of thumb here..."* — `2022-02-15-faith-in-the-storm.md`
- *"The congregation at Joliet likes to laugh at me because I constantly identify different passages as 'one of my favorites.'"* — `2015-02-02-why-i-want-to-go-to-heaven.md`

## 8. Dry sarcasm against secular pieties

Italicized or capitalized scorn aimed at progressive cliches, NBC News framing, "thoughts and prayers" mockery. He's gentle with believers and sharp with the secular optimism that competes with Christ.

- *"Of course, NBC painted her as a Symbol Of Hope amid devastation and despair, a promise of Better Days Ahead."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`
- *"Those who put their trust in princes would be well served to study some history and learn how trusting princes works out."* — `2022-07-06-thoughts-and-prayers.md`

## 9. The earned "I get you" / kin-address

Late in a hard piece, he turns and addresses fellow strugglers as kin — not as objects of his rebuke but as members of his own tribe.

- *"If you are a member of my grudge-holding tribe, rethink that."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
- *"I get you (Really! I do!), but it's time to do the hard work of letting go."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`

## 10. "Look at" / "Consider" pulpit imperative

Verb-first verse handoffs. Sermon-trained — he points at the text and tells you to look.

- *"Look here at Philippians 1:1-2."* — `2017-01-09-philippians-part-1.md`
- *"Consider Jesus' description of it in Mark 9:42-48."* — `2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md`

## Distinctive openings (5)

1. *"So apparently I have pneumonia and a partially collapsed lung."* — `2023-01-23-white-noise.md`
2. *"I hold grudges. It is one of my biggest spiritual failings."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
3. *"I am. I know it. ALS robs its victims of dignity along with everything else."* — `2022-12-07-disgusting.md`
4. *"Sons are hard. At least, I find it so with my son."* — `2022-09-20-sons.md`
5. *"This is the last time that I will ever stand before this congregation and preach a sermon."* — `2022-09-13-the-bible.md`

**Pattern:** stunningly direct opening sentence. No preamble. No setup. The reader is already inside the situation by the second sentence.

## Distinctive closings (5)

1. *"Fear them. Hate them. Fling them away from you as you would a viper or scorpion. Now is the time, not tomorrow or the next new year. Unless you act, the white noise of your sin will lull you into continued sleep."* — `2023-01-23-white-noise.md`
2. *"This world isn't wonderful, and it never will be. Our Savior is wonderful, and He never will be anything else."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`
3. *"Create. Give. Lose. The world will think you're an idiot, but God won't."* — `2022-05-04-create-more-than-you-consume.md`
4. *"May I have the courage to take it."* — `2022-08-16-uselessness.md`
5. *"Pray, then, and do not lose heart. Pray for me, and pray for all the other godly goals that are on your heart. Pray, and keep praying."* — `2022-07-06-thoughts-and-prayers.md`

## Things Matt rarely does

- **Lyrical/poetic prayer closings.** He almost never ends with a constructed prayer — he ends with a verdict or a kin-address.
- **News-feed concreteness.** Matt argues from Scripture and personal experience, rarely from headlines (though `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md` is a partial exception).
- **Long abstract intros.** He gets in fast.
- **Soft hedging.** When he believes something, he says it. He'll qualify on facts, not on conviction.

## Sentence rhythm patterns

### When he goes short

Matt deploys short sentences (under 8 words) at three specific moments:

1. **Arrival sentences** — when he wants the reader to land.
   - *"This is evil. It is wrong."* (`2023-01-02-grudges.md`)
   - *"Sin is fire, brethren! Sin is poison! Sin is death!"* (`2017-07-24-why-peter-failed.md`)
   - *"Easy is also deadly."* (`2017-07-24-why-peter-failed.md`)
   - *"Earning your way to heaven doesn't even work for apostles."* (`2021-08-18-the-rich-young-ruler-and-grace.md`)

2. **Sentence-fragment punctuation** — single-word or short-phrase paragraphs that register a turn.
   - *"Ouch."* (`2022-09-20-sons.md` — after a hard truth from his old friend)
   - *"Not so."* (`2022-09-13-the-bible.md` — refusing the "grace evaporates after baptism" framing)
   - *"No more."* (`2022-08-24-leaving-the-pulpit.md`)
   - *"And yet."* (`2022-05-04-create-more-than-you-consume.md` — pivoting after the Bezos description)

3. **Verdict closings** — covered in section 4 above.

### When he goes long

Long sentences (over 25 words) appear when he's working through theological argument or running a parallel-clause buildup. The long sentences are almost always *additive* — comma-cumulative or semicolon-cumulative — not Latinate-subordinate. He won't nest a clause inside a clause inside a clause; he'll lay them in a line.

- *"He had this moment in mind before He ever said 'Let there be light.' He has directed the history of the human race toward this point. There is no way that God, hours before the consummation of the ages, is going to say 'OK, fine, let's go to Plan B.'"* (`2017-07-24-why-peter-failed.md`)
- *"By His power, the dust of the slaughtered refugee, the COVID victim, and the ALS sufferer will be raised up to new life."* (`2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`)

### The colon-and-list move

Matt uses a colon to set up a deliberate three- or four-part list more often than most preachers. The list is almost never bulleted; it's run-in.

- *"All my life, I have wanted to be useful: to my family, to my friends, to my church, and to my God."* (`2022-08-16-uselessness.md`)
- *"Three guiding aims of the Christian, then, are these: Love God with everything you have. Love your neighbor as much as you love yourself. Love your brother more than you love yourself."* (`2015-02-02-why-i-want-to-go-to-heaven.md`)
- *"He gave us a conscience. We all had the opportunity to honor Him and walk righteously before Him. Did we take it? We did not."* (`2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md` — colon-style cadence without the literal colon)

### The em-dash for the sharp pivot

Em-dashes are reserved for the **sharp pivot** — the moment where he wants the reader to turn, fast.

- *"Most successful personal worker I've ever known is Westley Pollard, elder of the Dowlen Rd. church in Beaumont — When Lauren and I still lived there, she once ran into him at Walmart."* (`2022-04-05-passing-for-normal.md`)
- *"He gave us the opportunity to find salvation through His Son. Do most take advantage of that? They do not."* (`2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md`)

He does NOT use the em-dash for parenthetical asides — for those he uses literal parentheses (see tell #6 above). This is consistent across the archive.

## Transition phrases he favors

Watch for these transition phrases as Matt-voice signatures. Frequencies are number-of-posts-containing-the-phrase across the 1639-post archive (case-insensitive, counted via `grep -li`).

- **"However,"** — appears in 1242 posts. His default mid-paragraph pivot. Heavily used.

- **"Instead,"** — appears in 801 posts. The pivot from refusal to recommendation. *"Instead, our hope is in Jesus."* (`2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`); *"Instead, the losses that I mourn the most are of my opportunities to serve others."* (`2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`).

- **"Of course,"** — appears in 479 posts. Used to concede to an obvious objection in two words and roll past it. *"Of course, NBC painted her as a Symbol Of Hope…"* (`2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`); *"Of course, this appalling language is hardly gratuitous."* (`2022-12-07-disgusting.md`); *"Of course, when ALS takes my ability to speak…"* (`2022-08-16-uselessness.md`).

- **"Indeed,"** — appears in 399 posts. Used to reinforce. Where another writer would say "really" or "in fact," Matt writes *"Indeed, more subtly, the weakness that is the subject of Paul's boasting is not only the thorn in the flesh."* (`2022-02-10-a-man-is-judged-by-his-strength.md`); *"Indeed, the belief that we can rely on the latter is one of the great illusions of Western civilization."* (`2021-07-07-the-problem-of-other-peoples-suffering.md`).

- **"Years ago,"** — appears in 253 posts. His preferred memory-opener. Almost always followed by a contrast with the present: *"Years ago, I read this passage as Paul being Paul."* (`2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`).

- **"Nonetheless,"** — appears in 150 posts. Used to acknowledge a real concession before resuming the argument. *"Nonetheless, the people around me do not treat me poorly despite ample reason to do so."* (`2022-12-07-disgusting.md`); *"Nonetheless, my life does not allow me the luxury of wagging my finger at all those poor deluded souls…"* (`2016-09-28-fixing-our-hope.md`).

- **"Frankly,"** — appears in 57 posts. When he's about to say something he expects readers to push back on. *"Frankly, if our teaching on baptism doesn't start with the resurrection, we're doing it wrong."* (`2019-02-13-the-hope-that-is-in-us.md`); *"Frankly, I find this astounding."* (`2018-04-05-the-future-of-song-worship.md`); *"Frankly, I love everything about it."* (`2022-02-15-faith-in-the-storm.md`).

- **"Of late,"** — appears in only 8 posts (5 pre-ALS, 3 late). Rare, but when it appears in late voice it carries weight as a marker of recent change. *"Of late, it seems like I've been collecting a lot of compliments…"* (`2022-08-16-uselessness.md`); *"Of late, I have been drawn to James' discussion of suffering…"* (`2022-08-16-uselessness.md`); *"However, my opinion of Boomer has changed of late."* (`2022-04-25-loyalty.md`).

What he **doesn't** use much: "Notwithstanding," "Be that as it may," "On the other hand" (rare). His transitions are mostly the handful above, deployed in characteristic positions.

## Pre-ALS voice vs late voice — what's distinct?

The voice fingerprint is continuous across both periods. What changes is **what he does with it**. See `references/late-voice.md` for the full treatment, but in short:

**Pre-ALS register (2014–early 2021):**
- Longer pieces (Sermons run 3,000–5,000 words; even Meditations often over 1,000)
- Bolded sub-headers in sermons (FIRST, SECOND, THIRD)
- Argument-from-essayist's-setup opens (Facebook friend's post, news article, attendance sheet)
- Closings often "let's…" / "we should…" / "let us…" — corporate imperative
- More content-driven (CoC distinctives, hymn theory, translation reviews)
- Self-indictment present but not the *frame*

**Late voice (mid-2021 onwards):**
- Compressed pieces (Bulletin Articles 600–900 words; even Sermons trim down)
- Fewer or no bolded sub-headers
- Diagnosis-stamped opens that drop the reader cold into a situation
- Closings are gavel-bangs and verdicts, often first-person
- Re-reading move: "I thought I understood this verse. I didn't."
- Self-indictment as the frame; he indicts himself before turning to the reader
- Grace gets louder; obedience-policing gets quieter
- "Of late" / "At the end of my life" / "Now that I know that my life is going to be considerably shorter" become signature openers

When you want the late-voice register specifically, read the eight anchors listed in `references/few-shot-anchors.md`.

## When Matt uses questions, when he refuses to ask one

Matt asks a lot of rhetorical questions, and they have a specific rhythm. Watch for these:

- **The setup-and-flat-answer.** He poses the question, then answers it himself, often with a flat-confession-style answer.
  - *"What's God supposed to do? Confirm His word with lots of miracles? He's tried that lots of times. It didn't work. Reveal Himself directly to people? Last time God did that, they crucified Him."* (`2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md`)
  - *"Is the Lord a creator or a consumer?"* (`2022-05-04-create-more-than-you-consume.md`)
  - *"What, then, of my usefulness?"* (`2022-08-16-uselessness.md`)

- **The triplet question.** He stacks three parallel questions, often in a row, to drive the implication.
  - *"What if he never learns to apply himself? What if he wastes his potential in self-indulgence and self-pity?"* (`2022-09-20-sons.md`)
  - *"How about when you know that people just like you are being callously slaughtered, and you're fleeing for your life? How about when you look into your future and see a refugee camp or worse?"* (`2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`)

- **The hint-style question** that he immediately undercuts with the answer in the same sentence.
  - *"'Do I have to?' They ask. Hint: asking whether we have to carry out a difficult spiritual work is not usually from God."* (`2023-01-02-grudges.md`)

**When he refuses to ask:** When the answer would be obvious. He won't manipulate the reader with an obviously rigged question. If a verdict is the right tool, he uses the verdict — see his closings.

## Humor texture — what kind of funny he is

Matt's humor is **dry, specific, and self-deprecating, almost never aggressive toward people he knows.** When humor turns sharp, it turns on secular pieties or on himself.

- **Self-deprecation.** *"I'm a terrible poker player."* (`2016-01-15-powerball-and-covetousness.md`); *"I'm a writer, and sometimes it takes me 1500 words to say 'Good morning.'"* (`2015-10-20-talking-about-macy-part-1.md`); *"By contrast, the guy who is focused on what he wants is sitting at home on the couch watching *The Price Is Right*…"* (`2022-05-04-create-more-than-you-consume.md`).
- **Concrete absurdity in service of theology.** *"At my urging, my wife once put a set of rat traps in my mother-in-law's Christmas stocking to help her with her infestation."* (`2022-04-25-loyalty.md` — used to land that he was no fan of Boomer); *"the pickles in his storage room that were canned in 1994 are going away FOREVER"* (`2014-02-19-decluttering.md`).
- **Imagined Bible characters as commentary.** *"If Kermit The Frog were a Bible student, he might wonder why there are so many songs about Revelation 3:20."* (`2022-04-11-he-stands-at-the-door-and-knocks.md`); *"It's like pitting this year's NBA All-Star team against an all-star team of the greatest basketball players of all time."* (`2018-04-05-the-future-of-song-worship.md`).
- **Capitalization as scorn.** *"Symbol Of Hope amid devastation and despair, a promise of Better Days Ahead"* (`2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`); *"to Pay Good Money for things"* (`2014-02-19-decluttering.md`); *"Bible classes I love so much"* — italicized/capitalized for sarcastic emphasis.

What his humor is NOT: ironic detachment from his own positions, "edgy" punching down at outgroups, joke-for-joke's-sake one-liners disconnected from the theology, mockery of named individuals (the political pollster Boomer bit gets joked about, but the pollster isn't named or made a point of derision; the dog is). He's wry, not mean.
