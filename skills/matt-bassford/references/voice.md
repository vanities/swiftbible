# Matt Bassford — Voice analysis

Concrete rhetorical and syntactic patterns observed across ~30 sampled posts. Each tell has 2–4 examples with filenames so you can verify and find more.

## 1. Flat single-line confession

A bald admission of fault dropped as a complete paragraph. No qualifier. Then he builds his argument from there.

- *"I hold grudges. It is one of my biggest spiritual failings."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
- *"I am. I know it. ALS robs its victims of dignity along with everything else."* — `2022-12-07-disgusting.md`
- *"I'm not eager to die."* — `2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`
- *"By nature, I'm a planner."* — `2016-09-28-fixing-our-hope.md`

**Why it works:** lowers the reader's defenses before he asks them to look at themselves. Matt rarely indicts the reader before indicting himself first.

## 2. Inverted/paradox aphorism

Pivoting on a cliche, contradicting an expectation, or holding two opposing truths in a single line. He almost never wrote a meditation without one.

- *"Hell isn't other people. Heaven is."* — `2015-02-02-why-i-want-to-go-to-heaven.md`
- *"This world isn't wonderful, and it never will be. Our Savior is wonderful, and He never will be anything else."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`
- *"To live is Christ and to die is Christ."* — `2017-01-09-philippians-part-1.md`
- *"From a selfish perspective, heaven wins every time."* — `2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`

**How to write one:** find a cultural/cliché phrase ("hell is other people," "what a wonderful world," "to die is gain"). Either invert it ("hell isn't other people. Heaven is.") or own it harder than expected ("the fatal flaw of life under the sun is that it's fatal").

## 3. Concrete domestic detail as on-ramp

Posts open in laundry rooms, hair salons, with Boomer the chihuahua, with Marky on the couch. He resists abstract opens.

- *"My in-laws own a Chihuahua named Boomer."* — `2022-04-25-loyalty.md`
- *"Yesterday, I got my hair cut."* — `2022-04-05-passing-for-normal.md`
- *"So apparently I have pneumonia and a partially collapsed lung."* — `2023-01-23-white-noise.md`
- *"Of late, it seems like I've been collecting a lot of compliments about how well I have been handling the changes that ALS has brought."* — `2022-08-16-uselessness.md`

**Why it works:** the universal-second-person voice ("we," "you") feels more earned when it grows out of a specific, ordinary moment.

## 4. Verdict-style closing imperatives

Three to five short clauses, command voice, no flourish. His late closings are gavel-bangs, not prayers.

- *"Fear them. Hate them. Fling them away from you as you would a viper or scorpion. Now is the time, not tomorrow or the next new year."* — `2023-01-23-white-noise.md`
- *"Create. Give. Lose. The world will think you're an idiot, but God won't."* — `2022-05-04-create-more-than-you-consume.md`
- *"There is no other sensible way to live our lives than to go all-in on Jesus."* — `2016-09-28-fixing-our-hope.md`
- *"May I have the courage to take it."* — `2022-08-16-uselessness.md`
- *"This world isn't wonderful, and it never will be. Our Savior is wonderful, and He never will be anything else."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`

**Recipe:** state the verdict, then a single short imperative or a parallel triplet. Don't pivot into a prayer; let the verdict be the last word.

## 5. Bible-geek precision

Quotes "the back half of Phil 1:21," names paper weights and editors, distinguishes textual variants. He's a working preacher *and* a translation nerd, and he doesn't hide it.

- *"the back half of Philippians 1:21"* — `2022-01-11-to-live-is-christ-to-die-is-gain.md`
- *"the 38-gsm Tervakowski ThinOpaque in my Cambridge Wide-Margin"* — `2017-01-13-crossway-single-column-journaling-bible-preaching-teaching-and-study.md`
- *"Look here at Philippians 1:1-2."* — `2017-01-09-philippians-part-1.md`
- *"Consider Jesus' description of it in Mark 9:42-48."* — `2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md`

## 6. Self-aware aside in parentheses

Wry footnote on himself mid-argument. The parenthetical never advances the argument; it acknowledges that he's aware of how he sounds.

- *"(my poor wife has discovered far too often)"* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
- *"(though I certainly would have been thankful if they had!)"* — `2022-07-06-thoughts-and-prayers.md`
- *"(Really! I do!)"* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`

## 7. Specific congregation/place anchor

Names Westley Pollard at Dowlen Rd., Clay's rule of thumb, Jackson Heights, Joliet. He never writes "the Church" when he can write "the Joliet church" or "Jackson Heights."

- *"The most successful personal worker I've ever known is Westley Pollard, elder of the Dowlen Rd. church in Beaumont."* — `2022-04-05-passing-for-normal.md`
- *"I like Clay's rule of thumb here..."* — `2022-02-15-faith-in-the-storm.md`
- *"The congregation at Joliet likes to laugh at me because I constantly identify different passages as 'one of my favorites.'"* — `2015-02-02-why-i-want-to-go-to-heaven.md`

## 8. Dry sarcasm against secular pieties

Italicized or capitalized scorn aimed at progressive cliches, NBC News framing, "thoughts and prayers" mockery. He's gentle with believers and sharp with the secular optimism that competes with Christ.

- *"Of course, NBC painted her as a Symbol Of Hope amid devastation and despair, a promise of Better Days Ahead."* — `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md`
- *"Those who put their trust in princes would be well served to study some history and learn how trusting princes works out."* — `2022-07-06-thoughts-and-prayers.md`

## 9. The earned "I get you" / kin-address

Late in a hard piece, he turns and addresses fellow strugglers as kin — not as objects of his rebuke but as members of his own tribe.

- *"If you are a member of my grudge-holding tribe, rethink that."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`
- *"I get you (Really! I do!), but it's time to do the hard work of letting go."* — `2023-01-02-grudges.md`

## 10. "Look at" / "Consider" pulpit imperative

Verb-first verse handoffs. Sermon-trained — he points at the text and tells you to look.

- *"Look here at Philippians 1:1-2."* — `2017-01-09-philippians-part-1.md`
- *"Consider Jesus' description of it in Mark 9:42-48."* — `2021-11-11-straight-talk-about-hell.md`

**Pattern:** stunningly direct opening sentence. No preamble. No setup. The reader is already inside the situation by the second sentence.

## Things Matt rarely does

- **Lyrical/poetic prayer closings.** He almost never ends with a constructed prayer — he ends with a verdict or a kin-address.
- **News-feed concreteness.** Matt argues from Scripture and personal experience, rarely from headlines (though `2022-03-17-its-not-a-wonderful-world.md` is a partial exception).
- **Long abstract intros.** He gets in fast.
- **Soft hedging.** When he believes something, he says it. He'll qualify on facts, not on conviction.
