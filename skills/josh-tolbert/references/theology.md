# Josh Tolbert — theology

**Read the caveat first.** Josh is living, and this file is assembled from 73 transcripts of Bible
classes and occasional sermons, not from anything he wrote to state his beliefs. He is a *teacher
working through a text*, and he says so — he marks his confidence constantly, changes his mind out
loud, and corrects himself the following week. Nothing here should be reported as "Josh Tolbert
believes X" without the citation attached and the hedge intact.

Each claim below is graded:

- **Stated** — he says it plainly, more than once or at length.
- **Stated once** — clear, but resting on a single passage in a single recording.
- **Implied** — consistent with everything he does, never asserted.
- **Unconfirmed — needs Adam** — the corpus is too thin or too tentative to say fairly.

Citations are `filename:line`, relative to `speakers/josh-tolbert/sermons/`.

---

## The distinctive thing

His theological contribution in this corpus is not a set of positions — those are the ordinary
non-institutional Church of Christ ones. It is an **epistemology of doctrine**: a sustained
insistence that you must be able to show the passage, and that "this is what I was taught" is not
an answer. He applies it hardest to his own tradition.

**Stated.**

> "Sometimes we fall into the trap of giving an answer that is really an I statement more than it's
> a God statement." — `…you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt:661–662`

> "They'll say, why don't we have women preachers? Well, because only the men are supposed to do
> that. But how do you know? Because that's the doctrine I was taught. That's really the answer
> that they're giving, is I have a doctrine I was taught, but I don't really know why we don't let
> women do that. I don't really know."
> — `…you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt:649–656`

> "I have always heard phrases like, we speak where the Bible speaks, we're silent where the Bible
> is silent. Which is not always true, even though we have good intentions when we say that."
> — `…why-i-am-still-a-christian-…-1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:46–48`

The same move in the Old Testament: *"I may run into the danger of using my definition of blameless
rather than trying to fit with God's definition."*
— `…god-s-definition-of-holy-leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:40`

## Scripture

**Inspiration and reliability — stated.** He treats the text as true and as predictively
prophetic, and meets critical arguments on evidence rather than dismissing them.

> "I fully reject it. I think it's written by Isaiah or written by the disciples of Isaiah. I'll go
> with that, because after all, our Gospels were not written by Jesus, but they're about Jesus, so
> I'm okay with that … but I'm not willing to go further than direct apostle, direct or direct
> disciple, just like I'm not willing to take a gospel of Jesus written a few centuries later."
> — `…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:50–55`

Note the shape: he rejects multiple-authorship theories of Isaiah while conceding a *disciples-of*
option, and draws his line at direct discipleship. That is a hedged position, not a slogan.

**Canon — stated once**, at length in one sermon. He describes actually working the question:
*"How can I know that these are the right books? … There's apocryphal books that the Catholics are
still using. There's apocryphal books that the Catholics even are not using."*
— `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:131–134`. He appeals to ordinary textual-transmission scholarship rather than
to internal testimony alone (`…:172–178`).

**Extra-biblical sources — stated.** Usable, but discounted openly: Josephus exaggerates
(`…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:236`), Maccabean writers and Josephus both flatter themselves
(`…nehemiah-9-11-2024-mIPxeJ3wYPY.txt:413`), Google is unreliable and premillennial
(`…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:78–80`), Talmudic material is worth bringing in but is late
(`…nehemiah-7-3-2024-UywwHpoFciA.txt:247`).

**Reading the Bible badly — stated.** *"It's possible to read the book and come out with a wrong
conclusion if you are not willing to listen to actually what it is saying. If I use the book as a
weapon, if I use the book to justify my position…"* — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:162–167`

## Old Testament law, and why it is for Christians

**Stated.** The Leviticus series exists because he thinks the law is where God's mind is legible,
and that the New Testament vocabulary is unreadable without it.

> "You want to know the mind of God? Leviticus is it. … And it's going to make things clearer in
> the New Testament. When he talks about being blameless, how do we know what blameless really
> means?" — `…leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:33–38`

**Typology — stated.** The old covenant anchors and foreshadows the new; the remnant that returned
from exile prefigures a second and final one in Christ.

> "Deuteronomy's covenant of Moab is a foreshadowing of our relationship to the coming Messiah."
> — `…from-creation-to-eternity-…-W96EXQbSqIU.txt:453–454`

**Discontinuity is real and explained — stated once.** Used as an argument *against* the Quran:
God's law changes from Moses to Christ, but scripture explains why it changed, and the Quran does
not (`…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:210–215`).

## Grace, works, and salvation

**Stated.** He affirms salvation by grace and is explicit that grace is not earned — while equally
explicit that the question "how then should we act?" is not thereby closed.

> "At the end of the day, grace is, I messed up. Please save me anyways."
> — `…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:234–235`

> "You and I, if you take out the blood of Christ, we'll never see God … He decided this. We were
> irrelevant. He said, if you say to me, I want to be with you, that's sufficient. How should his
> people act? We already have salvation. So what should we be? How should we act? Does it matter?
> These are things that we have grappled with for 2,000 years."
> — `…deuteronomy-1-29-2025-OZ6AIKT5HXo.txt:588–595`

> "Grace saves you because you have turned and cried out to him for help."
> — `…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:617–618`

He names unconditional election and once-saved-always-saved as one end of the spectrum and
declines them (`…deuteronomy-1-29-2025-OZ6AIKT5HXo.txt:596–600`), but the discussion in the
transcript is a survey of options rather than a developed refutation.

**Baptism — implied, plus autobiography.** He was baptised at ten, out of fear, and does not think
fear was the wrong motive: *"I don't think that's necessarily wrong because what it means is that
you have transitioned from I learned information to I believed the information I was taught."*
— `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:65–66`. The congregation's position that baptism is for salvation appears only
as an objection he reports outsiders making (`…you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt:630–631`) — he
clearly holds it, but **he never argues for it in this corpus.**

## The Christian life

**Stated.** Being a Christian is not a feeling and not an inheritance; it is discernment plus
deliberate action.

> "A child of darkness says, I think this is what God would like me to do. A child of light says, I
> know this is what God wants me to do because I did some investigation."
> — `…becoming-children-of-light-uVeYKpFz4GE.txt:188–189`

> "I don't understand someone who says, I want to worship God, and doesn't take a moment to wonder
> how God wants to be worshipped." — `…becoming-children-of-light-uVeYKpFz4GE.txt:177–179`

**Suffering — stated, at length.** Faith does not remove sorrow, and sorrow is not evidence of
missing faith. Jesus' incarnation is for solidarity in suffering, not merely for knowledge of it
(`…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:575–580`). *"That's what life is — it is suffering with
brief moments of okay and occasional joy."* (`:564–566`). His prescription is purpose over emotion,
connection with God, and focus on others (`:547–582`).

**Cost — stated once.** *"There are things Christians lose when we become Christians. And sometimes
people go back. And those people can become very angry towards God and other Christians."*
— `…deuteronomy-1-1-2025-BValdlu33Zg.txt:470–472`

## Worship

**Stated once, in two 2021 classes.** A hymn must carry revelation, knowledge, prophecy, or
teaching; a song's origin matters less than whether it is scriptural; the congregation edits
unscriptural lines out of otherwise good hymns rather than dropping them
(`…singing-with-understanding-…-jQ94aWFjt_c.txt:19–31`,
`…does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-…-1C72Yy135MA.txt`). **Caution:** these two files are the most
dialogic in the corpus; several of the strongest sentences in them belong to class members, not to
Josh. See the worked example in [`teaching-method.md`](teaching-method.md).

## Apologetics

**Stated, in one sermon.** He surveys and rejects Islam, modern Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism,
Norse/Egyptian/Babylonian/Sumerian paganism, Mormonism, Jehovah's Witnesses, Catholicism, and
mainstream Protestantism, giving a specific reason for each rather than a blanket dismissal
(`…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:205–320`). Two things worth noting about *how* he does it:

- He concedes what he finds attractive: *"honestly I think I would probably be Hindu before I would
  be Islamic or following Judaism. The idea of rebirth and karma makes a lot of sense, and it is a
  very Christian idea."* — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:249–251`
- He rates opposing arguments fairly before answering them: of Neil deGrasse Tyson's argument from
  the history of explanation, *"That makes good sense, doesn't it? That's a rational argument."*
  — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:380`

**Creation — stated once.** He accepts microevolution and rejects macroevolution, arguing from
what he considers evolutionary disadvantages rather than from a young-earth timeline
(`…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:341–370`). **He does not state a position on the age of the earth or on the
length of the Genesis days.** Unconfirmed — needs Adam.

## Compared with Matt Bassford

Same congregation, same fellowship, same positions — different centre of gravity. Matt argues from
the New Testament epistles and turns on a polished sentence; Josh argues from Old Testament
narrative and history and turns on a reconstructed background. Where Matt states a conclusion, Josh
states a confidence level. See [Matt's theology reference](../../matt-bassford/references/theology.md).
