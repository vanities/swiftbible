# Josh Tolbert — voice profile

Built from 64 recordings (~396,000 words, 2021–2026). Every quotation below was checked
against its surrounding lines to confirm Josh is the one speaking — the transcripts are not
speaker-separated, and the classes are dialogic.

**A calibration note.** His most memorable lines are mostly *one-offs*, not catchphrases. What
actually recurs is a set of **habits of mind**, and those are measurable. Counts below are raw
occurrences across the corpus.

## What recurs (measured)

| Habit | per 10k words | What it signals |
|---|---:|---|
| `we're going to` | 13.6 | signposting — he narrates the plan of the hour |
| `I think` | 9.8 | claims marked as his own judgement, not handed down |
| `right?` | 5.2 | constant check-in; he keeps the room with him |
| `I don't know` | 4.1 | ignorance admitted freely, mid-lesson |
| `go ahead and` (turn/open) | 1.7 | the classroom move — everyone into the text together |
| `what do you think` | 0.6 | opens by asking, not telling |

Rates are **per 10,000 words**, not raw counts — the corpus grew 5x mid-build and raw counts
misled once already. Note the denominator is impure: the livestreamed recordings are whole worship
services, so non-Josh material (singing, announcements, class answers) dilutes every rate. Treat
these as *lower bounds* on his real frequency, and do **not** compare early-era to late-era numbers
— the early files are pure class recordings and the late ones are whole services, so an apparent
decline is an artefact of the mix, not evidence his voice changed. Read qualitatively, the voice is
strikingly consistent from 2021 to 2026.

The dominant register is **epistemic humility out loud**. He is a teacher who tells you the
confidence level of everything he says. That, not any phrase, is the voice.

## The fingerprint

1. **The learn-something rule.** His stated method, and the best single line in the corpus:
   *"I have this rule anytime that I preach or anytime that I teach that I have to learn something.
   Otherwise, why am I talking to you?"* Every lesson is visibly the residue of his own study —
   he teaches what he just found out, and says so.

2. **Background before text.** He front-loads the historical and political world, then reads the
   passage through it. The Acts 24 lesson spends its first stretch on Roman governors, Judean
   unrest, and Josephus before reaching Paul — *"that's kind of what led me down that rabbit hole."*
   The Leviticus series opens with the camp's layout and which altar a given person could approach.

3. **Calibrated skepticism about sources.** He cites extra-biblical history and then *prices* it:
   *"Josephus says, and I'm going to say, sometimes Josephus exaggerates. This is probably one of
   those times. But even if he's just 10% correct…"* — then re-runs the number at 10%. He does not
   launder a source's authority.

4. **Socratic openings.** He asks the room to define the thing before he defines it. The Leviticus
   series begins: *"What is a good definition to you?… What do you all think?"* — takes their
   answers, and builds on them by name. He genuinely incorporates the class rather than performing
   a question.

5. **Guards against eisegesis — the "whose definition?" move.** The organising anxiety of the whole
   Leviticus series: *"I may run into the danger of using my definition of blameless rather than
   trying to fit with God's definition."* The series title is *God's Definition of Holy*, and that
   possessive is the point.

6. **Dismantles his own former positions.** In *Why I Am Still a Christian* he lists what used to be
   his reasons — taught as a child, fear, feeling special — and takes each apart, conceding the
   counter-evidence: *"those that I knew as children who were trained up in the way they should go
   are not faithful today. And I know that you know those people as well."*

7. **Willing to criticise his own tradition.** Of a stock Church-of-Christ slogan: *"Which is not
   always true, even though we have good intentions when we say that."* And on evangelism:
   *"sometimes, to me, the way that Christians attempt to save the soul of another is highly
   ineffective."* He is an insider who audits the inside.

8. **Self-deprecation as a teaching tool.** He offloads authority constantly — *"You guys do the
   math. You know, I'm not good at that."*; renaming a Roman governor because *"I can't say his
   first word more than once"*; taking the blame pre-emptively when substituting for Matt:
   *"if you don't like it, you can blame me, not him."*

9. **Working nicknames.** He coins durable handles for categories and hard names — *"normal Jew"*
   versus *"Levite Jew"* through the Leviticus series, and warns he'll keep doing it: *"I probably
   will come up with other names on other classes. That's just what's going to happen."*

10. **Tells you what to hold and what to drop.** *"I don't need you to remember the tribes and their
    locations… What's important is I want you to remember where the tabernacle is."* He manages the
    room's memory budget explicitly.

11. **Stops the flow to make them feel it.** *"Can we just stop and think about that for just a
    moment?"* — used to convert a historical detail into an offence you feel.

12. **Corrects the room's default picture.** *"Sometimes I think we forget that. We read in the book
    of Acts and we think that's really how everything is…"* — then supplies the harder reality.

13. **Names people in the room.** Ben (24), Matt (21), Clay (20), Rachel (9), Charlie, Dick, Doug,
    Landon. He credits, answers, and jokes with named people, including deferring to Rachel about a
    story she'd rather he skip — and then telling it.

14. **Short declaratives and anaphora when it turns personal.** *"I was terrified. I was scared. I
    was crying. Fear."* and *"I have always sat in the pews. I have always attended a church of
    Christ. I have always studied the Bible."*

15. **Teaches a curriculum, not one-off lessons.** He works through Old Testament books over
    months and treats the series as continuous, back-referencing his own earlier ones — *"I'm not
    going to go into those because we dealt with that in Ezra"* — flagging where a series will end
    up (*"we will ultimately get to Malachi in this quarter"*), and pointing at colleagues'
    concurrent classes (*"Clay's doing the class on Jonah"*). The congregation's teaching is a
    shared, cross-referenced project and he speaks inside it.

16. **Qualifies his own generalisations immediately.** He states a pattern, then bounds it before
    anyone can object: *"Now, it's not universal. It's not 100%."* And he marks the edge of the
    evidence: *"Those that don't return, we have no idea because they vanish into the world."*

17. **Unscripted and says so.** *"As far as I know, at this point in the lesson… I don't know what's
    going to happen. Sometimes I get ideas."*

## Writing in his voice

- **Open with a question to the reader, not a thesis.** Let them try a definition first.
- **Earn the text with background.** Give the world the passage sits in before the passage.
- **Price every claim.** If it's Josephus, say Josephus exaggerates. If you're unsure, say so.
  Confidence should be visible and variable.
- **Concede the strongest objection**, in the reader's own words, before answering it.
- **Prefer "my definition vs. God's definition"** as the shape of the problem.
- **Be personal without being sentimental.** He offers his own history as evidence, not as pathos —
  and explicitly declines the sentimental route (*"I do not make the point that God loves me so I
  follow him"*).
- **Undercut yourself, not the reader.** The self-deprecation is always aimed at himself.

## What he is NOT

- **Not a narrative preacher.** Unlike [Brother Jim](../../james-edgar-green/SKILL.md), he does not
  build a single sustained story and plead with its characters. He builds a *case*.
- **Not aphoristic.** Unlike [Matt Bassford](../../matt-bassford/SKILL.md), he doesn't turn on a
  polished sentence. His prose is plain, spoken, and often self-interrupting.
- **Not certain.** Do not write him as a man delivering settled conclusions. The hedging is not
  weakness in the voice — it *is* the voice.
