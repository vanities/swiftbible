# Josh Tolbert — recurring themes

Every quote below was located in a transcript and read in context to confirm Josh is the

read around it before reusing anything. The transcripts are **not** speaker-separated.

## 1. "Whose definition?" — the danger of reading yourself into the text

The organising anxiety of his teaching, and the explicit thesis of the Leviticus series
(*God's Definition of Holy*). He does not want a word to mean what he already assumed it meant.

**Anchors**

| Recording | Why |
|---|---|
| `20210728-…-god-s-definition-of-holy-leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt` | series opener; states the thesis outright |
| `20210804-…-god-s-definition-of-holy-pt-3-7-21--HgVfm5s023Q.txt` | sin and guilt offerings; the definition applied |
| `20211203-fall-focus-series-becoming-children-of-light-uVeYKpFz4GE.txt` | the same move in the New Testament |
| `20220725-sunday-evening-worship-you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt` | the move applied to doctrine, not just vocabulary |

> "When he talks about being blameless, how do we know what blameless really means? Unless you
> have some understanding about how God's mind functions and how he thinks. Otherwise, I may run
> into the danger of using my definition of blameless rather than trying to fit with God's
> definition."
> — `…leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:38–40`

> "A child of darkness says, I think this is what God would like me to do. A child of light says,
> I know this is what God wants me to do because I did some investigation."
> — `…becoming-children-of-light-uVeYKpFz4GE.txt:188–189`

> "I don't understand someone who says, I want to worship God, and doesn't take a moment to wonder
> how God wants to be worshipped. I don't really get that."
> — `…becoming-children-of-light-uVeYKpFz4GE.txt:177–179`

## 2. Background first — the political world *is* the exegesis

He reconstructs the world around a passage before he reads it, sometimes for half the hour. This
is the single most visible structural habit in the corpus.

## 3. Exile, remnant, and return

The spine running through Ezra, Nehemiah, Isaiah, and his topical work. The pattern he keeps
tracing: God's people fail, are scattered, a remnant returns, and that return is itself a
foreshadowing of a second and final one in Christ.

**Anchors**

| Recording | Why |
|---|---|
| `20240512-sunday-am-worship-from-creation-to-eternity-05-12-2024-W96EXQbSqIU.txt` | the whole arc in one lesson; Haggai → Micah → Isaiah → Malachi |
| `20240704-wednesday-bible-class-nehemiah-7-3-2024-UywwHpoFciA.txt` | why the returners became monotheists |
| Ezra series (5 transcripts, Apr–Jun 2024) | the first return, worked verse by verse |
| Isaiah series (14 transcripts, 2026) | the prophetic side of the same pattern |

> "That covenant that if we will not follow God, and we walk in the sins of the people of this
> world, and we turn aside, and we are cast into the darkness of sin, that if we turn and return
> to him, we will be a remnant. … Deuteronomy's covenant of Moab is a foreshadowing of our
> relationship to the coming Messiah."
> — `…from-creation-to-eternity-…-W96EXQbSqIU.txt:449–454`

> "They go to exile and they become monotheistic. They isolate themselves to their own people,
> those that return do. Those that don't return, we have no idea because they vanish into the
> world."
> — `…nehemiah-7-3-2024-UywwHpoFciA.txt:279–281`

## 4. Faith that has been stress-tested — and the reasons that did not survive

His most personal material. He spent about a year on the question after friends he grew up with
left the faith. He dismantles his own former reasons before offering current ones, and he refuses
the sentimental route.

**Anchors**

| Recording | Note |
|---|---|
| `20250331-why-i-am-still-a-christian-3-30-2025-1QkhEtf0QU4.txt` | the lesson on its own — **start here** |
| `20250331-sunday-morning-worship-why-i-am-still-a-christian-033025-yVZb4YFZfok.txt` | the same sermon inside the whole service; independently transcribed, so punctuation and small wordings differ |

> "While a true statement, those that I knew as children who were trained up in the way they should
> go are not faithful today. And I know that you know those people as well."
> — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:36–38`

> "One thing you will notice in this lesson is, I do not make the point that God loves me, so I
> follow him. It's never really mattered all that much to me."
> — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:22–24`

> "I did the legwork. If you are here and you are a Christian and you doubt, you have the
> responsibility to do the legwork … I did not use any emotional arguments here. Emotions are
> fickle. I use math. I don't even like math, but one plus one is two."
> — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:457–466`

> "Currently in 2025 there is no argument that I know of that can do that."
> — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:475`

## 5. Depression, suffering, and the refusal to make faith a cure

He argues against a slogan he has seen on church signs, and does it by reading a large amount of
scripture rather than by asserting.

**Anchor:** `20240819-sunday-pm-worship-depression-with-faith-8-18-2024-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt`
(a lesson he says is one piece of a larger, still-unfinished series on faith — line 211).

> "I have seen signs that say something like, where sadness ends, faith begins. So if you have
> sadness, you can't have faith, is the argument. And I disagree."
> — `…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:218–220`

> "We are going to read a massive amount of scripture because I'm not developing Josh's argument.
> I'm developing what the scriptures say."
> — `…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:221–222`

> "Let me tell you that's what humanity is … it is suffering with brief moments of okay and
> occasional joy."
> — `…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:565–566`

> "He had to come down to go through the suffering so that when you and I stand before him and we
> say, I am depressed, he says, yeah. Yeah. And we are comforted because we saw him be depressed."
> — `…depression-with-faith-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:578–579`

Related, in a different key: `20251016-wednesday-fall-focus-avoiding-vengeance-10-15-2025-0TU7R3vJifo.txt`
(what to *do* with an enemy's fall, reframed from a prohibition into an instruction).

## 6. "An I statement, not a God statement" — authority and inherited doctrine

He holds standard non-institutional Church of Christ positions but is sharply critical of holding
them *because they were handed down*. The demand is that you can show the passage.

**Anchors**

| Recording | Why |
|---|---|
| `20220725-sunday-evening-worship-you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt` | Korah's rebellion; the clearest statement in the corpus |
| `20210512-…-does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-5-09--1C72Yy135MA.txt` | the same test applied to a practical worship question |
| `20250331-why-i-am-still-a-christian-3-30-2025-1QkhEtf0QU4.txt` | audits a stock slogan of his own tradition |

> "Sometimes we fall into the trap of giving an answer that is really an I statement more than it's
> a God statement. … We need to know our scriptures well enough to be able to answer them with
> scripture, because otherwise we're answering with ourselves and I don't know about you guys but
> I don't have a lot of power."
> — `…you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt:661–667`

> "They'll say, why don't we have women preachers? Well, because only the men are supposed to do
> that. But how do you know? Because that's the doctrine I was taught. That's really the answer
> that they're giving … but I don't really know why we don't let women do that."
> — `…you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt:649–655`

> "I have always heard phrases like, we speak where the Bible speaks, we're silent where the Bible
> is silent. Which is not always true, even though we have good intentions when we say that."
> — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:46–48`

## 7. Singing you can explain

Two Sunday-AM classes in 2021, taught while substituting for Matt Bassford, on whether a hymn's
origin or its ordinary secular use bears on singing it in worship. Unusually dialogic even by his
standards — **the highest mis-attribution risk in the corpus.**

**Anchors**

- `20210428-sunday-am-bible-study-singing-with-understanding-pt-3-4-25-2-jQ94aWFjt_c.txt`
- `20210512-sunday-am-bible-study-does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-5-09--1C72Yy135MA.txt`

> "How will I benefit you? Or if I come to you singing, how will I benefit you unless I bring you
> some revelation or knowledge or prophecy or teaching. Do you guys have any initial thoughts …
> What about from the song leaders? You are literally coming to the congregation singing. What does
> this verse indicate to you you need to do in order to prepare?"
> — `…singing-with-understanding-…-jQ94aWFjt_c.txt:23–27`

> "Joyful, joyful, we adore thee always gets me, too, because it's built off of Ode to Joy. And I
> always have a little stutter with it because I played that in instruments and all of that."
> — `…does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-…-1C72Yy135MA.txt:34–37`

## 8. Defending the text against its critics — on evidence, not assertion

He knows the critical arguments, states them fairly, and then says why he rejects them. He is
equally willing to say the popular Christian answer is bad.

**Anchors**

| Recording | Argument met |
|---|---|
| `20260416-wednesday-bible-class-isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt` | multiple-authorship ("divisionist") theories of Isaiah |
| `20250102-wednesday-bible-class-deuteronomy-1-1-2025-BValdlu33Zg.txt` | late-dating of Deuteronomy, via Canaanite fortification |
| `20250331-why-i-am-still-a-christian-3-30-2025-1QkhEtf0QU4.txt` | canon, textual transmission, other religions, evolution |
| `20220120-sunday-pm-worship-paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt` | how to *use* a hostile or inflated source |

> "I fully reject it. I think it's written by Isaiah or written by the disciples of Isaiah. I'll go
> with that, because after all, our Gospels were not written by Jesus, but they're about Jesus …
> but I'm not willing to go further than direct apostle, direct or direct disciple."
> — `…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:50–54`

> "Josephus says, and I'm going to say, sometimes Josephus exaggerates. This is probably one of
> those times. But even if he's just 10% correct, this is a massive amount of people."
> — `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:236`

> "Some of my classes that you have sat through, I will say things in them because I know you're
> going to hear those arguments. I don't believe them. I think that they're garbage arguments. But
> you're going to hear them. And if you're not prepared for that, you've never heard it in your
> life, it could floor you."
> — `…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:150–154`

## Themes the corpus does *not* support well

Say so rather than reaching. **Unconfirmed — needs Adam** for any of these:

- **Marriage, parenting, and family life as a subject.** He mentions a wife and a brother in
  passing (`…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:113`) and uses family analogies, but there is no lesson on the topic.
- **Evangelism method.** He criticises how Christians evangelise
  (`…1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:20–21`) without ever teaching a positive alternative at length.
- **Eschatology.** He rejects premillennialism in a one-line aside
  (`…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:79`) and nowhere develops a position.
- **Church organisation, elders/deacons, benevolence, institutionalism.** Effectively absent,
  which is notable given the fellowship.
