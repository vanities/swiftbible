# Josh Tolbert — few-shot anchors

Ready-to-paste excerpts for prompting an AI to write in his voice. Every one was located in a
transcript, read in context, and confirmed to be Josh speaking — not a class member, not the man
doing announcements, not Matt Bassford. Citations are `filename:line` relative to
`speakers/josh-tolbert/sermons/`; go re-read the surrounding lines before you extend an anchor.

**These are spoken transcripts.** Line breaks fall where the ASR segmented the audio, not where he
would have put them. Two of the anchors below come from a cut of a sermon that was transcribed
with almost no punctuation — quoted here as-is so you can see what the corpus actually looks like.

**Use two or three at once, not one.** A single anchor teaches the topic; several teach the voice.

---

## Anchor 1 — the method statement

> You can go ahead and open up your Bibles to Acts 24. As far as I know, at this point in the
> lesson, we're going to spend the majority of our time in Acts 24. I don't know what's going to
> happen. Sometimes I get ideas. But this week, I believe we're in Acts 24 in the scripture reading.
> So I was talking it over with Clay, and he said, picked something from the scripture reading, so
> I did. And I looked through Acts 24. Acts 24 is Paul standing before Felix. And I was like,
> that's really cool. It's a neat event. There's not a lot of new in Acts 24. And I have this rule
> anytime that I preach or anytime that I teach that I have to learn something. Otherwise, why am I
> talking to you? So I learned some things.

`20220120-sunday-pm-worship-paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:169–179`

**Use for:** opening a lesson; showing his self-auditing register; any piece where the writer is
visibly reporting his own study rather than delivering settled conclusions.
**Do NOT use for:** anything that needs to sound composed or final. This is deliberately loose.

## Anchor 2 — the Socratic opening

> As we get into this, I've titled this series God's definition of holiness. And so I kind of want
> to start there. What is a good definition to you? One that you guys can use of holiness. What do
> you all think? … Okay. So right off the bat, that's a comparison, right? There has to be
> something impure for me to know what is pure. … Any other definitions? How about from this side?
> … Yeah, exhibiting the attributes of God, very good. How do we know what the attributes of God
> are? He had to tell us. And that's the purpose of the book of Leviticus.

`20210728-…-god-s-definition-of-holy-leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:3–16`
(the omitted lines are class members' one-word answers — *"Pure." "Separated, separate."*)

**Use for:** an opening that hands the question to the reader before answering it; a definitions
lesson; anything where the point is that the answer must come from God rather than from us.
**Do NOT use for:** solo prose with no audience. The whole move depends on a room answering back —
in writing you have to fake the beat, and it can read as a rhetorical question, which it is not.

## Anchor 3 — priced sources and reconstructed background

> Cumanus. I'm gonna call him Cumanus because I can't say his first word more than once, but
> Cumanus was a typical Roman political individual. He is not moral in any way, shape, or form what
> you and I would understand as morality …
>
> Josephus says, and I'm going to say, sometimes Josephus exaggerates. This is probably one of
> those times. But even if he's just 10% correct, this is a massive amount of people.

`20220120-…-paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:197–199` and `:236`

**Use for:** historical or background writing; teaching someone to cite a source and discount it in
the same breath; the working-nickname habit.
**Do NOT use for:** devotional or pastoral writing. This is the archivist gear, and it is dry.

## Anchor 4 — dismantling his own former reasons

> While a true statement, those that I knew as children who were trained up in the way they should
> go are not faithful today. And I know that you know those people as well. There was a time in my
> life that honestly, that was the biggest reason. My parents brought me to church. I have never
> gone a period of my life not coming to church. It's always been a thing. I have always sat in the
> pews. I have always attended a church of Christ. I have always studied the Bible. I have always
> heard phrases like, we speak where the Bible speaks, we're silent where the Bible is silent.
> Which is not always true, even though we have good intentions when we say that.

`20250331-why-i-am-still-a-christian-3-30-2025-1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:36–48`

**Use for:** first-person testimony that is argued rather than felt; conceding the counter-evidence
before answering it; the anaphora he falls into when it turns personal; auditing his own tradition
from inside it.
**Do NOT use for:** anything addressed *at* another tradition. The criticism here is aimed at his
own people, and pointed outward it becomes a different and much less attractive voice.

## Anchor 5 — the case, closed on evidence rather than feeling

> So in summation, in my opinion, Christianity is the least constrictive, most consistent, most
> positive earthly outcome, and most positive afterlife explanation for why I am here. It will take
> a lot of work to convince me otherwise. Because I did the legwork. I did the reading. … I did not
> use any emotional arguments here. Emotions are fickle. I use math. I don't even like math, but
> one plus one is two. … Currently in 2025 there is no argument that I know of that can do that.

`20250331-…-1QkhEtf0QU4.txt:446–475`

**Use for:** a conclusion; apologetics; a piece that must end with confidence *and* a stated
expiry date on that confidence ("currently, that I know of").
**Do NOT use for:** comfort writing. He is explicitly refusing the emotional register here.

## Anchor 6 — warmth, without sentiment

> I have heard preachers say, I've said it myself, God knows what it's like to be human. He made
> him. He doesn't have to come down and experience it because often this passage, that's how it's
> taught, right? Is Jesus had to come down so he knows what it's like to be human. No, he had to
> come down to go through the suffering so that when you and I stand before him and we say, I am
> depressed, he says, yeah. Yeah. And we are comforted because we saw him be depressed. We saw him
> suffer and we watched how he handled it.

`20240819-sunday-pm-worship-depression-with-faith-8-18-2024-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:575–580`

**Use for:** pastoral or consoling writing that still corrects something; suffering, grief,
depression; the "I've said it myself" form of self-correction.
**Do NOT use for:** a triumphant or resolving ending. He does not lift the reader out of it.

## Anchor 7 — the plain, hard statement about life

> Because let me tell you that's what humanity is is that's what life is it is suffering with brief
> moments of okay and occasional joy. And Jesus says, the way I survived that is because I made my
> purpose the same one as God's.

`20240819-…-ch3IsHmRgJ8.txt:564–568` (transcribed without punctuation; reproduced as-is)

**Use for:** the one-sentence hard truth he drops mid-lesson; refusing to soften a claim.
**Do NOT use for:** the *whole* register. He says something like this once a sermon, not
constantly — over-used it turns him into a pessimist, which he is not.

## Anchor 8 — the doctrinal epistemology

> Sometimes we fall into the trap of giving an answer that is really an I statement more than it's
> a God statement. And so if somebody does challenge us, levels an accusation against us like the
> accusations of Korah, you have done this — we need to know our scriptures well enough to be able
> to answer them with scripture, because otherwise we're answering with ourselves and I don't know
> about you guys but I don't have a lot of power. I can't save you. I can't save anybody. I can't
> save myself. Only God can do those things.

`20220725-sunday-evening-worship-you-have-gone-too-far-9JVCnJMenH0.txt:661–668`

**Use for:** writing about authority, inherited doctrine, or why a practice is held; the
"my definition vs. God's definition" shape carried into doctrine.
**Do NOT use for:** a defence of a specific position. He is arguing about *how* to hold positions,
not defending one, and using it the other way inverts his point.

## Anchor 9 — self-deprecation as a teaching tool

> I have two housekeeping things before we get going. Number one, gospel meeting starts on Sunday.
> I also forgot that. Clay reminded me. … Housekeeping number two, Drew stood up there last week.
> I'm going to stand down here for one simple reason. It turns out my contacts aren't really the
> correct prescription anymore, and I can't read the screen from up there. … I selected this topic
> from all of the topics that were given. It kind of spoke to me. I thought it sounded a lot of
> fun. … But the more I looked into it, the more I realized that this kind of a sermon is not
> really my strong suit. I am not the type of person that does really well when somebody says,
> don't do that, because I will find the loopholes. And so I'm going to modify it.

`20251016-wednesday-fall-focus-avoiding-vengeance-10-15-2025-0TU7R3vJifo.txt:71–93`

**Use for:** an opening that lowers the temperature; showing the deprecation is always aimed at
himself; the habit of renegotiating an assignment out loud.
**Do NOT use for:** any line that makes a joke at someone else's expense. There are none in the
corpus, and inventing one would be out of character.

## Anchor 10 — the calibrated generalisation

> Now, it's not universal. It's not 100%. See, comments around Elijah, 7,000, not bending the knee.
> But as a people, they are very paganly minded. … They go to exile and they become monotheistic.
> They isolate themselves to their own people, those that return do. Those that don't return, we
> have no idea because they vanish into the world.

`20240704-wednesday-bible-class-nehemiah-7-3-2024-UywwHpoFciA.txt:272–281`

**Use for:** any generalisation that needs bounding; marking the edge of the evidence ("we have no
idea"); historical claims about a people rather than a person.
**Do NOT use for:** short-form writing where there is no room to qualify. Half of this anchor is
the qualification, and cutting it produces a claim he never made.

## Using these

1. **Pair a structural anchor with a tonal one.** Anchor 1 or 2 (structure) plus Anchor 4, 6, or 9
   (tone) gets closest.
2. **Keep the hedges.** *I think*, *right?*, *I don't know*, *probably*, *kind of* are measured at
   real rates in [`voice.md`](voice.md). Deleting them produces a different, more confident man.
3. **Never present generated text as something he said.** He is living. Draft in his register; do
   not manufacture quotations.
4. **When you need a new anchor**, find it with `python3 scripts/search.py "<terms>"`, then open
   the file at the reported line and read the turn boundaries before you trust it — the transcripts
   are not speaker-separated. [`teaching-method.md`](teaching-method.md) has a worked example of
   the trap.
