# Josh Tolbert — teaching method

The classroom-mechanics counterpart to [`voice.md`](voice.md). That file is about how he
*sounds*; this one is about how he *runs an hour*. Both are drawn from the same 73 transcripts.

Citations are `filename:line`, relative to `speakers/josh-tolbert/sermons/`.

---

## The shape of a class

A Wednesday class is remarkably consistent across five years and five books:

| Stage | Typically | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Housekeeping | 30 s | Announcements he half-forgot. *"I have two housekeeping things before we get going. Number one, gospel meeting starts on Sunday. I also forgot that. Clay reminded me."* — `…avoiding-vengeance-…-0TU7R3vJifo.txt:71–74` |
| 2. Where we are | 1–3 min | Names the text, then reviews the previous class before anything new. *"Last time we finished off the discussion of Nadab and Abihu…"* — `…leviticus-pt-6-UrA_o4PAluw.txt:5` |
| 3. The opening question | 2–10 min | Asks the room to define the term or make the application *before* he does. |
| 4. Background | 10–30 min | The political, historical, geographic world. Often the largest single block. |
| 5. The text | the remainder | Read aloud, in stretches, stopping to connect back to the background. |
| 6. Comments | throughout and at the end | *"Any comments anybody has on that before we go a little further? Because I got more, but maybe you guys do."* — `…deuteronomy-1-1-2025-BValdlu33Zg.txt:458–459` |

He invites comment constantly: **559 occurrences of "any comments / questions / thoughts"** across
the corpus, about 9.7 per 10,000 words — roughly eight times per class.

## The five moves

### 1. Socratic opening — ask before telling

He puts the definition to the room first, takes several answers, names the people who gave them,
and builds his lesson on top of what they said. It is not a rhetorical question.

> "As we get into this, I've titled this series God's definition of holiness. And so I kind of want
> to start there. **What is a good definition to you?** One that you guys can use of holiness. What
> do you all think?"
> *(class: "Pure." )* "Okay. So right off the bat, that's a comparison, right? There has to be
> something impure for me to know what is pure."
> *(class: "Separated, separate.")* "Any other definitions? How about from this side?"
> — `…god-s-definition-of-holy-leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:3–14`

The variant for a narrative text is to ask for the application rather than the definition:
*"Can you make an application to today?"* — `…deuteronomy-1-1-2025-BValdlu33Zg.txt:459`.

### 2. Background before text

He will spend a third of a class on the world around a passage. The point is always stated: the
history is what makes the passage land.

> "We're going to be talking a lot about politics tonight. Because when I started, I was just
> really curious who Felix was, and that's kind of what led me down that rabbit hole. … So that's
> really what we're going to talk about, and then we're going to read through Acts 24 and hit the
> highlights **once we understand the political world that Paul walked into**."
> — `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:179–185`

For *Fall of Babylon* he walks four Babylonian successions and two coups before opening Daniel 5,
then names the payoff in one line: *"I say all of this to understand Babylon is weak here."*
— `…fall-of-babylon-…-0bxU9FnXRLE.txt:531`

### 3. Priced sources

Extra-biblical material is cited *with its discount attached*, in the same breath. He never
launders a source's authority, and he re-runs the number at the discounted rate.

> "Josephus says, and I'm going to say, sometimes Josephus exaggerates. This is probably one of
> those times. But even if he's just 10% correct, this is a massive amount of people."
> — `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:236`

The same instinct applied to tradition and to search engines:

> "So just so that if you ever Google it, that is the opinion Google has. Google also has the
> opinion that Isaiah is about premillennialism. So be aware of that as well when you look it up."
> — `…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:78–80`

And to the state of his own thinking:

> "I fully reject it. I think it's written by Isaiah **or** written by the disciples of Isaiah.
> I'll go with that…"
> — `…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:50–51`

### 4. Managing the room's memory budget

He tells the class explicitly what to hold and what to let go, and he coins working nicknames for
categories and for names he can't pronounce — announcing that he is doing it.

> "And so when you organize the — and I don't need you to remember the tribes and their locations.
> … What's important is I want you to remember where the tabernacle is."
> — `…leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:53–55`

> "Because if you are a normal Jew — I'm going to call him normal Jew and I'm going to call him
> Levite Jew. I probably will come up with other names on other classes. That's just what's going
> to happen."
> — `…leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:69–72`

> "I'm gonna call him Cumanus because I can't say his first word more than once."
> — `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:197–198`

He also declares in advance what he will *not* chase: *"I'm not going to get hung up on what is
the mountain of the Lord. I'm not going to do it, I think. If I do get hung up on it, please
remind me."* — `…isaiah-4-15-2026-2CS6kMu1UCU.txt:72–74`

### 5. Handling questions and answers

- **He names people.** Across the corpus: Clay (57 mentions), Ben (39), Matt (36), Rachel (16),
  Charlie (14), Blaine (12), Doug (9), Dick (8), Landon (7).
- **He calls on individuals by name** and then hands the floor over — *"so I can sing whatever I
  want. Ben?"* — `…does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-…-1C72Yy135MA.txt:47`.
- **He credits colleagues' concurrent classes** rather than duplicating them: *"Clay's doing the
  class on Jonah, so if you want to know some of those ideals…"*
  — `…nehemiah-7-3-2024-UywwHpoFciA.txt:275`.
- **He reads the room's silence out loud**: *"I see a lot of hands going like this or like this,
  but nobody goes like this. Let me get a volunteer."*
  — `…does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-…-1C72Yy135MA.txt:105–106`.
- **He absorbs blame rather than assigning it**, especially when substituting: *"Matt usually
  starts off with a concept for us to think about. And this is the one I picked for today. He
  didn't leave me one, so this one's all me. So if you don't like it, you can blame me, not him."*
  — `…does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-…-1C72Yy135MA.txt:3–6`.

## The curriculum, not the lesson

He teaches a whole Old Testament book over months and treats the congregation's teaching as one
shared, cross-referenced project. Within a single Nehemiah class he back-references a finished
series, flags where the current one will land, and points at a colleague's:

> "I'm not going to go into those because we dealt with that in Ezra, that's one of their big
> messages." — `…nehemiah-7-3-2024-UywwHpoFciA.txt:285–286`
>
> "We will ultimately get to Malachi in this quarter." — `…:292`
>
> "Clay's doing the class on Jonah." — `…:275`

Sequence in the archive: *Jesus in the Minor Prophets* (2020) → Leviticus (2021) → 1 Samuel (2022)
→ 2 Samuel (2023) → Ezra (2024) → Nehemiah (2024–25) → Deuteronomy (2025) → Isaiah (2026).

## What he is willing to say out loud

These are not asides; they are how he holds authority in the room.

- **That he is learning as he goes.** *"I have this rule anytime that I preach or anytime that I
  teach that I have to learn something. Otherwise, why am I talking to you? So I learned some
  things."* — `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:178–179`
- **That he is unscripted.** *"As far as I know, at this point in the lesson, we're going to spend
  the majority of our time in Acts 24. I don't know what's going to happen. Sometimes I get
  ideas."* — `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:170–173`
- **That the assignment doesn't suit him, so he's changing it.** *"The more I looked into it, the
  more I realized that this kind of a sermon is not really my strong suit. I am not the type of
  person that does really well when somebody says, don't do that, because I will find the
  loopholes. And so I'm going to modify it…"*
  — `…avoiding-vengeance-…-0TU7R3vJifo.txt:91–93`
- **That he got the timing wrong.** *"All right, we are out of time. … Oh, I do, don't I? This is
  not Wednesday, is it? Man, I shouldn't have rushed. Okay, so we actually have nine, ten more
  minutes."* — `…from-creation-to-eternity-…-W96EXQbSqIU.txt:455–458`
- **That he was wrong, naming the class member who was right.** *"I do have a correction. I need
  to get in before I forget it. In chapter 7, in verse 8, it does say that the skin of the burnt
  offering is given to the priest. In our first class, somebody made that comment, and I was
  wrong."* — `…god-s-definition-of-holy-pt-3-…-HgVfm5s023Q.txt:2–4`
- **That he can't do the arithmetic.** *"You guys do the math. You know, I'm not good at that."*
  — `…leviticus--MtxfZIo3khM.txt:115–116`

Every one of these is aimed at himself. He does not deprecate the class.

## Attribution: the trap, with a worked example

The recordings are **not diarized**, and his classes are genuinely dialogic. A class member's
answer sits in the transcript in exactly the same shape as his own sentences, with no marker.

Here is a real 20-line stretch from the hymn class. Lines 48–63 are **Ben**, not Josh:

```
47   so I can sing whatever I want. Ben?            <- Josh, calling on Ben
48   You may have already covered this. Sorry, ...  <- Ben starts
49   So, what I was going to say is so many of the psalms that we have in our hymnals
...
63   can be perfectly fine.                          <- Ben ends
64   Anybody else?                                   <- Josh resumes
```
— `…does-the-origin-of-a-hymn-matter-…-1C72Yy135MA.txt:47–64`

Attributing line 53 (*"hardly any of the hymns in our books were written by members of the
church"*) to Josh would put another man's opinion in his mouth. **Read the turn boundaries.**

### Rules of thumb

1. **A question ending in a name is a handoff.** *"Ben?"*, *"Yeah."*, *"Got a thought?"* — what
   follows is usually not Josh.
2. **"Any comments / Anybody else / Okay" is usually him taking the floor back.**
3. **On Sunday and livestreamed files, skip the top of the file.** Announcements, prayers, and
   congregational singing come first — in `…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt` his portion starts at
   **line 169**; before that is hymn text. The `.srt` beside each `.txt` gives timestamps if you
   need to check against the audio.
4. **The Matt-and-Clay file is two men, unlabelled.** In
   `…-overcoming-the-temptation-of-prosperi-KhX3eiKsrSM.txt`, Josh hosts and Matt Bassford is the
   guest (Clay Gentry is away). Nothing in the text says who is speaking.
5. **The text is ASR output.** Expect wrong proper nouns — "Tertullian" for Tertullus
   (`…paul-and-felix-rkuCjQw1yjk.txt:182`), "Vintidus Cumanus" for Ventidius Cumanus (`:196`),
   mangled hymn lyrics throughout. Fix an obvious mis-transcription silently if you quote it, or
   mark it `[sic]` — but never build a point on a garbled word.
