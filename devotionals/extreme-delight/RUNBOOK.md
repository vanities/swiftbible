# Extreme Delight — Sunday Devotional Series Runbook

A recurring Sunday-only custom devotional series for SwiftBible. Each entry confronts the suspicion most Christians carry that joy is suspect — possibly hedonism, probably worldly. The goal is to recover the biblical witness that delight is commanded.

**Why Sunday only:** PostHog data shows Sunday gets ~28% of all weekly devotional views. Custom Sunday content is where the audience actually is.

**Why "Extreme Delight":** Joy as obedience. Each devotional names a place where God commands feasting, beauty, dancing, sensory pleasure — and where the refusal to delight is its own kind of unbelief.

**Sister series (round-robin rotation):** Grace → Rest → Delight → Faith. See [Extreme Faith RUNBOOK § Rotation](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md#rotation) for the full rotation table.

---

## Shared structure

The markdown skeleton, four-beat structure, voice rules, and drafting workflow are identical across all four series. See [Extreme Faith RUNBOOK](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md) for the full reference. Series-specific differences are the schedule and per-devotional notes below.

**Voice anchor reminder:** The bold subtitle is a personal admission. The prayer returns to first-person. The body universalizes to "you" / "we." **I → we → I.**

## Series schedule

The 12 Delight topics queue across the round-robin rotation — Delight gets every 4th Sunday. Order is not fixed; pick the topic that matches what you've been thinking about.

Each entry contrasts the **comfortable middle** with the **extreme claim** about delight as obedience. The diagnostic: a secular reader nods at *"joy is good."* A secular reader does NOT nod at *"God commands feasting, dancing, beauty, sensory pleasure, and erotic love as forms of worship."* That's the wince.

**Strong runner-ups:**
- "Joy unspeakable and full of glory" (1 Pet 1:8)
- The fruit of the Spirit IS joy (Gal 5:22)
- "Enter into the joy of thy lord" (Matt 25:21)
- "These things have I spoken... that your joy might be full" (John 15:11)
- Nehemiah 8:10 — "the joy of the LORD is your strength"
- "Shout for joy" — the imperative shout (Ps 5:11, Ps 32:11, Ps 66:1)

**Diagnostic for "is this extreme enough?"**: if a serious Christian could nod along thinking *"yes, joy is important,"* it's not extreme yet. The extreme-delight claim should make the dutiful Christian feel exposed for treating delight as optional or suspicious.

**⚠️ CoC theological note:** Delight topics on worship music must NOT advocate for instruments in the congregational assembly — CoC tradition is a cappella (Eph 5:19, Col 3:16). OT psalms about instruments are not NT precedent for instrumental church music. Topics on OT festivals should draw the principle (commanded celebration) without binding the OT festival on Christians. See [Extreme Faith § CoC theological framework](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md#coc-theological-framework) for topic-specific guidance.

**⚠️ Matt Bassford note:** Matt's worldview was *world-is-broken / hope-is-elsewhere.* He'd push back on a too-celebratory present-tense Delight series — he'd want joy anchored eschatologically (the *coming* feast, the *promised* delight). When using the Matt skill on a Delight devotional, expect his pushback to lean toward "the world is groaning, but the feast is coming." Borrow his eschatological frame; resist his earthly pessimism if a particular delight is meant to be present-tense.

**⚠️ Song of Solomon (Week 3):** This will push some of the audience harder than any Faith hardship. Watch PostHog. If churn spikes, consider treating it as an optional/runner-up rather than a scheduled main entry.

## Per-devotional notes

### Week 1 — Eat with joy (2026-05-24)

Ecclesiastes 9:7-10 is one of the strangest passages in scripture. The Preacher has spent eight chapters lamenting the vanity of life under the sun. Then suddenly, an imperative: *"Go thy way, eat thy bread with joy, and drink thy wine with a merry heart."* He doesn't pause to qualify. He doesn't add "but in moderation." He commands joy in food and drink as a response to the fact that life is fleeting. *Because* it's fleeting.

Modern Christians have a deep instinct that delight is a little embarrassing. Suspicious. Possibly hedonism. Definitely worldly. The Preacher would tell us we're wrong.

Lead with: the meal you ate while scrolling. The wine you drank guiltily. The dessert you skipped because you were "being good." The Christian instinct that *enjoying* something undermines its sanctity.

**I anchor possibilities:** *"I have eaten meals I did not taste. I have drunk wine I did not enjoy. I have called this discipline."* / *"I am suspicious of my own pleasures. I am told this is humility. I am not sure that's what it is."*

Anchor verses: Eccl 9:7-10. Possibly: 1 Tim 4:4-5 (every creature of God is good, received with thanksgiving), 1 Tim 6:17 (God gives us all things richly to enjoy), Acts 14:17 (he filled our hearts with food and gladness).

The pivot: gratitude isn't a substitute for delight. *"Thank you for this meal"* before you eat is good. *Tasting it* is the obedience. The Preacher commands the joy, not the report of joy.

End the empathy+Bible mix on: the quiet Christian suspicion that joy is for after, not now. The Preacher disagrees. He commands it now. *"Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might."* Including the eating and the drinking.

Prayer: short, specific. Returns to the I-anchor — *"I have eaten meals I did not taste. Teach me to enjoy what you have given without apology."*
