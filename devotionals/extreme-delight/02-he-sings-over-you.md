# June 21 — Zephaniah 3:17: He sings over you

**I believe God loves me. It is in every prayer I have ever prayed. But when I picture his face while I pray, the face is patient, and a little tired, and I notice I have never once imagined him singing.**

> *"The LORD thy God in the midst of thee is mighty; he will save, he will rejoice over thee with joy; he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing."*
> **Zephaniah 3:17**

## The face you picture

You believe God loves you. It is on the first page of everything you were ever taught. God is love. Christ died for you. Nothing can separate. You would sign your name under all of it.

Now try a harder question. Does God like you?

Watch what just happened in your chest. Love, we have filed under doctrine. He loves me because he is love, the way the sun is warm, the way a policy covers you. A standing arrangement. But liking is personal. Liking has a face. And when most of us picture the face God makes when we cross his mind, we picture patience. Tolerance. The look of a Father committed to a child he is enduring.

So we pray like tenants and obey like employees, and we call the distance reverence.

That is the comfortable middle. Loved, certainly. Enjoyed, never.

## The dark book that ends in a song

Zephaniah is one of the darkest books in the Bible. Three chapters, most of them fire. The day of the LORD is coming, the idols fall, the proud city gets leveled. Nobody embroiders Zephaniah on a pillow.

Then the last page turns, the judgment is spent, and the prophet starts shouting at the survivors: *Sing, O daughter of Zion; shout, O Israel; be glad and rejoice with all the heart.* And he gives the reason, and the reason is one of the strangest verses in the Bible. The mighty God is standing in the middle of his people. He saves. He rejoices over them with joy. He rests in his love, settled, nothing left to prosecute, because *the LORD hath taken away thy judgments.* And then the last clause arrives:

> *"...he will joy over thee with singing."*
> **Zephaniah 3:17**

God sings. Over you.

Think about what singing is. Nobody sings from duty. Duty signs forms and keeps appointments. Singing is what joy does when it will not stay inside the body anymore. A man hums over a workbench when the work is going well. A mother sings over a crib for no reason the baby could ever earn. Scripture reaches for the most overflowing sound it knows and puts it in the mouth of Almighty God, aimed at his people.

And mark where the song sits. It comes after the fire, over a people just pulled through judgment with nothing in their hands. They had not assembled an impressive year. The joy is not a performance review. It is a Father with his child back.

## You are the scale model

If that sounds too good, listen to how Jesus argues for it. He points at you.

> *"If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?"*
> **Matthew 7:11**

What man among you, if his son asks for bread, hands him a stone? You, says Jesus, being evil, on your worst week, still know how to give your kids good gifts. Your own fathering is the scale model he reasons from. Whatever is in your chest for your child, the argument runs one direction: how much more.

Today is Father's Day. Somewhere today a father is watching his kid do something small, tie a shoe wrong, tell a joke badly, fall asleep mid-sentence, and his chest is cracking open for no productive reason at all. If you have ever felt that, even once, you have held the scale model in your hands.

> *"Fear not, little flock; for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom."*
> **Luke 12:32**

Good pleasure. Handing you the kingdom is the part he enjoys. Isaiah says it with a wedding: *as the bridegroom rejoiceth over the bride, so shall thy God rejoice over thee.* Stand a groom at the top of the aisle and watch his face. That face. The psalmist says it flat: *the LORD taketh pleasure in his people.*

## Letting it land

The objection comes fast, and it sounds humble: he has seen me. Committed love you can believe in, because commitment is a decision. Enjoyment feels contingent on being enjoyable, and you know the inside of your own week.

So go back to where the song sits. Zephaniah's singing God is singing over people fresh out of the fire, saved by his own hand, their judgments carried off. The delight rests on what he did and on whose you are. In Christ the record is taken away, and what is left in the room is the Father and the child, and the Father is glad.

Here is the cost, and it is a strange one. Being enjoyed is harder to receive than being corrected. The dutiful Christian would rather be useful than be delighted in, because useful feels earned, and delight cannot be. You will want to argue. You will want to point at your week. The chapter does command your joy too, *Sing, O daughter of Zion,* but look at the order of operations. The command comes with a reason attached, and the reason is his singing. Yours is the echo.

So the assignment this Sunday is small and very hard. Stand still and be sung over. Believe Luke 12:32 is about your Father's actual face. You know the look you give your own kid when he runs at you down the hallway. Hold that picture. Now believe it is aimed at you, today, with this week's record already carried off.

He is not enduring you. He is rejoicing over you, at rest in his love, singing.

## A prayer

Father,

I believe you love me.
I have said it since I was small.
What I have never quite believed is that you like me,
that anything about me has ever made you glad.

I picture your face patient and tired.
Your prophet says you are singing.

Take away the face I invented
and give me the one in the verse:
mighty, near, at rest in your love,
joying over me with singing.

I have nothing in my hands from this week.
Sing anyway.

***You sing over me. Let me stand still long enough to hear it.***

Amen.
