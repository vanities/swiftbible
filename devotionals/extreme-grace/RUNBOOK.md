# Extreme Grace — Sunday Devotional Series Runbook

A recurring Sunday-only custom devotional series for SwiftBible. Each entry reveals a place where God's grace is more lavish, more inverted, more scandalous than the comfortable middle has settled for. The goal is to leave the reader stunned at the grace they keep underestimating — disorientation, not feel-good comfort.

**Why Sunday only:** PostHog data shows Sunday gets ~28% of all weekly devotional views. Custom Sunday content is where the audience actually is.

**Why "Extreme Grace":** Grace at its honest extent. Each devotional names a specific way Christians have softened, smallened, or domesticated God's mercy until it fits inside their own moral imagination. The faith-extreme position is that real grace doesn't fit.

**Sister series (round-robin rotation):** Grace → Rest → Delight → Faith. See [Extreme Faith RUNBOOK § Rotation](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md#rotation) for the full rotation table.

---

## Shared structure

The markdown skeleton, four-beat structure, voice rules, and drafting workflow are identical across all four series. See [Extreme Faith RUNBOOK](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md) for the full reference. Series-specific differences are the schedule and per-devotional notes below.

**Voice anchor reminder:** The bold subtitle is a personal admission. The prayer returns to first-person. The body universalizes to "you" / "we." **I → we → I.**

## Series schedule

The 13 Grace topics queue across the round-robin rotation — Grace gets every 4th Sunday. Order is not fixed; pick the topic that matches what you've been thinking about.

Each entry contrasts the **comfortable middle** (where Christians have settled) with the **extreme claim** (what scripture actually says about God's mercy). The diagnostic: a secular reader nods at *"God is loving and forgiving."* A secular reader does NOT nod at *"God prefers ex-murderers, adulterers, and traitors for his most important work."* That's the wince.

**Strong runner-ups:**
- The lost coin / the lost sheep — God *searches* (Luke 15:1-10)
- Rahab the prostitute in the genealogy of Christ (Matt 1:5)
- "Father, forgive them" from the cross (Luke 23:34)
- The Pharisee and the tax collector — only one went home justified (Luke 18:9-14)
- "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise" — the *today* (Luke 23:43)
- Onesimus the runaway slave / Philemon (Philemon 1:10-18)

**Diagnostic for "is this extreme enough?"**: if a polite Christian would nod along thinking *"yes, God is forgiving,"* the claim hasn't arrived yet. The extreme-grace position should make the reader uncomfortable with how much they've been domesticating God's mercy.

**⚠️ CoC theological note:** Grace devotionals must not collapse into "just call on the name" or "just believe." Several Grace topics need careful framing — see [Extreme Faith § CoC theological framework](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md#coc-theological-framework). Key distinctive: salvation in the New Covenant comes through faith expressed in baptism for the remission of sins (Acts 2:38, 22:16, Rom 6:3-4). The grace is real and lavish; it's also paired with obedience as response (*"two halves of the same whole"*). Specific topic flags: W3 (thief — Old Covenant), W5 (Saul — baptized after Damascus), W12 (calling — paired with baptism in Acts 22:16).

## Per-devotional notes

### Week 1 — The Father runs (2026-05-10)

The parable's climax is the Father's run. In first-century Middle Eastern culture, a dignified man does not run — running requires hiking your robe, exposing your legs, looking undignified. The Father runs anyway. He doesn't wait for the son to finish his rehearsed speech. He doesn't extract a confession. He doesn't make the son earn his way back. He runs. Then the older brother story is the second half — extreme grace also goes to the dutiful one who refuses to come in.

Lead with: the rehearsed apology you have been carrying for years. The conviction that you have to clean yourself up before you can come home. The polite Christian belief that God forgives, technically — but quietly that he prefers people who didn't really need it.

**I anchor possibilities:** *"I have rehearsed my apology for years."* / *"I have been keeping a list of my sins so that I can show God I take them seriously."* / *"I have always believed God forgives. I have not always believed he runs."*

Anchor verses: Luke 15:11-32 (the whole parable). Possibly add: Rom 5:8 (while we were yet sinners), Heb 7:25 (saves to the uttermost).

The pivot: the Father runs *before* the speech. Verse 20 — *"when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him."* The compassion comes before the confession. Re-read that verse. It's everything.

End the empathy+Bible mix on: the older brother. We've all been him. The faith-extreme grace is also for the brother who refuses to come in. Even the son who never left needs this Father.

Prayer: short, specific. Returns to the I-anchor as refrain — *"I have rehearsed my apology for years. You have not let me finish. Teach me to come home before I'm ready."*

### Week 2 — The respite he used (2026-06-07)

The shadow entry in the Grace series. The other twelve topics are luminous mercy; this one is the back of the same coin. The "surprising grace" is how *lavish and long* God's patience with Pharaoh was — ten plagues, and God relents the moment Pharaoh begs, every single time. The "wince" is that patience despised has a terminus.

Lead verse Exodus 8:15 — *"when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart."* The keystone: Pharaoh used the mercy itself as the occasion to harden. He spent the respite.

**Anti-Calvinist spine (load-bearing).** This is *the* Calvinist proof-text (cf. Rom 9:17-18). Stay anti-Calvinist by following the sequence Matt Bassford lays out in `speakers/matt-bassford/posts/2018-01-16-pharaohs-hardened-heart.md` and `2018-05-07-pharaohs-hard-heart.md`: God is never said to harden Pharaoh's heart before 9:12; Pharaoh is never said to harden his own heart after 9:34. Pharaoh hardened himself first; God's hardening was *judicial* confirmation (the "God gave them up" pattern of Rom 1:24-28), not unconditional reprobation. Don't let the draft imply God overrode a free will.

Romans 2:4-5 is the theological center for the Grace framing: the riches of God's goodness, forbearance, and longsuffering are *meant to lead to repentance*; despised, they become the thing we presume on, and "hardness" treasures up wrath. Heb 3:13 (*"while it is called To day... the deceitfulness of sin"*) and Prov 29:1 carry the warning; 2 Pet 3:9 lands it grace-side (the respite you're in *is* mercy; the door is open today).

**CoC framing:** apostasy is real and congenial here (you *can* run out of rope — Heb 6:4-6, 10:26-31), but keep the door open: the call is repentance-as-response *today*, not "you've already lost it." Don't collapse into "just believe / just call."

Matt's `2018-10-01-reforming-too-late.md` ("tomorrow-itis"; Josiah; windows that close) is the empathy-beat source. Borrow the *reasoning*, not his text.

Prayer: returns to the anchor as refrain — *"Lord, I have heard your patience as permission. Soften me before I finish the job myself."*
