# September 20 — 1 John 5:21: To be seen

**A friend's good news landed in the group chat and something in me went quiet, and I hated that it did.**

> *"Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen."*
> **1 John 5:21**

## The pull

You know the loop. You post something good and then you cannot leave it alone. You check who liked it. You refresh. You check again four minutes later, and the number went up, and that did something small and warm in your chest that you'd be embarrassed to describe out loud. Then it stalls, and you feel that too.

The phone goes face-down on the table and you are aware of it the entire meal, like a person standing behind your chair. You pick it up without deciding to. First thing in the morning, before your feet are even on the floor, your hand already knows where it is.

It's built to do this to you. The little number that sometimes jumps and sometimes sits there is the same machinery as a slot machine, and the reason you keep pulling is that you never know which time it'll pay. We don't call this an addiction because everybody has it, and a thing everybody has stops looking like a problem and starts looking like the weather. But sit with the honest word for being unable to put something down, for reaching for it before you're awake, for the low hum of needing it. There's a word. We just don't use it about ourselves.

## The thing you'd never post

Then there's the other half, the part that never makes it onto anybody's feed.

You're scrolling, and a friend is doing better. A promotion you didn't get. A house you can't afford. A trip, a marriage that looks effortless, a guy exactly your age who just sold his company. And before you can stop it, something in you sours. Not anger, nothing that loud. Just a little drop in the chest, a quiet *must be nice,* a reflex to find the flaw in their good thing so it weighs less.

And you hate it. You don't want to be the person who's secretly deflated when someone he loves wins. You're genuinely afraid of becoming him. But the feed is training you into him anyway, one comparison at a time, a thousand reps a week, your neighbor's best moment against your ordinary Tuesday, over and over until the bitterness has worn a groove.

## The word

The Greek for idol is **eidolon** (εἴδωλον). The root is *eidos*, the look of a thing, its appearance, the way it shows up to the eye. It's the root hiding in *idea* and *video*, the root for *I see*. An eidolon is an image, a phantom, a thing whose whole existence is being looked at, with nothing behind it. John ends his entire letter on it, no transition, just the last line and *Amen*: *keep yourselves from idols.* The slab of glass in your pocket is a river of *eidola*, faces and lives that exist, for you, only as something to look at.

And Paul names the second half of it, the envy, as the very same sin:

> *"Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth... and covetousness, which is idolatry."*
> **Colossians 3:5**

Covetousness *is* idolatry. So the jealousy on the couch isn't a side effect of the idol. It's the idol, wearing the face you're most ashamed of. Think about what the tenth commandment actually forbids: *thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house... nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's* (Exodus 20:17). Now picture a machine that scrolls you an endless wall of exactly that. Your neighbor's house, your neighbor's spouse, your neighbor's ox, forever, refreshing all day. We built the thing the tenth commandment was warning us about and we keep it next to the bed.

There's a king in scripture who got destroyed by a feed. The women came out singing after a battle, *Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands.* A song about whose numbers were bigger. And the text says *Saul eyed David from that day and forward* (1 Samuel 18:7-9). One comparison song, and a king is poisoned for the rest of his life. The feed sings you that song about everyone you know, every time you open it.

Underneath both halves, the pull and the envy, is a master. Jesus moves from doing things *to be seen of men* to this, later in the same sermon: *No man can serve two masters... ye cannot serve God and mammon* (Matthew 6:24). An idol never stays a hobby. It always promotes itself to master. Paul drew his line there:

> *"All things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any."*
> **1 Corinthians 6:12**

## What is that to thee

Is comparing yourself to other people even a sin? It's a fair question, because half the time it doesn't feel like envy. It just feels like noticing. Paul answers it about as plainly as he answers anything. People who size themselves up against each other, he says, are *measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves,* and then he lands it gently: they *are not wise* (2 Corinthians 10:12). Not wicked. Not wise. A poor use of a good mind.

Because mostly what comparison does is rob you. Solomon watched a man do honest, skillful work and get nothing back for it but the envy of his neighbor, and he called the whole arrangement *vanity and vexation of spirit* (Ecclesiastes 4:4), his phrase for chasing the wind, running hard after a thing that was never there. Every hour you spend ranking your ordinary day against someone's edited best one is an hour lifted clean off the front of your one life, and it doesn't come back. The energy you burn being quietly bothered by a stranger's vacation was supposed to go to your kids, your work, the friends in the room with you.

Peter did it too, minutes after Jesus told him how he was going to die. He looked over at John and asked, *Lord, and what shall this man do?* What about him. Jesus didn't lecture him about it. He just said the truest seven words you could tape to the back of your phone: *what is that to thee? follow thou me* (John 21:21-22). Mind your own road. You've got one life and one Lord, and neither of them is found by looking sideways.

## What it makes you

Be honest about the cost, because it's quieter than a scandal and it does more damage. Envy doesn't announce itself. It just hollows you out from the inside while you keep looking fine on the outside.

> *"A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the rottenness of the bones."*
> **Proverbs 14:30**

The bones. The part that holds you up, going soft where nobody can see it. And the pull does its own slow work, because you become like what you give your eyes to. The Psalm said it about the old idols: eyes that cannot see, ears that cannot hear, *they that make them are like unto them* (Psalm 115:8). Spend enough of your day on faces that can't see you and you go a little unseeing yourself, harder to be present at your own table, slower to notice the person actually in the room.

Paul says you can't help being shaped by what holds your gaze:

> *"But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image."*
> **2 Corinthians 3:18**

You're going to be turned into the likeness of whatever you can't stop looking at. There's no setting where you behold nothing. Behold the feed and you become a hollow, anxious, envious image. Behold Christ and you're slowly changed into his. The only live question is which face is in front of you all day.

And here is the mercy that loosens the grip on both. You are wearing yourself out to be seen by people who are barely looking, and to keep up with lives you only see the edited version of, while the God who actually sees you has had his eyes on you the entire time. The first person in scripture to give God a name was a used-up runaway servant girl, alone in a desert with nothing, and the name she gave him was *Thou God seest me* (Genesis 16:13). He saw her. He sees you, the edited and the unedited, and he hasn't looked away.

When that lands, the comparison song goes quiet, because you stop needing to do *better* than anyone. *Godliness with contentment is great gain* (1 Timothy 6:6). And the bitterness you were afraid of can finally turn the right way out: instead of dying a little when a friend wins, you get to do the thing Paul says is actually possible, *rejoice with them that do rejoice* (Romans 12:15). You can be glad. You can put the image down, and pick the people back up.

## A prayer

Father,

You have seen me all day,
and I spent the day refreshing a screen to be seen by strangers,
and souring at the ones who are doing better.

You know the pull.
You know how fast my hand finds it in the morning.
You know the quiet drop in my chest
when someone I love gets the good news I wanted.
I'm ashamed of it, and I can't fix it by myself.

Break its power over me.
Pull my eyes up off the glass and onto you,
long enough to start looking like you again,
present at my own table,
glad when my friends are glad.

You are the God who sees me.
I don't need anyone else to.
Let that be enough today
to set the image down.

Amen.
