# June 28 — Romans 3:23: Miss the mark

**I am not a bad person. I have used this sentence as a defense. I have been winning the wrong game.**

> *"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God."*
> **Romans 3:23**

## I am not a bad person

You did not murder anyone today.

You did not steal. You did not lie, except the small social ones nobody counts. You did not commit adultery. You did not get drunk. You did not even, now that you think about it, raise your voice.

By the metric you have been quietly using your whole life — the comparison to people who actually do bad things — you came out fine. You came out better than fine. You came out the kind of person who would be respected at the funeral.

This is the metric most Christians actually live by. Not the catechism's metric. The neighborhood's. The internal scoreboard that grades you against the murderer on the news, the addict who can't get sober, the family member who blew up his life.

You always pass.

It has not occurred to you, in years, to fail.

## A word in archery

The Greek word the New Testament uses for *sin* is **hamartia**. ἁμαρτία. It is not, originally, a moral word. It is an archery word.

It means: *to miss the mark.*

You draw the bow. You aim. You release. The arrow lands somewhere — maybe close, maybe far — but it does not hit the bullseye. That is hamartia. The miss.

Paul uses it in Romans 3:23 with another archery word stacked behind it. Read the verse again, slowly:

> *"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God."*

The KJV translators heard the archery in the Greek. *Come short.* The arrow falls before the target. Hamartia is the swing of the bow; *hystereō* — "come short" — is the falling-shy. Two archery words in the same sentence. Paul is painting a picture. He is not asking whether you crossed a moral line. He is asking whether your arrow reached the gold.

What is the gold?

The glory of God.

Not "be better than the murderer." Not "don't do the big things." The bullseye is the *glory of God* — and the standard, in case you missed it, is Christ himself, the only one who ever hit it. *"In all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin"* — Hebrews 4:15. He is the arrow that flew to the gold.

You haven't done that. Not once.

> *"Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect."*
> **Matthew 5:48**

The word there — *teleios* — means *complete, finished, hitting the goal it was made for.* That is the bullseye. Not a moral checklist. The whole life lived perfectly toward God.

Have you, today, loved God with all your heart and all your soul and all your mind and all your strength?

Have you, this hour, loved your neighbor as yourself?

The tally on the rules looks fine. The arrow is in the dirt.

## The Pharisee always passes

There is a parable Jesus tells that almost no one preaches honestly. Two men go up to pray. The Pharisee thanks God that he is not like other men — not an extortioner, not unjust, not an adulterer, not even like that tax collector over there. He fasts twice a week. He tithes everything. By the rules, he is winning.

The tax collector — a man who has, in fact, broken the rules — beats his chest and says one sentence: *"God be merciful to me a sinner."*

Jesus's verdict: *"This man went down to his house justified rather than the other."* Luke 18:14.

Read that again. The man who kept all the rules went home unforgiven. The man who knew he was off-target went home justified.

What was the difference? Both prayed. Both came to the temple. Both used the right words.

The difference is this: the Pharisee was looking at the wrong target.

He was aiming at *being better than the tax collector.* He hit that target every time. Bullseye after bullseye. And he never noticed that the bullseye he was hitting was not the one God had drawn. He had drawn his own target on the wall, hit it, and called himself righteous.

The tax collector was looking at the right target. He could not pretend the arrow had landed near it. It hadn't.

## I have been winning the wrong game

Here is what *miss the mark* does to the comfortable middle: it levels it.

You and the murderer on the news are not, biblically, on different sides of a line. You are at different distances from the same bullseye. Maybe — maybe — your arrow is closer to the gold than his. But neither of you hit it. Neither of you got within ten feet of it. *All have sinned and come short.* The "all" is doing work in that sentence. It does not mean "all the obvious bad people." It means *all.*

This is the part that is supposed to wound. If you have spent your life feeling fine because you are a decent person, the gospel has to wound you here, or it cannot get any further. The gospel cannot enter through the door of *I'm doing okay.* That door is locked from the inside.

But once it wounds you, it does not leave you bleeding.

> *"For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God."*
> **1 Peter 3:18**

The just — the one who hit the mark, every time, for thirty-three years — for the unjust. The arrow that found the gold dies in place of the arrows that fell short. You are not asked to start hitting the bullseye on your own. You are asked to be in him.

The faith-extreme position is that *"I am not a bad person"* is not a Christian sentence. It cannot be. It is a sentence from another religion, the religion of comparison, where the standard is your neighbor and the prize is going home feeling fine.

The Christian sentence is the tax collector's: *God be merciful to me a sinner.*

It cost something to walk to the temple and say that. It costs something now to mean it. But that one sentence, meant honestly, is the door into a different scoring system entirely — a scoring system where Christ's arrow is the one God measures, and you are asked to come into him, in the obedience of faith, and be counted with the one who never missed.

> *"There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus."*
> **Romans 8:1**

*In Christ.* Not *as good as Christ.* Not *trying to be Christ.* In him.

You will keep missing the mark, even after you come into him. That is why repentance is daily and not once. The arrow keeps falling short. The grace keeps reaching it. Both halves of the same whole.

## A prayer

Lord Jesus,

I am not a bad person.
I have used this sentence as a defense
for as long as I can remember,
and I am only now noticing
that the gospel does not fit through that door.

Show me the bullseye.
Show me the glory of God,
and show me how far my arrow falls short.
Wound me here, if you have to,
because the door of *I'm doing okay*
is locked from the inside,
and you are waiting on the other side of it.

I have missed the mark.
I miss it today.
I miss it in ways I cannot see
and in ways my neighbor cannot see —
and the smaller misses are the ones the world counts.

You hit the mark for me.
Cover me with that.
Re-aim the bow.
Teach me to walk in you,
where the score is your score
and not mine.

Amen.
