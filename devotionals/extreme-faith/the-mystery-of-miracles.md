# August 23 — Mark 9:24: The Mystery of Miracles

**I always wondered how the apostles could watch Jesus and still doubt. Then God healed Zeke, and I caught myself doing the exact same thing.**

> *"And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief."*
> **Mark 9:24**

## The science bus

Angela and I believe. We are also the kind of people who reach for the explanation first. My friend David is the same. We are the science bus. So when we talk about a miracle, we start with the physics of it. Quantum entanglement. The double slit. Some other branch of the wave function where the outcome went our way.

There is a costume like that for everything, if you want one.

But I wrote a while back about something I cannot stop seeing. The biggest leap in modern AI came from a paper called *Attention Is All You Need*. And the strangest result in physics is that attention itself changes the experiment. You watch the particle, and it behaves differently than when you do not. Attention collapses all the things that could happen into the one thing that does.

Here is what we forget. Prayer is the oldest form of attention there is.

> *"And all things, whatsoever ye shall ask in prayer, believing, ye shall receive."*
> **Matthew 21:22**

Ask, believing. Turn your whole attention toward God and the thing you are afraid of, and it does not stay formless. You can call that an attention mechanism, or a collapsed wave function, or a shifted dimension. You can reduce it to whatever you want. The attention was God's idea first. He built a universe that answers when you turn toward it.

## A heart they could not read

A few months into Angela's pregnancy, the doctors found something in Zeke's heart. Chambers that did not look right. Blood moving where it should not be. They said the words *Down syndrome*, because the kind of heart they were watching often comes with it. So we drove to Vanderbilt, week after week. They took another picture of a heart too small to fix, and sent us home not knowing.

I was finding my way back to church around then. So I prayed. And somewhere in those weeks the fear went quiet and turned into something I can only call certainty. I kept telling Angela that God was going to take care of it. The night Zeke was born, before the doctors came back with the tests, I told her he was going to be fine. There would not be one thing wrong with him. I knew it. I knew it in my heart, and I knew it in my faith. I was not trying to predict it or prophesy it. I just believed it, all the way down.

He was fine. The heart they had watched for months was whole. The doctors had no good explanation. And I went looking for one anyway.

There is a father in the Gospels who would understand me. He brought his sick boy to Jesus and asked Him to help, if He could. Jesus turned the *if* around: *all things are possible to him that believeth.* And the father said the truest thing anyone has ever said about faith.

> *"Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief."*
> **Mark 9:24**

Believing and doubting in the same breath. Reaching for the same God with both hands. That is most of the real faith I have ever had.

## Post hoc

There is a name for what my brain did in that hospital. Post hoc. After the fact, you build the clean, reasonable story for why the thing was always going to happen anyway.

It is the same move we make on the apostles. We read about men who watched Jesus open blind eyes and raise the dead, and still walked away unsure, and we cannot understand it.

> *"But though he had done so many miracles before them, yet they believed not on him."*
> **John 12:37**

How. How do you stand in front of it and explain it away. And then I stood in front of mine and started explaining. A misread scan. A lucky outcome. Inexperienced techs. Every instrument wrong at once. The devil does not need you to deny the miracle. He is happy to let you keep it, as long as you have somewhere else to set it down.

You can always find the mechanism. The mechanism was made too. The same God who answered wrote the physics the answer came through. You can trace every wire in the wall and still be wrong that no one is home. The Master Craftsman built the house and the rules of the house, and we are the ones living in it, mistaking the rules for the absence of the Builder.

So here is the extreme. Look at the healthy child. Look at the cleared scan, the thing you begged for and got. Say it was God, out loud, and do not take it back by morning. The reasonable explanation will always be sitting right there on the table. Leave it. Let it be a miracle.

I think about heaven sometimes, and about looking back on the morning I almost talked myself out of what God had just done. I think I will feel about myself exactly what I feel about those apostles. *How could you not believe? It was right there in your arms.*

## A prayer

Lord, I believe. Help my unbelief.

Thank you for Zeke. Thank you for the heart you made whole when all I could do was pray over it. I am sorry for how fast I went looking for another reason. I am sorry for the part of me that would rather be right than grateful.

Teach me to give you the credit and not flinch. When you answer, let me say it was you, and mean it, and leave it standing. Give me the nerve to call a miracle a miracle.

You made the attention. You made the wiring. You are still here, and you are still listening.

Help my unbelief.

Amen.
