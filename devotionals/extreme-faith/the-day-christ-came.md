# July 26 — James 4:14: The Day Christ Came

**I have always meant to get right with God. I just keep meaning to.**

> *"Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away."*
> **James 4:14**

## Someday soon

I am not fighting God. That is what makes it so easy to miss.

I like God. I am grateful, honestly grateful, for the people I love and the work I get to do and the slow gold of an autumn morning. And I fully mean to get serious about all of it. Soon.

Maybe you know the feeling. You are not running from anything. You have just never picked a day. Not this week, because work is buried and the calendar is full. Not this month, because the season is hard and there will be more room to breathe later. After the wedding. After the kids are grown. After this stretch of life settles into something quieter, you will pray the prayer you keep meaning to pray, have the conversation with God you keep rescheduling, finally close the distance.

And here is the strange mercy of it. The intention feels almost as good as the act. You plan to follow through, so the not-yet feels harmless. Postponing does not feel like a sin. It feels like wisdom. It feels like good timing.

## A man on a Monday

My grandfather preached one sermon I have never been able to shake. He told it as a story, about an ordinary man he called John Jones.

John wakes early on a warm Monday and decides to walk to work. He is a good man and a grateful one. His wife is a Christian; John is not, not yet, he has just never come around to it. On the walk he steps over a cracked sidewalk and tells himself the world is far too solid to ever burn. He passes a newlywed couple's car still trailing ribbons. He hears a neighbor's radio reporting that the wars are winding down and the whole world might finally be at peace. And the old accusation surfaces again, the one he can never quite answer: *John, you have done nothing about any of this.*

So right there on the sidewalk he makes his plan. He will obey. He will. Not Wednesday, because that is bowling night. Not Sunday, because he and a friend have one last fishing trip before the weather turns. The next Sunday, then, or the one after that. He is grateful, and he means it, and he is still turning *someday soon* over in his mind as he lifts his foot to step up onto the curb.

That is when the trumpet sounds.

> *"For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God."*
> **1 Thessalonians 4:16**

The world John thought was too solid to burn comes apart in his hands. He runs home. The bed is unmade and empty. His wife is gone, his children are gone, all of them ready in a way he never got around to being. He hides in the basement and begs the God he always meant to obey to let him stop existing.

## The verdict

At the judgment, John is found guilty. My grandfather named the charge in a way I have never forgotten. John was *"not guilty of murder, not guilty of adultery, not guilty of drunkenness, but guilty of neglect. Guilty of thievery, for he had stolen all the grace and the goodness that God had given him, and had given nothing in return."*

That is the sin most of us are actually in danger of. Not the lurid ones we are quietly proud to have avoided. The respectable one. Receiving everything, returning nothing, and calling the delay wisdom.

The neighbor's radio had it exactly backward.

> *"For when they shall say, Peace and safety; then sudden destruction cometh upon them, as travail upon a woman with child; and they shall not escape."*
> **1 Thessalonians 5:3**

The day will not wait for your calendar to clear. It will not wait for the wedding or the empty nest or the quieter season you are certain is coming.

> *"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ."*
> **2 Corinthians 5:10**

And to the part of you that fully intends to follow through, only later, the Spirit says one word back.

> *"Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation."*
> **2 Corinthians 6:2**

Now. The most dangerous word you own is *later*, because it wears the face of obedience so well. You can spend an entire life meaning to obey and never once do it. John believed God was real. He believed Christ died for him. He simply never came around to putting him on, never let himself be buried with Christ in baptism and raised to walk a new life (Romans 6:3-4), because there was always one more good Sunday to spend on something else.

The grace is free, and it is already on the table. Reaching out to take it is the work of a single day. This one. The vapor gives no notice before it lifts.

## A prayer

Lord Jesus,

I have always meant to. That has been most of my religion. I meant to pray more, to mean it more, to finally hand you the part of my life I keep holding back for later.

I am sorry for the years of meaning to.
I do not know what tomorrow holds, and I am done pretending I do.
Let today be the day I stop intending and start obeying.
Take what I have put off giving you, and take it now, while it is still called today.

Not someday soon.
Today.

Amen.
