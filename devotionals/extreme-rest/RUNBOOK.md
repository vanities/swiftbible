# Extreme Rest — Sunday Devotional Series Runbook

A recurring Sunday-only custom devotional series for SwiftBible. Each entry confronts the modern Christian assumption that productivity is virtue and rest is recharge. The goal is to make the reader uncomfortable with their own busyness — to expose hustle as a quiet form of unbelief.

**Why Sunday only:** PostHog data shows Sunday gets ~28% of all weekly devotional views. Custom Sunday content is where the audience actually is.

**Why "Extreme Rest":** Cessation as confession. Each devotional names a place where stopping is the most Christian thing you can do — and where the inability to stop reveals what you actually trust.

**Sister series (round-robin rotation):** Grace → Rest → Delight → Faith. See [Extreme Faith RUNBOOK § Rotation](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md#rotation) for the full rotation table.

---

## Shared structure

The markdown skeleton, four-beat structure, voice rules, and drafting workflow are identical across all four series. See [Extreme Faith RUNBOOK](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md) for the full reference. Series-specific differences are the schedule and per-devotional notes below.

**Voice anchor reminder:** The bold subtitle is a personal admission. The prayer returns to first-person. The body universalizes to "you" / "we." **I → we → I.**

**Diagnostic for "is this extreme enough?"**: if a successful Christian could nod along, it's not extreme yet. The extreme-rest claim should make the productive Christian feel exposed.

**⚠️ CoC theological note:** Rest topics touching the Sabbath should frame cessation as *the principle of Sabbath fulfilled in Christ* (Heb 4) — not as binding Christian Sabbath command. Use Heb 4, Mark 2:27, Matt 11:28-30 for NT theology of rest; OT Sabbath texts (Ex 20, Lev 25) work as principle-illustrations, not commands for Christians. See [Extreme Faith § CoC theological framework](../extreme-faith/RUNBOOK.md#coc-theological-framework).

**⚠️ Overlap with Faith Week 12:** Faith Week 12 ("Cease entirely") and Rest Week 2 ("A 24-hour confession") both touch the Sabbath. Recommended: let Rest carry the cessation theme; swap Faith Week 12 for a runner-up. The two series can share the underlying theology but framing differs — Faith is *demand*, Rest is *trust.*

## Per-devotional notes

### Week 1 — The bread that breeds worms (2026-05-17)

In Exodus 16, God provides manna daily. Two unbreakable rules: don't gather more than you need, and don't save it for tomorrow. The Israelites who tried to store it — *"it bred worms, and stank."* The next morning, more manna. New every morning.

The lesson is brutal and clear: the moment you start storing tomorrow's bread, you're not trusting God for it. You're trusting your storage.

Lead with: your savings account. Your retirement plan. Your fridge stocked for the week. The quiet logic of *just-in-case.* The way "wisdom" and "trust" started feeling like opposites and you didn't notice.

**I anchor possibilities:** *"I have a six-month emergency fund. I am told this is wisdom. I am not always sure it is trust."* / *"I have built a small fortress against tomorrow. I have called it stewardship."* / *"I have never gone hungry. I have not yet learned to trust the morning."*

Anchor verses: Ex 16:13-21 (the manna rules), Matt 6:11 (give us this day our *daily* bread — Jesus's prayer assumes daily provision, not weekly), Phil 4:19 (God supplies according to His riches). Optional: Prov 30:8-9 (give me neither poverty nor riches — feed me with food convenient for me).

The pivot: God isn't asking you to be foolish. He's asking you to remember the storage is not the source. The storage is for *acquaintance* with the source. The day you forget which is which is the day the bread starts breeding worms — even if it doesn't show yet.

End the empathy+Bible mix on: most of us are too far from hunger to feel manna's lesson in our bodies. The discipline of cessation (Sabbath, fasting, giving away large) is partly how Christians stay close enough to remember.

Prayer: short, specific. Returns to the I-anchor — *"I have built a small fortress against tomorrow. Teach me to eat today's bread without saving the leftovers."*
