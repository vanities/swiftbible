# June 14 — Hebrews 4:9: A 24-hour confession

**I cannot remember the last time I stopped for a whole day on purpose. Rest keeps happening to me by accident, at the end of my strength, instead of ever being something I chose.**

> *"There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God."*
> **Hebrews 4:9**

## Busy is the new fine

Ask anybody how they are doing. Count the seconds before you hear the word busy. We say it like a rank. The full calendar is our proof that we matter, and the empty hour is a problem to be solved.

Even our rest has become work. Sleep has a score now. Vacations have itineraries. Days off come with a list. We rest the way an athlete ices a muscle, so it can perform tomorrow. Rest answers to output. Output answers to nothing.

Underneath the busyness there is usually a quiet belief, and it does not survive being said out loud: if I stop, things fall apart. Somebody has to hold all of this together, and as far as my schedule is concerned, that somebody is me.

So the stopping never comes. There is always one more thing, and we are always almost done, and most of us have not had a whole day of nothing in so long we cannot remember what we would do with one.

## The first thing God ever called holy

The Bible opens with six days of work and then a stop. *And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it.* Stay on that word. Sanctified. Made holy. It is the first time holiness appears in scripture, and it is attached to a day. Before there is a holy mountain, a holy temple, or a holy priesthood, there is a holy rest. Weariness had nothing to do with it. God stopped to enjoy what he had made, and then he handed the stopping to us.

> *"The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath."*
> **Mark 2:27**

A gift, the way bread is a gift. And when God wrote that gift into Israel's week, listen to the reason he attached to it:

> *"And remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt, and that the LORD thy God brought thee out thence through a mighty hand and by a stretched out arm: therefore the LORD thy God commanded thee to keep the sabbath day."*
> **Deuteronomy 5:15**

You rest because you are not a slave anymore. Egypt never had a day off. Pharaoh's brick quotas did not pause for anybody. So a whole nation going still one day in seven was a sermon the world could watch from a distance: we have been bought out of Egypt, and our bread comes from God's hand, on God's schedule. The stopping itself said something. It was a creed you performed with your body.

We have stood in this lesson before. A few weeks ago it was the manna, the bread that bred worms the moment it was hoarded. Same chapter, other side: some of the people went out on the seventh morning anyway, baskets on their hips, and Exodus reports the result in four words. *And they found none.* The God who spoiled the stored bread also left the seventh-day field empty. He was teaching one thing from both directions: provision does not come from refusing to stop.

## The rest that remains

Be careful here, because the New Testament is blunt about where the Sabbath command stands now.

> *"Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ."*
> **Colossians 2:16-17**

The day was a shadow; Christ is the body that cast it. Nobody gets to bind a Sabbath on you, and this devotional is not trying to install one.

And yet Hebrews, written to Christians, keeps the word alive:

> *"For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his. Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief."*
> **Hebrews 4:10-11**

There remaineth a rest. The deep version of it is the gospel itself: ceasing from your own works the way God ceased from his, resting from the exhausting project of justifying yourself, trusting a finished work instead. Obedience keeps its whole place in the Christian life. Earning loses its place forever.

And notice the disease Hebrews names. The wilderness generation missed God's rest *because of unbelief.* Hebrews treats restlessness and unbelief as one condition. The man who cannot stop striving does not yet trust the one who finished. Then the writer hands us the strangest command in the chapter: labour to enter the rest. It is the one place scripture tells you to work hard at stopping, and it says that because stopping is hard.

> *"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."*
> **Matthew 11:28**

Rest, in the end, is a person. He gives it. You come with empty hands and receive.

## The confession

So here is the extreme-rest claim. Pick a day. Stop for the whole of it. Twenty-four hours with no output, no catching up, no optimizing, nothing that smells like the week. No law requires this of you; Christ already fulfilled that one. And that is exactly what makes it worth doing. A stop nobody commanded says more than a stop somebody scheduled. It becomes a confession. A whole day of nothing, freely chosen, says out loud what hustle quietly denies: the world does not depend on me. My worth was settled at a cross, and never at a desk. God runs everything without my help, because he already does.

Count the cost honestly, because there is one. The first hours will itch. The phone will call to you from the other room, and you will reach for it the way a smoker pats his shirt pocket. That reflex is information. Whatever you cannot stop doing for one day has stopped being your tool. An appetite that never takes no for an answer is called a master, and a man can own a great many things all week and still meet his owner on Saturday.

Some seasons make a clean twenty-four hours a fantasy. There are newborns and night shifts and animals that do not care what day it is. The confession is the chosen stopping, at whatever size you can honestly choose, and the principle scales down without breaking. Just do not let it scale to zero. Zero is a confession too.

And when you do stop, something almost embarrassing happens: everything keeps going. The sun comes up without your alarm. The church survives the week. The inbox waits, and the world does not so much as wobble.

> *"It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep."*
> **Psalm 127:2**

The watchman stays up all night, the psalm says, and the city's safety was never coming from him. Take the day. Catch God holding the world without your help. He has been doing it the whole time; the day off is just when you finally sit still long enough to watch him work.

## A prayer

Lord,

I have kept Pharaoh's schedule as a free man.
Nobody is holding the whip but me.

I have called the full calendar importance,
and the empty hour a problem,
and my hurry has been preaching a quiet sermon
about who I think holds the world.

Forgive the creed in my pace.

Give me one day with empty hands.
Let the itch come and pass.
Let me watch the sun come up without my help
and learn something true about you from it.

***You hold the world without me. Let me stop long enough to see it.***

Amen.
