# The Day Christ Came to Judge the World

> *The second known delivery of this sermon by James Edgar Green ("Brother Jim") — preached at the
> Spring Hill Church of Christ on December 22, 1998, about twelve years after the Greensburg delivery
> ([the-day-christ-came.md](the-day-christ-came.md)). Transcribed from the family's FLAC recording and
> lightly edited for reading: clear transcription errors corrected against the King James scriptures he
> quotes and, where the audio was unclear, against the earlier delivery; his 1998 wording and his
> changes (the Coca-Cola truck, the updated radio news, the longer invitation) are kept. The recording
> begins a few words into the sermon.*

---

…I could not help but be moved by the biblical truths that this little lesson, this tract, contains. And so I gratefully acknowledge that whatever good there might be in this lesson, I owe to brother David Roper, and whatever bad there is in this lesson, I owe to myself.

But the lesson brings into focus very sharply the limitations that are imposed upon human language. For if it were possible for me this morning to have a full command of the words and the moods and the shades of meaning, to express vividly the ideas — and ideas are just the mental images of the truth that this lesson contains — there isn't a single person who would leave this building this morning without being firmly dedicated to the service of God. Now, I don't expect that to happen. I don't expect that everyone here this morning will leave firmly dedicated to the service of God. But that sad fact is simply owing to the limitations we must cope with in trying to express our thoughts.

The lesson is a narration of a story. I'm going to tell you a story. The names in the story are assumed names, but the characters may well represent someone you know. And, alas, they may well represent you, or they may represent me. The story is about a man, and we'll call him John — just because John is a common name, we'll call him John Jones. The name could be your name or mine.

## The morning

The story begins on a Monday morning in the autumn months. John is awakened early. He is awakened by a beam of autumn sunlight. And John feels rested and relaxed, even though it is an hour earlier than his usual time for rising to go to work. And he thinks that, since it is a beautiful, warm autumn morning, and there won't be many more days such as this before bad weather sets in, he will walk to work. So he gets up and dresses quietly and quickly.

And as he drinks his morning coffee, he gazes in upon the sleeping form of his wife, Mary. And he smiles as he sees how comfortably she sleeps and takes her rest. And as John Jones leans his hand against the post of the bedroom door, he thinks: *How wonderful you are, Mary, and how thankful I am that God has given me a wife like you.*

Strange that John Jones should be thinking thoughts like this, because John is not a servant of God. John is not a Christian. But his wife, Mary, she is a Christian. And John has never come around yet to the point of obeying the Lord. And as he gazes in upon her, he thinks, *How wonderful you are, Mary, and I am thankful that God has given me a wife like you. And I know there isn't a single phase of your life that your faith has not influenced. You are what you are because you are a Christian. And I love you for that.*

And as he turns from that door, he gazes in upon the sleeping form of his little boy, Billy. And he smiles as he sees how soundly he sleeps, how he snores, just before the awakening hours of the morning. And once again this feeling of gratitude, this feeling of pride, crept over John Jones, that this was his little boy. And then he turns from that door and looks in upon the sleeping figure of his little girl, Susie. And John Jones knew that God had been good to him — good to him despite the fact that John Jones had given nothing back unto God. Funny that he should be thinking thoughts like this.

## It is good to be alive

And as he walks out the door and fills his lungs with the warm autumn air, he thinks, *It's good to be alive. It is good to be alive.* And the world is quiet. There are no sounds except the thrilling symphony of the songs of a thousand birds that thrust forth their songs of gratitude and praise to their Maker.

And John, why can't you understand? As he looks down the street on which he lives, he sees all the beautiful leaves on the trees, all the different shades of red — and there's still green in the trees — all the different shades of yellow and orange, all glistening and shining in the bright autumn sun. And John, why can't you understand that this is perhaps an omen, a message? John, why can't you see that all of nature is dying, that it is going to sleep? And John, why can't you see that a dying nature depicts a dying humanity?

But John doesn't think of the fact that all of nature is going to sleep, dying. And he does not think of the fact that he himself may die someday. The world is quiet before the invading army of breadwinners like John. There are no screaming children playing in the street. There are no housewives chattering across the back fence. And the air is pure from the putrefying influence of burnt gasoline that will soon be pouring from the army of automobiles that will be invading the streets and boulevards. And it is a quiet, beautiful morning. And John Jones is alive, and he is happy, and he is grateful to be alive.

## The sidewalk

And as he begins his walk down the sidewalk, his thoughts go back to how good God has been to him. And he remembered a sermon he heard. When was it? Was it last Sunday? No, no, it wasn't last Sunday, because that was yesterday.

*And I remember when little Susie came in to ask me and to beg me to go to church. And I remember I told her that I was tired. And then I realized I wasn't tired, because I had slept an hour longer this morning, because it was Sunday morning. Then I told her, "Well, Daddy has to do some work around the house. Susie, you go on with Mother. I have to — let's see — oh, yes, I have to clean and paint the rain gutters before bad weather comes."* And then John smiled somewhat shamefully as he remembered the excuse he had made to his little girl. And he remembered how Billy had wanted to stay home too, because Daddy stayed home.

But he remembered he had, on occasion, gone to church, and he had seen there, seated in the pews in a row, the Lemons family, the Curry family, and the Lowens family. And he thought how wonderful this was — that father and mother and children would all sit together, would listen and learn together. And once again John was face to face with the realization that he was letting his family down, that he was letting his God down, that he was neglecting important things.

But he remembered he had heard a sermon two or three Sundays ago, back in August, when the preacher had preached on the judgment. And he remembered when the preacher had read from Second Peter, where he had said that the earth and all of its works shall be burned up. And John didn't like to believe that. And as he walked along, he deliberately planted his feet upon a section of sidewalk and said, "This has been here for years and years. It is too everlasting." And then he walked down the street a little farther and came to a section of sidewalk that was cracked, broken. And John said — after he thought for a moment — "I suppose that the most durable things, the things that are material, can fade away." And then he continued his walk on down the sidewalk.

## The neighbors

And as he passed the home of the Currys, he saw Mrs. Curry stick her head out the door, and she looked both ways. And John stopped for a moment and smiled. No doubt she had just gotten up — her hair was a mess, and she still had her robe on. And she looked both ways, up and down the street, thinking that no one was looking, and she sneaked out on the porch and got the paper and went back in the house again. And John walked on down the sidewalk. It is just a world that is coming alive. And John Jones is alive, and he is grateful.

And as he walked, he could smell the odor of bacon frying across the street, coming from the Ingrams' home. And parked by the curb was a car all decorated with ribbons and crepe paper, and placards were printed on the car. And he remembered that the previous Saturday the Ingram girl had married the Williams boy, and they had flown away to California on their honeymoon.

John, why can't you understand that in Matthew 24, as in the days of Noah, they were marrying and giving in marriage — and so shall it be when the Son of Man shall come? As in the days before the flood, they were eating and drinking — and so shall it be when the Son of Man shall come. These are warnings, John. Why can't you understand them?

## Peace and safety

And he walks on, and he hears the steady, resonant tones of a man's voice speaking. And he realizes it is across the street, coming from a little white house that sits back off the road, where old man Glazier lives. The old fellow lives all alone, and he is hard of hearing, and he has his radio turned up loud. And the morning news is on. And he heard the commentator say that during the night, the leaders of Iran and Israel and Palestine and Iraq had gotten together, and they had discussed and negotiated peace talks. And it seemed like, once again, the world was going to enjoy peace.

John, why can't you remember a passage from Paul in First Thessalonians, the fifth chapter — when they are saying *Peace and safety,* then comes sudden destruction upon them, as travail upon a woman with child, and they shall in no wise escape? But John never dreamed of that.

As he walks on down the street, his steps alive with youth, his raincoat over his arm in case the day should bring showers, John is still musing over how good it is to be alive. And as he passes the neat-trimmed houses, he is grateful for God, he is grateful for country and for job and for luxury and for family. He is grateful for so many things. And yet the same nagging, accusing thought kept occurring to John: *John, you have done nothing about these things. You have done nothing.*

## Someday soon

And then John realized: *John Jones, you're going to have to do something to show God that you're grateful for all this. You're going to have to obey your Lord. You're going to have to obey him and show him that you're grateful for all of these things.*

When are you going to do it, John? *Well, I can't this Wednesday night — that's the night that me and the fellas go bowling. And, well, maybe Sunday I'll go. No, no — that's the weekend that Jerry and I had planned to get one more fishing trip in before bad weather comes along. And one of these days, I'm going to obey the gospel. I'm going to show God.*

And then he stopped at the corner of Oak Street to let the Coca-Cola truck pass that was making its deliveries. And John was musing over what he should do, what he was going to do, and what he intended to do someday soon. And then he resumes his walk after the Coca-Cola truck passed. And John was just raising his foot to put it up on the curb when it happened.

## When it happened

When it happened.

Now, John had read, and had heard read many times, in the fourth chapter of First Thessalonians, that the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, and with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God. And John had contemplated for a little while what that might mean. He had thought of a shout, and he had thought of a trump, and he had thought of the voice of the archangel. But he did not realize, nor did he dream, that it would be like this when it happened.

There was a shout, but it was more than just a shout. It ripped the air. It pierced to the very center of the earth, to the very marrow of his bones. And it stopped everything. And John Jones knew that this was it. And he began to cry out, "Oh, no, Lord. No, no, not yet. I am not ready, Lord."

And then, all at once, as if by magic, instantaneously, his heart had stopped beating. There had been a change. His hands were cold and clammy and lifeless. In the wink of an eye, a change had come over him. And half a block down the street, the Coca-Cola truck had wrecked and turned over, and beverages were spilling out into the street. And he thought, "Is the driver hurt?" And then he realized: what difference does it make if the driver is killed? Everyone is killed — and yet everyone is still alive.

It was a state of emergency. Everything was thrown into upheaval and chaos. And suddenly there struck across the dazed mind of John Jones one thought: *I must get home. I must get home as quickly as I possibly can.*

## Running home

And he turned and began running down the same street he had walked up just moments before. But it seemed like an eternity — a lifetime — ago. And the white, neat-trimmed houses were rocking and reeling, and they were crumbling. And as John passed that section of sidewalk that was cracked and broken, he thought, "What difference does that make now?" — because the whole street had opened up into a deep, wide chasm, and cars and trees, and the car of the Williams boy and the Ingram girl, were tumbling down into it. And John ran without hearing the sound of his steps, but feeling the crude vibration of his feet pounding the sidewalk. And he ran as hard as he could run.

And suddenly there arose before this man a spectacle that he could not believe. He thought he was imagining things, and he told himself, half-consciously, "I must watch and see if this happens again." And suddenly, there from the lawn of the Johnson family, the ground opened up, and a body came slithering up through the ground and drifting into the air. It was a resurrection. Bodies were coming up from the ground all over. And John ran as hard as he could run.

## The empty house

Now he could see the rose lattice on the porch of his little home. He had to get home quickly — Mary, Billy, and Susan were there. He turns up the walk and runs through the door that was open, and he screams, "Mary!" And there is no answer. And, groping blindly, he fumbles down the hall to the bedroom — and there's the bed, empty, unmade. Mary is gone. And he started down the hall and stumbled sprawling headlong across a piece of broken furniture, and he raises and pulls himself up and looks into Billy's room. He's gone. Susan is gone. And he stumbled to the kitchen. There was no Mary. Dishes were broken. The kitchen was in general disarray. And there on the counter was a note that he had left Mary, telling her that he was walking to work, that the car was in the garage, and that there was a coffee pot he had left on. But Mary was gone.

And suddenly John Jones knew: *Mary was ready to go. Billy and Susan were ready to go. And I am here all alone.* And then he thought, "Well, now, if they're gone, I must go too, because Jesus said that all men shall be gathered before him."

## I must hide

And one thought wedged itself into the numb mind of John Jones: *I must hide. I must hide. I cannot let God find me here.* And then he thought, "Where can I hide?" And then — the basement. He thought of the basement. And he opened the door and thrust his body through and fell sprawling to the basement floor, because the steps had broken away from the doorway. And he felt something damp with his hands, and it was a ruptured fuel tank, and oil was on his hands. And then he was horrified with the thought of fire. *What if a fire should happen?*

And then, ironically and bitterly, John Jones began to laugh. *A fire,* he thought. Peter said that the world and all that is therein shall be burned up. What difference will a hundred gallons of fuel oil make now? And he pulled himself to his knees and began to crawl into a corner. He must find some place to hide, because he is not ready to meet God. And he crawled into a dark corner, and he put his face into his hands, and he began to plead: "Please, God. Please, God. Please cover me up. Please let the house fall on me. Why was I ever born? Please, God, let me die. Let me become non-existent."

But there is one thing that John Jones never asked himself. He never asked himself: *John Jones, why have you waited so long to pray for God's help? John, why didn't you obey your Lord a long time ago? Why did you act and live as if your God did not exist? Why did you act as if the Lord had been crucified upon a totem pole instead of the cross of Calvary? Why have you waited so long?* And, John Jones, don't you know that you would not have obeyed him if he had given a thousand sons upon the cross — because you didn't love him enough, you didn't believe him enough?

## The great white throne

And as John Jones lay there babbling and crying and begging for God's help and mercy, suddenly he felt himself being lifted up into the air. And he remembered that the Lord had said that all nations shall be gathered before him, and they shall be judged. And he remembered that Paul had said, in Second Corinthians the fifth chapter and the tenth verse, that we shall all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, and we shall be judged according to the things we have done in our body.

And suddenly, from the basement, the floor just opened up, and the ceiling folded itself away, and by some unseen hand the roof was flung from the house. And he felt himself going up, up. And he closed his eyes and said, "I will not go. I will not look. I will not face him." And then he looked back at the earth, at a distance, as the earth exploded — her plains, her beautiful rolling hills, her gorgeous purple mountains, her oceans, her deserts, her lands, her houses, her wealth, her beauty, her riches were all burning up. And nothing was left.

And John Jones was going to judgment. And as he approached, he closed his eyes. But closed eyes could not close out the grandeur of the beauty that he saw. For, just as John had said in Revelation the twentieth chapter and the eleventh verse, he saw before him the great white throne, and him that sat thereon, from whose face the heavens and the earth fled away, and there was no place left for them. And John Jones saw the dead, the great and the small, standing before the throne of God.

## On the left hand

And John looked, and there was Mary, and there was Billy, and there was Susan. They were on the right hand. And John took his place upon the left hand of the throne. And he looked over, horrified, upon the smiling face of his wife and two lovely children. And Mary turned and looked at him, and she smiled. And he could not hear what she said, but he could see her lips say, "John, we love you. We love you so much. But we can't help you now. You see, darling — we just can't help you anymore."

And John Jones reaches out for his family, and he could not move, because there was an awful kind of gravity that kept him glued to his place. And then his name was called: *John Jones.* And mechanically, reluctantly, he moved forward, and the books were opened. And John Jones was judged, and he was found guilty. Not guilty of murder, not guilty of adultery, not guilty of drunkenness — but guilty of neglect of the gift that God had given him; guilty of thievery, because he had stolen the grace and the goodness and the blessings of God, and had given nothing in return. He was guilty of turning his back upon the Lord, of treading his blood-bought covenant under his feet. *John, you're guilty, and you must pay for all of eternity.*

And he felt himself being lifted up and hurled into outer space, away from the presence of the great white throne and him that sat thereon. And he drifted down, down, down, to a horrible kind of planet, where there were real lakes and rivers of streaming liquid fire, scorched rocks, incinerated earth. And here John could hear the frigid howls of demons and the curses of men. He could see nothing, he could feel nothing, except the agony of the terrible loneliness into which he had been thrust. And he will remain upon this cursed planet, to run and to grope and to crawl and to cry out and to fall, for all of eternity — because he went to judgment without his Christ.

## Now all of this is just a story

Now, all of this is just a story. It never happened. Thank God it never happened. But it will happen, just as surely as you're sitting here this morning — just as surely as you're sitting here today — unless you are in Jesus Christ, unless you recognize your obligation to God while you are living.

Now, please listen to me. Please listen to me this morning. If you are outside of Jesus Christ, listen to me today. I want you to think. I want you to think where you are, and what would be your fate this day, should it happen to you. If this description should come true tomorrow morning — I dare you to think, because if you think, you will act. You think about where you stand with God today.

Oh, someone might say, "I wouldn't respond today, because just as surely as I walked down that aisle today, someone would say, 'Look at that man, he's afraid,' or 'Look at that woman, she's scared of all those things the preacher said.'" Listen to me. I did not say the things I said this morning to scare you. I believe that I know my own heart — I did not say these things to scare you. But if you sit in your seat today and walk out of this building without confessing the wonderful, life-giving name of Jesus Christ, and walk into the jaws of a never-ending eternity without God, it is because you will not think.

You know, you might have many more opportunities to hear the gospel preached, and I hope and pray that you will. But why should you? Why should God give you many more opportunities? Listen — today in this world there are people who have never heard the name of Jesus Christ mentioned, and they will die without ever hearing it. And you have heard it over and over again. Why has God been so good? I must truthfully say, I do not know. But would you please think today — would you think just enough to know that God has had a purpose in giving you all of these opportunities?

## The invitation

Will you come today, believing that Jesus Christ is the Son of God; repenting, which is a change of mind, turning away from serving the world to wanting to serve and please God; confessing that faith, that you believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God; and being baptized into him to wash away your sins, being made one with him, added to his body, his church? If you are here and subject to this invitation, we pray that you will come. We plead that you will come this morning.

Now, if you are here this morning and have once done these things and remained faithful for a while, but have wandered back into the beggarly elements of the world, we plead with you to come this morning. You don't want to be found on the left hand of the throne of God at the day of judgment. If you have gone back into the ways of the world, I know that you want to be on the right hand of the throne also. If you're here this morning and subject to the invitation, then you need to come repenting, confessing, and asking the prayers of the church, that your sins might be forgiven, that you might be added back into the fold.

If you're here, subject to the invitation, will you not come at this time, while we stand and sing the invitation? Give me your hand, and give God your heart.

> *There's a great day coming, a great day coming,*
> *There's a great day coming by and by…*
>
> — "There's a Great Day Coming," Will L. Thompson, 1887

### A note on this transcript

Brother Jim preached this sermon at least twice. This is the **Spring Hill Church of Christ** delivery of **December 22, 1998**; the earlier **Greensburg** delivery (~1986) survives on YouTube and is transcribed in [the-day-christ-came.md](the-day-christ-came.md). The two are clearly the same sermon, re-preached: in 1998 he updated the radio "news" inside the story from the late-1980s headlines (Lebanon, Iran–Iraq, El Salvador) to 1998 ones (Iran, Israel, Palestine, Iraq), changed the milk truck to a Coca-Cola truck, and added a second appeal in the invitation to the Christian who has "wandered back into the beggarly elements of the world."

This reading copy was hand-edited from a machine transcript (mlx-whisper, large-v3-turbo). Clear transcription slips were corrected against the King James scriptures he quotes and, where the 1998 audio was unclear, against the Greensburg delivery: "the very marrow of his bones"; "this feeling of pride crept over him" (the machine heard "cry"); "struck across the dazed mind" (heard "a cross of the day's mind"); "the rose lattice on the porch" (heard "road slatteth"); "it was a resurrection" (heard "a judgment"); "wedged itself into the numb mind"; "the frigid howls of demons" (heard "fringed"); and the Revelation 20:11 quotation, which the machine doubled and scrambled. His 1998 wording and his deliberate changes are preserved. The verbatim machine transcript and a timestamped version are kept beside this file.
