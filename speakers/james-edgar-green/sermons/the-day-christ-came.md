# The Day Christ Came

> *A sermon by James Edgar Green ("Brother Jim"). Transcribed from the only known recording
> ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RIGZ0vIL0Mw)) and lightly edited for reading — caption

> smoothed, paragraphs added. His words and order are preserved. The verbatim auto-caption is kept

---

The subject of my lesson this morning is the day that Christ came to judge the world.

The lesson was suggested to me by the reading of a little tract written by brother David Roper. As I read this tract, I could not help but be moved by the way he expressed the biblical truths that this lesson contains. And so I gratefully acknowledge the suggestions that this little tract made to the lesson. Whatever good there might be in this lesson, I owe to brother David Roper. And whatever bad there might be in this lesson, I owe to myself.

The lesson brings into focus, very sharply, the limitations that we must cope with — the limitations that are imposed upon human language. For if it were possible for me this morning to have a full command of all the words and the moods and the shades of meaning, to express vividly the ideas of truth that this lesson contains, there wouldn't be a single person leave here this morning without being firmly dedicated to the service of God. Now, I don't expect that to happen. But that sad fact is simply owing to the limitations that we must cope with in order to try and express our thoughts.

The lesson is a simple narration of a story. I'm going to tell you a story. It is somewhat different from the manner in which I usually present lessons, but I think you will find it most interesting, to say the least, and I think it will keep your attention.

The names in this story are assumed names, but the characters could well represent someone you know. And, alas, they could well represent you, or they could represent me. The man we will call John — because John is a common name. We will call him John Jones. This name could just as well be your name or my name.

## The morning

John is awakened early. He is awakened early by a beam of autumn sunlight. And since John is rested and relaxed, even though it is an hour earlier than his usual time for getting up — and this is a Monday morning — he gets up. He decides that since it is a beautiful, warm autumn morning, and since there won't be many more days like this until winter invades, he will walk to work. So he gets up and he dresses himself quietly and quickly.

And as he drinks his morning coffee, he gazes in upon the sleeping form of his wife, Mary. He smiles as he sees how comfortably she sleeps and takes her rest. And as he leans his hand upon the doorpost of the bedroom, he thinks to himself how good God has been to him in giving him a wife like Mary.

It's strange that John should be thinking thoughts like this, for John is not a child of God. He is not a Christian. And Mary is a Christian. But John has never come around to the point of obeying his Lord — of putting him on in baptism. And as he looks at Mary, being so thankful that she is a Christian, he realizes that there isn't a single phase of her life that her faith has not influenced. She is what she is because she is a Christian. And John thought to himself, "Mary, I love you for that."

And as he turns from that door, he gazes in upon the sleeping form of his little boy, Billy, and he smiles as he sees how soundly he sleeps. Once again this feeling of gratitude, this feeling of pride, crept over John Jones — that this was his little boy. And he looks across the hall at the sleeping figure of his little girl, Suzy. And he knows that God has been good to him. God has been good to him despite the fact that he has given nothing back to God. God has been good to him.

## How good it is to be alive

As he walks out the door, he fills his lungs with the warm autumn air, and he thinks how good it is to be alive. *How good it is to be alive.*

And the world is quiet. There are no sounds except the thrilling symphony of a thousand birds that thrust forth their songs of praise to their Maker. As John looks down the boulevard on which he lives, he sees all the brilliant colors of the leaves. And I don't have to tell you how beautiful autumns are around here, because you live in the garden spot of it. He sees how the leaves are glistening — the reds, the yellows, the oranges, and even some green still mingled in — and how the sunlight reflects off of them.

And John, why can't you understand that this is perhaps an omen? That it is perhaps a warning? John, why can't you understand that all of nature is dying?

But John doesn't think of the fact that all of nature is going to sleep, that all of nature is dying. And John doesn't think of the fact that he himself, someday, will die.

The world is quiet before the invading army of breadwinners like John Jones. There are no screaming children playing in the streets. There are no housewives chattering across the back fence. And the air is pure from the putrefying influence of burnt gasoline that will soon be pouring from the army of automobiles that will soon be invading the streets and the boulevards. It is a quiet, beautiful autumn morning. And John Jones is alive, and he is well, and he is happy, and he is grateful to be alive.

## The sidewalk

As John walks down the sidewalk, his thoughts go back to how good God has been to him. And he remembered a sermon he heard. When was it? Was it last Sunday? No — that was yesterday.

"I remember when little Suzy came in to wake me up, to ask me to go to church with her and Mommy and Billy. And I told her, 'No, no, Daddy is tired.'" And then he realized he wasn't tired — he had only slept an hour later because it was Sunday morning. "And I told her, 'Well, Suzy, you go on ahead to church with Mommy. I have to do some things around the house. I have to — let me see — oh, yes, I have to fix the rain gutters. I have to paint them before bad weather sets in. And you go to church with Mommy.'" And he smiles, somewhat shamefully, as he remembers the excuse he had given to his little girl Suzy. And he remembered how Billy wanted to stay home too, because Daddy stayed home that morning.

But John remembered he had, on occasions, gone to church with his family, and he had seen seated there in the pews, in a row, the Lemons family and the Curry family and the Lawrence family. And he thought how beautiful that was — that families, father and mother and children, would come and sit in one row and listen and learn together about God. And once again John Jones came face to face with the realization that he was letting his family down, that he was letting his God down, that he was neglecting important things.

And he remembered a sermon he heard — when was it? Two, three Sundays ago, back in August — when the preacher had preached on the judgment. And he remembered how the preacher had read from the book of Second Peter, where Peter said that all the earth, and all that is therein, and all its works shall be burned up. Now John didn't like to believe that.

As he walked along, he stepped upon a portion of sidewalk and thought to himself, "Why, this sidewalk has been here for years and years. It's not going to burn up. This world is too good. It's too beautiful. It's too everlasting." And as he walked on, he came to a section of sidewalk that was broken — cracked. And John stopped for a moment and thought, "Well, I suppose that the things that are most durable, the things that are material, will eventually crumble and fade away." And he continues his walk on down the sidewalk.

## The neighbors

As he passed the home of the Currys, he saw Mrs. Curry stick her head out the door. He realized she had no doubt just gotten out of bed, because she still had her robe on and had curlers in her hair. And John thought she looked like a woman from outer space might look, with all the little antennas sticking out of her head. She looks up the street and down the street, both ways, and — thinking no one was looking — she steps out onto the porch, gets a newspaper and a bottle of milk, and goes back into the house. And John walks on down the street. Just a world that is coming alive. And John Jones is alive, and he is grateful to be alive.

As he walks, he could smell the odor of bacon frying across the street in the home of the Ingrams. And parked by the curb was a car decorated with ribbons and crepe paper, with placards written on it. He remembered that the Ingram girl had gotten married that Saturday before, to the Williams boy, and that they had flown away to California on their honeymoon.

John, why can't you understand that the Bible says, in Matthew 24, that as in the days of Noah they were married and given in marriage, and so shall it be when the Son of Man shall come? As in the days before the flood, they were eating and drinking — and so shall it be when the Son of Man shall come. These are warnings, John. Why can't you understand them?

## Peace and safety

And John walks on, and he hears the steady, resonant tones of a man's voice speaking. He realizes it is coming from a little white house that sits back off the road, where old man Glazier lives. The old fellow lives all alone and is hard of hearing, so he has his radio turned up high to hear what the commentator is saying. And John hears the commentator all the way out to the street, saying that during the night a ceasefire had been arranged over in Lebanon; and that Iran and Iraq had arranged for a settlement of peace between them; and that in South America, El Salvador and those countries down there had been sent to the tables to negotiate a peaceful way that they might have peaceful coexistence with one another once again. And it seemed like the whole world was going to enjoy peace once again.

But John, why can't you remember a passage from Paul, in First Thessalonians the fifth chapter, when they are saying *Peace and safety* — then comes sudden destruction upon them, as travail upon a woman with child, and they shall in no wise escape? But John never dreamed of that.

As he walked on down the street, his steps are alive with youth, his raincoat over his arm in case the day should bring showers. And John is still musing over how good it is to be alive. As he walks past the neat-trimmed houses, he is so grateful for God, and he is so grateful for country, and he is so grateful for job and luxury and family, and he is so grateful for so many things.

## Someday soon

But then the same nagging, accusing thought keeps occurring to John. *John, you have done nothing about these things. You have done nothing.*

And then John realized: "I'm going to have to do something to show God that I am grateful for all of this. I am going to have to obey my Lord. I'm going to have to obey him and show him that I am grateful for all of these things."

Well, when are you going to do it, John?

"Well, I can't this Wednesday night, because that's the night me and the fellas go bowling. And maybe Sunday — no, no, I can't go Sunday, because that's the Sunday that Jerry and I had planned to get one more fishing trip in before bad weather comes. And maybe the next Sunday, or the Sunday after that. And I'm going to obey the Lord. I'm going to show him. I'm going to show God that I'm grateful for all these good things he has given me."

And John stopped at the corner of Oak Street to let the milk truck pass that was making its early morning deliveries. And he was still musing over what he should do, and what he was going to do, and what he intended to do someday soon. And then he resumed his walk after the milk truck had passed. And he was just raising his foot to put it upon the curb when it happened.

## When it happened

When it happened.

Now John had read, and had heard read many times, in the fourth chapter of First Thessalonians, that the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, and with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God. And he had contemplated a little what that might mean. He had thought of a shout. And he had thought of a trump. And he had thought of the voice of the archangel.

But little did he realize, little did he know, what it would be like when it happened. There was a shout. But it was more than just a shout. It ripped the air. It pierced to the very center of the earth, to the very marrow of his bones. It ripped the air. And it stopped everything.

And suddenly John Jones knew that this was it. And he began to cry out, "Oh, Lord, no — no, Lord, not yet. I am not ready, Lord."

And then, all at once — instantly — John Jones knew that his heart had stopped beating. There was a change. His hands were cold and clammy and lifeless. In the wink of an eye, a change had come over him. And half a block down the street, the milk truck that had passed had wrecked, and milk was flowing out into the street. And John thought, "Well, what if the driver is hurt?" And then he realized: what difference does it make if the driver is killed? Everyone is killed, and yet everyone is still alive.

It was a state of emergency. Everything was thrown into upheaval and chaos. And one thought wedged itself into the dazed mind of John Jones: *I must get home. I must get home.*

## Running home

And so he turned and started running blindly back down the street he had just come up a few moments ago. And it seemed like an eternity ago. It seemed like a lifetime.

And the white, neat-trimmed houses were crumbling. They were rocking and reeling. And as he passed the section of sidewalk that was cracked, the crack had opened — he realized, "What difference does that make now?" The street had opened up into a wide chasm, and trees and cars — the car of the Williams boy and the Ingram girl — were tumbling down into it. And the air is filled with inhuman noises, screaming women and cursing men. And John Jones ran without hearing the sound of his steps, but feeling the crude vibrations of his feet pounding the sidewalk as he ran.

And he looked up into the sky. And the sun hung there like a black ball that had been short-circuited. And he remembered, in Matthew 24, that the Lord had said the sun shall be turned to darkness. And there across the sky, on the other side, was a great horrid red ball of moon. And he remembered that the Lord had said the moon shall be turned to blood. And it was hanging there as a great liquid pool, casting its horrid red hues across the black, boiling, confused sky. And stars are falling, as if from a tree of overripened fruit that had been shaken by the wind and the storm.

And suddenly there arose before this man a spectacle that he could not believe. He thought he was imagining things, and half-consciously he told himself, "I must watch and see if this happens again." And suddenly, from the Johnsons' home, the ground opened up and a body came slithering up through the air. It was a resurrection. Bodies were coming out of the ground everywhere.

## The empty house

And John ran just as hard as he could run. Now he could see the rose lattice on the porch of his little home. He had to get home quickly, because Mary, Billy, and Susan were there. And he turned and ran up the walk and ran through the door, which was open, and screamed, "Mary!" And then there was no answer.

And groping blindly, he fumbles his way to the bedroom door. The bed is empty, unmade. Mary is gone. And he turned and started running down the hall, and he fell over a piece of broken furniture and fell sprawling headlong. And he pulled himself up and looked in — and Billy is gone. He looked — and Susan is gone. And he stumbled to the kitchen. There is no Mary. Dishes are broken; the kitchen is in general disarray. And there on the counter was a note that he had left Mary, telling her that he was walking to work and that the car was in the garage in case she needed it during the day. And there's a coffee pot — Mary had been making coffee — but Mary was gone.

And suddenly John Jones knew: *Mary was ready to go. Billy and Susan were ready to go. And I am here alone.* And then he thought, "Well, if they're gone, then I must go too, because the Lord said that all men shall be gathered before him."

## I must hide

And one thought wedged itself into the numb mind of John Jones: *I must hide. Where can I hide?*

And then he thought of the basement. And he opened the door and thrust his body through, and he fell sprawling to the basement floor, because the steps had been broken away during all the upheaval and chaos. And he felt something damp with his hands, and he realized it was fuel coming from the ruptured tank. And he thought all at once, "What if a fire should happen?" Then he laughed — ironically, bitterly, he began to laugh. *What if a fire should happen?* Why, Peter said that the world and all that is therein is going to be burned up. What difference will a hundred gallons of fuel oil make now?

And he pulled himself up to his knees and began to crawl into a corner. He must find some place that God cannot find him, for he was not ready to go. And he pulled himself into a dark corner and put his face into his hands and began to plead: "Please, God — please, God — please cover me up. Please let the house fall on me. Why was I ever born? Please, God, please let me die. Let me become non-existent."

But there's one thing that John Jones never asked himself. He never asked himself: *John Jones, why did you wait so long to pray for God's help? John Jones, why didn't you obey your Lord a long time ago? Why did you act and live as if your Lord had been crucified up on a totem pole instead of the cross of Calvary? Why did you wait so long, John?*

And you know, John Jones, it wouldn't have made any difference if God had given a thousand sons upon the cross of Calvary. You wouldn't have obeyed him anyway, because you didn't love him enough, and you didn't believe him enough.

## The great white throne

And as John lay there babbling and crying and begging for help and for God's mercy, suddenly he felt himself being lifted up into the air. And you remember that the Lord had said that all the nations shall be gathered before him, and they shall be judged. And Paul said, in Second Corinthians the fifth chapter and the tenth verse, that we shall all appear before the judgment seat of Christ and give an account of the things done in the body.

And suddenly the basement floor opened up, and the ceiling folded itself away, and the roof was flung from the house by some unseen hand. And he closed his eyes and said, "I will not go. I will not go. I will not look. I will not face him." And then he opened his eyes and looked back to earth for just a second. And at a distance he saw the earth explode — her plains, her beautiful rolling hills, her gorgeous purple mountains, her oceans, her deserts, her lands, her houses, her wealth, all of her beauty and riches, were burning up. Nothing was left.

And as John Jones was going to judgment, as he approached, he closed his eyes. But closed eyes could not close out the grandeur of the beauty that he saw. For, just as the Apostle John had said in Revelation the twentieth chapter and the eleventh verse, he saw before him a great white throne, and him that sat thereon, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was no place found for them. And John saw the dead, the small and the great, stand before the throne.

## On the left hand

And John looked, and there was Mary, and Billy, and there was Susan — and they were on the right hand. And John took his place upon the left hand of the throne. And he looked over, horrified, upon the smiling face of his wife and two lovely children. And Mary turned for a moment, and she looked at him, and she smiled. And he could not hear what she said, but he could see her lips say, "John, we love you. We love you so much. But we can't help you now. You see, darling — we just can't help you anymore."

And John reached out for his family, but he could not reach them, because there was a terrible kind of gravity that held him to his place. And then he heard his name called: *John Jones.*

And then, mechanically and reluctantly, he moved forward, and the books were opened. And John Jones was judged, and he was found guilty. Not guilty of murder; not guilty of adultery; not guilty of drunkenness — but guilty of neglect. Guilty of neglecting the gift that God had given him. Guilty of thievery — for he had stolen all the grace and the goodness that God had given him and blessed him with, and he had given nothing in return. He was guilty of turning his back upon the Lord. He was guilty of treading the blood-bought covenant of Jesus Christ under his feet. He was guilty. *John, you are guilty, and you must pay through all eternity.*

And he felt himself being lifted up and hurled into outer space, away from the presence of the great white throne and him that sat thereon. And he drifted down, down, down, into a horrible kind of planet, where there were lakes and rivers of streaming liquid fire, scorched rocks and incinerated earth. And there John could hear the frigid howls of demons and the curses of men. He could see nothing, but he could feel the terrible loneliness into which he had been thrust.

And John Jones will remain upon that planet, to grope and to run and to crawl and to cry out and to fall, through all eternity — because he went to judgment without his Christ.

## Now all of this is just a story

Now, all of this is just a story. All of this is just a story. It never happened. Thank God it hasn't happened. It hasn't happened to you yet.

But it will happen to you this morning — just as surely as you sit here — just as surely as you sit here this morning and walk out this door into a never-ending eternity without obeying your Lord.

Now — not one thing that has been said here this morning, not one thing, is based upon speculation. Not one word, not one idea, is based upon speculation. Every word has been based upon a biblical truth. Now I want you to listen to me this morning. Please listen to me this morning. If you're here this morning and you're outside of Jesus Christ — listen, will you please listen?

You need to obey the gospel of Jesus Christ this morning. For we don't know — what if this should come true tomorrow morning? What if God should call you tomorrow morning? Where would you be?

Someone will say, "Oh, I wouldn't come down the aisle this morning. Surely as I came down the aisle this morning, someone would say, 'Look at that man, he's scared,' or 'Look at that woman, she's afraid of all those things the preacher said.'" Listen to me. The things I said this morning were not in order to scare anyone. I know my heart. The things I said this morning are to cause you to stop and think — to stop and think where you are and where you stand.

Should God call you this day — you might have many more opportunities to hear the gospel preached, and I hope and pray that you will. But ask the question: *why* should you have many more opportunities? Listen — today there are countless numbers of people living in the world who have never heard the glorious, life-giving name of Jesus Christ mentioned, and they will die without ever hearing it. So why has God been so good to you? I don't know. But please, give thanks this morning. Will you give thanks just enough to know that he has had a purpose in giving you these opportunities?

## The invitation

If you're here this morning, and you have never obeyed the gospel of Christ, we invite you to come — believing that Jesus Christ is the Son of God; confessing that faith; repenting, turning away from the errors of your old ways, the ways of the world, to do God's will; and being buried with him in baptism to wash away your sins, that you might be made one with him, that you might be added to his body, the church.

If you're here this morning and subject to the invitation, we encourage you to come, while together we stand and sing the invitation song.

> *There's a great day coming, a great day coming,*
> *There's a great day coming by and by,*
> *When the saints and the sinners shall be parted right and left.*
> *Are you ready for that day to come?*
>
> *Are you ready? Are you ready?*
> *Are you ready for the judgment day?*
>
> — "There's a Great Day Coming," Will L. Thompson, 1887

### When and where it was preached

**The sermon was preached at the Greensburg Church of Christ in 1986** (confirmed by Adam, his grandson). No date is on the recording itself.

A word on why 1986, since a literal reading of the tape might suggest otherwise: the radio "news" *inside* the John Jones story — a ceasefire in Lebanon, Iran and Iraq settling peace, El Salvador "sent to the tables to negotiate" — is part of the story Brother Jim is telling. It is scene-setting for a world lulled into "peace and safety" just before the end (1 Thessalonians 5:3), not a report of that morning's actual headlines. Read as the narrative device it is, it dates nothing. Family memory is the authority, and it says 1986, at Greensburg. That is almost certainly **Greensburg, Pennsylvania**, whose neighbor **Mount Pleasant** is named in the closing announcements and whose autumn is the "garden spot" he was preaching to.
