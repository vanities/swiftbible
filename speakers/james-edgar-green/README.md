# James Edgar Green ("Brother Jim")

Gospel preacher in the Churches of Christ. Adam Mischke's grandfather (R.I.P.).

This archive holds his preaching for reference, voice study, and memorial — sitting beside
[`../matt-bassford/`](../matt-bassford/). Where the Matt Bassford archive is built from 1639 blog
posts, this one begins with **two recordings of one sermon** — "The Day Christ Came," preached at two
congregations about twelve years apart. Everything here is built carefully and honestly from those
recordings, and the voice profile and biography are explicit about what is known versus what still
needs Adam to confirm. (More tapes are expected; each new one widens the picture.)

## The sermon

**"The Day Christ Came"** — a narrative "gospel-meeting" judgment sermon. Brother Jim tells the
story of John Jones, a good and grateful man who is *not a Christian* and always means to obey "some
day soon" — bowling on Wednesday, fishing on Sunday, the next Sunday or the one after. The Lord
returns ([1 Thess 4:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Thessalonians+4%3A16&version=KJV))
while John is on his ordinary Monday walk to work, raising his foot to a curb. His Christian wife and
children are taken; John is judged at the great white throne ([Rev 20:11–12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+20%3A11-12&version=KJV))
and found guilty — not of murder or adultery, but of *neglect* and *thievery*: he took all of God's
grace and gave nothing back.

- **Source:** <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RIGZ0vIL0Mw>
- **Acknowledged source:** a tract by *brother David Roper* (Brother Jim credits him for the idea)
- **Closing hymn:** "There's a Great Day Coming" (Will L. Thompson, 1887)
- **Preached:** Greensburg Church of Christ, **1986** (confirmed by Adam) — almost certainly
  Greensburg, **Pennsylvania** (its neighbor Mount Pleasant is named in the closing announcements, and
  western PA's autumn is the "garden spot" he addresses). See the dating note in the sermon file for
  why the in-story radio "news" is not a reliable clock.

## Across the two deliveries

The same sermon, re-preached about twelve years apart, with the topical details swapped for each era —
which is how we know they are genuinely two events and not one mislabeled file:

| | Delivery 1 — Greensburg (~1986, YouTube) | Delivery 2 — Spring Hill C. of C. (1998-12-22, FLAC) |
|---|---|---|
| Radio "news" in the story | Lebanon ceasefire, Iran–Iraq settling, El Salvador talks | Iran / Israel / Palestine / Iraq peace talks (the Oct 1998 Wye River accord) |
| The delivery truck | milk truck | Coca-Cola truck |
| The invitation | unbaptized only | adds an appeal to the *wayward Christian* |
| Source / transcript | YouTube auto-caption | clean FLAC → Whisper large-v3-turbo |

What stays fixed across both is the spine: the John Jones frame, the verdict of *neglect* and
*thievery*, "crucified upon a totem pole instead of the cross of Calvary," and the closing hymn. See
[the voice profile](../../.claude/skills/james-edgar-green/references/voice.md) for the full
keep-vs-change breakdown.

> **Note on dating.** Adam gives Greensburg as **1986**, but recalls the two as "nearly 10 years
> apart," and the late-1980s news inside delivery 1 (the Iran–Iraq *settlement* was 1988) would fit a
> first delivery nearer **1988–1990**. Worth pinning down; see the sermon's dating note.

## Known from the recordings

- Addressed as **"Brother Jim"** ("Thank you, brother Jim. Excellent lesson.").
- **Church of Christ** preacher: a cappella congregational singing, baptism for the remission of
  sins, the gospel-meeting *invitation* ("come… while together we stand and sing the invitation song").
- Preaches in the **King James Version**, quoting from memory.
- A **narrative preacher** — builds a single long story rather than a points-outline, and breaks
  into the story to plead with the character directly ("John, why can't you understand…?").
- A fellow preacher, **"brother Dave," was preaching in Mount Pleasant** around the same time
  (mentioned in the closing announcements — a possible regional clue, not confirmed).
