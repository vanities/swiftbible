*Now the firstborn said
to the younger, "Our father is old, and there is no man on the earth to
come in to us as is the custom of all the earth. "Come, let us make our
father drink wine, and we will lie with him, that we may preserve the lineage
of our father."*

*--Genesis 19:31-32*

The story of Lot and Sodom is surely one of the most
wretched of the entire Bible, and in this wretched story, there is no more
wretched episode than the tale of Lot and his daughters.  Of all the population of Sodom, Gomorrah, and
all the cities of the plain, these three are the only survivors.  They’ve seen firsthand the devastation that
accompanies disobedience to God.



And yet, as Lot and his daughters are dwelling together in a
cave above the valley, his daughters demonstrate that although you can take the
girl out of Sodom, you can’t take Sodom out of the girl.  They devise and carry out a scheme to get Lot
drunk and then commit incest with him, so that he will father children on them.  This is not one of the stories that little
kids learn about in Bible class.  It
revolts us, and it would revolt even people in the world if they knew about
it.  And yet, just as with all the other
stories in the word, this one has been preserved for us, and we need to study
it and learn from it.

Although this story teaches us once again about the folly of
turning to drink and the disasters it can cause in our lives, that’s not really
the primary lesson of the tale.  Instead,
the main thing we must take from it is that the moral decisions that we make
affect not only us, but also our children. 
It is impossible for us to shelter them completely from evil influences,
but nonetheless, we must do everything we can to prepare them for
righteousness, both by our own example and by keeping them as much as possible
from the things that will lead them astray.

When Lot first pitched his tent toward Sodom, he never
dreamed that it would lead to depravity in the cave above Zoar.  Likewise, too many Christian parents today
never dream that their intermittent attendance at services will lead their
children to forsake the assembly entirely. 
They never consider that when they skip Wednesday night Bible study to
take their children to a softball game or school activity, they are teaching
them that other things are more important than God.  They don’t realize that computers in children’s
bedrooms and smartphones with no restrictions are open doors for
wickedness.  They fail to grasp that a
child allowed to go the world’s way will in time give himself completely to the
world.  And then, when single children
and entire generations are lost to the Lord, the parents shake their heads and
say, “I just don’t see what I could have done differently.”  Let’s refuse to walk that same dark path with
our children.  Let’s dedicate ourselves
to keeping them for God.
