*"Please say you
are my sister, that it may be well with me for your sake, and that I may live
because of you."*

--Genesis 12:13

Sadly, the people of God do not always behave in a way that
is a credit to their Creator, and so it is with Abram in this story of his
wandering.  As a result of a famine, he
has traveled to Egypt.  While there, he
becomes afflicted with the fear that the Egyptians will desire his beautiful
wife, Sarai, and kill him to take her. 
His solution to the problem is to lie and tell the Egyptians that Sarai
is his sister.  Pharaoh takes Sarai for
his wife, but God punishes him with plague for taking another man’s wife.  Pharaoh recognizes the deception, and he
ejects Abram from Egypt.



Of course, lying has not passed from the earth since the
time of Abram.  All of us are tempted to
lie, and at some point, all of us have lied. 
Like Abram, we see some kind of trouble looming on the horizon, and like
Abram, we think that the only way we can get out of it is to deceive someone
else.  It might be that we want to avoid
a confrontation with our wife by telling her that she looks wonderful in that
dress; it might be that we want to get our sales figures for the month up by
telling the customer that Widget X is in fact very reliable.

Regardless of to whom we’re lying or why we’re lying, the
same problems always show up.  First of
all, no matter how skilled we are at deception, almost all of our lies get
found out eventually.  Despite all of
Abram’s best efforts, it wasn’t long before Pharaoh found out the truth about
him and Sarai.  If Pharaoh had been the
sort to murder men and marry their widows, Abram would have ended up in an
unmarked grave anyway.  So too it is with
us.  The more we lie, the more we have to
keep lying to keep up the appearances of the first lie, and the more we keep
lying, the more likely we are to be found out. 
As a result, lying does not actually avert the day of reckoning.  It just postpones it.

Second, the biggest problem with both Abram’s lie
then and our lies today is that they demonstrate a lack of faith.  Abram didn’t need to protect himself by lying
about Sarai.  He already had God to
protect him.  As long as he had God on
his side, there was nothing that Pharaoh was going to be able to do to
him.  Sadly, in the crisis, Abram didn’t
believe that.  He trusted in his own
cleverness and lied.  The same is true
for us.  God has promised us that He will
care for us if we follow Him.  We need to
have faith in His power to deliver us, not faith in our own lies.
