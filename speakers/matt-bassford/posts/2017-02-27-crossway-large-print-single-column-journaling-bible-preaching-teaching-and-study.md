**OVERVIEW.** This Bible has a truly ridiculous amount
of room to write in.  However, it’s a
heavy Bible, and it’s completely without any cross-references.

As always, I used the LPSCJB in my work for two weeks.  I preached two sermons from it, taught three
Bible classes (once I forgot it in my car), and used it at my desk throughout
the time period.  Here’s what I thought:



**PREACHING.** I’m not quite as doctrinaire about this as
I was a year ago (perhaps because I’ve found myself preaching fewer expository
sermons), but it’s nonetheless still true that if I’m preaching out of a Bible,
I want to be able to write in it.  The
LPSCJB fulfills that requirement in spades.

I’ve already remarked on the Foundation Side-Column
Reference-like feel of the LPSCJB.  This
is a big brick of a Bible, with something like 1600 pages.  However, there’s one significant difference
between the SCR and the LPSCJB.  In the
outer margin, the SCR has an inch-wide reference column, all the better to hold
those 96,000 NASB cross-references.  The
LPSCJB, however, has no references. 
Instead of the SCR’s inch of unlined space to write in, it has two
inches of lined space.  Basically, you
could write your life’s story in the margins of this one if you cared to.

The paper opacity here is decent, though it looks worse than
it actually is because the larger print is darker, heavier, and shows through
more.  I didn’t write in the Bible any,
but my photographer did (we had agreed that it would belong to him after I was
done reviewing it).  He writes with a
lighter, more precise hand than I do, but my impression is that there’s less
ghosting and bleed-through in the LPSCJB than there is in my Cambridge
Wide-Margin Reference.  The paper in the
LPSCJB even feels thicker.  The
Wide-Margin has been my workhorse wide-margin Bible for the past two years, so
the LPSCJB will be Just Fine for anybody who wants to write in it.

Of course, the paper in the LPSCJB can’t measure up to the
paper in the Foundation wide-margin Bibles of 15 years ago, but the same is
true of the paper in any modern wide-margin Bible.  In this decadent age, in which we worry about
things like whether paper-mill workers are going to get cancer from the paper
they’re making, the LPSCJB’s level of opacity is about the best we can hope
for.

Other than that, the LPSCJB did fine in the pulpit.  The print is large enough that even the
superscript verse numbers are conspicuous. 
I didn’t miss a verse citation or a reading in any of my sermons, though
the formatting of the ESV may have helped there.  Like the ESV editors, I believe that Mark 9:1
and Philippians 4:1 more properly belong to the preceding, rather than the following
contexts.  The editors’ decision to put
headings at Mark 9:2 and Philippians 4:2 rather than a verse earlier helped me
to conclude the readings where I wanted.

The only negative here is the LPSCJB’s size.  There aren’t any large-print wide-margin
Bibles with decent paper that are smaller than three pounds, and the LPSCJB
isn’t either.  As a result, I didn’t roam
around with it during my sermons.  It
spent the whole time sitting on the pulpit, except when I picked it up to
actively read from it.

**TEACHING.** Honestly, the LPSCJB didn’t fare as well
here.  My favorite Bible for teaching
auditorium Bible classes (in which I habitually rove around the front of the
auditorium) is a Pitt Minion.  The Bible
is so light that I can wander around with it in my hand for 45 minutes and not
notice it’s there.  Not so for the
LPSCJB.  It spent Bible class pretty much
nailed to the lectern.

Also, I like cross-references in my teaching Bibles, and the
LPSCJB doesn’t have any.  The
regular-sized Crossway Single-Column Journaling Bible is a tweener Bible, like
most Crossway Bibles are.  It doesn’t
have the full 80,000+ ESV reference suite, but it does have references to
Old-Testament passages cited in the New Testament and parallel passages in the
Synoptic Gospels.  Usually, those are the
only references I care about.

The LPSCJB, by contrast, has translators’ notes, but not a
single cross-reference to be seen. 
Usually, I can manage to teach a Bible class just fine without them (I
have a pretty good memory for the Scripture), but the LPSCJB’s trial period was
an exception.  I’m currently teaching a
class on Revelation, and a controversy arose over Revelation 13:10 (the passage
reads differently in the ESV than in the NASB and NKJV).  It would have been awfully helpful if the
LPSCJB had had a cross-reference that sent me to Jeremiah 15:2, which supports
the ESV rendition.  Alas, no.

Interestingly, though, not even an ESV tweener Bible would
have given me the reference.  You need a
full-on reference ESV for that one.  The
moral of the story may be that I should only teach Revelation out of a
reference Bible, so that it will catch the references I miss!

**STUDY.** The LPSCJB has nice big 9.5-point font,
so it didn’t give me any troubles with eyestrain, even on the mornings when I
had gotten less sleep than I wanted the night before and was low-grade headachy
and surly.  I do worship with a sister in
Christ, though, who uses the LPSCJB and says she has to wear reading glasses to
comfortably read the text, so this Bible may not be precisely ideal for older
eyes either.

I missed a cross-reference suite in it when I used it at my
desk too.  Just as it provided me with no
help in solving the Revelation 13:10 conundrum during Bible class, so too it
provided no help the next Thursday morning. 
This was less than ideal. 
Amusingly, I was able to locate Jeremiah 15:2 in an NASB reference
Bible, even though the passage doesn’t support the NASB rendering.

Nor did the size of the LPSCJB make it easy to use.  This is a long Bible.  I can’t set it in my lap, position myself a
comfortable distance from the keyboard, and still be able to read the whole
thing.  The top inch or two disappears under
the desk.  Note that my Wide-Margin
Reference performs better here because even though part of it disappears under
the desk too, it has a much wider upper margin. 
When you get right down to it, as much as I appreciate large print in a
Bible, I don’t appreciate the size and weight that go with it.  I’m likely to spend my life using a Bible
that’s as small as I can get away with.

**CONCLUSION.** The LPSCJB is in many ways a sellout
Bible.  It aims to deliver larger print
on decent paper with lots of room to write, and it does so by sacrificing just
about everything else.  Philosophically,
I approve.  The day may come when I have
to use a Bible like this one in the pulpit because my eyes won’t tolerate
Bibles with smaller print.  However,
because larger print isn’t such a pressing need for me right now, I miss all
the other elements that are sacrificed. 
I think this one deserves an 8/10 for preaching, teaching, and study.
