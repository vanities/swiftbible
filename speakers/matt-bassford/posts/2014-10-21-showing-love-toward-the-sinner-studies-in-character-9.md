*But Shem and Japheth
took a garment, laid it on both their shoulders, and went backward and covered
the nakedness of their father. Their faces were turned away, and they did not
see their father's nakedness.*

--Genesis 9:23

After the gloomy chronicle of first sins that makes up so
much of the early parts of Genesis, it’s a relief to encounter the godly,
respectful conduct of Shem and Japheth. 
When Ham came to them, filled with glee at their father’s drunken
indiscretion, they showed that they were heirs to the righteousness of Noah,
even when he himself wasn’t being righteous. 
They took a blanket, walked backward, and covered him without exposing
him to their eyes as well.



Though hopefully none of us will find ourselves in the same
situation as these two upright men, we can still learn a great deal from their
behavior.  Many of the principles that
governed their actions apply to us when we are dealing with the sin of another.  If we approach these situations with their
wisdom, we too will gain favor with God.

First, we should learn never to glory in the failure of
another.  Although the Scripture doesn’t
describe it explicitly, it’s not hard to imagine the contemptuous laughter with
which Ham must have portrayed his father’s pathetic state to his brothers.  We see none of that from Shem and
Japheth.  Sadly, when many Christians
learn of another’s transgression, their attitude much more closely resembles
Ham’s.  It’s almost as though they’re
glad to see the sin because of the opportunity it gives them to be proud and
laugh it up.  That attitude is every bit
as sinful as the behavior it mocks.  Sin
should never be a laughing matter to us. 
We should greet it not with sneering, but with sorrow and compassion.

Second, we should learn to take action to help.  Shem and Japheth didn’t just wait for Noah to
sober up.  Instead, they covered him lest
one of their wives should see him in such a shameful condition.  Like them, we should be too upright and too
loving to stand idly by and contemplate the iniquity of another.  When we see our brother in sin, it’s our
responsibility to go to him, not for the pleasure of getting him told, but so
that we can help him be saved from it.

Third, as we act, we should minimize the harm.  When Shem and Japheth went to cover Noah,
they walked backwards so that his disgrace would not be exposed to them.  Likewise, when we help a sinner, there’s a
noisy, disrespectful way to go about it, and a discreet, respectful way.  There’s no need to let half the church know
about somebody’s private sin.  There’s no
reason to run a brother down like a dog when all the rest of us are sinners
too.  Instead, let’s heal while doing as
little harm as possible.
