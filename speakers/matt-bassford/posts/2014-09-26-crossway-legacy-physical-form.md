I had coveted a Legacy ever since I started reading about
Bibles in early 2013.  I considered
purchasing one and using it for my primary preaching, teaching, and study
Bible, but I decided to use the Foundation side-column reference Bible I
already owned for that purpose instead.

Then (not as a justification for buying more Bibles, oh,
no!), I decided that I needed a Legacy to serve as an ESV-translation
reader.  My wife bought one for me the
next Thanksgiving as an early Christmas present.  I used it as a reader for about three weeks
before switching to a Pitt Minion. 
Thereafter, the Legacy sat in the Bible stack until I put it through its
rotation of two weeks each of preaching, teaching, and study; reading; and
travel.



**VITAL STATISTICS**

Translation:  English
Standard Version (2011)

Cover:  Genuine
Leather

Binding:  Sewn

Trim Size:  6” x 9”

Text Block Width:  1¼”

Weight:  2 pounds, 11
ounces

Column:  Single

Layout:  Paragraphed

Font Size:  9

Words of Christ: 
Black

Cross-References:  No

Ribbon Marker:  1

Overall Score:  13/20

I was pretty excited when I found a new genuine-leather
Legacy online for $35 (I had to tell my wife where to buy it, after all).  Because of my experience with the soft
genuine-leather covers from Foundation, I figured that the genuine-leather
grade would satisfy my longing for a quality reader.

Alas, it was not to be. 
My first reaction on encountering my Legacy’s cover was dismay.  I’m sure that Crossway isn’t lying to me, and
that the cover was manufactured from the skin of a single animal.  However, the “manufactured” part of that
statement was and is much more prominent than the “animal” part.  The cover was thin, glossy, and hard.  It reminded me much more of the
bonded-leather Bibles I owned than it did of the genuine-leather editions.  It was much less pleasant to handle than my
TruTone Crossway personal-size reference Bible ever had been.

Upon closer inspection, there appear to be several
manufacturing-related reasons for the thinness and the stiffness.  The first has to do with corners.  The corners on my Foundation SCR were cut
into wedges before they were crimped together. 
On some SCR Bibles, those little wedges of leather have a tendency to
become separated and start sticking up, which is unsightly and probably
shortens the lifespan of the cover.

In my Legacy, by contrast, the corners are not cut.  The uninterrupted leather is simply crimped
together.  I speculate that whatever
machine manufactures corners on leather-bound Bibles isn’t able to crimp the
corners when the leather is thicker. 
This means that either a) the corners are cut as in my SCR, or b) the
whole cover is made from a thinner, crimpable leather.  The corners of the Legacy, then, are
stronger, but the aesthetic appeal of the whole suffers.

Second, the grain impressed on the Legacy cover is very
strong and pronounced, much more so than with any of my other leather-bound
Bibles.  At a guess, the pressure
necessary to produce such a prominent grain also compresses and stiffens the
leather.

I did my best to break in and massage the Legacy’s cover
into shape.  Well, let’s be honest.  I folded, spindled and mutilated in the hope
that the cover would soften up.  It did,
kind of.  When I lift it up and tap on it
with my fingernail, it doesn’t sound like I’m tapping on a sheet of thick
plastic anymore.  When I open the Bible
in one hand, it droops over the edges of my fingertips and thumb somewhat.

However, in the course of all the folding, spindling, and
mutilating, I started a little tear at the bottom of the front endpaper,
whereupon I stopped.  The Bible is still
not pleasant to touch.  The grain on it
feels like the fake wood grain on one of those plastic park benches.  It may prove durable, but aesthetically, the
genuine-leather Legacy cover is a disappointment.

The text block of the Legacy makes a much more pleasing
impression.  Like almost all Crossway
Bibles, it is sewn, which keeps me from having to change Bibles more often than
I change my socks.  The paper is also
thicker than is normal for Bible paper. 
This may contribute to the unusual density of the text block.  The Legacy comes in at only two ounces
lighter than my Foundation SCR despite being half an inch thinner.  Sitting on my desk, it doesn’t look like it
would be as heavy in my hand as it is. 
The only negative presented by the text block is its cockling.  The cockling is moderate throughout the
Bible, but significant around Isaiah. 
It’s not anywhere near as dramatic as in the SCR, though.

All in all, I’m not nearly as pleased with the cover and
text block of the Legacy as I had hoped and expected to be.  The cockling is a sight problem, but the
stiffness and general unlovability of the cover is a much more significant one.  However, anything that reminiscent of a park
bench has got to be durable, right?  I
give the physical form of the Legacy a 3/5.
