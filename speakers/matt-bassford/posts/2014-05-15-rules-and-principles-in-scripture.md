*But solid food is for
the mature, who because of practice have their senses trained to discern good
and evil.*

-- Hebrews 5:14 (NASB)

Many people believe that the Bible is a great big rulebook,
in which everything that pleases or displeases God is spelled out in
excruciating detail.  The reality is very
different.  Certainly, there are many
areas in which God’s word sets out specific, bright-line rules.  However, it is equally common for God to set
forth broad spiritual principles and expect us to apply those principles in an
intelligent and godly way.



The word operates in this way for at least two reasons.  First, God’s desire for us is not that we
become rule-following robots, but that we learn to be disciples of Jesus.  If we never develop our own capacities for
moral reasoning, we can never become like him.

Second, even if God had expanded the Bible to enough volumes
to fill a library, His word still could not provide a specific answer to every
spiritual question.  The U. S. Code
contains dozens of volumes, but gaps in federal law are litigated every
day.  A Bible of that size would still
contain “loopholes” for the ungodly to squirm through, and it would be as
mystifying to the godly as the law of the United States is to its citizens.

It is our responsibility, then, not merely to consider the
letter of the law of Christ, but to apply its spirit.  Different Christians will sincerely arrive at
different answers about what that application should be.  As we consider our answers, we must take care
that we are not seeking to justify our conduct or exalt our wisdom, but only to
glorify God.
