In [my last post in the series](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2017/02/comparing-six-volume-crossway-readers.html), I looked at the history of two multi-volume
Bibles:  Bibliotheca and the ESV Reader’s
Bible, Six-Volume Set (a.k.a. the Six-Volume Set).  I also talked about how either multi-volume
Bible will be a superior reader to a standard one-volume Bible.  One-volume Bibles are always hindered by
space constraints and usually hindered by reference helps so that it’s
impossible for them to rise to the level of a Bible with neither problem.  However, as alike as they are, there are a
number of ways in which Bibliotheca and the Six-Volume Set are different too,
and those things are also worth exploring.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiLaEks3EID35hy2yW1M2jT0S75v4yfXdhhCRzbVcVvGLkeGIaX0pYwLdBLPhN8CUzJ-rW3CupfPgPEVgdOBs8Uol7YVHp0jsGY25py_TgjdIA2W4dkfZH7_loATZdLTCGi9eVgEj37JQ/s640/EXT+ESV+color+compare.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiLaEks3EID35hy2yW1M2jT0S75v4yfXdhhCRzbVcVvGLkeGIaX0pYwLdBLPhN8CUzJ-rW3CupfPgPEVgdOBs8Uol7YVHp0jsGY25py_TgjdIA2W4dkfZH7_loATZdLTCGi9eVgEj37JQ/s1600/EXT+ESV+color+compare.jpg)



These differences begin with the exterior.  Both sets are the same kind of thing:  a number of volumes (five in the case of
Bibliotheca, while the Six-Volume set contains, stunningly, six) in a
multivolume slipcase.  However, this
basic form is executed very differently.



**SLIPCASES.** Let’s start by considering the
slipcases.  The Bibliotheca slipcase is an
unmarked dark gray.  All that keeps it
from being a box missing its front is chevron-shaped indentations on either
side of the opening.  These indentations
are intended to serve a function much like the thumb holes in a single-volume
Bible slipcase.  You’re supposed to be
able to grab the book at the indentation so you can take it out of the slipcase
without having to turn the slipcase downward and shake it.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhmHo4d1_yP2llFH_615La_8_KEzC2xeRXvKZsMlXxR0paX2XB35rPmSi1tU9TtgFyc4kOexpuzDEA18g3CRaV2g25gtJqGo1zYjFHZ3VPZ3tjLtjH7eE2S-d1Bec7h58A1Cjco8IV7nw/s640/EXT+Bibliotheca+slipcase+chevrons.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhmHo4d1_yP2llFH_615La_8_KEzC2xeRXvKZsMlXxR0paX2XB35rPmSi1tU9TtgFyc4kOexpuzDEA18g3CRaV2g25gtJqGo1zYjFHZ3VPZ3tjLtjH7eE2S-d1Bec7h58A1Cjco8IV7nw/s1600/EXT+Bibliotheca+slipcase+chevrons.jpg)



Sadly, in practice, this doesn’t work so well.  You can’t reach the three inner volumes of Bibliotheca
from the indentations at all, and even for the outer volumes, one side of the
book isn’t enough to grip it and pull it out. 
If I want to get a volume out, I have to tilt the whole slipcase down
and catch the volumes as they slide out. 
This is not precisely convenient.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhp_vH9Py4PxmbQhjWo_0fiK4IOAtE7alPYhBOrJSG1YIy7czA6QUGQL5T7KALK_rqlH5dozxNUZp0_no_6IfT_V79N_h2PzJs9Ouw-BvWRL834hVvk_5T3G0cD9NAKD3vUvXthjTDqGg/s640/EXT+Biblio+pulling+out.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhp_vH9Py4PxmbQhjWo_0fiK4IOAtE7alPYhBOrJSG1YIy7czA6QUGQL5T7KALK_rqlH5dozxNUZp0_no_6IfT_V79N_h2PzJs9Ouw-BvWRL834hVvk_5T3G0cD9NAKD3vUvXthjTDqGg/s1600/EXT+Biblio+pulling+out.jpg)



The Six-Volume Set slipcase, by contrast, is much better
executed.  For one thing, it’s much
sturdier than the Bibliotheca slipcase, even though it’s wider.  Crossway achieved this effect by having it
manufactured out of cardboard that’s twice as thick as the cardboard in the
bibliotheca slipcase.  In consequence,
when I take one of the volumes of Bibliotheca out and shake the slipcase, it’s
lost enough rigidity that the whole thing wiggles back and forth slightly.  I can’t do that with the slipcase for the
six-volume set, even with a volume missing.

The slipcase of the Six-Volume Set is also easier to
use.  Instead of being rectangular with a
chevron-shaped indentation, its sides are trapezoidal with the narrow part
toward the top.  As a result, the first
inch of the top of each volume is exposed. 
This allows me to slide each volume out slightly by pulling on the
headband area, then grasping the book and pulling it out the rest of the way.  I don’t need both hands to extract what I want
to read.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEho8qs6pzfXUCTVDjSmmy3r3lNEkErsQpFemj9mWdp1DM2VT5cPt_FGVHW_Ad7QuGW-FdIC0cLumR-Js_QQPIojRLf0iH6j8omidaq03Szy2P-JHXNb1jQ5RWrdvIR2uhSyecYmcMwf1A/s640/EXT+slipcase+trapezoid.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEho8qs6pzfXUCTVDjSmmy3r3lNEkErsQpFemj9mWdp1DM2VT5cPt_FGVHW_Ad7QuGW-FdIC0cLumR-Js_QQPIojRLf0iH6j8omidaq03Szy2P-JHXNb1jQ5RWrdvIR2uhSyecYmcMwf1A/s1600/EXT+slipcase+trapezoid.jpg)



Also, rather than being featureless like the Bibliotheca
slipcase, the Six-Volume Set slipcase contains a thoughtful feature
inside.  There are strips of bookboard a
tenth of an inch high running from the back of the box to the front.  Each one is the width of the text block of
the volume that is intended to be sitting there, so that when the volume is
slid into place, the text block rests on the bookboard strip.  This keeps the text block from sagging over
time and prevents volumes from tipping over when their neighbors have been
removed.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgeuTgXyizs9XYec4S7ODNJHR_NrKk9hgqclHZt1azeAJEwmO82rYMrqrm1c3NPMciqW47ltPMilAXQYxfOsSYbVSNVqDa16D-dl_rVeWlwjDSWEgzDiGRaO46x6l9u6q9usojrilJKSA/s640/EXT+slipcase+strips.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgeuTgXyizs9XYec4S7ODNJHR_NrKk9hgqclHZt1azeAJEwmO82rYMrqrm1c3NPMciqW47ltPMilAXQYxfOsSYbVSNVqDa16D-dl_rVeWlwjDSWEgzDiGRaO46x6l9u6q9usojrilJKSA/s1600/EXT+slipcase+strips.jpg)



In terms of ornamentation, there’s no clear winner.  If you like plain, you’ll like the looks of
the Bibliotheca slipcase better.  If you
like fancy, you’ll prefer the ornamentation on the sides of the Six-Volume Set
slipcase.  In terms of function, though,
the latter is much better thought-out.

**VOLUME
EXTERIORS.** Here too, the aesthetic
of Bibliotheca is notably understated. 
The spine of each volume contains only “Bibliotheca”, a dividing line,
and the name of the particular volume, for instance “The Writings”.  By contrast, the Six-Volume Set pursues a
much more elaborate, *Masterpiece Theatre*-esque
aesthetic.  Each spine contains volume
name (“Pentateuch”), the ESV logo, volume number (“I”), “English Standard
Version”, and Crossway, each bit of information set off by dividers of varying
elaborateness.  Personally, I prefer the
latter (it looks much more interesting on a bookshelf), but that’s entirely a
judgment call.

Because I wasn’t about to spring for the calfskin-over-board
version of the Single-Volume Set (too rich for my blood!) both the version I
did buy and Bibliotheca are clad in book cloth. 
The book cloth of the Six-Volume set is slightly coarser and has more of
a texture to it, though not nearly as much as the downright nubbly book cloth
of the cloth-over-board Crossway Interleaved Journaling Bible.  I like the more pronounced texture, but this
is entirely subjective too.  The ribbons
in the Six-Volume Set are slightly thicker and appear to be slightly
better-quality, if you’re one of the people who cares about ribbons.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiA2mN9AQpUuFx7my7mlMeZkD3Capp6GKdPoCXdb0_V98x3xN489FFfq_zBzFPnnHLLNVBWv5GjGDUVpll617uCEyiEl9omrDyWKIkqIOvSBiYtxONZ7PxdKPFkVyomQeUV-MQ_AcooyQ/s640/EXT+ESV+spine+details.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiA2mN9AQpUuFx7my7mlMeZkD3Capp6GKdPoCXdb0_V98x3xN489FFfq_zBzFPnnHLLNVBWv5GjGDUVpll617uCEyiEl9omrDyWKIkqIOvSBiYtxONZ7PxdKPFkVyomQeUV-MQ_AcooyQ/s1600/EXT+ESV+spine+details.jpg)



It is true, though, that Bibliotheca spices its design up a
little bit by making each volume a slightly different color.  The Pentateuch-and-Former-Prophets volume is
nearly black; the New-Testament volume is nearly white, with the volumes in
between having covers of intermediate shades. 
The Six-Volume Set, on the other hand, is of a uniform dark brown.  I see advantages to both, but no overall
advantage to one over the other.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh7Q72hdIlNXHXyNCm5vB0CuJ6amH5b9nYeM2dUEg-upe2u3I2Mw4Et4ddzUweZ6bavH0SxBwWgE5mcd-ciAcW8YjgPYfHGNSChRRO2-SFKoUJXxIUpR7LKEk2e1NbndR-oGRDeEbyGmA/s640/EXT+color+compare.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh7Q72hdIlNXHXyNCm5vB0CuJ6amH5b9nYeM2dUEg-upe2u3I2Mw4Et4ddzUweZ6bavH0SxBwWgE5mcd-ciAcW8YjgPYfHGNSChRRO2-SFKoUJXxIUpR7LKEk2e1NbndR-oGRDeEbyGmA/s1600/EXT+color+compare.jpg)

**CONCLUSION.** Yes, you shouldn’t judge a book by its
cover, but every Bible geek knows that covers matter.  I don’t see anywhere where Bibliotheca is
poorly executed, but I think the Six-Volume Set is simply better done.  Crossway’s institutional experience and
bigger design team (I think Adam Lewis Greene did all the Bibliotheca design
himself) give them the edge here.
