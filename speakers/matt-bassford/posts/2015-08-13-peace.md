*“Peace I leave with
you; My peace I give to you; not as the world gives do I give to you. Do not
let your heart be troubled, nor let it be fearful.”*

- John 14:27 (NASB)

We live in an affluent society.  All of us except for the very poorest have material
goods in abundance.  However, peace often
does not appear on the list of our possessions. 
Despite our wealth, we suffer from a lack of contentment.  Americans spend countless hours and countless
millions of dollars on the search for peace, yet are no more fulfilled at the
end of the search than at its beginning.



Against this cultural backdrop, our Lord’s promise of peace
is all the more striking.  However, we
must be careful to pay attention to the limitations that He places on it.  The peace of Christ is *not* the peace of the world. 
It is not the peace of ease, the peace of absence of conflict, or the
peace of a trouble-free existence.  These
things are often promised by the preachers of the prosperity gospel, but they
have nothing to do with genuine Christianity.

Instead, the peace of Christ is the peace He identifies in
the second half of the verse.  It is the
freedom of our hearts from trouble and fear. 
This freedom is not the result of outward circumstances.  We can go through terrible conflict and
dramatic adversity, yet retain this peace within us.  Instead, it is the fruit of trust and
faith.  When we confide our lives to
Jesus, we can rest in His assurance that He will protect us.

This is the peace that allows men and women of God to rise
above trial.  They are content in
ordinary circumstances, but they remain content even when nothing seems to be
going right.  In the midst of the worst
grief that this life has to offer—the loss of a spouse, the loss of a child,
the imminence of their own deaths—they grieve, yes, but that grief is like fog
drifting around the rock of their faith. 
The blessings that matter most to them are blessings beyond the power of
the devil to take.  Today, it may not be
well with them, but they know that it will be well.

However, this priceless peace does not come without
cost.  If we want to have Jesus to rely
on in the dark times, we have to seek Him all the time.  If we’ve never made the effort to know Him,
how can we expect to trust Him?  None of
us would feel comfortable entrusting our valuables to a stranger; still less do
we feel safe in entrusting our souls to one. 
Only as we taste and see that the Lord is good can we come to understand
that He is good all the time.

Paradoxically, this
is what keeps the world’s multitudes who long for peace from finding peace in
Jesus.  They don’t want a friend; they
want an insurance policy.  They don’t
want a King; they want an EMT.  Because
they aren’t willing to surrender their lives to Him, they will never have the
opportunity to surrender their cares to Him either.

They hurt.  They
suffer.  They cry for peace, but peace
continually escapes them.  It is not that
Jesus is unwilling to help them.  It is
that they are unwilling to seek Him. 
They will give anything for peace except the one thing that they must
give, and they will seek it anywhere except from the One who can truly give it.
