*So Sarai said to
Abram, "See now, the LORD has restrained me from bearing children. Please,
go in to my maid; perhaps I shall obtain children by her." And Abram
heeded the voice of Sarai.*

--Genesis 16:2 (NKJV)

In our last encounter with Abram, we saw him at one of the
high points of his faith.  Here, we see
him at one of the lowest.  God has
assured him that he is going to have heirs from his own body; in fact, God has
even made a covenant with him about it. 
However, when he returns to his tents, the physical reality of his life
intervenes.  He still doesn’t have any
children; his wife is, for all intents and purposes, barren.  There is no sign that God’s promise is going
to be fulfilled under the status quo, perhaps ever.



In these desperate circumstances, Abram and Sarai decide to
act.  Rather than waiting on God’s
promise, they try to fulfill the promise themselves.  Sarah offers Abram her maid Hagar, and Abram
takes her to wife and goes in to her instead. 
In a sense, the plan does work. 
Hagar does have a son, Ishmael. 
However, this decision leads to bitter strife in Abram’s household, and
it doesn’t bring God’s promise any closer to being fulfilled.  Both Abram and Sarai would have been better
off if they had chosen not to try to kick-start God’s plan at all.

There are many times in our lives when, like Abram and
Sarai, we grow weary of waiting for God’s way to work.  Sometimes, this occurs in the religious
realm.  We look at our modestly sized
assemblies, then consider the thousands and thousands of people who run after
falsehood, and wonder what’s going on. 
We faithfully follow the commandments of God, and become discouraged
when our obedience does not seem to make our lives better or happier.

Such moments, though, are not an excuse to doubt.  They are a call to faith and patience.  Sooner or later, God will work His will
out.  That will may not be what we
want.  It may not appear for months and
years.  It may not even appear in this
life at all.  However, God has not
forgotten us, and He has not forgotten His promises.  In heaven if not before, our faith will see
its reward.

The worst thing we can do while we wait is allow our doubt
to move us to abandon God’s plan.  So
what if human innovations offer the hope of bringing in large crowds to our
worship services?  So what if we think we
can be happier by abandoning our lackluster spouse, or by adopting the world’s
strategies for getting ahead?  Nothing
good lies down those roads.  They appear
to offer us what we want, but when we get there, we only find out that it isn’t
really what we wanted at all.  Only God’s
way works, and if we have to wait for that way to develop, so be it.
