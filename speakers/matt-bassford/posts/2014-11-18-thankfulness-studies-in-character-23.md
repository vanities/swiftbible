*And Sarah said,
"God has made me laugh, and all who hear will laugh with me." She
also said, "Who would have said to Abraham that Sarah would nurse
children? For I have borne him a son in his old age."*

*--Genesis 21:6-7*

Many parts of Genesis are not particularly uplifting.  Because the Bible so unflinchingly records
the sin of even its greatest heroes, we often come away from the study of the
word feeling vaguely disappointed that people 4000 years ago weren’t any better
than people today.  Certainly the story
of Sodom is not one to bring cheer to the Christian heart.



However, just as still happens today, there are times in
Genesis when a Bible character gets it right, and we see one of those times in
the way that Sarah reacts to the birth of her son.  When God promised her that she would have a
child, she laughed in disbelief, but when she does conceive and give birth, she
turns the joke around in a very warmhearted way.  She names the child Isaac, which means “he
laughs” in Hebrew, in a self-deprecating nod to her original reaction.  Furthermore, she makes clear that despite her
own lack of faith, God has blessed her anyway, and she couldn’t be more
thankful.

In our society, thankfulness is something of a lost
art.  For many people, when someone does
something nice for them, they accept it with a shrug of entitlement, as their
due.  Of course, when someone isn’t nice
to them, it’s time to call for a firing squad. 
Sadly, even some Christians go through life with this kind of chip on
their shoulders.

Inevitably, this flawed outlook affects our relationship
with God too.  We like to pretend that we
are big fierce self-reliant red-blooded Americans, but in reality, we are
completely dependent on His mercy and His providence for everything we
have.  Too often, we view things like the
right to pray and the opportunity to assemble with a casual contempt that
forgets how precious those things are and the price that was paid for
them.  Even when we are in some difficult
situation and we pray to God for help, once we get that help, we accept it
unthinkingly and move on.  All of this
points to a mindset problem that all of us share to some extent.  When we are blessed, it’s all too easy to
forget to think of them as blessings.  We
zero in on ourselves and lose focus on God.

Interestingly enough, we can rediscover our attitude of
thankfulness by listening to our children. 
When a small child prays, typically, he’s not interested in help at
school or new toys or any of the scaled-down equivalents to the things we pray
for.  Instead, his prayer is usually one
long string of things he’s thankful for. 
Sometimes, this thankfulness reaches the level of comedy, as Junior
solemnly expresses his gratitude for his bed, the kitchen sink, the grass in
the front yard, and innumerable other things we don’t even consider.  We laugh, but perhaps we should be
learning.  Isn’t God responsible for all
those things?  Wouldn’t we miss them if
they were gone?  There’s no such thing as
excessive thankfulness.  Let’s work on
increasing our gratitude today.
