In my evangelistic work, I like to use a trick that I
learned about from Dempsey Collins a number of years ago.  The trick is this:  when you’re studying with somebody, be sure
to study from a set of Bibles that are identical.  That way, if you’re studying along and
realize that the student doesn’t know Genesis from Revelation, you can turn to
John 3:16, find the page number, and tell them to turn to that page
number.  Otherwise, you spend the whole
study session passing Bibles back and forth, which is time-consuming and
demeaning.



When I do this, my preferred text block is the Crossway
Thinline, which of course is an ESV.  For
years, Crossway has marketed a Bible called the Value Thinline that is the
perfect giveaway Bible.  It’s poly-bound
and Smyth-sewn, so it’s durable (I preached out of a Crossway poly Bible for
five years and still didn’t wear it out completely, and most people won’t put
preacher levels of wear on a Bible), and it’s also super-inexpensive.  I’m not aware of any other Bible in any other
translation that offers comparably high quality at a comparably low price.

As a result, when somebody shows up for a study with a
borrowed Bible, with Grandma’s KJV, or with no Bible at all, I hand them a
Value Thinline.  The page numbers line up
with the page numbers in my slightly nicer Crossway Thinline, so no matter how
well they know the Bible, I can still lead them to the truth.

Four or five months ago, I mentioned to one of the brethren
at Joliet that my stock of giveaways was getting low (a good problem to
have!).  He noticed that Value Thinlines
were on sale ($5 each!!!) at christianbook.com, so being a good dude, he bought
me 15 of them.

However, I didn’t have occasion to use the page-matching
trick with any of the new batch until this Monday, when I was studying with a
brand-spanking-new brother in Christ, whom I’d baptized the day before.  I wanted us to look at Galatians 2:11-14, so
I flipped to the passage in my Thinline and told him, “Look at p. 973.”

He paged through his Bible for a little bit and replied,
“Um. . . this Bible doesn’t even have a p. 973.”  I took it from him, looked at it, and saw
that he was correct.  The pages didn’t
match.

My world began to tilt crazily upon its axis.  This has worked for *years*!  What gives?

Further inspection revealed that none of my 13 remaining
Value Thinlines actually had the Crossway Thinline text block in them.  Instead, according to the copyright page,
they all used the setting for the rather different Crossway UltraThin.  I was bumfuzzled.  Was this some huge manufacturing mistake?

In my bewilderment, I e-mailed my contact at Crossway and
set out the situation.  Upholding
Crossway’s reputation for excellent customer service (I’ve dealt with those
people for years, and they’re good folks), she replied promptly, both
apologizing and explaining.

Apparently,
in a lineup overhaul some months back, Crossway decided that it needed to
replace the Thinline text block in the Value Thinline with the UltraThin text
block.  Despite the name, the Value
Thinline no longer has anything in common with all the other Crossway Thinlines
(Thinline and Heirloom Thinline). 
Instead, the budget version of the Crossway Thinline was now the
Crossway Premium Gift Bible.  The
Crossway rep offered to make things right by sending me 15 Premium Gift Bibles
as replacements, which I gratefully accepted. 
Like I said, wonderful customer service!

Anyway, as of a few months back, here are the multiply-used
Crossway text blocks and the Bibles that share them:

**Crossway Thinline text block:** Crossway Heirloom Thinline, Crossway
Thinline, Crossway Premium Gift Bible, Crossway Pew Bible.

**Crossway UltraThin
text block:** Crossway UltraThin,
Crossway Value Thinline

If you want to employ the page-matching trick, be sure to
use Bibles from the same family.

It’s worth noting, though, that in 2017, Crossway has big
plans for the setting it first used in the Crossway Large-Print Value
Thinline.  The Large-Print Value Thinline
may be an even better giveaway Bible than any of the others.  It has 10-point font, as opposed to the 8- or
7.5-point font in the Thinline and UltraThin settings, respectively.  As a result, students whose close-in vision
isn’t so good can read the Large-Print Value Thinline more easily.

According to the production schedule on crossway.org,
Crossway is going to start printing a Large-Print Thinline (to make things more
confusing, this will not be the same Bible as the Large-Print Thinline
Reference in all of its variations, either) in a non-value trim, so that the
teacher can have his genuine-leather Large-Print Thinline with pages that match
those in the Large-Print Value Thinline. 
I wouldn’t be surprised to see Crossway start printing a Large-Print Pew
Bible in the next few years, either.

As a result, the Large-Print Value Thinline may soon become
the giveaway Bible of choice.  Until
then, though, the best candidates for page-matching (in my opinion) all use the
Crossway Thinline text block.  Just
remember that it’s the Premium Gift Bible, not the Value Thinline, that lines
up.
