In recent days, I’ve seen few Christians claiming that
Donald Trump is the candidate that they want to vote for.  I’ve seen quite a few more claiming that he
is the candidate they must vote for. 
They argue that voting for him is the last opportunity we will have to
rescue our country from moral collapse and consequent disaster.

I can see it.  If the
Republican candidate were anyone but Trump, Hillary Clinton would be enough to
drive me to the polls.  Obviously, no one
knows what she will attempt while in office or whether she will succeed.  However, I cannot endorse either her
character or her politics.  I think she
is personally corrupt, and she is certainly no friend to Bible-believing
Christians.  If elected, she will appoint
Supreme-Court justices who will defend abortion and expand the jurisprudence of
“tolerance” that comes at the expense of the religiously conservative.  She would like to turn faith into a dirty
secret held only in private.



However, she isn’t running against Generic Republican.  She’s running against Donald Trump, certainly
the most bizarre Republican candidate for president in my lifetime.  I don’t particularly feel like digging
through his dirty laundry again, so suffice it to say that I believe he is an
evil man, just as she is an evil woman. 
I am not at all convinced that if elected, he will advance a
faith-friendly political agenda.  I’ve
seen nothing in his character that indicates that he can be relied on to
appoint conservative Supreme-Court justices, and as I’ve written [here](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2016/07/abortion-and-politics.html),
there’s no reason to believe he will be successful even if he makes the
attempt.

Nonetheless, I acknowledge that it is possible that Trump is
the answer.  It could be that like
Jeroboam II in 2 Kings 14, he is an evil man whom God will use to temporarily
rescue an evil nation.  However, if a
Jeroboam II is the best God can come up with for the U.S., we should take
notice from what happened to Israel after his death.  Within 30 years, the Assyrians had taken the
last Israelite king, destroyed Samaria, and carried the Israelite nation off
into captivity.  If Trump is a Jeroboam
II (and I don’t think anyone can say anything better about him than that), he
offers respite, not hope.

However, even if the election will not prove to be
spiritually pivotal at a national level, it may well prove to be so at an
individual level.  As with any other
action in life, we must be guided by the Scriptures and our conscience.  Whether we vote Clinton, Trump, Johnson,
McMullin, or abstain altogether, the day will come when we will have to give an
account for our choice, and the standard of judgment will be the word, not some
political platform.

We must make sure, then, that we are not becoming
participants in the unfruitful works of darkness, that we have not been
unequally yoked with unbelievers, that we do not call good evil and evil good,
and above all, that we are moved by love, not by fear.  Fear is an alluring motivation these
days.  I myself cannot help but be afraid
at the direction I see our country taking.

However, if we act from fear of anyone but God, the results
cannot be good.  In Revelation 2:10,
Jesus says “Do not fear” to the embattled church in Smyrna, even though some of
its members are about to be taken, imprisoned, and tortured to death.  Would He say less to us?

If we are guided by our fears, it is often the case that our
decisions will cause us to be overtaken by the very thing we fear.  The leaders of the antebellum American South
led their states into secession because they feared that Abraham Lincoln would
free their slaves.  Four years and half a
million lives later. . .  Of course, as
liars, alcoholics, and adulterers know, the same principle functions on an
individual level too.

Vote your conscience, but don’t vote for anybody because you
are afraid.  Especially, don’t vote Trump
because you are afraid.  I think doing so
will create more problems for American Christians quicker than anything else.

Our political discourse today thrives on attributing the
attributes of extremists in a group to the entire group.  Some terrorists are Muslim, so we ought to
treat all Muslims like terrorists.  It is
undeniable that some Trump supporters are, in Clinton’s infamous phrase,
deplorable people.  They’re racist,
nativist, and a host of other things that are inimical to the spirit of Christ
(of course, plenty of Clinton supporters are deplorable people too, but that’s
not going to matter).

If we align ourselves with such people because we’re afraid
we’re about to be persecuted, persecution is exactly what we will get.  If evangelicals go Trump en masse, they will
be tarred with the same brush as the racists and nativists.  Their alignment with the wicked will allow the
Democratic ascendancy when Clinton wins (which she will, barring some
cataclysm) to argue that there never has been any principle behind conservative
Christianity, that it has always been nothing more than a mask for bigotry and
meanness.  Once Christians have been
painted as Bad People, the government will begin treating them as Bad
People.  Repression will breed extremism
which will breed more repression which will breed a future we don’t want to
contemplate.

I am uncertain whether at this point such a future is even
avoidable.  I do know, though, that the
very worst thing we can do is to abandon our convictions because of our fears
in an effort to avoid it.
