Yesterday morning when I checked MSN News, I was greeted by
a revealing duo of articles.  The first
was entitled ["7 of Hillary Clinton's Biggest Flip-Flops"](http://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/7-of-hillary-clintons-biggest-flip-flops/ar-AAiq6u6),   and the second, ["Here Are All of Donald Trump's Flip-Flops on Big Issues"](http://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/here-are-all-of-donald-trumps-flip-flops-on-big-issues/ar-BBuVbFh). When combined with the obfuscations, broken
promises, and outright lies that both candidates have left in their wake, these
articles make for a damning character portrait in each case.  Neither leader will hesitate to utter an
untruth or break a commitment if they can gain an advantage, however fleeting,
by doing so.  Even in an era when “lying
politician” is practically redundant, both Trump and Clinton have distinguished
themselves by their mendacity.

However, I fear that their presence in the limelight reveals
a problem not with them, but with the whole nation.  We are selecting leaders who reflect our own
values.  These are the people that
millions of people selected as the representatives of two major parties, all
the while knowing that neither will tell the truth when a lie will do.  Apparently, such voters don’t believe that
integrity is a particularly relevant trait in a national leader, from which I
can only conclude that they don’t place much weight on integrity as a personal
trait at all.

I can’t help but be reminded of Jeremiah 5:31 (NASB), which
laments,

The prophets prophesy falsely,

And the priests rule on their own authority;

And My people love it so!

But what will you do at the end of it?

I have no idea what the Lord has in store for the United
States, but I believe that sin carries its own inevitable consequences with
it.  I don’t want to open the
pro-/anti-vaxxer can of worms, but there’s a sense in which social integrity is
like herd immunity.  Basically, once a
high enough percentage of a particular population becomes immune to a disease,
the disease can no longer continue to exist in the population.  Its effects will only be localized, and it
will die out.  However, once the number
of immune people drops below a threshold (that varies from disease to disease),
the disease will persist because vulnerable people will continually come in
contact with it.

I think integrity is like that.  In a society where most people are basically
honest, their honesty will dissipate the bad effects of the few who
aren’t.  The social structure is strong
enough to withstand the rot of the wicked. 
However, as more people become dishonest and spread their dishonesty to
others, eventually a tipping point is reached where the entire society becomes
dishonest.

We see evidence of this already in a wide number of areas,
from [massive scandals in military procurement](http://www.defenseindustrydaily.com/fat-leonard-pleads-guilty-navy-awaits-more-charges-against-more-officers-029311/) to the contempt with which millions of ordinary people treat their marriage
vows.  My in-laws recently tried to buy a
house and had their down payment stolen by crooks who had hacked into their title company's e-mail account.  Sadly, many Christians are part of the
problem too.

All in all, it’s about a million miles away from the Psalm
15 portrait of the righteous man who swears to his hurt and does not
change.  Why hurt, our culture asks.  Why not change?  It’s only words, after all!

The costs of this behavior are not obvious, but they are
immense.  Rather than simply going about
our business trusting others, we must instead spend resources guarding against
the likelihood that others are untrustworthy. 
I’d like to buy a car on Craigslist (it’s cheaper, for one thing), but
I’ve heard such bad stories about private sellers that I’m afraid to.  There are private sellers out there who would
love to sell me a car (you get more money, for one thing), but they can’t
because I can’t distinguish the honest from the crooked.  Instead, the car dealer ends up profiting from
both of us, and so does the state government, which now gets to collect sales
taxes on the transaction.

There are a million other ways in which the citizens of our
nation pay the price for dishonesty, and I’m sure the total cost is in the
billions, if not the tens or hundreds of billions.  Our budget deficit may pale in comparison to
our moral deficit, and that’s not even beginning to count the vast and
increasing non-monetary costs.

Morals make a difference to a nation, and those who have
been so busily employed in tearing down the old system of Judeo-Christian
ethics will eventually discover to their dismay that they have torn down
everything else in the process.  Sin is a
reproach to any people, but it’s also a terrible idea, even from the viewpoint
of sheer pragmatism.

When the foundations are destroyed, what will the righteous
do?  Micah has a few ideas (though it’s
worth noting what eventually happened to the nation in which Micah lived):

The godly person has perished from the land,

And there is no upright person among men.

All of them lie in wait for bloodshed;

Each of them hunts the other with a net.

Concerning evil, both hands do it well.

The prince asks, also the judge, for a bribe,

And a great man speaks the desire of his soul;

So they weave it together.

The best of them is like a briar,

The most upright like a thorn hedge.

The day when you post your watchmen,

Your punishment will come.

Then their confusion will occur.

Do not trust in a neighbor;

Do not have confidence in a friend.

From her who lies in your bosom

Guard your lips.

For son treats father contemptuously,

Daughter rises up against her mother,

Daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law;

A man's enemies are the men of his own household.

But as for me, I will watch expectantly for the LORD;

I will wait for the God of my salvation.

My God will hear me.

- Micah 7:2-7, NASB

Maybe nobody else is trustworthy, but God is, and we have it
on good authority that the one who trusts in Him will not be disappointed.  Sure, the wicked can wreck things up pretty
good for themselves, but in the end, they can’t touch us.
