Yesterday, I wrote about the difference between a song
leader and a worship leader, and I pointed to three practices outside the
assembly that distinguish the worship leader. 
Of course, a would-be worship leader who does everything right outside
of the assembly but can’t make a hymn work when he’s actually leading it has
missed the mark.  Here are three more
areas for the leader to focus on during worship:

**Unlock your
heart.** There are few things more
difficult to define than the difference between an expression that is heartfelt
and one that isn’t.  A preacher who is
passionate about his sermon, even if he never raises his voice, simply doesn’t
sound the same as one who is going through the motions.  A hymn written from the heights of joy or the
depths of despair sounds nothing like a hymn written as an intellectual
exercise.  Similarly, a song leader who
is emotionally involved in what he is leading will convey a completely
different impression than one who is not. 
In none of these cases is there an obvious tell, but somehow, everybody
just knows.

The worship leader, then, must lead in a way that is
emotionally expressive and genuine.  For
many men, particularly those from stiff-upper-lip families, this can be a
challenge.  However, such men have a
powerful ally in the music.  A good hymn
tune will appeal to you to open your heart up. 
Listen to it.  Let it.

This is important because a great hymn can only be understood
with the emotions as well as the intellect. 
When I described earlier how I would lead “Sun of My Soul”, that might
have conveyed the impression that I lead like a computer, coldly calculating
how each phrase should sound.  Nothing
could be further from the truth!  I’m not
aware of thinking these things while I’m leading.

Instead, this is what I feel.  It is how the hymn makes me want to sing and
help everybody else sing.  Somehow, via
any number of subliminal cues, I communicate that to the congregation.  When I’m emotionally invested, I do a far
better job of presenting the meaning of the hymn than when I am not.

**Lead like a
servant.** A worship leader is not a
soloist.  He is not up in front so
everybody can admire his wonderful voice (assuming he has a wonderful voice to
admire, which I don’t).  Instead, his
role is to help everybody else worship. 
When the worship leader is most effective, the congregation is following
him, but they’re not really aware of doing so because their minds are filled
with thoughts of God.  A worship leader
focuses attention not on himself, but on the Lord.

The leader is best able to do this when he connects
emotionally with the members of the congregation.  Eye contact is extremely valuable here.  Because the worship leader has perfected his
technique and his understanding of the hymns he is leading before he ever blows
pitch, he doesn’t have to pay attention to those things.  Leading worship is easiest when you’re
leading from memory.  Then, you can sing
to your brothers and sisters rather than singing to the hymnal.

The sensation of leading a church in worship together is
both unmistakable and indescribable.  You
are a magnifying glass focusing the light of the sun.  Everyone is in sync on a level that no other activity
can approach.  There might be a hundred
mouths singing, but there is only one heart. 
It’s not easy to reach that state, and it’s even harder to stay in it,
but that is when the power of congregational singing is truly unlocked.

**Offer a Spiritual
Sacrifice.** Worship, after all, is
not about establishing and maintaining an emotional connection with the
congregation.  It is about glorifying
God.  There have been times when I’ve
gotten so caught up in leading that I forgot why I was singing what I was
singing.

Even though you can make eye contact with Brother So-and-So
and God is invisible, He is still the point of the whole exercise.  The worship leader has to spare part of his
attention for further thought on what he is singing and endorse the point of
the hymn, both intellectually and emotionally. 
If the leader is focused on God, that too will communicate itself
somehow and help the Christians in the pews keep themselves focused too.

**Conclusion.** As all of this should make clear, the
worship leader has a lot of balls to juggle (along with maybe a knife or two,
depending on the congregation)!  Leading
worship, as opposed to leading singing, is a demanding activity that requires a
lot of preparation beforehand and a lot of effort in the moment.  It should be exhausting, just as preaching a
sermon should be exhausting.  If it
isn’t, you aren’t investing enough in it.

Worship leading isn’t for everybody.  However, the men who are willing and able to
shoulder this load both offer a blessing to their brethren and bring glory to
God.  Congregations need good corporate
worship.  It is the spiritual mortar that
holds a church together.  Without it,
both members and the whole are vulnerable to all the attacks of Satan.  With it, they can stand strong together in
the strength of God.
