*So when the woman saw
that the tree was good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree
desirable to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate. . .*

-- Genesis 3:6a

This passage, of course, records the first sin ever
committed in human history.  To the
modern reader, though, the amazing thing about Eve’s transgression is not how
foreign her behavior it is, but how familiar.



None of us have ever been commanded not to eat of the tree of
knowledge of good and evil, so the devil isn’t much for the fruit-merchant
business these days.  However, he
continues to appeal to us in just the same way as he appealed to Eve, through
what John describes in 1 John 2:16
as, “the lust of the flesh and the lust of the eyes and the pride of
life.”  Just as Eve was enticed by those
three things to sample the forbidden fruit, we today are enticed by them to
disobey the law of God.

However, in presenting the fruit to Eve in such an alluring
way, Satan was hardly making an honest offer. 
His sales pitch was founded on deception.  It was probably true that the fruit was
yummy-tasting and yummy-looking.  It was even
true—sort of—that the fruit gave Eve the same perception of good and evil that
God had.  What Satan neglected to
mention, though, was that the ability to discern good from evil had
consequences.  Once Eve learned what the
right thing to do was, she became responsible for doing it.  When she failed to do it, God counted her
failure as sin.  This sin of Eve, and the
sin into which she enticed her husband, Adam, created the world of sin and
death that gives us so much grief today.

When Satan waves his temptations around in front of us
today, we need to see them for the bait they are.  The devil doesn’t have our best interests at
heart any more than he had Eve’s best interests at heart.  Every temptation contains the same core
deception.  Every time, Satan is
attempting to fool us into believing that some harmful thing is actually good
for us instead.

What this tells us, then, is that whenever the devil
presents us with a worm, we should look for the hook.  Every time, no matter how cunningly
concealed, it will be there, and if we give in and sample the lure, Satan will
use our foolishness to jerk us around wherever he wants to go, which is certain
to mean disaster for us.
