Back when Bill Nye first came to my attention, about 15
years ago now, pretty much everything I heard about him was positive.  I had several homeschooled friends who used
his “Bill Nye the Science Guy” videos as part of their science curriculum.



In the years since, however, Nye has abandoned his test
tubes and beakers for the more contentious arena of political and ethical
debate.  I suspect that he has concluded
that his large following among homeschoolers and other conservatives has given
him the opportunity to wean them from what he considers to be their misguided
beliefs.  Early last year, for instance,
he had a well-publicized debate with young-earth creationist Ken Ham.

Last week, he decided that he needed to join the
long-running controversy about abortion as well.  Once again, his targets appear to be
conservatives.  His aim is to use Science
to convince those poor deluded pro-lifers of the error of their ways.  You can find his video and a print summary of
the arguments in it here: http://www.cnet.com/news/bill-nye-brings-science-to-bear-on-the-abortion-debate/

The first argument that he makes is that the pro-life
position is based on ignorance.  He
asserts that most people who oppose abortion are unaware that not every
fertilized egg results in a live birth. 
He points out, correctly, that fertilized eggs often fail to implant in
the uterine lining, and that pregnancies often go awry in a number of other
different ways, such as miscarriage and stillbirth.  He concludes that in order to be consistent
with a pro-life position, we would have to jail every man who fertilized an egg
that didn’t result in a live birth, along with every woman who produced such an
egg.

Where to begin?

First of all, though I am passionately pro-life, I actually
did know that not every fertilized egg produces a live birth.  Like most people with pro-life convictions, I
both took and passed high-school biology. 
Honors biology, even.  I got a B+
on the year, as I recall.

More than that, I have, shall we say, a certain amount of
personal experience with that particular fact. 
On September 19th, 2008, my daughter Macy was stillborn,
unexpectedly, at full term.  I know that
not every pregnancy produces a live baby. 
I know it in my bones, to the very depths of my soul.  I know it in a way that only lying crumpled
on a hospital floor, sobbing in agony, can teach.

For the rest of my life, every time somebody I love or even
somebody I know gets pregnant, I will fear for her, and worry about her, and
pray diligently for the health of the expectant mother and baby both, and I
will never, ever breathe a word about any of it to any of the women involved,
because she doesn’t need my dread in her head.

And Bill Nye wants to inform me that not every fertilized
egg results in a live birth; indeed, most do not.  All right. 
Got it.

Strangely enough, having gotten it, I find that I have
become not pro-choice, but more strongly pro-life.  Like any reminder of mortality, it teaches me
that human existence is precarious and that life is fragile and precious.  Just think of all the things that have to go
right for a single sperm cell and a single egg to result in a perfectly healthy
baby!  Through many dangers, toils, and
snares, we have *all* already
come.

Life is a miracle. 
Life is a gift.  We can neither
create nor sustain it on our own apart from the blessing of God.  I don’t know why some live and some die, but
that’s His business, not mine.  When it’s
beyond us to say, “This one will live,” we must not take it upon ourselves to
say, “This one will die.”  None of us can
fathom the mysteries of such a choice.

A little later on in the video, Nye attributes anti-abortion
legislation to “men of European descent”. 
I’ll have more to say on that later, but first I must observe that in my
experience, the most devoutly pro-life people I know are women.  They’re women who have felt the first
flutters and kicks of new life within themselves, who have endured the despair
of stillbirth, the silent tragedy of miscarriage, whose aching wombs have not
known and may never know the presence of somebody else.  Regardless of who runs for office, those are
your pro-life zealots.

I haven’t taken a poll, I admit, but I suspect that they too
have a pretty good idea that not every fertilized egg results in a live
birth.  In addition, they are aware that
people die outside of the womb.  None of
this strikes them as a reason to be indifferent to the extinction of a life *in utero.*

Instead, it strikes them as a very good reason to defend
what life they can.
