*Abram dwelt in the
land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent
even as far as Sodom.*

-- Genesis 13:12

Lot is not one of the bad guys of the Bible.  In fact, he is described as “righteous Lot”
by Peter in 2 Peter 2:7.  However, he
does not appear in the ranks of the heroes of Hebrews 11, we don’t preach
sermons commending him, and we don’t name our children after him.  All of this disapproval is the result of the
mistake of Genesis 13:12.  Lot pitched
his tent toward Sodom, and it wrecked his life.



Lot should have known better.  As the very next verse makes clear, the
wickedness of the Sodomites was hardly a subtle, secret thing.  Nonetheless, Lot chose to put himself in the
midst of such evil people because he thought the grazing lands for his flocks
was better.  It may be that he weighed
the material advantages and the spiritual disadvantages and concluded that the
material was weightier; it may be that he just didn’t think about it at all.

Regardless, the allure of Sodom proved greater and more
dangerous than Lot could have imagined. 
Soon, he shifted from pitching his tent near Sodom to living in a house
in the midst of the city.  His daughters
married men of Sodom, and when the city was destroyed, it nearly destroyed Lot
as well.  The tumult cost him the lives
of his sons-in-law and wife, and the souls of his daughters.  According to the text, even Lot himself would
have lingered in the city if the angels had not forced him to depart.

We recognize how foolish and disastrous Lot’s decision was,
yet too often we fail to recognize when we make the same foolish choice.  We live in a world that is in many ways every
bit as wicked as Sodom.  Nonetheless,
many Christians choose to pitch their tents toward the world.  They adopt the speech of the world, the
entertainment preferences of the world, the fashions of the world, and the
priorities of the world.  Then, they are
shocked and saddened when they lose their spouses and their children to the
world just like Lot did.  Often, they
lose their own souls as well.

We must be wiser than Lot was if we do not wish to share his
fate.  As we navigate our way through the
perils of life, we must always remember that Satan’s snares are going to be
every bit as alluring to us as the valley of Sodom was to Lot.  The devil will entice us with immoral TV
shows that we want to watch, immodest clothing that we want to wear, ungodly
friends we want to associate with, and many other wicked things besides.  However, all of those things are not gifts
but traps.  They are designed not to
please us but to ruin us.  Avoiding Lot’s
fate requires willpower and determination, but that inner strength is necessary
if we want to make it to heaven.
