*Then Jacob awoke from
his sleep and said, "Surely the LORD is in this place, and I did not know
it."*

--Genesis 28:16

One of the great joys that we have as Christians is the
beauty of the written word of God.  The
Bible is not written in high-flown, flowery language.  However, the words of the Scripture are so
elegantly chosen, and its focus on the truths in a story so precise, that it
attains a poetry beyond the reach of any human pen.



We see this inspired eloquence on full display in the story
of Jacob’s ladder.  At this point, Jacob
has defrauded Esau twice, and he’s on the run lest his vengeful, warlike
brother come after him and kill him.  His
flight to Mesopotamia has taken him to the place that would be called Bethel in
years to come, which is now nothing more than a patch of rocky, barren
ground.  Jacob has so little with him
that he takes one of the rocks on the ground, puts it under his head for a
pillow, and dreams.

In his dream, he is granted a vision of angels ascending and
descending on a ladder that stretches up to heaven, and God Himself appears at
the ladder’s top.  He affirms that the
promises He made to Abraham apply to Jacob too, and, most of all, that He will
not leave Jacob until He has done for him all that He promised.  Jacob awakens from his dream with a whole new
attitude.  He realizes that despite his
fugitive status, despite having nothing more than the clothes on his back,
despite having to use a rock for a pillow, God was with him the whole
time.  He just didn’t know it.

In all of Scripture, there may be no more beautiful and
perfect metaphor for God’s dealings with His people than that one.  We may never have to abandon everything we
own and flee for our lives, but all of us go through hard times.  Through no fault of our own, that we can see,
at least, we are beset by suffering.  We
hurt, and we don’t understand why we have to hurt, and we start feeling sorry
for ourselves, and maybe, just maybe, we start wondering where God is in all of
this.  How come He isn’t granting our
prayers for X, Y, and Z, when our evil neighbor across the street doesn’t even
mention God except as a cuss word, and yet he’s doing just fine?

In times like those, we may never understand why, at least
in this life.  But we can always
understand who.  For reasons of His own,
God allows us to suffer, but that suffering is never without purpose, and it is
never without love.  Even in our most
miserable moments, God is where He has always been, right with us, and just as
was true with Jacob, He will not leave us until He has done what He has
promised.  That’s what we need to
get.  Once we do, even from the bottom of
the well-shaft of despair, we can say with Jacob, “Surely the Lord is in this
place, and I did not know it.”
