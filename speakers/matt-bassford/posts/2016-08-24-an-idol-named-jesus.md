It’s simple to make an idol and name it Jesus.  You agglomerate a bunch of your own beliefs,
opinions, and views, slap Jesus’ name on it like a bumper sticker, and there
you are!  Now you can say and do whatever
you want while still claiming to follow the Lord.

Logically speaking, this doesn’t accomplish a whole
lot.  I could name my dog Jesus too, but
that doesn’t make Jesus a dog.  Nor does
idol-naming win any particular favor from Him. 
In fact, unless you repent, you’re headed for Matthew 7:21-23.

However, on the plus (?) side, it does offer you the
opportunity to lead the people astray. 
If you’re trying to teach a bunch of Scripturally ignorant folks, who
don’t know the difference between the actual Jesus and the idol you’ve created,
you can use the idol to get them to buy into what you’re teaching, particularly
because the idol probably bears some resemblance to what they already want to
believe.  “Wow!  I never knew Jesus agreed with me on that!”  To the false teacher, an idol named Jesus is
a valuable companion.



So it is with Bromleigh McCleneghan.  I’ve already written about her
convictions [here](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2016/07/bromleigh-mccleneghan-and-what-bible.html),
but she keeps showing up, loudly proclaiming egregious falsehoods.  Recently, she’s taken [her views](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/acts-of-faith/wp/2016/08/22/sex-and-the-single-christian-why-celibacy-isnt-the-only-option/) on “Christian”
sexuality to the opinion page of *The
Washington Post*.

Not surprisingly, the nature of her idol is quite
clear.  Her highest value is “sex that
[is] mutually pleasurable and affirming” and “respect[s] the autonomy and
sacred worth of ourselves and our partners”--sexual autonomy *über alles*, in other words.  This is straight out of the creed book of
secular liberalism, and she applies it in predictable fashion.  “Straight, gay, bi, trans, intersex: we are
beloved,” she says, “and do God and ourselves a disservice if we are conformed.”

I agree emphatically with the first part (God loves
everybody!), but the second part is utterly inconsistent with the
Scripture-wide call for radical, transformative change.  We are not supposed to be conformed to this
world, but we are certainly supposed to be conformed to the image of His Son.

This is where the idol named Jesus comes in.  Being conformed to the image of the Son is
easy if you have constructed that image yourself in the likeness of your own
desires.  Indeed, the McCleneghan version
of Jesus is not merely frustrated (which the Biblical Jesus often was) but
sexually frustrated.  She writes, “[His
celibacy] might have been heard to bear sometimes.  We get a sense of his frustration, resignation
and loneliness on occasion (‘remove this cup;’ ‘the son of man has nowhere to
lay his head’).”

To be frank, this is somewhere between comical and
blasphemous.  Apparently, in the garden
of Gethsemane, when the sweat of Jesus became like drops of blood falling upon
the ground, He wasn’t reflecting on the agonies of His imminent
crucifixion.  He was saying, “Man; I
really wish I’d asked Mary Magdalene out!” 
Projection, much?

McCleneghan acknowledges that Jesus was “likely celibate”,
yet she somehow argues that His celibacy calls His disciples to *not* be celibate, at least if they don’t
want to.  Her logic seems to be that
Jesus in His celibacy was different, so everybody who follows a different norm
than conventional married sexuality is actually following Jesus.  The result is a thousand species of
self-defined “chastity”, which may well involve having sex with whomever or
whatever, so long as the all-important norm of sexual autonomy is respected.

Yes; Jesus was different, though I get the impression that
celibacy among first-century Jews was not a particularly counter-cultural
choice (John the Baptist, Paul, and the Essenes were all celibate).  However, it simply does not follow that to be
different is to be like Jesus.

A few months ago, my family and I visited the National
Museum of Art and saw some awe-inspiringly good sculptures by Degas.  Degas’ abilities as a sculptor are definitely
different than mine.  My four-year-old
son’s abilities as a sculptor are also different from mine.  Should we conclude, therefore, that Marky is
the next Degas?

To say the least, it is important to consider the direction and
not merely the existence of difference. 
Jesus calls us to a different understanding of sex, yes, but that
understanding has nothing to do with the sexual alphabet soup of 21st-century
America (are there really 33 different genders now?).  Instead, in Matthew 19:3-4, He says, “Have
you not read that He who created them from the beginning made them male and
female, and said ‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and
be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh’?”

There are a number of significant lessons here.  First, there are not 33 different
genders.  There are two.  It is not we who decide which gender we
are.  It is God.

Second, the creation of male and female teaches us
everything we need to know about sexual expression.  Sex is not about the search for pleasure with
any number of partners, the scratching of a fleshly itch.  Instead, it is the sacred joining of husband
and wife into one flesh, for life.  Any
sex that is not between husband and wife defies the will of God.

Indeed, this is true not only of sexual expression, but of
sexual desire as well.  Jesus says in
Matthew 5:27-28, “You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall not commit
adultery,’ but I say to you that everyone who looks on a woman with lust for
her has already committed adultery with her in his heart.”

This is what being different like Jesus actually looks like.  Our society these days is quite accepting of
fornication, adultery, homosexual activity, gender modification, and all the
rest.  When we accept those things too,
we are only being conformed to the world. 
However, when Jesus says to hundreds of millions of Americans, “Knock it
off with the porn already!”, that is truly a call to a transformed way of life.

Of course, most Americans have never heard of Matthew
5:27-28.  They’d much rather worship the
idol that McCleneghan and others like her have fashioned.  If they can soothe their consciences by
believing that somehow, this is following Jesus, so much the better.
