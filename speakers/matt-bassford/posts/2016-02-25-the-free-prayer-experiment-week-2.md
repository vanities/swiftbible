Week Two results:  one
conversation with Starbucks management, five minutes in store

Having learned my lesson from last week’s awkwardness, this
week, I bought my coffee and asked to speak with the manager before I put my
sign up.  That proved to be prudent.  Once again, the manager was very polite and
gracious.  However, she informed me that
according to her higher-ups, my sign was solicitation and in violation of
Starbucks’ anti-solicitation policies.  I
was welcome; my sign was not.



I still find this rather mystifying.  There is no anti-solicitation policy posted
on the Starbucks premises or on the Starbucks website (at least, not one that I
have been able to find).

Even if there is such
a policy someplace, I’m not at all convinced that what I am doing is
solicitation.  The dictionary definitions
for “soliciting” are either too broad (“to entreat or petition”, which would
make asking your friend to lend you some money impermissible on Starbucks
premises) or too narrow (“to solicit orders or trade, as for a business”).  I am not in the business of praying for
people.  Prayer is not a trade.  I just wanted to be nice, is all!

Sadly, I think the whole incident illustrates the sham
nature of modern American “diversity”. 
All sorts of beliefs and convictions are applauded, so long as they
don’t offend secular liberals.  When I
condemn what the Bible condemns, that makes me a bigot.  However, when I offer no-strings prayer to
people who are hurting, and somebody complains about me because they don’t like
Christianity, that’s my fault too. 
They’re allowed to exclude; I’m not, because their biases are culturally
acceptable.

I am both surprised and disappointed that things turned out
this way.  The manager did provide me
with a phone number for her superior, and I’ll be giving her a call, probably
sometime next week.  In the meantime,
I’ll be praying for all involved, especially the two women who complained.  Matthew 5:44 would have me do no less.
