After a delightful Friday night filled with multiply
vomiting children, I went downstairs to work on my Bible-class lesson for
Sunday, using, of course, my one-Bible-one-year Pitt Minion.  As I was flipping from Old to New Testaments,
I came upon a bone-chilling sight.  The
top corner of Page 575-6, containing Isaiah 31:6-9 and 32:8-13, had been torn
off.

I knew at once where to begin my investigations—with my  three-year-old son Marky, who is rascality
incarnate.  After dozens of denials
(which were undermined by his helpful and oh-so-superior sister), he finally
acknowledged the truth.  While “reading”
the Bible at the church building after Bible class Wednesday night, he had torn
the page.  I raced off to the church building
to see if I could locate the missing scrap, but to no avail.



I checked with Marky, got a better fix on where he had done
the tearing, went back to look again—and met the sister who cleans the building
on her way out.  Unasked, she went home
and sorted piece by piece through the trash she had collected, searching for my
missing scrap.  This provided me with a
breathtakingly beautiful example of John 13:12-15 in action, but not with my
page corner.  It was irretrievably gone.

As problems go, this isn’t even a first-world problem.  It’s more like a .25-world problem.  It’s a sign that everything in
my life is going so well (vomiting children notwithstanding) that I have the
emotional energy to spare in getting upset at a torn Bible page.

And yet, get upset I did. 
I spent the morning appalled.  I
considered swapping out my official 2015 Bible for a different one three days
into the New Year.  I refrained only
because the words of my “One Bible, One Year” post allowed me to switch Bibles
only if the original was lost or destroyed. 
A single torn page barely constitutes damage, let alone loss or
destruction.

The location is also significant here.  Though I believe in the importance of the
whole counsel of God, it’s not like I just lost Acts 2 out of my Bible.  I’ve never once quoted from anywhere in
Isaiah 31-32 in 10 years’ worth of sermons, and the odds of me doing so in 2015
were slim to none.  I know this, but I
still find myself reluctant to use the Bible I’ve committed to using.

I fear that this reveals quite a bit, both about me and
about my inability to settle on a Bible. 
Throughout 2014, insignificant defects, or things that weren’t even
defects, were enough to spur me to move on. 
That Bible crackled when I opened it. 
I banged up the gilding in that Bible when I was breaking it in.  I didn’t like the color combination in that
Bible (that I picked out myself).  That
Bible had thumb-indexing.  And so
on.  All of those Bibles were perfectly
usable, but it wasn’t enough for me to have a Bible that was usable.  I had to have one that was perfect.

Funny, isn’t it, how our perfectionism often focuses on
everything in our lives except ourselves? 
In addition to a perfect Bible, I also want a perfectly maintained
house, perfect health, a perfect family, and a perfect church.  Everybody else had better get cracking and
rise to the level of perfection with which I deserve to be surrounded!

Strangely enough, my attitude toward the imperfections of my
own character is much more accepting.  My
flaws are pretty much somebody else’s fault anyway, amirite?  If only Those Other People were better, it
would be easy for me to be better too. 
So it is that I easily find myself denying grace to others and denying
my own need for it myself.

Perhaps the moral of the story is that it is better for me
to use a Bible missing Isaiah 32:8-13 than one that contains it.  Perhaps I need to be reminded that if my life
were a Bible, it would be missing considerably more than a quarter-page from
one of the major prophets!  Whole books
might be absent.  Of course, it has ever
been thus with the people of God.  His
power is perfected not in strength, but in weakness, and if He can use me to
preach the gospel, I can probably use a Bible with a torn page.

Besides, sometimes a small annoyance conceals a very great
good.  I know why Marky had my
Bible.  He’s seen me reading it, and he
wants to be like Daddy.  The Lord knows
that I have my imperfections as a father too, but if I’m getting across to him
that the Bible is important and he ought to love it. . .  Well, that’s worth a few torn pages.
