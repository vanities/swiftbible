*“I am not worthy of
the least of all the deeds of steadfast love and all the faithfulness You have
shown to Your servant, for with only my staff I crossed this Jordan, and now I
have become two camps.”*

--Genesis 32:10



Sometimes, we like to take an occasional poke at the
character of Jacob.  Yes, he had his
whiny moments.  Yes, he was about as
crooked as a heap of rattlesnakes.  When
we do that, though, we must give equal time to his virtues.  Despite his flaws, there were moments when
Jacob got it amazingly, perfectly right.

We see one of these moments in Genesis 32:10.  Jacob has left the country of his menacing
uncle Laban, but now he is returning to the country of his even more menacing
brother Esau.  When last we saw Esau, he
was plotting Jacob’s death, and quite understandably so, because Jacob had just
finished cheating Esau out of his father’s blessing.  Now, Jacob’s messengers to Esau have reported
that Esau is headed his way with 400 men. 
Jacob naturally assumes that his brother is not about to let bygones be
bygones, so he seeks God’s help in prayer.

This, of course, is quite common.  Even the most ungodly men will pray when
their lives are in danger.  However, in
this prayer, Jacob does something that is quite uncommon.  He begins by referring to all of the things
that God has done for him, and humbly expresses the thought that he is unworthy
of even the least of God’s blessings.

Too often, this humility is something that is absent from
our prayer lives and from our lives generally. 
We live in a rich nation, and all of us have been blessed beyond measure
by God.  However, in many cases, this
great blessing has done nothing more than create in us a sense of
entitlement.  When good things happen to
us, we accept them as nothing more than our due.  However, when we encounter trial or hardship,
we blame God for it.  We cry out to Him
demanding to know why He has been so cruel when we are so deserving of only
good.

In reality, we are not as deserving as we think we are.  Even the best of Christians, men and women
who have spent decades dedicated to God’s service, have led lives that are far
from the perfection that God expects. 
The very last thing that any one of us wants is what we are entitled to
get, because all that we truly deserve is death.  Every breath that we take, every meal that we
eat, is a testimony not to our righteousness, but to His mercy.  We have those things not because we have
earned them, but because He loves us. 
Just like Jacob, we are unworthy of even the least of God’s
blessings.  When we enjoy His gifts, or
when we go to Him in prayer, let’s remember that, and give Him the attention
and the gratitude that He deserves.
