Let’s say that you are a Christian, have moved to a major
metropolitan area in the US, have visited the churches in the area, and are
deciding among these candidates.  Which
would you choose?



Congregation A has a sizable contingent of members who are
so legalistic that they all but deny the grace of Christ.  They have caused serious controversy in the
church.

Congregation B has some members who cling to a strict
understanding of God’s law that is explicitly, black-letter wrong.  Once again, this is causing trouble.

Congregation C is so worldly that some of its members don’t
hesitate to come to the assembly drunk.

Congregation D has been entirely led into apostasy by the
same false teaching from Congregation A.

Congregation E has several elders who are poised to become
false teachers and cause a split in the church.

Congregation F has two prominent members who can’t get along
to the point that they’ve been called down for it by name from the pulpit.

Congregation G contains a number of brethren who would
rather mooch support from the other members of the congregation than work.

Congregation H has Bible-study sessions that are marred by
obnoxious men who insist on “teaching from the pew” even though they know about
as much about the Bible as my three-year-old.

Congregation I has a preacher who is given to preaching that
is repeatedly and harshly negative.  When
asked about it, he replies that this kind of preaching is the only thing that
is keeping the bulk of the congregation from falling away.

That’s quite a list, isn’t it?  Names have been withheld to protect the
guilty, but I haven’t made up any of these churches nor any of their problems.  If you know me personally and think you
recognize a particular congregation, you probably do.  I’m sure that many readers have personally
experienced churches that are just as bad.

When we’re presented with problems like these, even if only
on a list like this, we recoil in horror. 
Being asked to actually become a part of a church filled with
unspiritual members like these? 
Ugh!  It’s like being led to the
Dumpster behind Golden Corral and being invited to sort through and eat what we
like best!

I don’t think any of
us can deny that churches in this country are rife with serious and even
disastrous spiritual problems.  We
consider what the Lord calls Christians to be, compare that to what Christians
actually are, and are horrified by the difference.  What’s happened to us?  Why aren’t we more like the Christians of the
first-century church?

Um.  About that.

Congregation A is the church in Jerusalem (Acts
15:1-7).  Congregation B is the church in
Rome (Romans 14:1-23).  Congregation C is
the church in Corinth (1 Corinthians 11:20-22). 
Congregation D is one of the churches of Galatia (Galatians 1:6-9).  Congregation E is the church in Ephesus (Acts
20:29-30).  Congregation F is the church
in Philippi (Philippians 4:2-3). 
Congregation G is the church in Thessalonica (2 Thessalonians 3:10-15).  Congregation H is the church in Ephesus,
again (1 Timothy 1:3-7).  Congregation I
is any of the various churches in Crete who had to deal with Titus (Titus
1:10-13).

We often talk about the “New Testament pattern”, what the
Scripture says about how churches should organize themselves, worship
communally, and so forth.  However, there
is another New Testament pattern—the pattern of churches with problems.  Every church addressed in any of the New
Testament epistles had *something* that
it was supposed to be working on.  The
Christians in those churches were imperfect, often spectacularly so.  They were frequently in danger of falling
away entirely.  It’s almost like they
were filled with penitent sinners who knew they had no hope outside of Jesus!

In short, churches in the first century looked an awful lot
like churches in the twenty-first century, and when we demand churches without
those problems, we are demanding something that rarely, if ever, has
existed.  This is not to say that church
problems don’t matter, merely that getting outraged about them is on par with
getting outraged about snow in Chicago in January.  It comes with the climate.

Besides, even if I did find a problem-free church, I’d wreck
it just as soon as I placed membership.
