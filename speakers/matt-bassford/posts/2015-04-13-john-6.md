To us, one of the strangest things about the ministry of Jesus is the way that the people He interacted with just didn’t get it.  The members of the Jewish elite who were His greatest enemies were certainly familiar with the Scriptures, but they never once realized that they fulfilled those passages in their arrest, trial, and murder of the Lord.  Similarly, Jesus warned the disciples plainly and repeatedly that He was going to be taken, crucified, and raised from the dead, but somehow, they never took the hint.

Of course, these problems didn’t only arise with respect to the death of Jesus, but with respect to the entirety of His teaching.  During Jesus’ encounter with the woman at the well, for instance, He’s telling her about salvation, but she spends most of the conversation mistakenly believing that He’s talking about water.  Just a couple of chapters later, the multitudes make the same mistake, of understanding a spiritual message in spiritual terms.  This morning, let’s see how this all plays out in John 6.



**Two Miracles**

Even though it ultimately doesn’t end up in a very happy place, the story of this chapter of John begins on a high note, with two miracles.  The first appears as the solution to the problem of A HUNGRY MULTITUDE.  John tells the tale in John 6:1-7.  One of the first things we notice from this story is that for Jesus, there was no such thing as getting away.  He and His disciples flee to the far side of the Sea of Galilee?  The multitudes follow.  Jesus and the disciples climb to the top of a mountain?  The multitudes follow.  It’s like Jesus is being chased by a zombie horde or something, and there is no way to get rid of them without leaving the lands of the Jews altogether.  This seems praiseworthy, but it actually isn’t.  As the rest of the chapter reveals, the multitudes don’t want truth.  They want physical goodies for themselves.

However, as determined as the crowds are, they aren’t very smart.  They have followed Jesus into the middle of a barren wasteland, there isn’t anywhere for them to get food, and, as we know from other accounts, it’s too late for them to go back where they came to get food.  Jesus knows what He’s going to have to do about this, but He tosses the ball back in the apostles’ court first.  The only one to come up with an answer, Philip, is thinking in terms of money, which they don’t have.  The problem appears insoluble.

This is important.  It shows that conditions have arisen that demand a spectacular miracle.  Neither the apostles nor the multitudes themselves have come up with a way for the latter to eat.  Also, Jesus didn’t stage-manage these circumstances into existence.  He didn’t invite the multitude to follow Him out into the middle of nowhere; they just came.  It’s not like the Lord would have had opportunity to stash hundreds of loaves of bread in the rocks.  If these people are going to be fed, it’s not going to be by natural means.

Of course, all of this is only setting up THE FEEDING OF THE FIVE THOUSAND.  This is one of the most famous miracles of Jesus’ ministry, and we see John’s description of it in John 6:8-14.  Apparently, of all the multitude, there is only one boy who has food with him:  five loaves and two fish.  I suspect his mom packed him a lunch or something.  However, there are still thousands of people on hand; in comparison to the need, one boy’s meal is a drop in the bucket.  Jesus, however, is able with His divine power to make the drop expand to fill the bucket.  Five thousand men eat all they want, along with an unknown number of women and children, and there are twelve baskets of leftovers to feed the poor.

Once again, friends, we are confronted with that hallmark of the ministry of Jesus:  the public, incontestable, spectacular miracle.  There is simply no way that this can be fake.  Simple logistics would have defeated Jesus’ efforts to forge a miracle here.  These thousands of people know that Jesus didn’t have a pack train of food with Him, but they also know that they have gotten a full meal from thin air.  No wonder they acknowledge Him as the Prophet!  There are countless people in the modern-day United States who claim to be miracle workers, but not one of them has ever done anything like this.

Also, John and the rest could not possibly have gotten away with claiming this happened if it didn’t happen.  Mark’s account of this, at least, is only written about 30 years after the events in question.  It’s preposterous to think that the people of Palestine would have accepted this story if they hadn’t already known about it.  It’s like Paul says to Agrippa in Acts 26.  These things weren’t done in a corner.

As though this weren’t enough, Jesus follows it up by WALKING ON WATER.  Look at John 6:15-21.  Jesus is forced into hiding by some overzealous crowd members who want to make Him king regardless of what He wants.  After milling around for a while, the disciples apparently figure that Jesus can take care of Himself, and they get back in the boat and start sailing home.  When the boat is out in the middle of the Sea of Galilee, Jesus pops up again, walking along on the surface of the water as easily as He would on land.  Understandably, they’re terrified.  Jesus, however, persuades them that He’s not a ghost or a demon or something, so they invite Him into the boat, which immediately returns to Capernaum.

This is one of the many strange stories about Jesus in the gospels, and it reminds us of something we often would prefer to forget.  Even as we try to make sense of the Bible, we must remember that God, who is the central figure of the Bible, simply isn’t going to make sense sometimes.  His thoughts are higher than our thoughts.  So too with Jesus.  Even though He took on the form of a man, He still thought like God.  No human being would decide to take a stroll across the surface of the Sea of Galilee and then teleport himself to his destination from halfway.  We’re used to living lives that are governed by time and space.  Jesus, though, has no concern for the natural laws that He Himself made up, and He feels free to violate them whenever He wants to.  This isn’t only the story of a powerful prophet.  It’s the story of someone more than human.

Finally in this section, we see the reaction of THE CROWDS to Jesus.  Here, let’s look at John 6:22-25.  There are two main points to draw from this.  The first is that indirectly, the miracle of Jesus walking on the water has 5000 witnesses.  The crowds saw Jesus’ disciples get into the boat.  They are certainly aware that Jesus Himself did not get into the boat.  They are watching carefully for Jesus because they want to make Him king.  And yet, the next day, they find that Jesus has gone, and, when they sail back to Capernaum themselves, they find that Jesus has gotten there ahead of them—by boat.  They didn’t actually see what happened, but it’s evident to them that something inexplicable has.

Second, let’s pay attention to how enthusiastic this crowd is.  They are so interested in Jesus that they are chasing Him all around the Galilean countryside.  If there had been pollsters around back then, Jesus’ approval rating would have been off the charts.  This has all the signs of a major advance for the kingdom of God.  However, as we know, the rest of the story quickly takes a very different turn.

This should remind us, friends, not to get caught up in the euphoria of the moment.  It’s good to enjoy good times, both in our congregation and in life generally.  However, we have to remember that they are certainly not guaranteed to go on forever, and we shouldn’t assume the future will be like the present.

**The Bread of Life**

During the follow-up to the feeding of the 5000, though, Jesus presents Himself as the true bread of life.  However, this conversation doesn’t go particularly well.  Indeed, it begins with A FAILURE TO COMMUNICATE.  Let’s read together here from John 6:26-34.  Jesus begins here by diagnosing the spiritual problem underlying His apparent success in winning over the crowds.  They are impressed with His ability to feed them from nothing, but they’ve missed the point.  The miracle isn’t actually a sign to them, because it hasn’t shown them or taught them anything.  They have recognized the physical event, but the spiritual implications have sailed right over their heads.

I get the feeling that the crowds are a little alarmed by these words.  They ask Jesus what they’re supposed to do about it, and He gives them the duh answer.  They’re supposed to believe in Him!  Note, by the way, that this passage sheds some interesting light on the supposed faith vs. works debate we spent last quarter discussing in Bible class.  All kinds of folks insist that baptism isn’t essential for salvation because we’re saved by faith, not works, and baptism is a work, so baptism can’t save us.  I’m quite confident that nowhere in Scripture is baptism ever described as a work that we do.  On the other hand, faith is described as a work we do—right here.  Faith vs. works doesn’t make much sense if faith is a work!

However, the multitudes miss the point here.  They use Jesus’ instruction to believe in Him as a way to demand another sign—ideally, a continual progression of signs that will keep their bellies full like God fed their fathers in the wilderness.  Sure, Jesus fed them yesterday, but now they’re hungry again and want more!

In response, Jesus tells them that they have missed the point again.  They shouldn’t be so concerned with earthly bread.  After all, earthly bread doesn’t have a lasting effect.  The next day you’re hungry again.  Instead, they should be seeking the true bread of God, because it will keep them satisfied.  Of course, He isn’t talking about physical bread at all.  He’s talking about the bread that sustains the soul.  However, they miss the point here too.  They continue to understand Jesus’ spiritual metaphor in material terms.

It’s easy for us to shake our heads about how dense the Jews were, but the ironic thing is that people all over the world today are making exactly the same mistake.  They look to Jesus for physical blessings instead of spiritual ones.  They seek to follow Him not because they want eternal life, but because they want their lives on earth to be better.  That attitude, friends, has nothing to do with the gospel.  God is gracious to us and blesses us physically, yes, but the rain falls on the just and on the unjust.  Indeed, in physical terms, first-century Christians were a pretty wretched lot!  If we want to imitate them, we will seek what they sought.

Next, Jesus describes THE SPIRITUAL FEAST that He is actually offering.  Let’s read here from John 6:35-40.  The multitudes have been trying to separate believing in Jesus and getting bread from heaven into two separate issues, but Jesus points out that they are exactly the same issue.  He is the bread of life, and it is believing in Him that will provide eternal sustenance.  We can be sure that the sustenance is eternal because the Father and Jesus both are determined to keep those who come to Him safe.

This, friends, should be the lens through which we see our existence.  All of us have lives that are filled with so many things:  we have our jobs, we have our finances, we have our families, we have our hobbies, we have our health, and so on.  As different as those things are, though, they all have one thing in common.  They don’t really matter.  If we lose our hobbies, our jobs, our money, our families, or even our lives, sure, we’re going to hurt.  We’re going to suffer.  However, as long as we still have Jesus, our lives are fundamentally fine.  On the other hand, if we lose Jesus, even if we keep all those other things, it’s a disaster.

At this provocative statement, THE GRUMBLING BEGINS.  Consider John 6:41-51.  Better than anything Jesus could have said, the Jews’ reaction here illustrates that they aren’t following Him.  They have seen the miracle.  They know beyond a doubt that Jesus fed thousands of people out of one boy’s supper.  However, they haven’t understood the miracle.  They haven’t realized that anyone who does such things is from God, so that whatever He says about Himself, no matter how outrageous, must be true.  Instead of accepting that it must be true that Jesus came down from heaven, they start arguing that it can’t be true because of what they already know about His earthly parentage.

In response, Jesus points out that the problem is theirs, not His.  They aren’t grumbling because Jesus has said something untrue.  They’re grumbling because they haven’t heard the Father and learned from Him.  These people aren’t believing because they’re so stuck on what they “know” that they aren’t listening.

Likewise, friends, when we come to the Bible, we need to throw out everything we think we know.  It’s easy to pick on the wider religious world here.  So many times, I’ve studied with someone who won’t accept what the Bible teaches on salvation because it doesn’t line up with what they’ve been taught before and already think is true.  These people don’t listen to the word.  They look for a reason to reject it.

However, it’s all too easy for this same mindset to appear in our congregation.  What matters is not what we think the Bible says.  What matters is what the Bible actually says.  As many here have, I myself have benefited immensely from all kinds of teachers who taught me all kinds of things about the word.  That’s a great blessing.  However, just because somebody with good intentions taught me something doesn’t mean it’s right.  Instead, we all have to constantly compare what we think is true with what the Scripture teaches, and if it doesn’t line up with the actual witness of the word, we have to change our thinking.

Finally in this section, let’s consider Jesus’ self-description.  The manna that His audience craves isn’t actually useful.  As Jesus points out, the Israelites in the wilderness ate the manna for 40 years, but they died anyway—not just physically, but spiritually.  Somehow, they could be fed by a miracle from God every day yet not trust in His promises.  Those, on the other hand, who eat the spiritual bread of Jesus won’t die.  Their faith will sustain them eternally.  Ironically, though, in order to give life to everyone else, Jesus is going to have to surrender His own flesh to death before He can be the bread of the believer.

Before the Lord’s Supper, then, let’s reflect on these things.  The bread that we are about to eat is a memorial of Jesus, the true bread.  The fruit of the vine we are about to drink is a memorial of His blood, the drink that keeps us from thirsting ever again.  However, it is not our physical consumption of these things that sustains us.  Instead, it is our spiritual consumption of them, by faith making Jesus a part of ourselves.  Do we live sustained by Him, or do we go around spiritually hungry?  Let’s consider this as we partake.

**Reactions to Jesus**

The final part of the story concerns two main reactions to Jesus.  He evokes these reactions Himself with a discussion of, of all things, EATING FLESH AND DRINKING BLOOD.  Let’s read here from John 6:52-58.  It’s almost impossible to talk about this passage without talking about the ways that others have misunderstood it.  This, of course, is the passage that people go to in order to support the doctrine of transubstantiation.  Transubstantiation is the idea that during the Lord’s Supper, at least during denominational variants thereof, the bread and the fruit of the vine literally become the flesh and the blood of Jesus.  In some ways, this is a puzzling doctrine because to all outward experience, the elements of the communion remain bread and fruit of the vine.  They look, smell, feel, and taste like food rather than like human flesh and human blood.  Proponents of transubstantiation, though, argue that even though the elements have the semblance of bread and wine, in reality they have actually become the body and blood of the Lord.  This they describe as both a miracle and as a mystery of the faith.

Now, I suppose that the power of God could transform crackers and grape juice into the flesh and blood of Jesus while continuing to seem like crackers and grape juice.  After all, nothing is impossible with God.  However, that leaves open the question of whether that’s what this passage is actually teaching.

Let’s consider it in the context of John 6.  Back in 6:32-33, Jesus promised His audience a spiritual bread that would give them life.  They thought He was talking about a physical bread that would keep them from being physically hungry again.  Next, in 6:41-42, He told them that He had come down out of heaven.  He meant a spiritual descent, but they thought He was talking about literally drifting down out of the clouds and got confused because they knew His parents.

Now, Jesus is telling them that they have to eat His flesh and drink His blood if they want to have life.  In a context where Jesus has, over and over again, been saying spiritual, symbolic things that cause confusion if we take them literally, why in the world would we take these words of the Lord literally instead of symbolically, especially when the result is confusing?  The text becomes a whole lot less difficult once we start looking at it in a spiritual rather than a fleshly sense, and I’m convinced it’s what the Lord intended.

The spiritual meaning, then, is this:  just as we eat the bread which came down out of heaven when we believe in Jesus, so too we eat His flesh and drink His blood when we take His life into ourselves, when we seek to become in spirit like He is.  The Lord’s Supper is certainly a memorial of the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus, but it is also supposed to be a reminder to us of this crucial spiritual goal.  Only when we sustain our spirits on Jesus does He abide in us, and we in Him.

Sadly, though, we see MANY DISCIPLES WITHDRAW because they understand Jesus’ words physically rather than spiritually.  This happens in John 6:59-66.  Rather than trying to drill down more deeply into the meaning of the Lord, these disciples take His words at face value and, once again, get confused.  They’ve eaten the miraculous bread and fish, but they haven’t fed on the word of God.  They are going to eat and die rather than eat and live.  Jesus tries to jolt them out of their fleshly way of thinking.  He points out that looking for fleshly meaning and fleshly sustenance is pointless.  Looking for spiritual meaning and spiritual sustenance is what matters.  However, many still reject Him and leave.

This is a perfect example of what we should not do when we encounter a hard saying of Jesus.  The Scripture can be difficult for a number of different reasons.  It can be difficult, as in this case, because it’s impossible to understand from a fleshly perspective.  However, there are also times when the meaning of the Scripture is perfectly clear, but it’s difficult because we don’t want to do it.  Regardless, it is nearly certain that sooner or later, the words of the Lord are going to trip us up a little bit.  Only if we are determined to continue following Him, regardless of whether it makes perfect sense, regardless even of whether it’s going to cost us something we hold dear, are we going to receive the eternal life He promises to those who love Him.

In contrast to those who withdraw, the story ends with PETER’S AFFIRMATION.  Let’s look at John 6:67-71.  This is one of those texts that shows so clearly the humanity of Jesus.  Just as we would be, He is disgusted by the hard hearts of those who saw a miracle yet refused to accept the miracle-worker.  He sarcastically asks the Twelve if they are going to leave Him too.  Peter adamantly denies that they will, but Jesus, still disgusted, points out that even among the Twelve, Judas is going to prove a traitor.

However, our example here is not Judas but Peter.  Here is the reason why we stick with Jesus despite all the hard sayings.  He is the Holy One of God, and He has the words of eternal life.  As Peter rhetorically asks, where else could we go?  There is no other hope but in Jesus.
