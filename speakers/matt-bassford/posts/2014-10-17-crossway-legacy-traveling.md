**SIZE.**  Every Bible is the product of compromises, and
this is even more obvious in the Legacy than in most.  The combination of the single-column layout
and the Renaissance-style outer and lower margins both put space between its
covers at a premium.

As we saw last week, the typesetters of the Legacy increased
the number of characters per line and words per page above that which would be
optimal for a reading Bible.  It
certainly would have been possible to format the Legacy with 60 characters per
line and 500 words per page, but such a Bible would have been an inch and a
half or more thick, about the same size as the hefty Foundation SCR.  The Legacy was originally conceived of as a
single-column thinline Bible, and there is nothing thin about a Bible that big!



On the other hand, if the Legacy doesn’t quite hit the
proportions on a page that would make it an ideal reading Bible, it doesn’t hit
the external proportions that would make it a good portable/travel Bible
either.  It certainly comes closer to
fitting in a glove box than does the Foundation SCR, but there’s still a lot of
Legacy sticking out when I try to close the compartment door.  A true thinline would have passed the
glove-box test, but the Legacy doesn’t.

The same thing is true of the Legacy in visits and personal
studies.  It’s not nearly as unsuitable
for those settings as a study or wide-margin Bible would be, but it’s no
hideaway, either.  I didn’t feel utterly
ridiculous talking to a shut-in with the Legacy sitting on my lap, but I definitely
noticed that I had it.

**CROSS-REFERENCES.**  The Legacy doesn’t have them.  It’s a text Bible.  In theory, this might have given me trouble
if some particularly difficult question arose in the course of a study.  In practice, nothing like that happened, and
I took great pleasure from the layout of the poetry of Jeremiah 31, which
happened to be the topic of one of those studies.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgpn-3Z8cxCvQyhh3Nz7nRZzJQtE_NR0XPaxajtkEa8V9FXzceJl4wqnz-YeXTcGRJPA09vFsD94C7zOyLNd2T1FKLbe27ovGDmXXK1jNJ6krFPszORQxy483UN5dHomw79weYb5HeAaA/s1600/T3.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgpn-3Z8cxCvQyhh3Nz7nRZzJQtE_NR0XPaxajtkEa8V9FXzceJl4wqnz-YeXTcGRJPA09vFsD94C7zOyLNd2T1FKLbe27ovGDmXXK1jNJ6krFPszORQxy483UN5dHomw79weYb5HeAaA/s1600/T3.jpg)

**CONCLUSION.**  Nobody is going to mistake the Legacy for a
purpose-designed travel Bible, and nobody should buy one for that role.  However, it isn’t a behemoth either.  It flunks the glove-box test, but only by a
little bit, and it doesn’t scream, “Here I am to beat you over the head with my
Bible!” to whomever you’re talking to. 
For these purposes, I give it a 3/5. 
Overall, the Legacy earns a 13/20.
