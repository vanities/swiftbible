Sometimes, it’s not our friends who tell us the things we
need to hear.  It’s our enemies.  The noise-to-signal ratio there may be pretty
high, but sometimes the signal is worth listening to.

One of my left-leaning friends posted another one of those
salvoes in the continuing online conflict over morality, [this blog post](http://johnpavlovitz.com/2016/05/26/dear-offended-christian-from-a-very-tired-christian/) by liberal Christian writer John Pavlovitz. 
It purports to be a letter to all those offended (conservative,
evangelical) Christians out there explaining why liberal Christians are mad at
them.



When first I read the post, my first reaction was to write a
rebuttal.  Many of the arguments in it
are both familiar and polarizing.  I
don’t run with a whole lot of libs, but I suppose that there must be people out
there who think that the fact that Jesus Never Said Anything About
Homosexuality is powerful and meaningful. 
The people I do run with have been hearing that for decades and can
return fire with half a dozen counter-arguments without having to think very
hard (“What about the implications of Matthew 5:17-19?”  “What about the implications of Matthew
19:3-12?”).

Honestly, it’s not a persuasive argument.  Maybe it’s not meant to be.  Maybe it’s meant to fire up the liberal tribe
against the conservative tribe, which proves its inferiority by its very
unwillingness to accept such a self-evident truth.  I don’t know. 
I confess to an allergy to bad arguments, wherever they come from.

There’s another issue here, though.  When I read through Pavlovitz’s two
dozen indictments of conservative Christians, pick the three flimsiest, and
spend 1000 words triumphantly debunking them, I’m doing exactly the opposite of
what I need to do.  Focusing on all the
places where I’m right and don’t need to change won’t make me more like
Christ.  Focusing on the places where I
have failed and do need to change will.

In that spirit, then, where does this self-described angry
liberal Christian have a point?

*I’m tired of you being more outraged by red
coffee cups and department store restrooms than by poverty and racism
and gun violence.*

*Valid
point, at least with two of the three.  I
don’t think the Bible has a whole lot to say about how I should handle gun
violence (besides not shooting anybody myself), but there’s black-letter
Scripture about how Christians should address poverty and racism.  There are more passages than I care to cite
that emphasize the importance of mercy and giving to the poor.  Note that this is not limited to the
“deserving” poor, as though it were even possible for someone to deserve
mercy.  Caring for the poor needs to be
part of every individual Christian’s life.*

*Similarly, Paul in Colossians 3:11 observes
that in Christ there is no Jew, Greek, Scythian, or barbarian.  Racism is incompatible with following Jesus,
and every Christian should make special efforts to show kindness and love to
those of other races.  Anybody who thinks
that this isn’t necessary because racism no longer exists in a meaningful way
needs to talk to one of those white-bread evangelical women who has adopted a
black or brown child.*

*I
have some friends in that category, and the stories they tell about the way
they’ve seen their children treated are enough to make you wince.  I’m skeptical that the government will ever
be able to wave its magic wand and make racism go away (I’m big on skepticism
about government), but if everybody who claimed to follow Christ started loving
their differently-colored neighbors as themselves, that would make a
difference.*

*As
for red cups and bathrooms, they make great crusades on Facebook, but they
don’t have much to do with discipleship. 
When Christmas isn’t in the Bible, who cares if it’s on a coffee cup?*

*Similarly,
I’ve seen too many self-described believers take pleasure in describing what
they would do to the pervert who went into the restroom where their little girl
is going potty.  Yes, in the
(extraordinarily unlikely) event that I felt my daughter was in danger, I would
go in and remove her from the danger. 
But offering violence to somebody, except in utter desperation?  As Jesus said to James and John in Luke 9:55,
I’m afraid that those disciples don’t know what kind of spirit they are of.*

*I’m tired of LGBTQ teens cutting their
forearms and jumping off buildings because they’re told by their church
friends that God hates them, because their Christian parents told them, because
their Christian pastors told them.*

According to the U.S. Department of Health and Human
Services, gay male adolescents are between two and three times more likely to
attempt suicide than their peers are.  My
cousin was gay and did, in fact, kill himself. 
I believe, and indeed am called by Jesus to believe, that the life of
every human being is precious.  These things
are an unalloyed tragedy, and we need to ask what we can do to minimize them.

It’s true that some of the problem is caused by cultural
factors.  I have a friend who works with
disadvantaged youths, and he tells me that gay and lesbian teens get expelled
from high school at disproportionate rates because they get in too many
fights.  We all know that kids can be
vicious and mean, and there’s not a whole lot we can do about that beyond
influencing the kids we come in contact with.

Nor does our society’s binary understanding of sexuality
help.  It proclaims that you’re either
straight or gay, and if you’re gay, it is hardwired, absolute, and defines your
whole personality.  I don’t think that’s
a Biblical position at all, and when it is unthinkingly read into texts like
Romans 1 and 1 Corinthians 6, disaster is the result.  It leaves the impression that some are going
to hell not because of what they do, but because of who they are, which is
utterly contrary to the spirit of Christianity.

However, we ourselves must ask whether we are diminishing
the harm or adding to it.  Once, a gay
friend of mine told me what it was like to sit in a Bible class where the
subject of homosexuality was being discussed when no one knew about his
temptations.  He came away from the
discussion feeling like he ranked somewhere between a pedophile and a mass
murderer.  Not surprisingly, he left the
faith shortly thereafter.  It’s awfully
easy for many of us to froth at the mouth about how the practice of
homosexuality is The Worst Sin Ever because we feel no inclination to practice
it.  For those who do, well. . .

The point here is not that we should back down from the
Biblical truth on the subject.  The point
is that we should speak truth in love. 
Whenever we’re talking about this sin, or indeed about any sin, we should
behave as though we are in the presence of someone who is struggling with it.  After all, that may indeed be the case.  We deny, and rightly so, that the defense of
Biblical morality is necessarily hateful. 
Let’s make sure that we aren’t conveying hate in the way we defend it.

There are other things that Pavlovitz had to say that
conservative Christians need to hear, even if they don’t make for easy
hearing.  However, I’m writing a blog
post and not a novel, so two will have to do for now.  Regardless, whether it’s on the Internet or
in person, willingness to listen should be a hallmark of the disciple.  If we aren’t willing to listen ourselves, how
will we ever get those who so desperately need to hear the truth to listen to
us?
