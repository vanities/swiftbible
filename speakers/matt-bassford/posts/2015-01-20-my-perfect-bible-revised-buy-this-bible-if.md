**OVERVIEW:** This new review segment is going to be a
(mostly) objective look at why one should buy a particular Bible, or not.

As I mentioned earlier, I’ve decided to do away with the
“Traveling” segment of my reviews.  They
simply weren’t doing enough analytical work to stick around.  This is their replacement.



Ever since I started reviewing Bibles, I’ve been
straightforward about the subjectivity of the process.  When I’m writing about the experience of
using a particular Bible, I can’t very well do other than use the perspective
of my own experience.  I think that’s
valuable.  Otherwise, my reviews would be
nothing more than a regurgitation of factoids from a publisher’s website.  Anybody with an Internet connection and a
brain can find out that the Crossway Legacy is single-column.  You don’t need me to tell you that.

Some of what I say will always be objective.  It’s an objective fact, for instance, that a
Bible with a glued binding won’t stand up to years of heavy use.  Most of it, however, represents nothing more
than my own opinion.  For example, I will
generally give double-column Bibles higher marks than single-column
Bibles.  This isn’t because double-column
layouts are inherently superior.  It just
so happens that they work better for me.

Over the years, I’ve come to appreciate that my opinions, no
matter how strongly I hold them, do not necessarily represent some foundational
truth of the universe.  Somebody else’s
preference for single-column Bibles is every bit as valid as my preference for
the opposite.  As a consequence, it may
be that a Bible to which I give a low score would work marvelously for somebody
else.

“Buy This Bible If” is an attempt to address this
deficiency.  In it, I’m going to try to
refine the characteristics of a Bible into a list of its most important
features, which should allow readers to make a judgment about the Bible on the
basis of their own preferences, not mine. 
I’ve found that the differences that I notice most are:

- Bible size

- Cover material/construction

- Translation

- Single vs. double column

- Font size

- Verse-by-verse vs. paragraphed

- Presence/number of cross-references
- Paper opacity

- Margin Width

- Retail Price

I prefer some of these options over the opposite, but that
doesn’t make the opposite bad.  If a
reader knows his preferences don’t align with mine, this should tell him when
he ought to take a look at a particular Bible anyway.  I will, however, also note objective flaws,
such as problems with construction or layout.

Hopefully, this will
make my reviews (even?) more useful to others than they’ve been previously.  Is there anything else that you think ought
to appear on this list?
