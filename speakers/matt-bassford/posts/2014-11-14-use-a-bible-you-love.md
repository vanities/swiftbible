This one is for every Christian.  Most of my Bible-related posts aren’t.  I know that when I lapse into extended
discussions of typography and paper opacity, 99 percent of believers couldn’t
care less.  To paraphrase a friend of
mine, you’re a geek when you care passionately about the minor details of some
field of endeavor, and I am unabashedly a Bible geek.

However, all the details fit together to make a larger
picture, and the larger picture is this: 
If you want to go to heaven, you should buy and use a Bible you
love.  I’m not talking about “the Bible”
in the abstract.  I’m talking about a
particular volume, with a particular set of physical qualities.



Those particular physical qualities should make you
happy.  You should like the color.  You should like the smell.  You should like the weight of the Bible in
your hands.  It should feel right.  When you open the Bible, you should think the
arrangement of words on the page is beautiful. 
You should be able to lose yourself in reading this Bible without any
trouble.  Using this Bible should make
you feel warm inside.  You should be glad
that you own it.

This is all utterly subjective.  There are no right answers here, nor any call
to judge anybody else’s answer.  If you
love the battered bonded-leather KJV that your mama bought you 40 years ago,
great!  Use it.  If you get warm fuzzies from opening the
Bible app on your iPhone, wonderful!  That’s
the Bible for you.  If your favorite
Bible is an Allan edition that you paid north of $200 for, with art gilt and
scratch-‘n-sniff goatskin, there’s no shame in that.  If anybody gets on his high horse and insists
that the money you paid for the Bible should have been given to the poor
instead, point him to Mark 14:3-9.

If you don’t love the Bible that you’re using now, find a
Bible that you do love.  Try out that
Bible app if you haven’t before.  If an
e-Bible doesn’t do it for you, take a trip to the largest Christian bookstore
within easy driving distance.  Go to the
Bible section, look at the translations you’d consider using, pull a Bible that
looks like it might work, and read it for five minutes.  If it’s shrink-wrapped, get one of the clerks
to open it for you so you can try it out.

Then ask yourself, “Does using this Bible make me happy?” If
it does, buy it (or go home and buy it online for about 40 percent less).  If it doesn’t, put it back on the shelf and
try another.  If none of the Bibles seem
to work for you, ask a Bible dork you know for help.  It makes us feel smart to tell people what
Bibles they should use.

Alternatively, try casting a wider net online.  Even though there is no substitute for the
experience of actually holding and using a Bible, it’s still possible to learn
quite a bit from reading.  Publishers’
and retailers’ sites will reveal a great deal about the Bibles they stock;
reviews will reveal even more.  If you
order a Bible and find out you don’t like it, send it back or give it away, and
try again.  Don’t give up until you find
a Bible that you emotionally connect with.

Why?  Because a Bible
you love is a Bible you will use.
