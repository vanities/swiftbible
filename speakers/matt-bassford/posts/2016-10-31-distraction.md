A friend of mine has an aunt who likes to say, “Quitting
smoking is easy; I’ve done it hundreds of times!”  In the same way, I’ve found that quitting
blogging about electoral politics is easy. 
I’ve done it, if not hundreds of times, at least half a dozen.  However, I find that the chatter on the MSN
front page and my Facebook feed has a way of dragging me back in, even though
there is nothing new to say.  Much like
the election itself, my writing degenerates into an endless recycling of the
same old arguments.



This is all the more problematic because the election ought
to be way, way, way down the list of my spiritual priorities.  I can complain about the election with my
like-minded friends.  I can grit my teeth
at online commentary from those who aren’t like-minded.  However, none of this advances the kingdom of
Christ in any significant way.  What’s
more, the election itself isn’t going to have a significant effect on the kingdom
either.

Even if we pick the candidate we like the least and assume
the very worst about them, Christians have lived and flourished under such
rulers before.  Exhibit A here is, of
course, the Roman Empire.  Biographies of
the emperors read like chronicles of human vice.  They engaged in every kind of bad behavior
imaginable, from bizarre sexual practices to persecution of Christians to
conquests of whole countries.  The Empire
itself was a deeply flawed state that built its wealth on the backs of
slaves.

However, even under rulers who were evil and corrupt, even
under a hostile and oppressive system of government, the early church grew and
prospered.  Disastrous political problems
did not lead to disastrous spiritual problems. 
In fact, from a Biblical perspective, the worst thing that happened to
the early church was its adoption as the state religion of the Empire.  Power and patronage caused the apostasy that
persecution couldn’t.

The point here is not that we should long for the good old
days when Christians were converted into cat food in the Colosseum.  There’s a reason why Paul urges Christians in
1 Timothy 2 to pray that the government will leave them alone.  Persecution is a problem, but it’s clearly
not a faith-destroying one. It may lead the uncommitted to fall away, but it
will make true disciples stronger.

Instead, the point is that we shouldn’t worry so much about
such things because they don’t really matter. 
It may be that if the wrong person is elected (which wrong person?), the
country will continue down its current evil path.  Frankly, that’s not our problem.  There is One who is the Judge of nations, and
we ain’t Him.  We are not responsible for
the existence of a crooked and perverse generation.  We are responsible for being a light in its
midst.

That’s where our work needs to be.  Rarely to never does the light of Christ
shine forth in heated political debates online. 
Our political activism does not impress others with our
spirituality.  Indeed, it often leaves
the opposite impression.

Instead, if we want to shine, we need to do it in the same
way that disciples 2000 years ago did it. 
Sometimes, politics wouldn’t leave them alone, but whenever they could,
they left politics alone.  They devoted
themselves instead to taking up their crosses and following Jesus.

This is hard.  It
always has been.  It requires us to
reform our lives so that they look like Jesus’ life.  People need to be able to look at us and see
His compassion for the needy, His impatience with religious hypocrisy, His
meekness and humility, His dramatically different understanding of the good
life, and most of all, His love for absolutely everybody.  Unlike today’s political pundits, every word
that Jesus spoke, every action that He took, was motivated by love.  The better we understand Him, the more awe He
inspires in us.

Nobody becomes more Christlike by accident.  Instead, we get there by loving the Lord our
God with *all* of our heart and *all* of our mind and *all* of our soul and *all*
of our strength.  Christ must be the
consuming passion of our lives.  If we
devote anything less to Him than everything we have to give, we can only find
failure.

That’s the problem with distraction.  The devil doesn’t have to lead us into
debauchery to defeat God’s plan for our lives. 
He just has to keep us occupied with things that are less
important.

Things like politics.
