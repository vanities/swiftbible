As wonderfully as the SCR has fared in every other area so far, no Bible can be perfectly suited for every task.  Travel is where the imperfections of the SCR begin to show.  Here are my impressions:

**SIZE.**  Ironically, it was a 2002 wide-margin SCR that taught me that taking a large Bible on vacation was an invitation to disaster.  Even though a narrow-margin SCR is slightly smaller than that, it doesn’t fare a great deal better.  Even though I knew it wouldn’t work, I went through the motions of trying to fit the SCR into my glove box.  It didn’t work.



The SCR is a great big beast of a Bible, and it gags a glove box.  I could fit the spine of the Bible into the compartment, but it was both too wide and too thick to allow me to close it.  If I were to be so foolish as to take an SCR on vacation, it would probably end up on the back seat floorboards, where my daughter would promptly throw up on it and ruin it.  I would rather travel Bible-less than expose a Bible I liked to such peril.

Around town, of course, I can simply toss whatever Bible I’m using on the front seat next to me as I drive to visit homebound members of my congregation, to lead kitchen-table studies, to preach funerals, and so on.  The SCR isn’t in any danger on short trips, but that doesn’t make it ideal for those trips either.

Basically, it’s awfully conspicuous in settings where an inconspicuous Bible would be better.  I like to have a Bible with me when I’m visiting shut-ins in case some spiritual question comes up, but the SCR looks like an arsenal, not a contingency plan.  It’s the kind of Bible you bring to clobber somebody with the club of the Spirit, and that is rarely the impression I want to make at the beginning of a personal visit!  It might be more suitable at a wedding or funeral, but even there, black leather and half an acre of page gilding are a touch more impressive than is ideal.

Overall, as my two weeks of testing the SCR as a travel/visiting Bible wore on, I started hunting for excuses to leave it at home.  Even my Crossway Legacy started looking like a less obtrusive alternative, and the Legacy is a big Bible all on its own.  I suppose that if I were a truly dedicated tester, I would have stuck it out, but I think that the fact that I didn’t want to use the SCR as a portable Bible for even two weeks speaks volumes all on its own.

**LAYOUT.**  Of course, once I’ve opened the SCR, no matter what the setting is, it’s everything I could desire it to be—monster font, 95,000 cross-references waiting to bail me out, the works!  Sadly, in order to have those resources available, I have to bring the Bible with me, which I don’t want to do.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEi4bL9GuQshJigWjZn5IP4ukYpAY8WBe7N4-5zrDTWhA3TETd2RgKGfJg06lNSsQKjnl36nmqA4U5mFTC65HmPRRq6WE8e8Ft43NcMlW6qieJyz7wC-4S3S_-mrlB3doyo4fnY6GnuxwQ/s1600/SCR_T3.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEi4bL9GuQshJigWjZn5IP4ukYpAY8WBe7N4-5zrDTWhA3TETd2RgKGfJg06lNSsQKjnl36nmqA4U5mFTC65HmPRRq6WE8e8Ft43NcMlW6qieJyz7wC-4S3S_-mrlB3doyo4fnY6GnuxwQ/s1600/SCR_T3.jpg)

**CONCLUSION.**  In short, all the features that make the SCR so effective in preaching, teaching, and study, and even as a reading Bible, inflate its physical footprint so much that it is too large to be a good travel Bible.  It’s not really fair to expect the SCR to do well in this role, but nonetheless, I rate it at 2/5.

**OVERALL SCORE:  16.5/20**
