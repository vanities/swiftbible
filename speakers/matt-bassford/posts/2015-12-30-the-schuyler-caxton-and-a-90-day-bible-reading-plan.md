You know a Bible is a good reader when you start creating
excuses to read it more.  At various
times, I’ve sat down with the Schuyler Caxton and read an entire book of the
Bible in one sitting—2 Corinthians, say—for no more pressing reason than
because I wanted to.

I’ve talked at length about the qualities of the
Select/Caxton text block that make it a good reader, but I also find that the
NLT as a translation lends itself to consuming the Bible in large chunks.  Sometimes, you’ll run into texts that make
you shake your head and say, “Man!  I
don’t know why they rendered it like that!” 
The rest of the time, though, it goes down very smoothly.

I’ve decided, then, that it’s time to take the Caxton on a
road trip.  Rather than adhering to my
normal through-the-Bible-in-a-year pace, starting January 1, I’m going to try
to read through the Caxton in 90 days. 
What’s more, rather than using one of the 90-day Bible reading plans
that are out there already, I’m substituting one of my own manufacture.

 Once I started looking at the extant plans, I decided that I
didn’t like the philosophy behind them. 
They seemed to be primarily concerned with keeping readings at a
particular length each day, rather than attempting to adhere to the natural
divisions of the text.  By contrast, I
think the latter is a more useful goal to pursue.  Unless you’re simply looking to brute-force
your way through the Bible in 90 days, it’s much more meaningful to read 2
Corinthians in a sitting than it is to read 1 Corinthians 10-16 and 2
Corinthians 1-6.  Even when two books of
the Bible share an author, each book still exists as a discrete literary unit,
with its own context and purposes.  I
believe that one of the best ways to divine such things is to read books of the
Bible, so far as is possible, as a unit.

In any event, I’m including the plan I devised at the bottom
of this post.  The readings aren’t of
equal length, but they do represent a semi-thoughtful attempt to take the books
of the Bible on their own terms.  All of
you are, of course, welcome to copy the plan and try it out on your own.  If you do, let me know how it goes!
