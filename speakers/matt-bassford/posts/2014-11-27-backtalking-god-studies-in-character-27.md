*Then Laban and Bethuel
answered and said, "The thing comes from the LORD; we cannot speak to you
either bad or good. Here is Rebekah before you; take her and go, and let her be
your master's son's wife, as the LORD has spoken."*

--Genesis 24:50-51

It’s difficult for us to think of our children today as
valuable possessions, but that’s certainly the way they were seen by the people
of 4000 years ago.  Sons were useful for
work, for defense of the family wealth, and as a retirement policy in old
age.  Daughters were not quite as
valuable as sons, but they became meaningful when they were married off to
cement financial and political alliances with others.



Only as we understand this can we understand the predicament
facing Laban and Bethuel.  Not only is
Rebekah someone they obviously love, but she also represents an important
asset.  And yet, here comes a servant
whom they’ve never met before who tells them that it is God’s will that Rebekah
travel to a distant land and marry a relative they’ve never seen.  They must have known that once they sent
Rebekah off, they would never see her again, as indeed they didn’t.  They also must have known that without
Rebekah as a bargaining chip, their lives in Mesopotamia would be more
difficult.

However, they don’t hesitate to let Rebekah go.  Their words reveal why.  Once God had determined what He wanted
Rebekah for, the choice was taken out of their hands.  There was nothing they could do but accede to
the Almighty.

This is a habit of thinking that we must learn too.  We have free will.  We can make our own decisions.  However, God also has the right to demand
obedience from us and punish us if we do not obey.  Despite this, we still get hung up on the
idea that disobedience is an option.  We
weigh the pros and cons of sin, its apparent advantages to us, and keep in the
back of our minds the thought that even if we sin, well, we can always repent
later.  Sometimes, our calculations come
out in favor of God, and we do right.  At
other times, we are deceived by our own lusts, and we sin.  This never works out quite as well as we
thought it was going to, but that doesn’t stop us from treating the next
temptation as a choice too.

If we want to be true disciples, though, we have to reach
the point where we don’t believe that sin is truly up to us.  Once we learn what God has decreed, that
should settle the issue for us for all time. 
It’s not up to us to speak bad or good, to assess the merits of
disobedience.  Our commitment to
righteousness has taken that decision out of our hands.  Instead, our only reaction to temptation must
be to say, “The Lord has spoken.”
