**OVERVIEW.** For the Bibles I use at the church
building, pretty much any format but single-column paragraphed will work
well.  Most of the time, I don’t need
anything more than light cross-referencing at the bottom.  Size-wise, I like Bibles that are light and
handy for teaching Bible classes, but they still need to be readable.

**INTRODUCTION.** I’m a preacher.  The Bibles I use are as important to me as a
sword was to a knight.  I preach from my
Bible, I teach classes from it, and throughout the week, I spend hours every
day studying it.  It’s extremely
important to me, then, to have a Bible that is well suited to fulfilling the
demands I place on it.  Here’s what that
looks like these days.

 **PREACHING.**In my original “My Perfect Bible” series,
I opined that the perfect preaching Bible had to be no less than 5 ½“ x 8
½“.  Those, after all, are the dimensions
of a sheet of copier paper folded in half, and I needed my Bible to hold my
half-sheet sermon outlines on the way up to the pulpit without displaying
unsightly fringes.

In the intervening year, I’ve decided I was wrong about
that.  Experience has proven that a
smaller Bible serves just as well, provided that my sermon notes are folded in
quarters rather than in half.  I used to
preach from a full manuscript that wouldn’t have tolerated folding in quarters,
but I shifted away from doing so several years ago.  However, my brain was still stuck in 2008, so
I didn’t realize I could get by with re-folded sermon notes until the process
of reviewing smaller Bibles made me realize it.

Also in the previous version of “My Perfect Bible”, I
insisted that only a verse-by-verse Bible was suitable for pulpit work.  I based this on several years’ worth of
missing citations in the pulpit while using the Crossway Personal Size
Reference Bible, which has a single-column paragraphed format.

However, once again, my bold declaration was betrayed by
experience.  Over and over again, as I
reviewed double-column paragraphed Bibles, I found myself saying, “Oddly
enough, I didn’t miss any citations, even though the Bible is
paragraphed!”  Eventually, it became
clear to me that I could use double-columned paragraphed Bibles quite well.  They aren’t quite as good, perhaps, as verse-by-verse
Bibles for preaching, but their citation problems are nowhere near as bad as
the problems I used to have with the PSR.

In truth, I think that only Bibles that are both
single-column AND paragraphed really bother me. 
I also noticed issues during my review of the Crossway Legacy, which
shares that format.  It may be a
character-scanning issue.  It’s much
easier to overlook a verse number in a line that is 70-plus characters long
than in a line that is only 40 characters.

**TEACHING.** However, there are some things that
haven’t changed about my tastes.  A year
ago, I preferred small, light Bibles for teaching classes.  The same thing is still true.  I want a Bible that is small enough and light
enough that I don’t think about or even notice holding it for 45 minutes.

However, my opinions on cross-references did change a little
bit.  Before, I opined that I wanted a
full cross-reference suite in a teaching Bible. 
However, I haven’t really been needing that or using it when it was
present.  Generally, when somebody asks
me a question, I’m able to remember at least the chapter where the answer is
located, and from there, it’s only a few seconds before I find the verse in
question.

There have been two exceptions to this rule:  citations for Old-Testament prophecies quoted
in the New Testament, and the deluge of Old-Testament references in the book of
Revelation.  In the former case, I got
quite a bit of use out of what I’ve taken to calling “tweener Bibles”, like the
various editions of Crossway’s Thinline setting.  Even though they don’t have a burly column of
references, they do have those prophecy citations, which is the bulk of what I
want to find on the spot and can’t remember.

I needed, though, a full-on reference Bible to pull all the
references in Revelation.  A tweener
Bible wouldn’t do it, obviously, and even my Nelson ASV Teacher’s Edition
wasn’t up to the task.  However, I
spot-checked both the NASB reference suite (95,000 cross-references) and the
ESV reference suite (80,000 cross-references), and either one of those would
have given me what I wanted.

The moral of the story, then, is that tweener Bibles are
fine with me, except in certain very specific studies.

**STUDY.** The first real point I made here last
time had to do with readability.  In
practice, at this point in my life, readability hasn’t proven to be an
issue.  I might struggle with staring at
a compact Bible for hours at a time, but anything larger than that (including a
Pitt Minion, even) will work for me. 
Generally speaking, most Bibles shouldn’t give me readability problems,
but if they do, that’s a big problem.

In terms of format, I still don’t like study Bibles (I may
be philosophically opposed to them, actually), and I still don’t write in
wide-margin Bibles.  However, I get more
use out of reference Bibles in my office than I believed before.  ‘Truth is, I’m lazy.  If I’m slouched back in my chair, and I want
to run down a cross-reference, I’d much rather glance down at the Bible in my
lap than sit up and type it into the computer. 
Reference Bibles, then, still have some value there.

Finally, my opinions on layout have also shifted
somewhat.  Here too, double-column
paragraphed Bibles have proven nearly the equal of verse-by-verse Bibles in
accuracy of citation.

It’s just those single-column paragraphed Bibles that give
me fits.  In fact, paragraphed Bibles are
arguably helpful.  Perhaps as a
consequence of using so many paragraphed Bibles through the past year, I’ve
found myself studying and presenting Scriptures in larger chunks—paragraph units
instead of verse units.  Admittedly, I
still take issue with some of the paragraphing decisions in various Bibles, but
it’s not like I’ve ever been enthralled with our chapter-and-verse divisions
either.  Sometimes those break up the
text in exactly the wrong place too.  In
short, I’ve begun to find paragraphed Bibles more useful than not.
