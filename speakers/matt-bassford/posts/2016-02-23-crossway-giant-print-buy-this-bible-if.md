Buy this Bible if:

* You’re OK with using a brick of a Bible.  Of course, it is true that even the heaviest
  Bible will sit just fine on a table or in your lap.  My review copy is currently in the possession of an 80-year-old woman who can't weigh more than 100 pounds, and she loves the thing.
* You don’t mind Crossway’s genuine leather.
* You’re looking for a good all-rounder translation that will
  serve well for both reading and close study.
* You at least tolerate double-column Bibles.
* You need a Bible that Bartimaeus could have read even before
  Jesus healed him.  “Giant-Print” in the
  title of this one is no lie!
* You prefer or can accept paragraphed Bibles.
* You’re fine with only minimal cross-references.
* You want a Bible with good, opaque paper.
* You like line-matched Bibles.
* You don’t need margins big enough to write in.
* You don’t mind paying for one of the more expensive
  mass-market Bibles out there.

Don’t buy this Bible if:

* You want a Bible that is small, light, and easy to carry.
* You want a premium cover without having to go through a
  rebinder.
* You’re a single-column fanatic.
* You’re dead-set on a verse-by-verse Bible.
* You demand a full cross-reference suite.
* You have to have a Bible with margins wide enough to write
  your life story in.
* You think a Bible shouldn’t cost more than a pair of jeans
  at Wal-Mart.

It’s probably also worth comparing the Giant-Print to the
only other giant-print Bible I’ve reviewed, the Foundation Giant-Print
Reference Bible.  Because the former is a
Crossway product, it’s an ESV, and the latter, as a Foundation product, is an
NASB.  If you’re either an ESV or an NASB
fanatic, your choice is already made.

|  |
| --- |
|  |
| Crossway Giant-Print |

If, however, you’re translation-indifferent, I think the
Foundation GPR is better in the pulpit, and the Crossway GP is better for
reading.  The GPR is lighter, which makes
it easier to hold, and the verse numbers and references are in a larger font than
their equivalents in the GP, which makes them easier to locate when you’re
preaching/teaching and want to find verses in a hurry.  By contrast, the GP has better paper than the
GPR, and its font is bolder and easier to read. 
It’s the stronger choice for something like a daily Bible reading.  I would recommend either one, though, to a Christian who has trouble with close-in vision.
