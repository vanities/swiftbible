Buy these Bibles if:

* You want a matched set of Bibles that are good for different
  things.
* You’re a visual learner and remember verses by where they
  are on the page.
* You’re willing to have a hefty study/church Bible, but want
  a light carry Bible too.
* You want to use a translation that’s well-suited for close
  study, even if it takes a little more concentration to read.
* You prefer double-column settings.
* You like paragraphed Bibles.
* You’re OK with reading a smaller Bible with less opaque
  paper, but want a Bible with more opaque paper for note-taking.
* You need at least one Bible with space for serious
  note-taking.
* Having a high-quality Bible is important to you.
* You’re willing to shell out the money to buy two high-end
  Bibles, a total of $325 retail altogether ($115 for the Pitt Minion; $210 for
  the Wide- Margin).  Note, though, that
  both Bibles are available online for considerably less than retail.

Don’t buy these Bibles if:

* You want to use one Bible for everything.
* You want to use different Bibles, but aren’t really a visual
  learner.
* Bible size doesn’t matter much to you.
* You want a smooth-reading translation, even at the expense
  of textual nuance.
* You like single-column settings.
* You like verse-by-verse Bibles.
* You can't even tolerate Bibles with see-through paper for reading.
* You don’t write in your Bibles.
* You’re just fine using mass-market Bibles.
* You don’t want to spend that much on a matched set.  The Zondervan NASB Thinline and NASB
  Note-Taker’s are also a matched set in the NASB.  Poly-cover versions of each retail for a
  combined $83, and they will be even cheaper online.  Of course, the Zondervan Bibles aren’t nearly
  as well-constructed and won’t stand up as well to prolonged use, but that’s why
  they cost about a third as much.
