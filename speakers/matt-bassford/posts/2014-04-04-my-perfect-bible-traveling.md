The third
event in my Bible triathlon is travel. 
Sometimes, the distance traveled is short, as in a trip to a coffee shop
or somebody else’s home to have a personal Bible study.  At other times, I’m going on vacation for a week,
and I want a Bible to take with me and maybe even preach out of somewhere along
the way.  This calls for a Bible with
some unique attributes.  My perfect
travel Bible is as follows:

**SIZE.** Small, small, small.  Here, though, the reason isn’t because I
intend to hold it for long periods of time (though I do my daily Bible reading
on vacation too).  Instead, I want a
Bible that is small enough to stow somewhere convenient so that I can keep it
out of harm’s way.



As with many
of my convictions, this one is the result of bitter experience.  Maybe this indicates my own carelessness, but
a large Bible that I take on vacation has the life expectancy of the ensign on
a *Star Trek* away team.  10 years ago, I had a lovely genuine-leather
wide-margin side-column-reference NASB that my father gave me.  It was even of the legendary 2002 printing,
with a made-in-the-USA text block and delightfully opaque paper.  I used it for a few years, but then I took it on vacation.

Because the
Bible was too big to stick in the glove box, I just tossed it in the
floorboards of the back seat. About Day Four of the vacation, a cooler full of
melted ice upended in the backseat.  My
Bible sat in the stew for hours, until we reached our destination and I figured
out what had happened.  By that time, the
Bible was ruined, and I trashed it.

The next
year, I took my current Bible on vacation too. 
It was one of those forgettable glue-bound bonded-leather NKJV’s.  It deserved to die, and it did, crumpled
beneath luggage in the trunk, as I recall. 
After that, I stopped taking Bibles on vacation.  Instead, I sheepishly borrowed one from
whatever friends or family we happened to be visiting once we arrived.

However, last
year, a brainstorm struck.  I could take
one of my Bibles on vacation *as long as it
was small enough to store somewhere safe!*Genius, I know.  My test is
simple.  If a Bible is small enough to
stick in my glove box, along with the mileage logbook, insurance cards, lug nut
key, and whatever else, it’s a good travel Bible.  If it isn’t small enough, it is a bad travel
Bible, and it probably will not survive my next vacation.  As far as I am concerned, the fact that a
small Bible is easy to transport from study to study is merely a bonus.

**CROSS-REFERENCES.**My
rule about cross-references is this.  If
I’m taking a Bible somewhere where I might face unanticipated questions, I want
it to have cross-references.  If I’m not,
I don’t care.  If there is *anywhere* that some off-the-wall question
might come up, it is a personal study!  I
once spent a couple of years studying with an older gentleman whom I suspected
of sitting around thinking up the craziest questions he could, just to see if
he could stump me.  When I’m put on the
spot and desperately scrabbling for an answer, cross-references can help me
scrabble more effectively.
