*And she conceived
again and bore a son, and said, "Now I will praise the LORD."
Therefore she called his name Judah. Then she stopped bearing.*

--Genesis 29:35

There are few characters in the Bible who excite our
sympathy as much as Leah does.  Because
of the machinations of her father, Laban, she ended up married to a man who
plainly loved her sister Rachel, not her. 
Thereafter, her husband worked seven years to win her sister, and once
Jacob had claimed Rachel, Leah became the second-class wife.  We see an echo of Leah’s desperation and
despair in Genesis 30, when she had to buy from Rachel the right to lie with
her own husband.



Jacob didn’t love Leah, but she had one thing going for
her.  Rachel was barren, but Leah was
abundantly fruitful.  Better still, she
didn’t just bear children.  She bore
sons, which were beyond price in the ancient Middle East.  As many women in her situation would, Leah
hoped that because she presented her husband with such precious offspring, he
would begin to love her.  In fact, she
gave her first three sons, Reuben, Simeon, and Levi, names that expressed her
hope for a better marriage.  It didn’t
happen.  Even though Rachel gave Jacob no
sons, he still loved her instead of Leah.

Eventually, Leah realized that her plan was a failure.  Even if she gave Jacob his own basketball
team, he wasn’t about to feel any affection for her.  This must have been a bitter realization,
especially since she was pregnant with a fourth son when she came to it.  When that fourth son was born, she named him
Judah, a name that gave glory to God instead of expressing her longing for her
husband.  Jacob was never going to give
her joy, but God could and did.

Leah’s story strikes such a chord with us because her
problems are so familiar.  Even though we
thankfully don’t have to deal with polygamy, family strife is all too
common.  We have all seen situations
where a wife does not love his husband, or a husband his wife, or parents their
children, or children their parents.  To
the one who is unloved, this absence of affection is often a wound that won’t
stop bleeding.  Year after year, decade
after decade, they hope that something will happen that will change the heart
of their estranged family member, but often, no change ever comes.  Without that love in their lives, they never
feel whole.

God never promises to protect us from that situation and
those like them.  He never guarantees
that we will find earthly happiness in our relationships.  If we expect other people to fulfill and
complete us, we are expecting too much. 
What God does promise, though, is that when we are torn by conflict and
loss, we can find healing in Him.  Our
Father can comfort us and make us complete in a way that no human can
match.  Whether we are blessed with good
relationships or burdened with bad ones, we must learn to say along with Leah,
“This time, I will praise the Lord.”
