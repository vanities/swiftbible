The Thinline NASB has done more
traveling than most Bibles I review.  It
happened to be up in the rotation when my family and I went on our spring
vacation, so it has seen Indiana, Kentucky, and Tennessee.  Also, it has become my favorite Bible to grab
whenever I’m going somewhere and want a copy of the Good Book along, whether
that’s to visit a shut-in or watch my kids for an hour at the local
playground.  Here is what I have learned
about it:

**SIZE.** The NASB is a
slightly-smaller-than-normal full-size thinline.  As such, it’s definitely pushing the upper
size limits for a true travel Bible.  I
was able to keep it in the glove box of our minivan throughout the week of our
vacation, but I did have to move some other things around in the glove box to
make it fit neatly, and even at that, it sometimes emerged from captivity
looking a little bit smushed.  Because of
the quality of the Leonards’ binding, it has handled such abuse remarkably
well, but it’s still just a leetle bit too big to be entirely comfortable on
the road.  Of course, the same thing is
going to be true of any thinline larger than a Pitt Minion, so it’s not much of
a criticism.

Similarly, the NASB Thinline is
not perfectly adapted for going with my children to the park either.  I cannot and never will be able to put a
full-sized thinline in a pocket, it’s too large for either of my children to
hold onto comfortably, and yet there are times when I want to walk them across
the street holding both their hands.  I’ve
taken to awkwardly trapping the NASB Thinline beneath one arm.  Maybe I need to start carrying a purse to
keep my Bible in.

Of course, this isn’t a
particularly fair test.  Any Bible I
could keep in a pocket isn’t a Bible I would want to actually read.  In addition, I wrote all of my other reviews
so far in the depths of the Illinois winter, so none of the other Bibles I’ve
examined have faced this particular trial. 
Nonetheless, the point remains: 
the Thinline NASB isn’t a true compact.

One other thing about the
Thinline NASB and the park.  As obnoxious
as super-thin Bible paper is in other settings, it is most obnoxious of all
when you’re trying to read the thing outdoors and a breeze kicks up!

**CROSS-REFERENCES.** The
Thinline NASB is a text Bible, so of course it doesn’t have any cross-references
anywhere.  I don’t recall a time in my
use of it, though, when this posed a problem. 
Of course, I also haven’t been involved in any particularly intense
Bible studies, either, so that may play a role.

**CONCLUSION.**I rate the Thinline NASB at 4.5/5
as a traveler.  Overall, the Bible scores
an impressive 18/20 when the Leonard’s rebind is taken into account.
