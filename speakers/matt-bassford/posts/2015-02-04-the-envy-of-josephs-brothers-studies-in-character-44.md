*"Come therefore,
let us now kill him and cast him into some pit; and we shall say, 'Some wild
beast has devoured him.' We shall see what will become of his dreams!"*

--Genesis 37:20

In recent weeks, we’ve looked both at Jacob’s favoritism
toward Joseph and Joseph’s lack of wisdom in revealing his dreams to his
brothers.  Sadly, these things have their
predictable fruit.  They arouse such
hatred and envy in Joseph’s brothers that when Jacob sends Joseph to see how
they’re doing, they seize him with the intent to kill him.  Reuben, the eldest, urges them to put him in
a pit instead of shedding his blood, and they listen to him.  Reuben plans to rescue Joseph and restore him
to his father later, but before he is able to do so, the rest of the brothers
sell Joseph into slavery instead.  Then,
they dip his coat in blood so it looks like he’s been slain by a wild beast and
report the sad “news” to Jacob.



Certainly, selling one’s own brother into slavery is an evil
thing to do, but what makes this spectacle all the more sad is that Joseph’s
brothers didn’t recognize their own position. 
After all, long after the bones of these ten men have dwindled into
dust, we still remember them and their fame. 
What’s more, each brother had been guaranteed a posterity by God.  Every one of them had hundreds of thousands,
if not millions, of descendants.  Some of
those descendants became even more famous than the patriarchs themselves.  Levi was the ancestor of Moses, Aaron, and
all the high priests of Israel.  Judah
was the forebear of David, Solomon, and the Lord Jesus Himself.  In truth, the brothers had little actual
cause for envy, but they got so focused on what Joseph said he had been
promised that they forgot about themselves.

When we envy today, we often have the same problem.  We focus on what someone else has and we
don’t, while we forget about what they don’t have and we do.  We envy a co-worker for the ease with which
he climbs the corporate ladder, but we overlook the fact that he has a
miserable family life and we have a wonderful one.  We envy a sibling her good looks, but we
don’t consider the gift for singing that we have that she doesn’t.  In truth, God created us all different, with
different circumstances and different talents, but every one of us possesses
something of value.

Even if that were not true, even if some other person is
superior to us in every way imaginable, we still have no cause for envy.  Just like the patriarchs had God’s promise and
the knowledge that they were part of His people, we have the same thing.  That gift of God outweighs all of His other
gifts put together.  Our good looks and
our talents and our money will last only as long as the earth does, but our
relationship with Him will last forever. 
When we put things in perspective, every Christian is in the only
situation that is truly enviable.
