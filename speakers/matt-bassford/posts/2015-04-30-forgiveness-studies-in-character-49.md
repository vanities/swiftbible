*Then Joseph gave a
command to fill their sacks with grain, to restore every man's money to his
sack, and to give them provisions for the journey. Thus he did for them.*

--Genesis 42:25

One of the commonest of human temptations is the temptation
to seek revenge on those whom we believe have wronged us.  Stories of vengeance fill both history and
literature, and plenty of Bible characters got into the act too.  David was a man after God’s own heart, yet
some of his very last words on earth were his instructions to his son Solomon
to kill David’s enemies.



However, we don’t see this appetite for revenge in the
character of Joseph.  By the time Genesis
42 takes place, the worm has turned completely. 
Joseph, the former slave, is now the second most powerful man in Egypt,
and the brothers who sold him into slavery have not come to him in the midst of
a famine, seeking to buy grain.  However,
because Joseph has taken an Egyptian name and is dressed in the costume of
Egyptian nobility, his brothers don’t recognize him.

If Joseph is determined to exact his revenge, this is the
perfect opportunity.  Joseph is so
powerful and in such high favor with Pharaoh that the Egyptian monarch wouldn’t
care if he executed his brothers in public. 
If he’s feeling retributive, he can cast them into the pits in Pharaoh’s
dungeon where he spent several miserable years. 
Joseph, of course, doesn’t completely rise to the challenge.  Instead of revealing his identity, he lies to
his brothers and puts them through a series of tests to determine whether their
character has changed.  However,
throughout this testing process, his desire to do good for them, despite the
evil that they had done him, does not waver. 
When they attempt to buy grain from him, he instructs his servants to
return their money to them in the sacks of grain.  Joseph rises above the desire for revenge.

As Christians, we need to have the same generous
spirit.  I once heard marriage defined as
“An unlimited series of opportunities to be forgiving,” and the same
description applies to life generally. 
In our lives, lots and lots of people are going to wrong us.  Some of them are going to wrong us
badly.  At times like those, the people
of the world love to retaliate.  They
return evil for evil because they figure the other guy deserves it.  However, we must rise above the cycle of
reprisal.  We need to learn to leave
vengeance in the hands of God.

We can do this for the same reason Joseph could.  As he had already seen, there was no wrong
that God could not right, no wound that God could not heal.  Joseph had no need to take revenge because it
was impossible for any human to hurt him in a meaningful way.  The same is true for us.  None of our enemies can separate us from the
love of God, and as long as we maintain that connection, our lives are
basically all right.  We must leave
vengeance to the vulnerable and love our enemies instead.
