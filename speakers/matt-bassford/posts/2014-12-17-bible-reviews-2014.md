Now that we've come nigh unto the end of 2014, I thought it would be useful to go back and collect links to all the Bible reviews I've posted during the year.  If you haven't caught all these, now would be a great time to go back and look at them.

However, even if you saw all these when I first posted them, there's still a reason to do a click-through.  Since the beginning, I've been blessed to work with Brian Meyer, who is both a Bible geek and one of the most talented photographers I've ever met.  I don't know one end of a camera from the other, but Brian spends hours meticulously (and "meticulous" is a good word for him generally) photographing all my review Bibles and then editing the results.

However, Brian has been dissatisfied from Day One.  Despite all of his painstaking work, none of his photos were appearing on the blog like he thought they should.  This week, he finally figured out why.  Blogger has an auto-rebalance setting that was turning all the whites in the photos a dingy gray.  Once he told me that, I turned the auto-rebalance off and re-uploaded his photos.  So, if you're tired of Bible pics taken with a cell-phone camera and want to see some truly gorgeous work, take a look!

As I look at this list, a few things stand out.  The first is that I will shell Bibles with glued bindings.  That's pretty much the Holman LPCR's only problem, but the Bible got downgraded severely in several areas because of it.

The second is that I come across as not much of a fan of big Bibles.  Size cost the Foundation SCR and, especially, the Foundation GPR some serious points.  In my scoring system, portability makes up 25 percent of a Bible's total score.  Despite its pedestrian score, though, I recommend the GPR more frequently than I do any other Bible, generally to Christians with failing eyesight.

I'm not going to go through and re-score all these reviews, but I think this does highlight a flaw with my rating system.  With the new year, I'm going to run a series of posts in which I revisit my Bible- evaluation criteria, and giving big Bibles their due is definitely something I'll look at.
