My call for post requests yesterday yielded, predictably,
some faux requests from friends who wanted to give me a hard time, but also
some people who genuinely wanted answers to a particular question.  Of the latter, the most touching request came
from Anonymous, who asked, “How to have a christian marriage in the modern day.
Please.”

Funny how much desperation you can put into a single line of
text, isn’t it?  There are few things
that hurt as badly as a marriage that isn’t working.  I wish that I knew the magic words to solve
all such problems, but I don’t.  However,
I will share what wisdom I have accumulated in the hope that it will help.



**Devotion to Christ**

For starters, a Christian marriage (which I understand to
mean “a marriage that fulfills God’s plan for marriage and glorifies Him”)
requires two Christian spouses.  There
are two ways in which this can go wrong.

First, you can have a marriage in which one or both spouses
do not even claim to be Christians. 
Sometimes, this is unavoidable (as when a spouse forsakes the Lord after
marriage), but it is something that unmarried Christians should seek to
avoid.  I don't think it's a sin for a Christian to marry a non-Christian, but it does often turn out to be a bad idea.  Marriage is the most intimate
relationship on earth.  It’s hard enough
for husband and wife to be of one mind and one heart even when they share the
same fundamental values.  When they do
not (as a Christian spouse and a non-Christian spouse cannot), conflict is
inevitable.

Second, you can have a marriage in which both spouses claim
to be Christians, but one or both are failing badly in their commitment to the
Lord.  Through the years, I’ve seen a
number of marriage gurus offer up marital advice to Christians that is almost
entirely Scripture-free.  Certainly, some
of that can be worthwhile, but I think it misses the point.

Marriage is not something that is separate from
discipleship.  Instead, marriage should
contain the highest expression of our discipleship.  The Christian ideals of humility, patience,
service, and self-sacrificing love must define the way that we behave in our
marriages.  When both spouses practice
these things, the marriage will prosper. 
Success in marriage is not the result of marrying The One.  It’s the result of a shared determination to
imitate Christ.

This determination remains important even when the other
spouse shows no interest in following the Lord’s example.  One spouse’s failure to meet their
obligations does not relieve the other spouse of their obligations.  Even if my wife does not respect me and
submit to me, I’m still responsible for loving her as Christ loved the
church.  Conversely, even if I don’t love
her and give myself for her, she’s still responsible for respecting and
submitting to me.  When we don’t think
that way, when we angrily refuse to treat our spouses right because they aren’t
treating us right, we forget that our primary responsibility is not to one
another, but to God.

**Commitment to the
Marriage**

The other way to have a Christian marriage is to stay
married.  Good statistics on divorce are
hard to come by, but anecdotally, all of us know that it’s quite common in
American society, to the point where increasingly, people don’t bother to get
married at all (which causes all sorts of other disasters).

This is exactly the opposite of the way that God wants things
to be.  When we consider Jesus’
discussion of marriage in Matthew 19, it’s easy to jump to the application in
19:9, but the heart of what Jesus is saying actually appears in 19:4-6:

And He answered and said, "Have you not read that He
who created them from the beginning MADE THEM MALE AND FEMALE, and said, 'FOR
THIS REASON A MAN SHALL LEAVE HIS FATHER AND MOTHER AND BE JOINED TO HIS WIFE,
AND THE TWO SHALL BECOME ONE FLESH'? So they are no longer two, but one flesh.
What therefore God has joined together, let no man separate."

God wants our marriages to last for as long as we do, and
the foundation of a lifelong marriage must be a husband and a wife who are
determined to remain married for life.

This is not always going to be easy.  In fact, it hasn’t been for my wife and
me.  When our daughter Macy died in
September 2008, the year that followed was very hard on us and very hard on our
marriage.  Both of us were so caught up in
our own pain that we had great difficulty finding the emotional resources to
reach out to one another.  It’s probably
true that if both of us had not been committed to the Lord, we would have
separated.

The thing about rough patches, though, is that they usually
don’t stay rough.  It may well prove
impossible to overcome the betrayal of adultery (which I think figures into the
Matthew 19:9 exception), but with time, other problems will generally
diminish.  As Lauren and I recovered
emotionally from our loss, our marriage recovered too, to the point that our
marriage today is a happy one.  I know
other couples that say the same thing. 
If you are willing to work through the hard times, you can make the
marriage work.

The original question specified “in the modern day”, but to
be honest, I don’t think time period has a whole lot to do with the success of
marriage.  It’s true that divorce isn’t
as stigmatized today as it was 75 years ago, but the basis of a healthy
marriage now is the same as the basis of a healthy marriage then.  The problem is that increasingly, people are
no longer trying to find that basis in the word of God.  If our marriage start with the Bible and go
from there (which I’m well aware is easy to say and hard to do!), they will
thrive as God intended.
