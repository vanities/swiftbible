When I decided to become a preacher in 2004, my father
responded by buying me every reference book that he thought I might possibly
ever need.  In most cases, his gifts have
proven quite useful.  I’m pretty
old-school, so I still use the hardbound ISBE, interlinear and Greek lexicon
that I have from him.

Sometimes, however, his presents went wide of the mark.  Most notably, he decided that because I now
was going to be doing a lot of Bible-reading in the pulpit, I needed Bibles
with large, indeed with giant, print. 
Now, it is certainly true that my eyesight is rotten, as in
can’t-see-lightning-without-my-glasses-on rotten.  However, I can read and do close work just
fine.  It’s the details beyond the reach
of my arm that escape me.  As a result, I
didn’t have a use for my new collection of giant-print Bibles, even the nice
genuine-leather Foundation NASB.



Time passed, I amassed a number of other Bibles, and the
Foundation GPR languished on my shelf. 
However, when I decided to start reviewing Bibles, I determined to
review the GPR too.  After all, the poor
thing had a genuine-leather cover and a sewn binding.  Surely it didn’t deserve to be ignored!  As a result, I took it down from its shelf,
dusted it off, and put it through its paces for two weeks.  The rest of this review explains what I
learned.

**VITAL STATISTICS**

Translation:  New
American Standard Bible, Updated (1995)

Cover:  Genuine
Leather

Binding:  Sewn

Trim Size:  6 ½” x 9
¼”

⅝”

Layout: 
Verse-by-Verse

Words of Christ:  Red

Cross-References:  Yes

Ribbon Marker:  1

As is true of the other Foundation genuine-leather covers
I’ve examined, the cover of the GPR is a respectable example of the breed.  It’s not the same as the cover for my
Foundation side-column reference.  The
leather has the same lightly stamped grain, but there are several
differences.  This leather doesn’t feel
quite as thick, the cover has an impressed line running around the perimeter of
the text block (which is a nice detail), and the endpapers are made out of
stiff paper rather than leatherette.  I’m
not thrilled with that choice of endpaper material, but I’ve had good success
with paper-endpaper Bibles in the past, and the endpapers on the GPR don’t feel
poorly attached.

Because of these various attributes, the cover of the GPR is
very, very flexible.  I haven’t used the
Bible much or done anything to break the cover in, but when I hold it in my
hand, the Bible overflows it nicely on both sides.  Part of this is simply the result of the
dimensions of the text block.  A Bible
this big is always going to be more flexible than a smaller edition.



Part of it, though, comes from the quality of the
cover.  As far as I can tell, Foundation
doesn’t make a premium-leather GPR, and really, they shouldn’t.  At the genuine-leather level, the cover of
this Bible is already doing everything I want the cover to do.  It’s not like a lot of other genuine-leather
covers, which are stiff, shiny, and repellent. 
Instead, it’s pleasant to hold and use. 
Though I haven’t used it a great deal, I imagine the cover would prove
to be durable as well. Foundation is to be commended for producing a Bible with
these characteristics at such a modest price point.

Within, it is quickly obvious that the GPR possesses that
pearl of great price, the sewn binding. 
The Bible doesn’t seem to be as tightly bound as many Bibles are, so it
will happily flop open widely enough to reveal its stitching in any number of
locations.  Also, the GPR opens flat in
Genesis and Revelation, and it probably would have done so right out of the
box, back in 2007.  The only grievance I
have against the text block is some light to moderate cockling, but even this
is less noticeable than it is in the other genuine-leather Bibles I own.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhyssemK49jqo0AwugCrdOHUZG52rKm88onmyLJyKvvWJAnzkJ4KlnLTP1GrI90kHiQeFTPvcQblnOdWq1Ewn6U9V1ifMTlk8yNZZqSxLaly748US0_hDyPkfUvatBI9kuo5AQjjMQMQQ/s1600/PF4.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhyssemK49jqo0AwugCrdOHUZG52rKm88onmyLJyKvvWJAnzkJ4KlnLTP1GrI90kHiQeFTPvcQblnOdWq1Ewn6U9V1ifMTlk8yNZZqSxLaly748US0_hDyPkfUvatBI9kuo5AQjjMQMQQ/s1600/PF4.jpg)

All in all, this is a solidly put-together Bible, which is
all the more impressive when we consider that it is available new online for
less than $40.  The only cause for
complaint that I have is that when I buy a genuine-leather Bible, I want it to
have leatherette endpapers.  Otherwise,
the paper endpapers may well turn into a failure point, particularly in a
larger Bible.  Having said that, though,
I still give this Bible a 4/5 in physical form.
