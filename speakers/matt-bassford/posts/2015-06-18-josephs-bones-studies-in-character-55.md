*By faith Joseph, when
he was dying, made mention of the departure of the children of Israel, and gave
instructions concerning his bones.*

*--Hebrews 11:22*

From the beginning of his life onward, Joseph is one of the
most spiritually impressive characters in the entire book of Genesis, and
nowhere is this more evident than in the content of his dying request.  At this point in time, Joseph has lived in
Egypt for perhaps as long as 80 or 90 years. 
In that stay, he has risen to become the second most powerful man in the
kingdom, after only Pharaoh himself. 
He’s been able to use this power to save his entire extended family from
famine, first by giving them grain and then by inviting the entire clan to
sojourn in Egypt.



However, despite this very Egypt-centric life, Joseph’s
final thoughts were neither about Egypt nor his own personal success.  Instead, he was focused on God and on his
promises.  Like all of the descendants of
Abraham, Joseph knew of God’s promise that someday, Abraham’s children would
inherit the land of Canaan.  For three
generations, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob had lived as wanderers in that land,
never owning more than an acre or two of it. 
Now, they’d been driven entirely out of the land by famine.

A reasonable man might assume that God’s plans had changed,
that the destiny of Israel now lay in the rich land of Egypt, where they had
such powerful allies.  Joseph knew
better.  In his 110 years of life, he’d
seen more than enough wild swings of fortune to know that the nation that
seemed so welcoming now could easily turn hostile later.  He knew, in fact, that the only certain thing
was God’s word.  He told his descendants
that one day God surely would bring them into the land of Canaan, and when that
day came, he wanted them to take his bones with them, so that he could at least
be buried in the promised land.  It took
400 years for this to happen, but in the time of Moses, the Israelites took
Joseph’s bones to Canaan with them, and everything happened just as he had
predicted.

Today, it is important that we also learn the faith of
Joseph.  Just like Egypt could have been
so enticing to him, the sinful world in which we live can be so enticing to
us.  Satan works in many ways, but his
ultimate goal is always to convince us that we should hope for nothing beyond
that which we see, that it is foolish to trust in the promises of God.  Once we believe him, once we start living for
this life only, we lose all hope of the life to come.  Instead, like Joseph, we must recognize that
God plays the long game.  It is possible
that the Lord will return in our lifetimes, but it is equally possible that we
will die before we ever see Jesus.  Once
again, the only certain thing is that God will fulfill His promises, and we
must trust in Him accordingly.
