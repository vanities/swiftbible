They say that the eyes are
the windows of the soul, but in Goldia’s case, I think the true window was her
cooking.  Everything I ever saw her cook
or bake was very simple, very traditional. 
Nothing came out of her kitchen that my great-grandparents wouldn’t have
recognized.  And yet, all that simple
food was as well prepared as it possibly could be.  If you were eating something she made, you
didn’t want anything else.

That, I think, is the way
that Goldia lived her whole life.  Jeremiah
6:16 reads, “Thus says the LORD, ‘Stand by the ways and see and ask for the
ancient paths, where the good way is, and walk in it; and you will find rest
for your souls.’”  I think that’s an apt
description.  She took simple,
traditional values and lived up to them as well as she knew how.  I don’t think there’s anything that Goldia
believed that my great-grandparents wouldn’t have recognized either.



People have been following
her way of life for thousands of years. 
Love your friends and neighbors. 
Love the people you go to church with. 
Love your family.  Love your
husband.  Love your God.  That’s all as simple as it can be.  My four-year-old can understand every word of
it.

There are people out there
who would sneer at Goldia’s values. 
They’d accuse her of living her whole life as a thoughtless drone.  I don’t think, though, that the simplicity of
her beliefs shows her thoughtlessness.  I
think it shows her wisdom.  She was
somebody who had this life thing figured out. 
She knew how to do it right, and she did it right.

I think we see the value of
her approach by the results she got. 
Jesse and Goldia were always careful with their money, but I don’t get
the impression that they ever had a whole lot of it.  Where they were rich, though, was in the love
of others.  Even after Jesse’s passing,
I’ve seen few people who were loved as well as Goldia was.  Without exception, her children and
grandchildren were and are devoted to her. 
Everybody she went to church with, and I suspect everybody who knew her
in the community too, loved her deeply.  In
the ten years I’ve lived here, I’ve never heard anyone utter an unkind word
about Goldia Fugett.

That love continues to show
up, even now that she’s gone.  For
instance, as often happens, the family asked me to arrange for the singers at
the funeral.  Often, that’s no small task.  People are busy, they can’t get away from
work for a whole hour in the middle of the morning, they’d like to help, but
really their hands are tied.

Not this time.  Instead, people who didn’t have time made
time, because it was for Goldia.  In
fact, I had people coming to me and asking me if they could help, because they
loved her and missed her and wanted to do something.  I’ve never seen that before.  Make no mistake about it; this was a special
lady!

Of course, as moving as all
this is, if it were not for one thing, Goldia would have lived her life in
vain.  But for her love and faithfulness
to God, she would be dead now and dead forever. 
However, because she lived and died as a faithful Christian, the hope
that she has is certain.  As John 11:25
reads, “Jesus said to her, ‘I am the resurrection and the life; he who believes
in Me will live even if he dies, and everyone who lives and believes in Me will
never die. Do you believe this?’”  Goldia
believed that, and she lived it.

Because she did, today, we
don’t have to mourn as do those who have no hope.  We know that Goldia’s spirit lives on in a
much better place than this one, where she has been reunited with the man she
loved all her life, where she will wait until the day the Lord returns and her
body too shall rise.

Goldia was a simple woman,
and her life teaches us a simple lesson: 
you get what you give.  All of us
want to be loved like Goldia was loved, but that’s not something we get by
wanting.  It’s something we get by
loving.  The love that we feel for Goldia
is only the return of the love that she showered on us.  If we want others to love us, we have to love
them first and prove that love like she proved it.

You get what you give.  It’s true in this life, and it’s true in the
next life too.  Goldia devoted her life
to her Lord, and in return, He will give her a far greater gift, the gift of
eternal life.  Jesus Himself warns us in
Matthew 16:25, “Whoever wishes to save his life will lose it; but whoever loses
his life for My sake will find it.” 
Goldia understood that, just as she understood so many things.  She surrendered her life to Jesus, so that
the love that we see so clearly in her is really His.  If she wants anything for us right now, it is
this:  that we walk that same ancient
path that she walked, so that one day, all of us
will follow her Lord to where she has gone.
