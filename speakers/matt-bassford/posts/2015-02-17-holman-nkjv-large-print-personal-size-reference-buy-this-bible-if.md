**Buy This Bible If. .
.**

* You’re looking for a moderate, hand-sized Bible.
* You prefer Bibles with low-premium leather covers.
* You’re looking for a fairly smooth-reading translation that
  is still suitable for close study.
* You like double-column Bibles.
* You want or need large print.
* You prefer paragraphed Bibles to the verse-by-verse variety.
* You like some cross-references but don’t need a full
  cross-reference suite.
* You’re fine with using Bibles with relatively thin,
  translucent paper.
* You like Bibles with line-matching.
* You don’t need a lot of margin space to write in.
* You’re willing to spend a moderate amount of money for a
  fairly nice Bible.

**Don’t Buy This Bible
If. . .**

* You prefer small Bibles that feel like a paperback in the
  hand and slip easily into a purse or coat pocket.
* You demand high-premium covers (constructed from calfskin or
  goatskin and edge-lined)
* You want a translation based on more modern manuscript
  discoveries, as opposed to a translation based on the Textus Receptus.
* You’re a single-column aficionado.
* You are only able to use Bibles with true giant print.
* You like Bibles that start each verse on a new line.
* You want a Bible with a full suite of 80,000-90,000
  cross-references.
* You expect your Bibles to have opaque paper with little
  show-through.
* You write copious notes or even sermon outlines in the
  margins of your Bible.
* You shudder at the thought of spending $60 (more like $40
  online) on a Bible.  Note, though, that
  there are polyurethane-cover versions of this Bible available for $20
  retail.  Also, if you can’t scrape
  together $40 to buy a Bible you need, you are either very poor, or you need to
  re-evaluate your financial priorities.
