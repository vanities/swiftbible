*"Now therefore,
please let your servant remain instead of the lad as a slave to my lord, and
let the lad go up with his brothers.”*

--Genesis 44:33

Throughout Genesis
44, Joseph continues to test his brothers. 
Last time, when they came to buy grain from him, he told them that he
wouldn’t sell them any more grain unless they also brought Benjamin, Joseph’s
only full brother, with them.  Even
though Jacob at first refuses to send Benjamin away from him, the famine is so
severe that he’s left with no other choice. 
Benjamin and the rest of the brothers return to Joseph, who treats them
well, but as they prepare to depart, Joseph has his steward plant a silver cup
in Benjamin’s sack of grain.  The
brothers are so sure of their innocence that they declare that whoever has the
cup shall become Joseph’s slave.  Then,
to their chagrin, Joseph’s steward finds the cup in Benjamin’s sack.



At this point, Judah steps up.  He tells Joseph that if Benjamin remains
behind, it will literally kill his father Jacob.  Judah declares that even though he is
innocent, he will serve as a slave in Benjamin’s place, if Joseph will only
release Benjamin.  This declaration is one
of the most striking examples of spiritual growth in the entire Bible.  After all, it was Judah’s idea to sell Joseph
into slavery in the first place.  Now,
though, Judah values his family so much more that he would rather be enslaved
himself than see them injured.  It is
this example of family affection that spurs Joseph to cry out and reveal
himself.

Judah’s conduct should hearten us in two main ways.  First, it proves that our families and the
other people around us can change.  When
Judah was young, he was a villain, no doubt about it.  However, the courageous offer that he makes
as an older man shows a heart filled with self-sacrificing love.  That heart emerged because Joseph was willing
to give him a second chance to prove himself. 
Likewise, sometimes the people in our lives do foolish and evil things
and need second chances if they are to prove themselves, and those are second
chances we need to be willing to extend.

This is true even of ourselves.  In this day and age especially, we often have
an immutable view of our character. 
We’re unfaithful, or a drunk, or hot-tempered, and that’s who we are,
and we can’t change.  Because of this
perspective, it’s easy for us to give up on ourselves and bow our heads to the
sin we don’t believe we can defeat. 
Thankfully, there is more hope for all of us than that.  With the Lord’s help, there is no foe we
cannot overcome in our struggle for righteousness.  The same sin that overpowers us now may be
the sin we easily defeat ten years from now. 
We can grow like Judah did.  We
need only possess the heart and the determination to do better.
