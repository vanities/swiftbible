*Now Laban had gone to
shear his sheep, and Rachel had stolen the household idols that were her
father's.*

*--Genesis 31:19*

By this point in Genesis 31, Jacob has recognized that he
must flee Laban and his household.  He
secretly consults with Rachel and Leah, and they determine that they are going
to side with their husband (and their children) instead of with their
father.  When Laban leaves the camp,
Jacob and his wives hurriedly gather all of their possessions and take off for
Palestine.  Rachel takes advantage of the
confusion to steal Laban’s idols too.



To the people of Jacob’s time, this would have been a big
deal.  Laban would not have believed that
his gods were omnipresent.  Instead,
their existence was focused on their idols. 
That meant that the physical statue of the idol itself was like a
rabbit’s foot on steroids.  If you
possessed it, your god would listen to you and help you.  If you didn’t, you were out of luck.  When Rachel stole Laban’s idols, she wasn’t
just stealing a piece of sacred artwork. 
She was stealing Laban’s connection with the sacred.  She was taking his supernatural favor and
appropriating it for herself.

This gives us a marvelous snapshot of where Rachel was
spiritually.  Like Ruth, she would have
recognized that by marrying Jacob, she had taken his God to be her God.  She had seen God bless him in ways that
coincidence could not explain.  And yet,
she wasn’t willing to leave her father’s household idols behind.  She knew that she and her family were headed
into an uncertain future, and during that time, she wanted not just the help of
the God of Abraham, but the help of idols too.

Even if we have never bowed down before a graven image, we
can still have problems with household idols. 
These problems arise when we trust in anything or anyone but God, when
we cling to the same coping strategies we would use even if we weren’t
Christians.  Perhaps we trust in our
wealth and enjoy dwelling on the security we have accumulated for ourselves in
our bank accounts.  Perhaps we trust in
food or alcohol and seek comfort in those things when life becomes
stressful.  Perhaps we trust in our
entertainment, and lose ourselves in books or movies or the Internet to escape
from reality.

There is an infinite number of household idols we could seek
out, but they all raise the same question. 
In times of trouble, why are we turning there instead of to God?  Is there power in those things, that they can
do more than give us the illusion of escape? 
Is God inadequate, that we need to supplement His power?  Ultimately, it’s a question of faith.  Do we believe that God is all the help we
need, or don’t we?  If we have the faith
we should, we will be able to leave the household idols behind for God.
