*By faith Abraham
obeyed when he was called to go out to the place which he would receive as an
inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going.*

--Hebrews 11:8

Most Christians, and indeed most people, are quite
conservative by nature.  We don’t like a
lot of chaos and unpredictability in our lives. 
We like things pretty much as they are, and we would prefer for the
quiet round of our lives to continue unabated, free from turmoil and confusion.  Because this is so, we dread the
unknown.  Once we venture into it, we
can’t predict what’s going happen to us, and if right now is pretty good, the
unknown is likely to be worse.



Abraham was doubtless a man with a nature much like ours,
yet into the unknown was precisely where God asked him to go.  As far as we can tell from the terse account
of Genesis, Abraham had been born in Ur, gone to Haran with his father when he
was still a young man, and lived there until the day God spoke to him.  At this point in his life, Abraham is 75
years old, well past the restlessness of youth. 
Just like us, he probably had no higher ambition than to stay where he
was and do what he was doing.

Nonetheless, when God told him to go, he went.  He packed up his wife, his nephew, and his
possessions, and headed out.  God hadn’t
even told him they were going to Canaan yet, so he likely had nothing more than
a vague direction to go by.  As important
as all the other considerations were to Abraham, God was more important.

There are times in our lives when we too come to Abraham
moments.  We’re absorbed in our daily
round, lost in our comforting, familiar routines, when we recognize that God
wants us to do something completely different. 
Every Christian not raised in the church has had an Abraham moment, when
they see that they have to leave behind the religious tradition of their
families and embark on a strange new course with a strange new church.  As Christians, we have them when we study our
lives in the mirror of the word and find ourselves wanting.  We see that we aren’t loving other Christians
as God expects, or working in the church as He desires, or seeking the lost as
He commands.

What we do in those Abraham moments defines our
destiny.  We can plunge into the scary
unknown, risking all but trusting in God, or we can shrink back, choosing to
remain with what is familiar and safe. 
It’s in moments like these, when the voice of caution echoes so loudly
in our hearts, that the promise of God must echo still louder.  If we obey, He will not forsake us.  He will defend us.  He will take care of us.  He will, and only through the obedience of
faith can we come to Him.
