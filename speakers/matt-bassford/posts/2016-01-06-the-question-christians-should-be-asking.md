The Internet is not usually a place for profundity, but
there are exceptions.  [This post](http://qz.com/584874/you-probably-know-to-ask-yourself-what-do-i-want-heres-a-way-better-question/) is one of them.  It contains some
language I can’t endorse, but other than that, I agreed all the way through.

The author’s point is simple.  Talking about what we want is
meaningless.  Everybody would like to be
rich, in good shape, happily married, and with a great family life.  We don’t have all of those things, though,
because we aren’t willing to pay the price to get there.  Instead, opines the author, rather than
asking, “What do I want?”, we should ask, “What am I willing to endure to get it?”

You want to be rich? 
Outstanding!  You’d better be
willing to sign up for lots of hard work, long hours, and sleepless
nights.  You want to have a great
marriage?  Stellar!  Start getting ready for a life filled with
patience, forgiveness, and self-sacrifice. 
If we refuse to embrace those things, it proves that we don’t really
want the goal either.

The author does not appear to be religious, but I believe
his point applies to the things of the spirit even more than it does in any
other area.  This, of course, starts with
Jesus.  From before the foundation of the
world, our Lord had the goal in mind of redeeming mankind from sin.  What a great goal!  Who (outside of the devil) wouldn’t want to
bless all the nations of the earth?

The price, however, was every bit as great as the prize
was.  In order to purchase our salvation,
Jesus had to give up more than anyone had ever given up and suffer more than
anyone ever suffered.  How terrible it
must have been for One who was sinless to bear all the sins of His people on
the cross!

Jesus understood that going in.  As we see in passages like Mark 14:32-42,
even His great soul was anguished by the thought of what He would have to
endure.  However, with uttermost
conviction, uttermost self-discipline, and uttermost love, He rose up and paid
the price.  He wanted to save us, and He
proved it by what He did.

Christ is our example and our pattern.  The essence of being a disciple of Jesus is
walking as He walked.  We are called to
imitate His love and self-sacrifice.

Somewhere along the line, though, this message doesn’t get
through to all too many Christians.  They
want to go to heaven.  Who doesn’t?  Even people who have never set foot in a
church building in their lives want to go to heaven.

However, an awful lot of self-described disciples don’t ask
what they will have to accept in order to go there.  His grace doesn’t make it easy for us to
inherit eternal life.  It makes it
possible, and those are two very different things.  It is with difficulty that even the righteous
are saved.

The true answer to the question reminds me of the joke about
how one day the government is going to simplify our taxes.  They’re going to send us all a 1040 with one
blank on it.  In the blank you put your
income, and that’s also what you owe in taxes. 
Simple!

Likewise, the form that Jesus hands us is very simple.  It has one blank on it too, a blank that’s
labeled “Everything you have”.  That’s
what we have to give.

Our brethren in the first century understood this.  The more I revisit the New Testament’s
accounts of what they said and did, the more I am awed by their courage, faith,
and strength of character.  They were
giants, men who stood unflinchingly by what they believed even as they were
being stoned to death.  Their longing for
heaven was so great that they were willing to surrender, and indeed did
surrender, everything to get there.

There can be no compromise, either for them or for us.  We are called to, among other things:

* Come out from among them and be separate.
* Count all things as loss for the sake of knowing Christ.
* Hate our father and mother and wife and children and
  brothers and sisters.
* Cut off our right hands if they cause us to sin.
* Put to death the deeds of the body.
* Make no provision for the flesh and its lusts.
* Enter the kingdom of God through many tribulations.
* Suffer shame for the name of Christ.
* Fill up what is lacking in the afflictions of Christ.
* Resist to the point of shedding our blood in the struggle
  against sin.
* Lose our lives for the sake of Christ.

That’s the price.  If
we aren’t willing to pay it, we don’t really want to go to heaven.
