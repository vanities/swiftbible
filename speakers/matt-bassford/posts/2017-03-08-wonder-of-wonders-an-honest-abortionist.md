Last month, my wife tipped me off about [this profile](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/02/08/magazine/willie-j-parker-changed-his-mind-about-abortion.html) in *The New York Times Magazine.* Its subject is Willie Parker, an
abortionist from Birmingham, AL who is publishing a book on his experiences in
April.

Clearly, nobody who performs abortions for a living is going
to be tops on my favorite-people list, but I have to hand it to the man.  At least he’s straightforward about what he’s
doing.



For years, advocates of legalized abortions have insisted
that while still in its mother’s womb, a baby is not a baby, but merely a
fetus.  Apparently, rather than unique
DNA, a heartbeat, or brain function, it is the passage through six inches of
birth canal that endows the fetus with personhood (except in cases of
partial-birth abortion, when, well, never mind).

Personally, I’ve always found this distinction risible, on
both religious and scientific grounds.  I
think more and more people are finding the latter persuasive as advances in
technology reveal more and more about children *in utero*.  It’s tough to
argue with 3D ultrasounds.  We call
beings with human DNA and human faces “people”.

From time to time, I will read an article by some abortion
provider that owns this.  They will
straight-up admit that abortion kills (it’s apparently tough to deny when you
deal with the carnage on a daily basis), but then go on to explain how that’s
really OK or at least necessary.

Mr. Parker is part of this straightforward crowd.  Here’s his argument:

“Here’s the thing: Life is a process, not an event. If I thought I was killing
a person, I wouldn’t do abortions. A fetus is not a person; it’s a human entity.
In the moral scheme of things, I don’t hold fetal life and the life of a woman
equally. I value them both, but in the precedence of things, when a woman comes
to me, I find myself unable to demote her aspirations because of the
aspirations that someone else has for the fetus that she’s carrying.”

Well, then.

In Internet discourse, it’s considered bad form to compare
someone with whom you disagree to Hitler. 
Such arguments are hyperbolic and emotional, and they show that you
don’t have a logical leg to stand on. 
They even have a name:  the *reductio ad Hitlerum*.  It’s got [a Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductio_ad_Hitlerum) and everything.  Because I want to be taken seriously, I try
not to compare people to Hitler.

However, isn’t taking a human being, slapping a name on them
that makes them subhuman, and then killing them—isn’t that what Hitler
did?  Wasn’t a key part of the logic that
sent Jews, Poles, Gypsies, et al off to the concentration camps their
designation as *Untermenschen*?  Once you strip somebody of their personhood,
you can do just about anything to them that you want to.

From Mr. Parker, we learn that life isn’t an event, it’s a
process.  By this, I think he means that
people accumulate value as they live. 
Personhood isn’t binary; it’s a sliding scale.  Babies in the womb are so far down this scale
that, though alive, they don’t qualify as people; they’re just “human entities”
(the “human” in the phrase fulfilling about the same purpose as “*menschen*” in “*Untermenschen*”, I think).  As
a result, Mom, who has attained fullness of personhood, is allowed to kill the
little human entity because it interferes with her aspirations.  Mom needs a little *Lebensraum*, if you will.

That is logic that you can use to justify nearly anything
you do to another human being.  Let’s
start with the low-hanging fruit: 
infanticide.  A newborn isn’t a
whole lot different from a fetus in terms of the personhood it has had the
opportunity to accumulate (once again, that trip down the birth canal wasn’t
all that transformative).  Why doesn’t
Mom have the right to deposit this noisy, obnoxious human entity in the trash
can in the ladies’ room and exit to pursue her aspirations undemoted?

On the other end of the scale, how about euthanasia?  I can make a pretty strong argument that the
terminally ill have lost a meaningful amount of personhood.  They certainly aren’t as useful to society as
they were.  They can’t do much anymore.  Their aspirations have shrunk to things like
“I want to die without hurting too much.” 
For that matter, many of them no longer retain their full
faculties.

It’s expensive to care for these people.  It’s hard. 
They demote the aspirations of their caregivers for *years*, sometimes!  My sister
would have a much less harried life if she weren’t caring for my terminally ill
father.  So. . . maybe it’s time to nudge
all these sick old people from the “person” category to the “human entity”
category and dispose of them accordingly. 
We have good drugs now.  It
wouldn’t even hurt very much—which is more than the abortionist can say.

The generalized form of both these arguments is this:  people in power have the right to kill the
powerless so long as they can come up with a rationalization for doing so.  I can, I want to, therefore I will.  Here is the true logic behind abortion.

I deny the existence of such a right with all of my
being.  I cry out with Jehoram in 2 Kings
5:7, “Am I God, to kill and to make alive?” 
According to Romans 13, governments have the right—from God—to execute
evildoers.  We accept that people have
the right to defend their own lives with force, even at the cost of the life of
the aggressor.  We are not required to
value the lives of others more than our own (“Love your neighbor *as* yourself.”).

Neither, though, are we permitted to value them less.   I don’t deny that motherhood, especially
single motherhood, is difficult.  However,
extinguishing the life of that child for the sake of mere convenience is an evil
that cosmic justice cannot overlook.  When
a house is built on bloodshed, surely the stone will cry out from the wall, and
the rafter will answer it from the framework!

I appreciate the forthrightness of Mr. Parker and others
like him.  I really do.  It allows us to do away with the pretense
that the abortion debate is about inert lumps of tissue:  cysts perhaps, or tumors.  It isn’t. 
It’s about people, and whether we think it’s OK to kill people who have
done nothing wrong.  That’s all the
framing the issue needs, and it tells us that one way or another, abortion in
this country will come to an end.  Either
we do it, or God will do it for us.
