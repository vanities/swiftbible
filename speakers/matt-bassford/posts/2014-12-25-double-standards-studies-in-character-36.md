*Now Jacob heard the
words of Laban's sons, saying, "Jacob has taken away all that was our
father's, and from what was our father's he has acquired all this wealth."
And Jacob saw the countenance of Laban, and indeed it was not favorable toward
him as before.*

--Genesis 31:1-2

Here, we see yet another evolution in the perfidious
attitude of Laban and his sons.  Jacob
had worked with Laban and his house for nearly 20 years by this point, and God
has blessed Laban because of Jacob.  Now,
he has asked for some return for his labor so that he can go home.  Laban strikes a bargain with him thinking
that he can swindle Jacob out of his decades’ worth of earnings, but Jacob is
cleverer than he is and turns the bargain around on him so that Jacob is
enriched.



Now, never mind that Jacob was the source of their blessing
for all of these years.  Never mind that
Jacob had the right to some of the wealth that he produced.  Never mind that Jacob’s agreement with Laban
was an honest one, and Laban cheated. 
Now, all of a sudden, Jacob is a parasite and a swindler, and Laban and
his family have been wronged!  It’s
evident that Laban’s code of ethics has one rule in it:  anything that benefits Laban must be right,
and anything that hurts Laban must be wrong.

This code of ethics has many practitioners today.  They are extremely concerned about the rights
and wrongs of a situation, but only as those rights and wrongs pertain to
them.  What is fair and right for them to
do may well be utterly unfair and wrong for someone else to do.  It’s OK for them to get the better of
somebody else through sharp business practices, but how dare somebody else gain
advantage from an honest trade with them! 
It’s OK for them to yell and scream and verbally abuse someone who
offends their sensitivities, but woe betide the man who looks at them
crossways!  To most outside observers,
people like this seem to be marvels of inconsistency, but in reality, their
practice is quite consistent.  All that
matters is them and what they want, and they always keep that goal in mind.

It almost (but not quite) goes without saying that this
philosophy is the height of ungodliness. 
Whether something is right or wrong does not depend on whether we are
the ones doing it.  We do not get to
operate by a different playbook than everyone else.  Instead, God and His word determine whether
an action is godly, and the word is impartial. 
When we do evil, it condemns us, and our preciousness in our own sight
does not change that.  When another does
good, the Scripture praises him, and his insignificance next to our
self-proclaimed magnificence does not change that either.  What’s right is right for everyone, including
us.  What’s wrong is wrong for everyone,
including us.  If we want to inherit
eternal life, we must inhabit a universe in which the center is not ourselves,
but God.
