I’ve often been known to remark that I was born in the wrong
century.  Even though I do have a soft
spot in my heart for modern conveniences like antibiotics and indoor plumbing,
I find the worldview of bygone days more appealing.  I get Bach. 
I don’t get sitcoms.

If I was born in the wrong century, the Saint John’s Bible was
*really* born in the wrong
century.  Modern American society is all
about cheapness, convenience, utility, and speed.  The most common question asked at Bible
bookstores is, “What’s the cheapest Bible you’ve got?”

The Saint John’s Bible, a modern-day illuminated manuscript,
is none of those things.  It cost more
than a million dollars to produce.  Every
word on its pages was painstakingly hand-lettered by a calligrapher.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiIijbxq2MIwH7zEPDmoBy2PuwgxmiYoutEsPXgLjf7K81UOp5PB8aKYDkxDqfIXB8WJe6FSoJgdqdkSYM6562W-CjDh3c7PZDUQRT5LVjK9l2himQHGrPaEsJh4WzDeJeOA40WMxFIHQ/s1600/Text+closeup.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiIijbxq2MIwH7zEPDmoBy2PuwgxmiYoutEsPXgLjf7K81UOp5PB8aKYDkxDqfIXB8WJe6FSoJgdqdkSYM6562W-CjDh3c7PZDUQRT5LVjK9l2himQHGrPaEsJh4WzDeJeOA40WMxFIHQ/s1600/Text+closeup.jpg)

The Bible is physically massive: every page
spread is the size of a whole sheepskin (the Bible having been written on vellum, not paper), and it fills seven volumes.  The entirety of the Saint John’s
Bible weighs about 180 pounds.  Finally, the project was conceived in 1995
and only completed in 2011.  It doesn’t
seem that a world filled with glue-bound Bibles and smartphone apps would have
much room in it for the St. John’s Bible.

And yet, like the bumblebee flies, the St. John’s Bible
implausibly and gloriously exists.

Every Bible geek on the planet, myself included, would like
to own the St. John’s Bible (“Mine! 
MINE!”).  However, the original is
the property of the Saint John’s Abbey and University in Collegeville, Minnesota.  I did not make the pilgrimage to Minnesota to
see it, and as of November 2014, the original is not being displayed
anyway.

Instead, a volume of the Heritage Edition (a full-size,
high-quality replica) came to see me, borne by Jason Engel, Saint John’s Bible
ambassador and all-around good guy.  I’d
mentioned to him that I’d be interested in seeing it sometime, thinking that
there might be a showing in Chicago’s northwest suburbs or something.  Instead, he messaged me that he would be
passing through Joliet in a few weeks and would be happy to stop and show me
the tome in his care, which happened to be the one containing the Gospels and
Acts.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEi_bzXVK5ve1tIunB0MPoyYi-U31p5s0NImUpzDWLC8BNiOTgTimp8vs84ksUQnJ7iYOTrkqY-NzSm6TYfrOun0NuM6BeTjeNqe1ZdNioElGTiWF8O55pT2NZYnKsimIR84rXptYWlrfw/s1600/Gospels-Acts+Title+Page.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEi_bzXVK5ve1tIunB0MPoyYi-U31p5s0NImUpzDWLC8BNiOTgTimp8vs84ksUQnJ7iYOTrkqY-NzSm6TYfrOun0NuM6BeTjeNqe1ZdNioElGTiWF8O55pT2NZYnKsimIR84rXptYWlrfw/s1600/Gospels-Acts+Title+Page.jpg)

I delightedly agreed, and so my
photographer and I got to spend 45 minutes admiring its bumblebee-ness.

The experience was not at all what I was expecting.  To be honest, I’m not sure what my
expectations were, really, other than to ooh and ahh at all the pretty
colors.  However, the Saint John’s Bible
was a good deal more grown-up than that. 
Its illuminations were. . . illuminating, thought-provoking and
symbolically rich as well as beautiful.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgzHfndpPgbhtDVDhSE2aUlUz9hfErJhGr37P7x52mtM9jFXkfUaWY-duxgqBMfXkmUfmQ3eteqrEST4UCAQbdq90XhDznyXa24qQkIaOoBVSwmToxiV2h0MMIAQl9xMVHdbcUxortQnw/s1600/Mark+12+Shema.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgzHfndpPgbhtDVDhSE2aUlUz9hfErJhGr37P7x52mtM9jFXkfUaWY-duxgqBMfXkmUfmQ3eteqrEST4UCAQbdq90XhDznyXa24qQkIaOoBVSwmToxiV2h0MMIAQl9xMVHdbcUxortQnw/s1600/Mark+12+Shema.jpg)



Perhaps this should not have surprised me.  I’ve been writing hymns for nearly 20 years
now, and allusion is one of the foremost tools of the hymnist.  Usually, the allusions I make are Biblical in
nature, but I’ve also been known to draw inspiration from the great hymns of
the past, as I seek to strike a balance between freshness of expression and
continuity of tradition.

It was fascinating—beyond fascinating, really—to see those
same values at work in a different discipline. 
Writer I may be, but I am no artist. 
I’m not very visually clever (which is why I critique Bible design
rather than designing Bibles myself), and my religious tradition is as
uninterested in sacred art as it is committed to sacred song.  It did not occur to me to wonder what those
seeking a visual rather than musical expression of faith would do.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhGRRTqUXFNW2h5RNrhdX6SZRHzFPvt34npo4ObuKVCuMkZV3H6_A-DrVaOx1sEye8-v2DspEaQ9jxdf1xQe237b6_F27LQpdw2bRbhrreAK4eLIgUiMqDLzhxxR1Yg4e5-XVnSScHuYg/s1600/Mark+5+illumination.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhGRRTqUXFNW2h5RNrhdX6SZRHzFPvt34npo4ObuKVCuMkZV3H6_A-DrVaOx1sEye8-v2DspEaQ9jxdf1xQe237b6_F27LQpdw2bRbhrreAK4eLIgUiMqDLzhxxR1Yg4e5-XVnSScHuYg/s1600/Mark+5+illumination.jpg)



I started figuring things out, though, literally from the
first page of the first gospel.  Matthew
begins, of course, with the genealogy of Jesus, a genealogy that is a great
deal more than a simple list of begettings. 
The illumination opposite it, then, must not only illustrate a few dozen
unpronounceable names, but also make that illustration thematic of the gospel
of Matthew as a whole.

Here’s what the illuminators did:  They took the familiar concept of the family
tree, with its many branches leading to a common descendant, and replaced it
with a menorah, the seven-branched lampstand from the furnishings of the
tabernacle and Solomon’s temple.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgo6_m86XuMNDeKYTYphexpxtExm6LeQiPHxM1D5QCTbtm_u3ClmbRXW-3dlne9OxSaEV0Qf-Tvgxus1d-T9at8HWwdmPtuLCKjhsnnAbyeFwFH0SFQNP39D2_QGSMSR_hmlFrMDlA64A/s1600/Matthew+1+illumination.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgo6_m86XuMNDeKYTYphexpxtExm6LeQiPHxM1D5QCTbtm_u3ClmbRXW-3dlne9OxSaEV0Qf-Tvgxus1d-T9at8HWwdmPtuLCKjhsnnAbyeFwFH0SFQNP39D2_QGSMSR_hmlFrMDlA64A/s1600/Matthew+1+illumination.jpg)



I have to say—that’s really clever.  A menorah is first of all a Biblical concept
in the way that a family tree is not.  It
is a reminder that Jesus was a Jew, as were all His forebears up to Judah
himself (How strange that we so easily forget the Jewishness of Jesus!).  It is also a foreshadowing of the content of
the entire gospel.  Of the four, Matthew
is unquestionably the most Jewish.  Its
goal is to present Jesus as the Messiah of Jewish prophecy and to illustrate
the supersession of Moses by Christ.  Even
when it records Jesus’ lengthy condemnations of the Pharisees, its focus
remains on first-century Jewish concerns. 
How better, then, to capture the essence of Matthew than by using a
Jewish symbol?

The opening illustration of John is equally apt.  John is noted for its high Christology, its emphasis on the divinity of Jesus. 
Commentators often describe John as a “cosmic trial”, in which Jesus
proves that He is God in much the same way that Jehovah reveals His divine
nature in the back half of Isaiah.  The
Saint John’s illuminators, then, created an illustration opposite John 1 that is
dominated by a towering figure in gold leaf balanced by golden script—the Word
made flesh indeed!

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgJK-VvslWH7BvmkhQMExbWybUtPYvYulGJ7-HJ5x76hTBWH7Q7-O6lb2ArHWdej_niox9Hl7Z5AII6Uqym5kIJVu8x8O9IRDhsZRZw9JM_R4e2W4fvgrndW33j4ngHafNhHNMwSeRGqg/s1600/John+1+zoomout.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgJK-VvslWH7BvmkhQMExbWybUtPYvYulGJ7-HJ5x76hTBWH7Q7-O6lb2ArHWdej_niox9Hl7Z5AII6Uqym5kIJVu8x8O9IRDhsZRZw9JM_R4e2W4fvgrndW33j4ngHafNhHNMwSeRGqg/s1600/John+1+zoomout.jpg)

In a wink to
tradition, the uneven top of the John 1 illumination is shaped like the
silhouette of an eagle, which is the animal traditionally identified with the
fourth gospel writer.  This in turn is
based on the description of the fourth living creature in Revelation 4:7, and Revelation
itself is commonly attributed to the apostle John too.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgc9neR6wfH_Gs3htaTUIjx8P9LG5C5eN_AZUzthTgCT4Qb7fQMEWmUvc5dMTbLCqDzFWBe91Ybehz5gpSCmLhTAhdDwhcFSNKQRGw_gTFv3K5oap4CCvdSa738WQKRqEJ3w-aSS5uxDA/s1600/John+1+closeup.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgc9neR6wfH_Gs3htaTUIjx8P9LG5C5eN_AZUzthTgCT4Qb7fQMEWmUvc5dMTbLCqDzFWBe91Ybehz5gpSCmLhTAhdDwhcFSNKQRGw_gTFv3K5oap4CCvdSa738WQKRqEJ3w-aSS5uxDA/s1600/John+1+closeup.jpg)

Basically, the Saint John’s Bible is like “Where’s Waldo?” for
those who know a lot about the Bible and Christian tradition.  It’s an Easter-egg hunt for Biblical
allusion.  However, not all the allusions
are Biblical.  Some nod to the past 1500
years of religious art.  The illumination
opposite Acts 4-5, for instance, almost could have been copied from a fresco on
the dome of a Byzantine cathedral.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEitEY_OmO8r5VtQnkenofNcCuiMroiZdbHVmv-AENLERsQo3ZRQKNLLq0G4WB2m20p4PArF0HOmabQzHAdW9qTV6hVV0rEtdO5MwUBSxY_LOsse16s2dIw5e5G2PEsnDoq7F6dJWiqNMw/s1600/Acts+4-5+illumination.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEitEY_OmO8r5VtQnkenofNcCuiMroiZdbHVmv-AENLERsQo3ZRQKNLLq0G4WB2m20p4PArF0HOmabQzHAdW9qTV6hVV0rEtdO5MwUBSxY_LOsse16s2dIw5e5G2PEsnDoq7F6dJWiqNMw/s1600/Acts+4-5+illumination.jpg)



The horse in the illumination of “the gates of Hades” in
Matthew 16 reminds me of something, for sure, but I can’t quite place it.  Maybe it’s the bug-eyed Satan in Dürer’s
woodcut “The Knight, Death, and the Devil”.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgRJTDJYZ6QPU3jUjXSOt8RH9VbYUPcPnTqaxMbQV4byTlafN67g78Tfypzlm2I2-z9DIfJlKGQd-8hPk9dtxRqSPGDsuSXOKALslYlqxYrHUz83L3Ba7GP51uNxa8wYF3Av3srBTRAPA/s1600/Gates+of+hell+AIDS.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgRJTDJYZ6QPU3jUjXSOt8RH9VbYUPcPnTqaxMbQV4byTlafN67g78Tfypzlm2I2-z9DIfJlKGQd-8hPk9dtxRqSPGDsuSXOKALslYlqxYrHUz83L3Ba7GP51uNxa8wYF3Av3srBTRAPA/s1600/Gates+of+hell+AIDS.jpg)

It all made me wonder if I could have more profitably spent my college
days acquiring a degree in art history.  Perhaps
if one attempted to recount all the allusions in the Saint John’s Bible, the
world itself could not contain the books that would be written!

However--and this is certainly heartening—the Saint John’s
illuminators didn’t feel bound to millennia of precedent either.  Next to the chthonic horse in “the gates of
Hades”, the bluish squiggles are a drawing of the AIDS virus.  The Beatitudes are illuminated by an abstract
giggle of calligraphy.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgFu98ipx-Fi3IjKrjjcVEIgcHhyj7p6WmlvJtbgodgif-mrED2qAWDANx3XWv7OVT_Tosr05OIJuJxsQOgDlO0Ih0hFEh-WqDyn7nbzHoNIPrWN0Q-1KNj5QenkgM0fnTclTmPLXd1Gg/s1600/Beatitudes+illumination.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgFu98ipx-Fi3IjKrjjcVEIgcHhyj7p6WmlvJtbgodgif-mrED2qAWDANx3XWv7OVT_Tosr05OIJuJxsQOgDlO0Ih0hFEh-WqDyn7nbzHoNIPrWN0Q-1KNj5QenkgM0fnTclTmPLXd1Gg/s1600/Beatitudes+illumination.jpg)

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgSppUdF_hgQkUcHIOd-6MAxtSJX4ggrQlKDkdU17XFVT3gIqAuI7r8ObLEqF2j9V3VMSRJnSWZyxzOSb0fO0nE7_Ay8nd5-UBvvBRbI_GlnoRwdNlFCYL0frE4z7DzVQAfSUuQnqgXSg/s1600/Beatitudes+illumination+closeup.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgSppUdF_hgQkUcHIOd-6MAxtSJX4ggrQlKDkdU17XFVT3gIqAuI7r8ObLEqF2j9V3VMSRJnSWZyxzOSb0fO0nE7_Ay8nd5-UBvvBRbI_GlnoRwdNlFCYL0frE4z7DzVQAfSUuQnqgXSg/s1600/Beatitudes+illumination+closeup.jpg)

Jesus’ “I am the
bread” discourse in John 6 is accompanied by geometric snowflakes of manna. . .

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhPziTRrh0Jr2d-LcRFxwI9mvtVVa1eEY9dveAf5vzaqe9duEy6XhbZjDx1gPUQR0F3EN7I7pT4qqy2ILwGljwLf70PypDZaHKeMA13s8x1soqYabndeai5JvptyKhCoWMwVffV_So3Qw/s1600/John+6+illumination.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhPziTRrh0Jr2d-LcRFxwI9mvtVVa1eEY9dveAf5vzaqe9duEy6XhbZjDx1gPUQR0F3EN7I7pT4qqy2ILwGljwLf70PypDZaHKeMA13s8x1soqYabndeai5JvptyKhCoWMwVffV_So3Qw/s1600/John+6+illumination.jpg)


. . .and
tessellated fish.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEg4-OTboR9XyC2bYJv2WDPBq9O6Bf-eYLHbHrZ20jdG7BWmffxIckJFhovIb8pMQk6D-3PA8O2RJ-iz0zFuQjamXXug10d1QvZY6bwMBiIWKz5FSodM5doyiG0Ggmhz9N8IPIlJfw2j1A/s1600/John+6+fish+closeup.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEg4-OTboR9XyC2bYJv2WDPBq9O6Bf-eYLHbHrZ20jdG7BWmffxIckJFhovIb8pMQk6D-3PA8O2RJ-iz0zFuQjamXXug10d1QvZY6bwMBiIWKz5FSodM5doyiG0Ggmhz9N8IPIlJfw2j1A/s1600/John+6+fish+closeup.jpg)



Sometimes, the illustrators “doodled” in the margins, like
the monks who created the Book of Kells did. 
Matthew 14 has a marginal mandala next to it, for no particular reason
at all.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjFboB8YMU5PZLjccqdwPiQzs0Z0Hpx61m-3tOZ6Ma_y0cwuv0Eluv8qe6UIbSfGXYGcEsqND-7rMXRxTmbQPxBeOBmMkxQFCCynn55eGgVRJ5_JZZhR9yLQq4cLmaAWO9p8U25lQMeEw/s1600/Marginal+mandala.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjFboB8YMU5PZLjccqdwPiQzs0Z0Hpx61m-3tOZ6Ma_y0cwuv0Eluv8qe6UIbSfGXYGcEsqND-7rMXRxTmbQPxBeOBmMkxQFCCynn55eGgVRJ5_JZZhR9yLQq4cLmaAWO9p8U25lQMeEw/s1600/Marginal+mandala.jpg)



In fact, I suppose the same thing might be said of the Saint John’s Bible as a whole.  The world
doesn’t *need*another gorgeous
illuminated manuscript.  It’s still the
same gospel as if it had been printed in China (ironic, that) and sold for
$9.99 at Wal-Mart.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjZDErVyrnRe9RY1ly_E5xoqVZqJDHxSDYETp4jJD3pW8HLDhW65AP6U1pDasV4DEs_y-A8DZfW6I_P8tepzDERjonIcAorCXBtSWiI069qJ3NUVWBjJS6SnGCMqjGfLDf34EpRR7uzTQ/s1600/Text+zoomout.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjZDErVyrnRe9RY1ly_E5xoqVZqJDHxSDYETp4jJD3pW8HLDhW65AP6U1pDasV4DEs_y-A8DZfW6I_P8tepzDERjonIcAorCXBtSWiI069qJ3NUVWBjJS6SnGCMqjGfLDf34EpRR7uzTQ/s1600/Text+zoomout.jpg)

However, I’m sure glad the world *has* another gorgeous illuminated manuscript.
