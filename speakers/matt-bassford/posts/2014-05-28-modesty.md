*Likewise, I want women to adorn themselves with proper clothing,
modestly and discreetly, not with braided hair and gold or pearls or costly
garments, but rather by means of good works, as is proper for women making a
claim to godliness.*

-- 1 Timothy 2:9-10 (NASB)

Few topics create as much
perennial outrage among the Lord’s people as modesty.  This appears particularly strange when we
consider the slender Scriptural foundation on which the discussion is
based.  Only a few passages in the Bible
address modesty, and on their face, they are concerned not with the revealing
clothing we consider immodest, but with showy dress meant to display wealth.



Nonetheless, immodesty (in the
dress of others, never the self) is a topic that sets the blood of many worthy
Christians to boiling.  With perhaps more
zeal than wisdom, they seek to generate specific rules from the broad
instructions of Scripture that they find frustratingly vague.  Armed with these guidelines for hemlines and
necklines, they seek to impose their personal standards on others.

However, when we consider these
well-intended but misguided attempts to bring order from chaos, we must be
careful not to leap to the other extreme. 
Modesty is certainly a problem today, and some clothing is certainly
inappropriate for Christians to wear.

In determining where to draw the
line, then, we must put away our rulers and open our Bibles.  God is concerned first of all with the heart,
and He teaches us that a heart that puts God first will choose dress that also
puts God first.  We must thoughtfully
consider our clothing in the light of His goals for our lives, and the choices
we make must never interfere with His work.
