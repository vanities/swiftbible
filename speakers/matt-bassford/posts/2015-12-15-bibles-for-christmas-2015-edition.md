“This
holiday season, give the gift that gives eternal life!”

I
should have been an advertising copywriter, no?

More
seriously, I believe that even in our tech-saturated society, there’s still a
place for an old-fashioned paper Bible in the lives of most Christians.  From the Bibles I’ve reviewed over the past
couple of years, here are my favorites.  All
of them are sturdily constructed with good settings.  I’m dividing them into premium (generally,
Bibles retailing for more than $100) and mass-market (generally, Bibles
retailing for less than $100) picks.  Of
course, these recommendations have everything to do with my own personal
preferences, so your mileage may vary, particularly when it comes to preaching
Bibles.  Note also that I’m only linking
to the section of each review that I find most pertinent, but all of these
Bibles are fully evaluated in reviews of four or even more parts.



**Best
Preaching Bible, Premium:  [Cambridge Wide-Margin Reference](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2015/03/cambridge-nasb-pitt-minionwide-margin_22.html)**

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhlps_2hU3Vpojxhk-pNT4GiNS7B2sGSv7zu64NJCy6mYOHc-jb_Mr7sqZdeL3Sx5Pmv2u08NcZM4BWP_vqKMD0Cz9g92lISeA3iVcVpMIWpvdZik9XJvFb4S7vx0IU_2at3DQCvxKVYA/s1600/PF2.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhlps_2hU3Vpojxhk-pNT4GiNS7B2sGSv7zu64NJCy6mYOHc-jb_Mr7sqZdeL3Sx5Pmv2u08NcZM4BWP_vqKMD0Cz9g92lISeA3iVcVpMIWpvdZik9XJvFb4S7vx0IU_2at3DQCvxKVYA/s1600/PF2.jpg)

To
be quite honest, I think every preacher who has good eyes and likes to write in
his Bible should be wielding one of these. 
You simply can’t beat the combination of Jongbloed printing, opaque
paper, cross-references, spacious margins, and relatively light weight.  Better still, the WM is available in NKJV,
ESV, NASB, and NIV, so you’re able to adopt whichever of those translations you
prefer.

It’s
true that these are expensive.  My
calf-split NASB WM retails for $210 (though you can get one online for $150 or
less).  However, just like a professional
carpenter spends more on tools than I do, I think a preacher is well served to
invest in the best-designed and -constructed Bible he can, regardless of
cost.  If you’re going to spend every day
for the next 10 years staring at the thing, money should be no object.

I
have no complaints about the WM I use. 
In fact, the more I use it, the better I like it.  Indeed, I like it so well that it’s
interfering with my Bible blogging.  I
don’t want to spend time testing other Bibles when I could be preaching out of
my WM!

**Best
Preaching Bible, Mass-Market:  [Foundation Wide-Margin In Touch (NASB)](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2015/09/foundation-wide-margin-in-touch-part-2.html)**

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhIldu_ufFOr9cPoY4AzSEFKw6HFDqkhYXbL6A-YCisFGFTV6lm8o37wchCavDMTdx1rpxIQrrLu1Loi_btP8dE5Qk8qmWSFsM-RDhrEcymUcvnOmYHYlhfyMQ6RO8ERerlucSps_oIxA/s1600/PF+open.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhIldu_ufFOr9cPoY4AzSEFKw6HFDqkhYXbL6A-YCisFGFTV6lm8o37wchCavDMTdx1rpxIQrrLu1Loi_btP8dE5Qk8qmWSFsM-RDhrEcymUcvnOmYHYlhfyMQ6RO8ERerlucSps_oIxA/s1600/PF+open.jpg)

I
far and away prefer wide-margin Bibles in the pulpit.  They let me write my sermon outlines in the
margin.  For whatever reason, though, I
haven’t had the opportunity to review very many wide-margin Bibles.

However,
even if I had reviewed a number of them, I think the In Touch would still be a
strong contender for this position.  It’s
only available in hardback, but the paper is that amazing super-opaque
French-milled stuff that Foundation used to put in Bibles 15 years ago.  I believe it’s literally illegal to produce
such paper today because of how polluting the production process is.  Whether that’s true or not, no one makes
paper like that anymore, so the In Touch is a relic from a bygone golden
age.

Similarly,
because the In Touch was printed in the US 15 years ago, before mass-market
Bible printing got outsourced to China, the print quality is comparable to that
of a Jongbloed Bible.  In short, the In
Touch has a premium-quality text block lurking in a hardback cover.  Combine that with a very functional
double-column text layout with plenty of white space, and you’ve got a great
pulpit Bible for the preacher of limited means.

**Best
Reading Bible, Premium:  [Tyndale Select/Schuyler Caxton (NLT)](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2015/12/tyndale-selectschuyler-caxton-reading.html)**

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgUi727kfWAs_ub_v2GG09OaOibfy6T8GWSnrNDkZf6TCoqGOYNzjxI-CmthK4T4bsw1IRvJeeTooiHrf4ybRJP15gBg91RxYh5mFHirCh3llz6bawpcsv-l_-l4F0b_6XM4yZF29qk_Q/s1600/DSC_0059.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgUi727kfWAs_ub_v2GG09OaOibfy6T8GWSnrNDkZf6TCoqGOYNzjxI-CmthK4T4bsw1IRvJeeTooiHrf4ybRJP15gBg91RxYh5mFHirCh3llz6bawpcsv-l_-l4F0b_6XM4yZF29qk_Q/s1600/DSC_0059.jpg)

A
recent entry into the marketplace, the text block that the Select and Caxton
share is just about as well optimized for reading as a Bible can possibly
be.  Admittedly, the print is only of
moderate size, so those who need reading glasses might be better advised to
look elsewhere, but in every other way, the Select/Caxton is designed to
present no obstacle between the reader and the text.  This is much harder than it sounds.  If money is no object, and you’re looking for
a strong reading Bible, look no further!

**Best
Reading Bible, Mass-Market:  [Trinitarian Bible Society Windsor (KJV)](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2015/01/trinitarian-bible-society-windsor_30.html)**

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjGWQo1UdJBL_L2kWUmsGARXe61N97p7DmBBeJQkgrmSy3p45YUDhh2ehnay3bzi0g1aVds_DdmpsES1HsI4_w2PLMqGX1iL-ntWikIRea8Hai3016I-Yf-m76zHnS5oM9J7BvFheDlXw/s1600/PF2.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjGWQo1UdJBL_L2kWUmsGARXe61N97p7DmBBeJQkgrmSy3p45YUDhh2ehnay3bzi0g1aVds_DdmpsES1HsI4_w2PLMqGX1iL-ntWikIRea8Hai3016I-Yf-m76zHnS5oM9J7BvFheDlXw/s1600/PF2.jpg)

This
Bible still has the highest score of any Bible I’ve ever reviewed (though it
benefits from having been evaluated before I fell in love with wide-margin
Bibles).  It’s in the mass-market
category by price, but it’s really a premium Bible by quality:  calf-split cover, Jongbloed-printed text
block.  Also, because the poetry of the
Old Testament is set in prose rather than in poetry in the Windsor (which is
the case for most KJV’s), it doesn’t offer the same layout obstacles to
comprehension that most double-column text Bibles do.  Once I’ve read through the Caxton, I intend
to start doing readings out of the Windsor, and I think anybody who is
interested in reading through the KJV would be well served to do the same.

**Best
Carry Bible, Premium:  [Cambridge Pitt Minion](http://bibleexchange.com/cambridge-nlt-pitt-minion-part-1-physical-form/)**

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhygGBFlaY8mtTFRnX0ZG0-MDgJ1WcEPS-ZoBkexhMHrsmOOtIJ_9u2KaW_smCbBRGTukn5F3fv1Bs_LI2vSL_kXUhitVZLV2W1YB5tsYbm2AEy_vJcZJ8mphpzhGkplTO1KKjtNAKihg/s1600/PF2.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhygGBFlaY8mtTFRnX0ZG0-MDgJ1WcEPS-ZoBkexhMHrsmOOtIJ_9u2KaW_smCbBRGTukn5F3fv1Bs_LI2vSL_kXUhitVZLV2W1YB5tsYbm2AEy_vJcZJ8mphpzhGkplTO1KKjtNAKihg/s1600/PF2.jpg)



This
offering from overseas is the classic high-end small-Bible layout.  In size, it’s even smaller than most
thinlines, but it still makes room for a full suite of references.  The 6.5-point font is recommended for good
eyes only, but it reads larger than one might expect because of the ultra-crisp
Jongbloed printing.  Also, it’s available
in a wide variety of translations:  KJV,
NKJV, ESV, NASB, NIV, and even NLT. 
Grab-and-go Bibles don’t get much better than this.

**Best
Carry Bible, Mass-Market:  [Zondervan NASB Thinline](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2014/07/zondervan-thinline-nasb-traveling.html)**

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjCxadTn0PNB_18CtePgh1lQWqKVg84XZRG0qPMv_Rh26PcSnJ0-keXukuJ0JSk2GgI8wKheDrxz6Fxik_8PpvNLAuVzzNTqh8Isc3UrZ8JqSv_7VPkAGOTaBSnfL0bSfrB3MJNiubpHQ/s1600/PF2.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjCxadTn0PNB_18CtePgh1lQWqKVg84XZRG0qPMv_Rh26PcSnJ0-keXukuJ0JSk2GgI8wKheDrxz6Fxik_8PpvNLAuVzzNTqh8Isc3UrZ8JqSv_7VPkAGOTaBSnfL0bSfrB3MJNiubpHQ/s1600/PF2.jpg)

It’s
true that in this Bible, quality starts to suffer a little bit because of
price.  The text block is printed in
China, and the poly cover is maybe even a little bit flimsier than the cover on
some other mass-market Bibles.  However,
the setting is a gem.  It’s about 15
years old at this point, but the layout is so classically beautiful that it has
aged remarkably well.  This is a
double-column text Bible *par excellence*.  In fact, if I’m going on a trip and I don’t
anticipate preaching while I’m gone, this is the Bible I will take with
me.  I’ve probably also spent more time
reading out of it than any other Bible.
