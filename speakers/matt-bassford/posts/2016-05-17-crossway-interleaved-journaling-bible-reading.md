**OVERVIEW.** Even though it’s a behemoth, the IJB was
a capable reader.  I particularly
appreciated its generous column width and clear print.

As is my practice, I piloted the IJB through two weeks’
worth of daily Bible readings.  Here are
my impressions:



**SIZE.** Truth be told, the IJB was a little
awkward as a reader, but only a little. 
When I’m reading from a larger Bible, I usually rest the volume on my
chest.  However, as it happened, I did
most of my readings in my office at the church building, which is cold enough
in the winter that I wear my coat throughout the week.  The interaction between Bible and zipper,
quilting, and so forth was uncomfortable enough that I eventually unzipped my
coat to give the Bible a more pleasant place to rest.  At home, where it’s warm enough that I don’t
wear a heavy coat around the house, this wasn’t a problem.

**LAYOUT.** Here, we encounter the bright side of the
law of unintended consequences.   The
Bible’s designers at Crossway knew from the beginning that this was going to be
a great big huge Bible.  Half the pages
were blank, and even the half that weren’t had wide outer margins.

As a result, the designers pulled every trick they could
think of to reduce both Bible size and page count.  Paragraphed Bibles are smaller than
verse-by-verse Bibles, so they went paragraphed.  Double-column Bibles are smaller than
single-column Bibles, so they went double-column.  Bibles with large pages have fewer pages than
Bibles with smaller pages, so they opted for a large format.  Finally, Bibles with smaller print are
smaller than Bibles with larger print, so they set it in a fairly small
font.  The intended consequence of these
decisions is that the printed part of the Bible is only about 700 pages long.

However, there was also an unintended consequence.  This 700-page Bible has great big pages with
small text in double columns.  As a
result, it hits my characters-per-line sweet spot.  The IJB has about 55 CPL, which is
perfect.  Each line of text is short
enough that I don’t get lost in lines of prose, but it’s long enough that the
poetic sections aren’t filled with one-word “widows”.  I thought it was great.  In fact, I liked it so well that I considered
buying an ESV Ultrathin, which has similar characteristics.

Despite its considerable width, the IJB doesn’t have any
gutter problems either.  Generally, the
thicker the Bible, the tighter the gutter, but that’s not an issue here.  I make the gutter in the IJB to be about ⅝”,
and that’s plenty to keep the text from falling into the Pit of Despair even in
the middle of the Bible.

Similarly, I appreciate Crossway’s decision to make this a
black-letter Bible.  Red-letter Bibles
are prone to all kinds of problems, but the IJB avoids them all by avoiding red
ink.  Even the best red-letter Bible can
only be the equal of a black-letter Bible as a reader, and most of them fall
short someplace.

Fourth, the IJB is line-matched.  There’s a sense in which this is a
belt-and-suspenders issue.  The paper in
the IJB is so thick and opaque (spoiler!) that I don’t think it would have
affected the readability of the Bible much for it not to be line-matched.  However, Crossway went ahead and line-matched
it anyway.  Good on ya, Crossway.

Finally, even though the print in the IJB is smaller than my
usual 8-point minimum, I didn’t have a problem reading it.  Last year, I discovered to my sorrow that the
6.75/7-point Lexicon No. 1 in my Cambridge NASB Pitt Minion was too small for
me to be happy with that Bible as a daily reader.  The 7.5-point Lexicon No. 1 in the IJB isn’t
a whole lot bigger, but apparently it’s bigger enough.

The real secret here is the leading.  Like many Crossway Bibles, the IJB is
generously leaded.  I make it out to be
about 9-point leading.  At this point in
my Bible-reviewing career, I’ve just about decided that I prefer a Bible with
smaller print and more leading to a Bible with larger print and no
leading.  In the latter kind of Bible, my
eye gets mixed up about which line it’s supposed to be reading.  Leading is important, and I think the leading
in the IJB puts it over the top and makes it into a reader that I find
comfortable.

**PRODUCTION.** Like nearly all mass-market Bibles, the
IJB is printed in China.  Over the past
couple of years, I’ve become more and more impressed with the quality of
Chinese Bible printing.  The print is
much more crisp and even than it used to be in Bibles from the other side of
the Pacific.  However, it seems like
there’s often one or two things that separate a mass-market China-print text
block from a premium Jongbloed text block. 
It’s like when a mediocre football team plays the champ nearly straight
up for 60 minutes, but they make a few mistakes that cause them to lose the
game.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh8alvevuG32fkAG1k03wwwsvQ7qMIGO6JZn8moU6JprkE5inUOJTulwd1-bQNAMV_kfeiU9BFaGA0aZI7sSyH4zyid8o3eFnrLls3zjPDqCOYCwgCDG6qEUE99T6bBkaGZF33-JU9jng/s1600/R+line+matching.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh8alvevuG32fkAG1k03wwwsvQ7qMIGO6JZn8moU6JprkE5inUOJTulwd1-bQNAMV_kfeiU9BFaGA0aZI7sSyH4zyid8o3eFnrLls3zjPDqCOYCwgCDG6qEUE99T6bBkaGZF33-JU9jng/s1600/R+line+matching.jpg)



For instance, when I read Judges 19:18 in the IJB, I noticed
a strange dark blotch, about the size of a pinhead, under the first word of the
verse.  I scratched at it to try to get
it to flake off, but instead of flaking, it smeared.  Apparently, during the printing process, a
small glob of ink had gotten stuck there. 
I seem to recall a few other ink speckles throughout the Bible too.  I’m OK with that in a great big Bible that
retails for 40 bucks, but it’s still not quite as good as a premium text block.

The printing could have been better, then, but it’s hard to
improve on the paper.  At 50 gsm, it’s
clear that the Crossway designers intended this Bible for art.  I’m not at all into that (my thinking is much
more abstract and symbolic), but for those who are, this is a must-consider
Bible.



**CONCLUSION.** In short, the IJB is a surprisingly
impressive reader.  The extra  bulk didn’t bother me very much, and the
layout was great.  Even for those who
aren’t into journaling, I can imagine this Bible serving as a 20-year
reader.  Every year, as you return to a
passage, you jot down your observations on the facing page.  As the years go by, you can compare what you
see now to what you saw before as a marker of your spiritual growth.  I think this one gets a 9/10 as a reader, and
it scores an overall 23.5/30.  Basically,
the only thing I didn’t like about it was that it was big.
