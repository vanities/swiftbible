It’s fair to say that for my wife, Christmas is both her
most and least favorite time of year. 
She loves the end-of-year rounds of having family in and exchanging
gifts and baking cookies, but she also knows that the month of December is when
my mind starts to turn toward next year’s budget.  I enjoy figuring out new ways to reduce our
spending each year.  She. . . does not.

Regardless of where we fall on the spender-saver continuum,
many of us now are thinking about next year’s finances.  When we do that, though, we must take care to
remember the most important line item—giving to the Lord’s work.  There are two things that infallibly reveal
our priorities:  the way we spend our
time and the way we spend our money.  If
we can find the cash for the latest tech toys, but we only come up with pocket
lint for the contribution, that says something about us.

Before I go on, let me add that the elders here are by and
large pleased with the contribution.  The
Jackson Heights church isn’t facing a financial crisis.  However, it’s still appropriate for each one
of us to consider our giving.  With this
in mind, let’s consider what the Scriptures have to say about the godly giver.



First, they tell us that the godly giver is **PURPOSEFUL.**Look here at 2 Corinthians 9:7. 
Whatever we put in the plate can’t simply be a matter of chance or the
money we happen to have left over at the end of the week.  Instead, it needs to be what we decided to
put there.

Back when I was in the training program at Dowlen Road, Max
Dawson and David Banning sat me down one day and had me fill out a
family-budget worksheet.  The very first
item on the worksheet, right at the top of the list, was “Contribution”.  They didn’t tell me how much I needed to put
in that category.  They did tell me,
though, that I needed to decide that number first and make everything else work
around it.

This is good advice for all of us.  If God is truly first in our lives, He needs
to be first in our budgets.  That’s an
appropriate return for how much He has blessed all of us, and it also reminds
us that everything else on that budget sheet belongs to Him too.  We’ve spent this whole year emphasizing the
importance of being heaven bound.  If
that’s where we intend to go, we ought to be laying up treasure there for when
we get there.

Second, the godly giver is **FAITHFUL.** Consider 2
Corinthians 8:10-11.  Here, Paul tells us
that our readiness to give needs to be matched by our actual contribution.  It’s great for us to be intentional about our
giving.  It’s great for us to have a plan
about how much we’re going to give to the Lord on the first day of the
week.  You know what’s better?  Following through on that giving plan, 52
weeks a year.

This is not necessarily easy.  All of us know that financial emergencies and
crises can come up during the year.  In
times like that, rather than trimming things elsewhere, the contribution is the
first thing that many brethren cut.

Sometimes, this is unavoidable.  If the primary breadwinner in a family loses
their job, yeah, you can’t give if you’re not prospering.  At other times, though, a budget crunch is
the result of failing to plan for the smaller emergencies that come up in every
year.  If the van needs new tires and you
pay for that by not giving for a month, that generally proves that you didn’t
budget wisely enough at the beginning of the year.

We avoid falling into this trap by not basing our giving
plan on the best-case scenario.  Maybe
nobody else knows what we committed to at the beginning of the year, but God
does.  We need to put enough in the
budget that we can keep that commitment through anything but a major crisis.

Despite this care, the godly giver also is **BOUNTIFUL**.  Paul makes this point in 2 Corinthians
9:6.  He tells us that what we reap will
be aligned with what we sow.  That’s true
if we sow sparingly, and it’s true if we sow bountifully.

Here, we must be careful to distinguish between the false
doctrine of the prosperity gospel on the one hand and the genuine Scriptural
promise on the other.  No, God does not
promise that He is going to make any of us rich.  However, He does promise that if we give
generously to His work, He will make sure that we always have the resources we
need to be generous.

I’ve seen this work itself out in the lives of my
parents.  They were dedicated savers,
which is where I get it from, but they understood that they had to give to God
first.  When we lived in New Jersey, my
dad was the church treasurer, and he told me once that his weekly contribution
was a quarter of the total contribution there.

Think that hurt my parents financially?  Think that interfered with my dad’s plans to
retire early?  Not a bit.  They trusted God, and He proved Himself
faithful for as long as they lived.  If
we trust Him today in our giving, if we don’t listen to our fears, He will
prove Himself faithful with us too.

Fourth, the godly giver is **JOYFUL.** Look at the
testimony of 2 Corinthians 8:1-2.  Several
characteristics stand out about the churches of Macedonia here.  First, these are people who are extremely
poor.  Despite their poverty, though,
they were extremely generous in contributing to the collection for the needy
saints in Jerusalem.

How can this be?  Why
would extremely poor people come up with a lot of money?  The answer lies in the third
characteristic.  The saints in Macedonia
were joyful givers.

If our poor, oppressed brethren in the first century had
reason to give joyfully, how much more do we? 
We enjoy material prosperity beyond the wildest dreams of those early
Christians.  We live in a country where
we can freely worship and practice our faith.

More important than those physical blessings, though, are
the spiritual blessings we share with them in Christ.  We have a Savior who loved us enough to die
for us and give us the hope of eternal life. 
Next to such a great gift, who are we to be grudging about giving so
little?  When compared to the sacrifice
of Christ, anything we might give will always be little!

Finally, the godly giver is **HOPEFUL**.  Paul explains why
this should be in 2 Corinthians 9:10-11. 
The first part of this we’ve already talked about.  Paul here reaffirms that if we are inclined
to use our money to sow for the sake of righteousness, God will give us all the
seed we need.  If we are bountiful
givers, He will be too.

Second, though, Paul says that the result of this spiritual
sowing will be a harvest of righteousness. 
Indeed, he spends the rest of the chapter explaining this in the context
of the Jerusalem collection.  According
to Him, the ultimate end of their generosity will be that God is glorified.

This is the hope that we can share in our giving too.  We are giving to God’s work, and God’s way
works.  Last Tuesday, I studied with
Michael Schmidt and baptized him into Christ. 
A soul was saved, and we know that soul is worth more than the whole
world.

Know what?  Everyone
here who contributes to the work of the Jackson Heights church contributed to
that result.  Your money supports me and
frees me to study with people like him. 
Given the value of a soul, there is literally nothing that you could
have done with your money that would have been a better investment.

That, brethren, is why we give.  There is nothing more important on earth than
the work that this church does.  The old
saying goes, “You can’t take it with you,” but that’s not actually true.  When we give here, we can give with the hope
that our contribution will have eternal significance.
