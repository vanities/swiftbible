*Therefore Sarah
laughed within herself, saying, "After I have grown old, shall I have
pleasure, my lord being old also?" And the LORD said to Abraham, "Why
did Sarah laugh, saying, 'Shall I surely bear a child, since I am old?'*

--Genesis 18:12-13

Sarah’s reaction to God’s declaration that she will have a
son is one that we often unthinkingly condemn. 
In retrospect, it’s so obvious to us: 
whatever God says, goes, so when He said that Sarah was going to have a
child, she should have just assumed that God knew what He was talking about and
believed.  Get with it, Sarah!  Duh!



Of course, what’s so obvious to us now wouldn’t have been so
obvious if we’d been standing in Sarah’s sandals.  Sarah didn’t have the Biblical record of
thousands of years of God’s dealings with His people to bolster her faith.  She didn’t have some spectacular miracle to
confirm the word.  All she had was the say-so
of what appeared to be a man speaking for God. 
Against that scanty evidence, she had the things she knew.  She knew that she was past childbearing
years.  She knew that she and Abraham had
spent decades praying for a son of their own, with nothing to show for it.  She knew that the only apparent result of
God’s promises so far was the miserable interlude of Hagar and Ishmael.  When we consider those circumstances in the
light of human wisdom, it was only logical that Sarah should laugh.

Before we get a crick in our necks from looking down our
noses at her, we need to consider whether our own lives contain similar
errors.  How much do we believe,
really?  When God warns us against the
perils of the lust of the flesh, how do we handle it?  Do we believe Him and reject ungodly,
lust-provoking entertainment?  Or do we
keep reading our favorite author or watching our favorite TV show because our
human wisdom tells us those things won’t hurt us?  When God tells us that the fields are white
for harvest, do we believe Him?  Do we
work diligently to teach the lost about Jesus? 
Or instead, do we allow human wisdom to cow us into silence because we
“know” that nobody is going to listen?

For the people of God, faith isn’t just about believing in
divine pregnancy predictions.  Faith is
about believing in everything He says, from precept to promise.  If we are truly people of faith, we will obey
every commandment He gives, and we will focus our lives on gaining the reward
He guarantees.  It’s easy to come up with
reasons why we shouldn’t do either one. 
That’s what tripped Sarah up, and we’re not any better than she
was.  It trips us up too, all the
time.  What’s important to us, though, is
not the mistakes we have made and the blind alleys we’ve gone down in the past.  What’s important are the choices we make
today.  Are we going to be people of
faith, or aren’t we?  Our lives will
reveal the truth.
