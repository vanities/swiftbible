**Buy This Bible If:**

* You want a Bible that is as portable and easy to hold as a
  paperback book.
* You’re willing to accept a moderately durable polyurethane
  cover.
* You want to use a translation that is suitable for either reading
  or close study.
* You prefer or tolerate double-column Bibles.
* You can read standard-sized (well, standard-sized for a
  Bible, at least) print.
* You want a paragraphed Bible.
* You’re fine with a Bible that only has cross-references to
  quoted Old-Testament passages, parallel Synoptic accounts, and that sort of
  thing.
* You’re willing to use a Bible with unusually low paper
  opacity, even for a Bible.
* You want a Bible that’s line-matched.
* You don’t intend to write more than the briefest marginal
  notes.
* You want a Bible that’s about as cheap as decently
  constructed Bibles come (about $28 retail, under $20 online).



**Don’t Buy This Bible
If:**

* You prefer large, hefty Bibles for some reason.
* You want either the improved looks or improved durability
  offered by a cover made from actual leather.
* You want a translation that is either easier to read (such
  as the NIV11) or is more precise for close study (such as the NASB).  Note, though, that of the two alternatives,
  the ESV is closer to the close-study end of the spectrum.
* You insist on using a single-column Bible.
* You need or want a large-print Bible.  Despite the name, this ain’t it.
* You want a verse-by-verse Bible, perhaps because you find
  them more suitable for the pulpit.
* You need a Bible with more than minimal cross-references.
* You require a high or even moderate degree of paper opacity.
* You need moderate or even wide margins for note-taking.
