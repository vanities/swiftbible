Like Mary’s little lamb, everywhere I went for two weeks,
the TRB was sure to go.  That really
wasn’t very far, at least as a preacher. 
I did some appointment preaching, and I think I had one study.  Here is what I thought of the TRB in those
endeavors:

**SIZE.** The TRB was the beneficiary of a
millennial event.  I cleaned out the
glove box.  Enough junk had accumulated
in there that during our most recent vacation, even my petite Thinline NASB got
squished.  The cover now bears a few, um,
we’ll call them “battle scars”, because “glove-box dents” doesn’t have much of
a ring to it.  Regardless, I decided that
enough was enough, took everything out that didn’t have to be in there. . .



. . . and the TRB still had trouble fitting.  I had to shove on the glove-box door to get
it to latch, and when I opened it again, the front cover was stuck in the
innards of the dashboard.  This was less
than ideal.  If I were to make a habit of
using the TRB as a travel Bible, I wouldn’t store it there, which means that I
wouldn’t use the TRB as a travel Bible.

Here, at long last, we encounter the trade-off associated
with the TRB.  As I have observed before,
the TRB is basically a Foundation Ultrathin with 250 pages’ worth of topical
Scripture references attached to the front. 
I never really used the topical references, but even so, I didn’t mind
having them there when I was using the Bible for preaching or reading.  125 sheets of Bible paper doesn’t weigh very
much.

However, they do increase the bulk of the Bible, enough so
that where I suspect an Ultrathin would pass the glove-box text just fine, the
TRB doesn’t.  It’s entirely possible that
someone else—a new preacher just starting out on his own, for instance—would
get enough mileage out of the topical index that the increase to just-a-Bible
size is worth it.  I don’t.  Given the choice between the TRB and the
Ultrathin, I would pick the latter every time.

**CROSS-REFERENCES.** The TRB certainly has these, in
spades.  I believe it’s the same
cross-reference system used in the Foundation SCR.  I didn’t use them.  This keeps on happening.  I am beginning to conclude that
cross-references are much more important to me in unfocused study by myself
than they are in focused study with others. 
In the latter setting, I pretty well know how the study is going to go,
and I know off the top of my head where the relevant passages are to be
found.  Others who are more reliant on
cross-references, though, may well appreciate what the TRB has to offer in a
comparatively small package.

**CONCLUSION.** Because the TRB is a just-a-Bible Bible,
it gets a just-a-Bible travel rating. 
3.5/5.  Overall, this gives the
TRB a total score of 15.5/20.
