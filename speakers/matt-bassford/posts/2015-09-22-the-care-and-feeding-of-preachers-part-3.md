**Spending Time with
Strong Christians.** It’s awfully easy
for preachers (and elders, for that matter) to fall into the trap of spending
the bulk of the time they spend with other Christians with the weakest members
of the congregation.  They’re the ones
who need our help the most, after all. 
If we aren’t around all the time to provide spiritual CPR, they’re going
to die on us!



There are two problems with this.  The first is that it is very, very demanding
work.  Think of it in terms of the
spiritual U-turn of 2 Timothy 3:16: 
reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness.  These are brethren who are headed the wrong
way, so you have to get them to stop going the way they’re headed, show them
the way they ought to be going instead, and help them to keep going that
way.  In other words, we have to
completely reverse their spiritual momentum. 
Sometimes, the effort feels like trying to keep a runaway train from
racing off a cliff!

Second, because this work is hard, it often does not
succeed, so it can be very discouraging. 
Sometimes, this is not the case. 
Sometimes, we are able to turn a sinner from the error of his way.  We are able to save his soul from death and
cover a multitude of sins.  Some of my
favorite memories from my work as a preacher involve exactly this.

However, much of the time, perhaps most of the time, this is
not what happens.  Instead, the sinner
who is determined to plunge off the cliff runs us over and then plunges off the
cliff.  This is intensely
frustrating.  We sink our heart and soul
into this guy, and now he’s screening our calls!

For many preachers, this can be a 1 Kings 19 moment.  We’ve done the best we can, but it looks like
the whole world is gospel-proof.  We’ve
depleted our emotional and spiritual resources, and we’ve got nothing to show
for it.  Maybe we ought to start selling
insurance instead.

This leads me to the conclusion that it’s important to spend
as much or more spiritually focused time with brethren who are strong.  Obviously, it’s not a good idea to build close
relationships with women other than your wife. 
You might succeed too well. 
However, most congregations have at least a few godly men who would make
excellent close friends.

My reasoning here is the inverse of my reasoning above.  When we’re working with men who are headed in
the right direction already, a little effort can produce a big change.  We’re not fighting their spiritual momentum.  We’re adding to it.  Similarly, those “easy wins” are encouraging.
 It’s so heartening to see a brother take
an even stronger stand for the Lord than he was before!

In addition, this kind of work often pays huge dividends
years or decades down the road.  I
believe that the single largest problem the church faces is lack of men who are
qualified and willing to serve as elders. 
The Bible sets out one approved pattern for church organization, but
most churches can’t follow the pattern for lack of raw materials.  I can’t substitute for elders, but I can help
produce them.  For this reason, the time
that we spend with godly men who are still raising their families may be the
most important effort of our work week.

When I say “time”, I particularly mean time with brethren
and a Bible.  It’s great to watch ball
games and go to air shows with other Christians.  That can be a useful form of work-life
balance, and we can certainly influence others for good in such a setting.  However, our influence pales in comparison to
the influence of the word of God.

We can help brethren encounter the word in any number of
ways.  Even when studying with a prospect
who is a man, invite another Christian along. 
After all, two by two is the Biblical norm.  If a brother is looking to teach an adult
Bible class for the first time, spend a few weeks studying the material with
him beforehand.  Have Bible studies with
guys for no higher reason than that Bible study is good.  You may well get even more out of the
experience than they will.
