I have a good friend who works in a Bible bookstore, and he tells me that one of the most common things he hears is, “Just give me the cheapest Bible you’ve got in X translation.”  I suppose that this viewpoint has a certain appeal to it.  After all, who doesn’t like a bargain?  

However, I don’t believe that it is the right viewpoint.  Indeed, I am so certain that it is not the right viewpoint that I intend to spend every Friday on this blog discussing physical Bibles and their merits.  Why?  Why spend all that time and all those pixels comparing various features if all that matters is cost?  Why care about Bibles?



In fairness to the people who go for the bottom tier at the Bible bookstore, I think most of them do so without much consideration.  It has not occurred to them that one Bible is significantly different from another.  If you can buy an identical good at a lower price, why not grab the bargain?  However, the mere fact that Bibles at different price points exist ought to tell us that another factor is in play—quality.

I know lots of people who love to cook.  However, none of those people by preference go and buy their pots and their knives at the dollar store.  I know several professional carpenters and tradesmen.  None of those men kit themselves out with the generic-level tools at Wal-Mart.  For that matter, I know a number of runners.  However, none of those runners try to get by with $15 shoes.

Why is that?  Cooks and carpenters and runners aren’t any dumber than the rest of us.  They like to save money too.  They know how to get to the dollar store and Wal-Mart.  They could buy the cheapest alternatives available, but they don’t.  In fact, the opposite is true.  Generally, the more dedicated the cook or the carpenter, the more they will spend on the tools of their trade, not less.

There are two factors at work here.  The first is an understanding of false economy.  We all know that the cheapest product is not necessarily the best value.  A $1 knife may have its handle fall off the first time you run it through the dishwasher.  A $2 screwdriver may wear out in six months.  The money that you “saved” by buying the low-end product in the first place will be more than spent on its replacement.

The second factor is effectiveness.  A higher-end pot will cook food more evenly than a low-end one, and it probably won’t introduce toxic metals into your food either.  $100 running shoes aren’t nearly as likely to leave you with crippling back and knee problems as the $15 specials on the end cap.  

A Bible is a tool too.  We read it to gain understanding of the will of God.  In fact, if we truly want to understand, we will have to read it repeatedly, all our lives.  The same principles that apply to other tools, then, apply to Bibles too.

First, buying cheap Bibles is a false economy.  It’s OK to buy a novel with a paperback cover and a glued binding.  We’re likely to read the book once at most and then stick it on a shelf.  

A Bible, on the other hand, is a book we ought to be using constantly, both at church and in our homes.  The same glued binding that can withstand being read once will crack in a year or two, even if it is only opened on Sunday mornings during the sermon.  Then, the whole Bible will start falling apart.  That doesn’t mean the owner is a good Christian.  It just means he bought a cheap Bible, and he’ll probably be going back to the Bible bookstore where he bought it to repeat the cycle.

Second, a quality Bible will be more effective.  Of course, a high-end NASB will have the same words in it as a low-end NASB, but a thoughtfully chosen, well-constructed Bible will promote understanding in a way that a poorly made impulse decision will not.  As any web page designer will tell you, format matters, and a Bible formatted with a specific purpose in mind will be a better tool for attaining that purpose.  There are Bibles that are optimized for preaching, Bibles that are suited for reading, Bibles that are appropriate for travel, and more.  The more time we spend considering our own Bible use and how those patterns line up with the Bibles we buy, the more effective our purchases will be.
