*"But now, do not
therefore be grieved or angry with yourselves because you sold me here; for God
sent me before you to preserve life.”*

*--Genesis 45:5*

After Judah’s noble offer to take Benjamin’s punishment,
Joseph reaches the point where he can’t control himself anymore.  He proclaims his identity to his
brothers.  Not surprisingly, their
initial reaction is terror.  After all,
the last time they had seen Joseph, they sold him into slavery, and now he is
in a position where he has the power of life and death over them.  Many people in Joseph’s position would have
taken the opportunity to avenge themselves for their years of suffering.



Joseph, however, shows his true colors in his reply to his
brothers.  He isn’t angry with them
because he recognizes that his life has been shaped by a higher purpose.  Even though his brothers chose to sin in
selling him into slavery, God was able to take their evil actions and use them
to protect his people.  All of the trials
that Joseph endured as a slave and a prisoner were meant to put him in the
right place at the right time.  If Joseph
had never been sold, he would never have gained the ear of Pharaoh, and he
would never have been able to provide for his family through seven years of
famine.  In the midst of his suffering,
Joseph may not have understood it, but looking back, he can see that it was all
for the best.

Sadly, Joseph’s experience of undeserved suffering is not
unique.  Today, many good and righteous
Christians endure trials that they did not invite.  Sooner or later, almost all of us will find
ourselves in that position.  When that
day comes, we need to take heart from Joseph’s wisdom.  The fact that we suffer does not prove that
God does not exist.  It does not even
prove that God does not love us.  God
could have easily prevented Joseph from being sold into slavery, but He chose
to allow it because He knew that He could produce good from Joseph’s brothers’
sin.  He knew that he could use the
suffering of Joseph to prevent greater suffering among His entire people in
just a few more years.

Likewise, even though we do not know it, even though we may
never know it until we begin eternity with God in heaven, God does not allow us
to suffer without reason.  Senseless evil
may happen to the unbeliever, but not to the child of God.  If we respond with faith and patience, our
suffering will always produce a greater good. 
Perhaps that greater good will arise within us.  Christians who pass through trials are
commonly more loving, more dedicated, and more thankful than they were
before.  Perhaps our suffering will give
us new opportunities with others.  Paul
himself spoke in 2 Corinthians 1 of comforting others with the same comfort
with which he was comforted by God.  We
cannot know, but God always knows, and as His children, we can always trust
that He chooses for the best.
