On April 16th, 2006, I preached my first sermon
as the preacher for this congregation. 
Today, almost exactly 11½ years later, I am preaching my last.  This is, without a doubt, the hardest time
I’ve ever had writing a sermon.  The
difficulty comes not only from my emotional involvement with the church here,
but from the finality of the experience.

My work is to declare the whole counsel of God, but every
time before now that I’ve walked down the pulpit stairs, I’ve done so in the
belief that next week I would get another bite at the apple, another
opportunity to tell people I love what they needed to hear.  Today, that’s no longer true.  I’ve run out of time to tell this church
everything, and figuring out the most important somethings is extremely
hard.  With that in mind, please bear
with me as I offer a few parting thoughts.



**Remember These Things**

In the spirit of Paul in Philippians 4 urging the brethren
to “think on these things,” I first want to urge you to remember these three
things.  The first of them is to **LOVE ONE ANOTHER.** Paul captures the importance of this
great commandment in Romans 13:8-10.  As
Paul points out here, love isn’t a do-nothing emotion.  It’s an emotion that demands expression in
every area of life.  If we don’t live
like we love one another, the love isn’t genuine either.

Over the past couple of years, it’s seemed to me that this
congregation has really been making strides in the area of loving one another,
and this loving spirit must continue and abound.

It has to start with the people who are currently members
here.  Over the past couple of months,
it’s become increasingly important to me that I can honestly say that I’m at
peace with everyone here.  I don’t have
anything against anyone, and so far as I know, nobody has anything against
me.  In fact, I’m not aware of any
grievances that any of the brethren here have against one another.  Unity and peace are vital, friends!  Do everything you can to preserve them.

Second, be willing to expand the borders of your love.  The Joliet church must never be a church for
the cool people, the pretty people, or the people who are like us.  It has to be a place where anyone who wants
to seek God is welcome.  It has to be a
place where recovering alcoholics, single moms, and have-nots of every kind can
find a haven.  Jesus loved those
people.  Sometimes, I wonder if He loved
them especially.  A church that belongs
to Him will love them too.

Next, remember to **BE
HOLY.** Here, of course, look at 1
Peter 1:15-16.  Be holy.  Be people who are dedicated to God.  Be a church that is dedicated to God.  This is particularly important when it comes
to Bible authority.  It’s become
fashionable in some circles these days to dismiss the importance of authority,
to say, “We’d rather focus on grace and love instead of on all those tiresome
little rules.”  Brethren, that makes
about as much sense as saying, “In my marriage, I want to focus on loving my
wife instead of on boring chores like washing dishes and taking out the
trash.”  In reality, it is my willingness
to serve my wife and sacrifice for her that reveals my love.  Likewise, our attitude toward following the
first-century pattern reveals our love for Christ.

Second, we must be holy not only when we come together, but
when we are on our own.  Here, I am
particularly concerned with what we do when other Christians aren’t around, and
maybe even when nobody else is around at all. 
I’m particularly concerned with secret sin.  The other day, I learned that a friend of
mine got in trouble with his wife because she caught him looking at porn.  Again. 
That can’t be us, whether it’s porn or whatever other secret sin we
cherish.  We can’t go on clinging to
those things in our hearts even when our mouths express the desire to
change.  That kind of behavior makes a
mockery out of the holiness that God expects from us.

Finally, remember to **SEEK
CHRIST.** We see Paul’s wholehearted
expression of this desire in Philippians 3:7-9. 
It’s funny.  We call ourselves
Christians.  We worship in a church
building that says “church of Christ” on the sign out front.  And yet, somehow, it remains awfully easy to
forget that it’s actually all about Jesus. 
I forget that sometimes, and I’m a preacher!

Don’t forget it. 
Don’t fall into the trap of saying that you love Jesus while spending
your lives treating other things as more important.  Love is an action.  Loving Christ means seeking Christ.  It means making the determination that
knowing Christ and gaining Christ is the primary purpose of your life.  Let everything else fall apart, but you’re
going to get that!

Again, it’s easy to pay lip service to this idea, but I want
you always to be relentlessly honest about whether you’re actually doing
it.  When there’s a conflict between work
and Christ, who wins?  When there’s a
conflict between family and Christ, who wins? 
When there’s a conflict between anything and Christ, who wins?  Paul says, “Be prepared to lose it all, if
that’s what it takes to gain Christ.” 
Nothing else can ever be more important than Jesus, even for a second.

**The Future**

In my last appearance this morning, and indeed ever, I want
to spend some time talking about the future of the church here.  Admittedly, it’s very difficult for me to
contemplate that future without me in it. 
However, even though I still don’t have any kind of prophetic gift, I’m
still pretty sure that there are three things in it that are going to be
important and one that isn’t.

Let’s start with the not-important thing.  The future of the Joliet church is **NOT ABOUT THE PREACHER**, either the one
you hire after me or the ones after that. 
Consider Paul’s comments on the importance of a given preacher in 1
Corinthians 3:5.  Apollos was famous as
an eloquent man.  Paul, by his own
admission, didn’t have much skill as a speaker at all.  And yet, so long as both men served God
faithfully, the differences between them didn’t amount to much.

As some of you know, my search for another work was much
longer and more complicated than I expected when I began it.  In consequence, over the past couple of
years, I’ve had more experience with churches that are looking for preachers than
nearly anybody.  I’ve seen a lot of
congregations look for a preacher the way that some women shop for shoes.  They envision this ideal in their heads, and
unless they find the ideal, they won’t be satisfied.  Unless some preacher is going to come in and
do everything right and transform the church, he won’t be good enough.  Usually, churches like this spend a couple
years wandering in the wilderness, lose people to other congregations or just
plain falling away, and end up hiring somebody they’d never have considered at
the beginning of the process.

It doesn’t hurt, I suppose, to have an ideal in mind, as
long as you’re aware that the elements of the ideal probably don’t exist in one
man.  Churches that want to hire Jesus,
except married and with 2.4 children, are going to be disappointed!  Furthermore, even if a congregation does
succeed in hiring the ideal preacher, the effect of his personality, abilities,
and skills actually will be quite limited.

Trust me on this! 
Since I announced that I was leaving, some of you have come to me and
expressed concern about how things will go here without me, and that’s very
kind of you, but it’s not at all warranted. 
In all my time here, I’ve never had the sense that I was contributing
something that another worker couldn’t.  I
am not the indispensable man.  No
preacher is.

The future of the Joliet church isn’t about the preacher,
but **IT IS ABOUT THE WORD.** Notice where Paul puts his emphasis in
his farewell address to the Ephesian elders in Acts 20:32.  It was true 2000 years ago, and it’s still
true today.  Preaching and teaching isn’t
about the man.  It’s about the
message.  The health of a church doesn’t
depend on the skills of the guy in the pulpit. 
It depends on whether he’s preaching the gospel or not.  Any church that can find a man who will
proclaim the truth faithfully and clearly, and who will live it besides, has
found what it needs.

Of course, for this congregation to thrive, even though the
word has to be on the lips of the preacher, that can’t be the only place where
it is.  The more devoted every member of
the Joliet church is to the word, the more this church will thrive.  The word is where the power is, and the more that
Christians seek out that power for themselves and the more they put it before
others, the more it will accomplish the work that God sent it to do.

This implies my next point, which is that the future of the
Joliet church is **ABOUT YOU.** Consider the words of Paul in Philippians
3:12-14.  Basically, the Christians of
any congregation can make one of two choices. 
Either they can choose to press on according to the upward call of God
in Christ Jesus, or they can choose to wander around at their current spiritual
level.  The collective impact of those
choices will be vastly greater than the impact of any preacher.

Long ago, even before I came here, Max Dawson told me that
every church has a ceiling.  Getting a
congregation to the ceiling is relatively easy; raising the ceiling is very
hard to impossible.  The ceiling of a
congregation, of course, is imposed by nothing other than the spiritual
limitations of its members.  If the
church is to accomplish more, they are the ones who must change.

When a Sunday-morning Christian decides to start coming on
Wednesdays too, that raises the ceiling. 
When a brother or sister steps forward to teach a Bible class for the
first time, that raises the ceiling.  Whenever
somebody volunteers to take on some humdrum but necessary task, or gathers the
courage to talk with a neighbor about the gospel, or checks up on a Christian
who hasn’t been around recently, all those things raise the ceiling.

Conversely, whenever people head in the opposite direction,
when they look to shed responsibilities, when they refuse to help, when they
get apathetic about church attendance or Christianity itself, all of those
things lower the ceiling.  There are
people who are members here who are on both of these paths right now.  I see it. 
I can’t decide for any of you. 
I’ve never been able to do that. 
However, if each of you will choose to rise up, then this church will
prosper.

Finally, and above all else, the future of the Joliet church
is **ABOUT GOD.** Look at Paul’s great doxology in
Ephesians 3:20-21.  Yes, the future of
this church is about you, but at the same time, it really isn’t.  It’s really about what you will allow God to
do through you.

If there is any lesson that I learn from reading through the
Bible, it is that God never loses. 
Sometimes it looks like He does, but He never actually does.  He has a plan, and He will work it out.  When the Babylonians sacked Jerusalem,
destroyed the temple, and carried the Jews away captive, it looked like God had
been defeated and His plan had failed. 
It hadn’t.  When the Son of God
hung on the cross, bleeding and dying while His enemies celebrated around Him,
that too looked like a defeat for God.  It
wasn’t that time either.

I suspect that the years and decades to come will be a bumpy
road for the Joliet church, as for all the churches of Christ in this
country.  On any number of fronts, the
devil looks like he’s winning.  This I
know, though:  he actually isn’t.  God will win, and if all of you will stick with
Him, then you will win too.  Maybe that
means that your life ends up looking like the life of Jeremiah, but today, we
don’t look back on Jeremiah’s life as a failure.  We look at it as a symbol of the faithfulness
of God, even in the midst of great darkness. 
The future always belongs to God, so if this church will continue to
belong to God, the future will belong to it too.
