*And He said, "Let
Me go, for the day breaks." But he said, "I will not let You go
unless You bless me!"*

--Genesis 32:26

In this portion of Genesis 32, we see perhaps the weirdest
story of the weird life of Jacob.  On the
night before Jacob’s uncertain reunion with Esau, he has sent his family away
and is left alone.  That night, a man
appears and, for no reason that the Scripture gives, starts wrestling with
Jacob.  This epic wrestling match lasts
the entire night.  Even when the man
dislocates Jacob’s hip, Jacob refuses to let him go without a blessing.  The man blesses him with the name “Israel”,
which means “wrestles with God”, and so reveals to Jacob that throughout the
entire night, his opponent in the wrestling match was divine.  Jacob limps away, marveling that he has seen
God face to face and yet survived.



This account raises more questions than it answers.  Why did God choose to take on a human
form?  Why did He start wrestling with
Jacob?  Why did Jacob wrestle with
Him?  Why was God anxious to leave once
the day began to break?  Perhaps most of
all, why did God bless Jacob for resisting Him so firmly when He could have
struck him down?  This last question may
be the key to understanding the entire story. 
Jacob wasn’t engaging in this wrestling match to disobey God or escape
from God.  Instead, he clung so
stubbornly to Him because he was seeking a blessing.

In our lives, we must recognize that at times, God may
challenge us in the same way that He challenged Jacob.  It is not, and has never been, God’s design
to make the path to Him as easy and obstacle-free as possible.  God must have known that even most so-called
believers would refuse to be baptized for the forgiveness of sins.  Why didn’t He say, “OK, if you’re so dead-set
on praying the sinner’s prayer, I’ll let that be sufficient for your
salvation”?  In truth, baptism
accomplishes exactly what God wants it to accomplish.  It screens out the unbelieving and
uncommitted so that only people who are truly honest and truly desire His
salvation will find it.

This testing process does not end simply because we become
Christians.  God does not answer our
every prayer the way we want just as soon as the words leave our lips.  He does not remove every hindrance from our
way and protect us from all harm.  He
knows that the hard times prove our faith and force us to grow in ways that the
easy times never can.  Our response in
these difficult times is key.  We must
never say, “I’m going to give up on praying for this, because it’s obviously
not His will.”  We must never say, “If
God loved me and wanted me to go to heaven, He would never have put me through
this.”  In fact, the trials themselves
are a sign of His love.  What He wants to
see from us is Jacob’s refusal to let Him go, and for that refusal, we too will
receive His blessing.
