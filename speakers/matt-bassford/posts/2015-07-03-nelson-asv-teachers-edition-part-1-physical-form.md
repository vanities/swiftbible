**OVERVIEW:** This was an old Bible with a trashed
cover but a decently preserved text block.  
I had it rebound by Leonard’s, and they did a phenomenal job on the
rebind.  My only concern is whether the decades-old
binding thread would hold up to sustained use.

**ORIGIN STORY:** As many of my Bibles are, this one was a
gift from my father.  It had been sitting
on a shelf in his office for the past 50 years or so.  My guess is it had never been used very much.  There are a few pages in it that bear the
hand of my mother, but every page of the Bible she read, she wrote on, so even
she hadn’t spent a great deal of time with it.

Nonetheless, time still took its toll on the Teacher’s
Edition.  By the time I got it, the cover
had red-rotted nearly to the point of disintegration.  As I understand it, red rot in leather is the
result of an overly acidic tanning process, which weakens the leather so much
that it begins to deteriorate in a few decades.

The cover, then, was trashed, but the text block itself was
still in pretty good shape.  It was sewn,
and even after the passage of who knows how many years, the sewing was still
intact.  However, I didn’t dare use it in
its rotted-cover form.  I quickly
realized that opening the text block without support from a functional case
(cover) would lead to the ruin of the text block too.

I determined, then, to rebind the ASV.  The setting, though it isn’t the same as the
ASV reprint that Star Bible offers, is very similar, so starting off with a
decent text block put me $100 ahead of the game.  After a lengthy prelude (which I’ll be
detailing in another post), I sent it off to Leonard’s Book Restoration to be
rebound in matte-black calfskin.  There’s
no point in describing the previous decrepit cover, so my Physical Form
comments will be addressed entirely to the Leonard’s rebind.



**VITAL STATISTICS**

Translation:  American
Standard Version (1901)

Cover:  Calfskin

Binding:  Sewn

Trim Size:  5 ¾” x 8 ¼”

Text Block Width:  1 ½
”

Weight:  2 pounds, 1
ounce

Column:  Double

Layout:  Paragraphed

Print Size:  9

Words of Christ: 
Black

Cross-References:  Yes
(oh my, yes)

Ribbon Marker:  1

**COVER.** The folks at Leonard’s, using magical
arts known only to themselves, determined that the original cover was
pebble-grain genuine leather.  All I know
is that if I had dared to try to hold the Bible by the original cover, I would
have been left with red-rotted fragments in my hand and a text block dumped on
the floor.  Hence the rebind.

I opted to rebind the Bible in matte-black smooth-grain calfskin
(and therein hangs a tale that I’ll be telling in the “Rebind” post).  For various reasons, I was a little uncertain
about how it would come out.  I need not
have worried.  The job that Leonard’s did
with it is quite frankly magnificent.

During my 2014 review of my rebound Zondervan Thinline NASB,
I mentioned several small imperfections with the work that Leonard’s did.  The same is emphatically not true here.  In every dimension, the leather overhang is
perfectly symmetrical.  I suspect if I
got out calipers and measured, it would be exact to the millimeter.  The corners are perfect.  The stamping is perfect.  The ribs on the spine are thin and precisely
executed.  This is one gorgeous Bible,
friends.

I also greatly enjoy the feel of the cover.  I’m such a proletarian Bible-buyer that I’ve
never owned a calfskin Bible before, so when I picked it up, my first thought
was “It’s like TruTone, only better!”  I
now know what all of those smooth-textured poly Bibles are trying to
imitate.  My wife, who is by no means a
Bible connoisseur, stroked it lovingly and informed me that she would like a
Bible bound in calfskin too.

It is even the most wonderful-smelling Bible I have ever
owned.  Most Bible-lovers are book people
too, so I daresay that most reading this have some concept of the way that
libraries smell.  It’s different from
bookstores.  Bookstores smell like fresh
books; libraries smell like gracefully aging older books.  The text block of this Bible, because it is
so old, smells like library.  That scent,
when combined with a very strong leather smell from the calfskin, is probably
about what heaven smells like.  I know it
looks (and sounds) ridiculous, but when I’m using this Bible, I find myself
pausing from time to time to sniff at it.

In short, as far as lovability goes, my ASV rebind is off
the charts.  It is a seductively lovable
Bible.  The rational part of me knows
that the difficult nature of the ASV as a translation makes an ASV Bible a
less-than-ideal choice for preachers in the 21st century.  However, I find myself piling up reasons why
I should use the ASV anyway, at least in part because the cover is so
alluring.  A good Bible cover will make
you want to use the contents.  This one
does.

As far as the more prosaic subject of durability, I don’t
have any concerns here either.  The
calfskin shows no signs of giving out, and the endpapers are the sturdy
leatherette I prefer (naturally, I did not flash the cash to have the Bible edge-lined).  At a guess, the text block is likely to fail
long before the cover does.

**TEXT BLOCK.** I don’t even know whether they made glued
Bibles back 60-plus years ago, but whether or not they did, this one
isn’t.  The text block is as sewn as it
can be.  I got to see this for myself
because the old spine had ripped part of the way off, exposing the back ends of
all those little booklets to my curious gaze. 
If anybody doubts that a Smyth-sewn text block can outlast even a
well-constructed cover, the fate of this Bible provides the proof.

However, I must confess that I do have some fear for the
text block’s durability in future.  Even
now when I open the Bible, the first and last signatures, particularly the
last, don’t fall open in line with the others. 
Instead, they stick out a little bit more.  The thread that connects them to the rest of
the text block just isn’t as tight anymore. 
I put a lot of mileage on the Bibles I use, and even though it may be
that this one would continue just fine without further problems, I fear that it
might not.

**VALUE.** Basically, I asked Leonard’s to do a
resurrection job on my ASV, and they came through wonderfully.  Before, the Bible was unusable; now, I can
preach out of it if I so choose.  As far
as I know, the only sewn, gilded-edge ASV on the market is the bonded-leather
Star Bible edition, which retails for $100. 
If you want an ASV for years of heavy use, you might be best served to
buy that one to get a new text block and then rebind it.  As it is, I now have a usable calfskin ASV
for less than the cost of bonded leather. 
That can only qualify as downright excellent value for money.



**CONCLUSION.** Unlike most of my ratings, this one is
unique to this particular Bible.  I’m as
thrilled as I can be with the rebind. 
However, my concerns about the durability of this text block that is
much, much older than I am knock the score down somewhat.  Here, I give this Bible 8/10.
