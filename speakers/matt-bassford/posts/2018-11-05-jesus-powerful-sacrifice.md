Last week, Shawn taught us about the sad story of the rich
young ruler.  Even though he had a life
filled with good works, it is most likely that he was eternally lost because of
his greed and his pride.  This is the
kind of story that should give us pause. 
If a man like that could be destroyed, how can there be any hope for any
of us, particularly if our sins are greater than his?

And yet, for the disciple of Jesus, we should not approach
the day of judgment with fear, but with confidence.  The apostle John tells us that we can know we
have eternal life.  How can this be?  How can it be true that a man like the rich
young ruler wasn’t good enough to measure up, and yet we have every reason to
say that we are heaven bound for certain? 
Shawn and I are going to be exploring this topic for the next several
weeks, and there are a number of reasons why we can say that we are heaven
bound for certain, but underlying them all is our subject this morning:  the powerful sacrifice of Jesus Christ.



Certainly the most awful thing ever to happen on this earth
was the death of Jesus on the cross, but that death was necessary because of **THE DEMANDS OF GOD’S JUSTICE.** These demands are clearly spelled out in
Ezekiel 18:4.  The fact of the matter is
that every last one of us belongs to God. 
He created us.  He sustains us
every day.  Without Him, we would cease
to exist.  What’s more, He is a good God
who tells us how to live in a way that will please Him and is even best for
us.  His commandments are for our good
always.  He loves us every day of our
lives, and He loves us so much that He wants us to spend eternity with Him.

Sadly, however, we do not respond to all of His love and all
of His blessings as we should.  Like a
bunch of foolish, self-destructive children, we turn aside from the good way to
walk in sin instead.  In our selfishness
and ingratitude, we continually defy our Creator.

Here God is, then, so perfect, loving and good, and here we
are, so ungrateful and evil.  It’s
impossible for a God like Him to tolerate people like us.  He deals with sin in the only way that He
can—by demanding the death of the sinner. 
The price for violating the divine law must be paid, and if we pay it
ourselves, the penalty must be an eternal, spiritual death, cast out from His
presence forever.  Make no mistake,
brethren; this is what every one of us deserves.

However, in addition to His other virtues, God is a God of
mercy.  He has every right to give us
what we deserve, but He doesn’t want to. 
He pities even the worst sinner and wants to save them.  As a result, He decreed that **ATONEMENT THROUGH BLOOD** was
possible.  Look here at Leviticus
17:10-11.  In this text, we learn two
critical things about blood.  First, the
life is in the blood.  For every living
creature, once you remove the blood from our bodies, we’re done for.  As a result, in a spiritual sense, blood
stands for life.

This makes possible the second characteristic of blood.  When it is offered to God according to His
will, it can make atonement for sin. 
This idea was the foundation of the spiritual economy of the Law of
Moses.

The Israelites of 1500 years ago were no better than we
are.  They were sinners too.  However, God still wanted them for His people
and wanted fellowship with them.  As a
result, there had to be some mechanism for dealing with the guilt of their
sins, and that mechanism was animal sacrifice. 
There were many different sacrifices under the Law, but they generally
followed this pattern of atonement for sin.

A man committed some sin, and so he would get the
appropriate animal and come with that animal to the tabernacle or the
temple.  There, the priest would have the
man lay his hands on the head of the animal while the priest killed it, poured
out its blood on the altar, and offered its body as a sacrifice.  As a result of this ritual, the guilt that
the Israelite incurred was transferred to the sacrifice, and it died so that he
could live.

However, this solution to the problem led only to another
problem:  **THE FAILURE OF ANIMAL SACRIFICE.**Here, consider Hebrews 10:1-4. 
Yes, you had all those priests under the law faithfully standing at the
altar offering sacrifices, year after year, for 1500 years.  However, there was a problem.  Those sacrifices, though they were enough for
a bodily, outward cleansing, were never enough to make the worshiper perfect in
conscience.  The problem was the entity
being sacrificed.  The lifeblood of a
bull or a goat couldn’t take away sins.

Once we start thinking about this, it makes perfect
sense.  After all, human beings are very
different from livestock.  We are created
in the image of God.  A bull or a goat
isn’t.  We have free will and can choose
to disobey our Creator.  A bull or a goat
can’t.

As a result, a human being trying can’t pay off the debt of
his sin with the blood of a bull or a goat. It would be about like me going
over to our game cabinet, taking out Monopoly, getting some Monopoly money out,
and trying to use that to pay my mortgage. 
In both cases, the currency just isn’t up to the task.  The life of a mindless beast isn’t valuable
enough to exchange for my life.

If I do send that Monopoly money to the bank, they might
laugh for a little bit, but they’ll send me a letter informing me that I still
haven’t paid what I owe.  In the same
way, the daily and yearly offering of the blood of animals was nothing more
than a reminder of the sins of the people. 
It didn’t pay the life-debt that those people owed God, and it could
never do so.

Instead, what was needed was **A BETTER SACRIFICE.** The
Hebrews writer continues on to explain in Hebrews 10:5-9.  Here, he bases his argument on a chilling
quotation from Psalm 40.  In this text,
David is prophetically speaking for Christ. 
He observes just what we’ve already commented on—that God was not
satisfied with burnt offerings and animal sacrifices.  Instead, to solve the problem God has
prepared a body for His Son.  As was
prophesied, Jesus came to earth to fulfill His Father’s will completely.

This was true in two main senses.  First, Jesus met the challenge that has
defeated all the rest of us.  Even though
He was tempted as we are, He remained sinless. 
Like the animals that were sacrificed under the Law were supposed to be
physically unblemished, Jesus was spiritually unblemished.

However, it was God’s will not only that Jesus should live
for Him, but that Jesus should die for all of us.  The sacrifice of an unblemished animal wasn’t
good enough.  Because of our sin, none of
us could save ourselves, much less save others. 
Jesus, though, possessed both free will and perfection.  He wasn’t merely a better sacrifice.  He was the best imaginable sacrifice.  His life was worthy to be given in exchange
for our lives, so He laid that life down for us.

Because of the power of that sacrifice, Jesus died **ONCE FOR ALL.** Let’s finish up our reading from Hebrews
10 with Hebrews 10:10-14.  When we think
about it, Jesus’ life isn’t merely equivalent to our lives.  Instead, because He is the Son of God and we
aren’t, it was and is infinitely more precious.

As a result, Jesus didn’t merely redeem one person who
believed in Him from sin.  He didn’t
merely redeem all the Christians of the first century.  Instead, His sacrifice reached both back and
forward in time, fully saving all of God’s faithful people under the Law and
also rescuing everyone who will put their trust in Him, all the way to the end
of time.  He died once for all, and after
His offering of Himself, He sat down at the right hand of the throne of God,
because there was no more work for Him to do.

You know, every time I look up at the night sky, I am awed
by the power of my Creator.  However, the
sacrifice of Jesus may reveal God’s power even more impressively than the
creation.  The value of the blood that He
shed to redeem us is greater than the worth of everything in the physical
universe.

This, brethren, is why we can be certain that we are heaven
bound.  This is why we can know that we
have eternal life.  It’s not that we’re
better than the rich young ruler, so that unlike him, we can do good deeds in
order to inherit eternal life.  If we try
to go down that road, we will fail just like the ruler did.

Instead, we can know that we have eternal life because we
have placed our trust in the power of Jesus to save us.  It’s not about us and our perfection.  It’s about Him and His perfection.  If we are faithful to Him, He will never
disappoint us.
