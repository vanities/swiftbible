Back at the beginning of this year, when I started talking
online about my dad’s brand-new wide-margin SCR, I couldn’t figure out some of
the comments I was getting.  To me, one
of the striking things about the new wide-margin text block was its slender
proportions.  It was a good ⅜” thinner
than my narrow-margin SCR from 2007. 
However, other online commenters didn’t see much difference at all in
thickness between the two text blocks.  I
was baffled.

Then, several months later, I realized the truth.  All narrow-margin Foundation SCR’s are not
created equal, even though they share the same ISBN.  At some point, Foundation switched production
from a bulky narrow-margin with grayish-yellow paper (which is the Bible I used
through the main part of this review) to a much trimmer volume with
higher-quality paper that was both whiter and more opaque.  I don’t know when the switch was made, but my
skinny SCR was printed in 2011.



To an SCR fanatic like me, this was a revelation.  I tolerated the paper in my bulky SCR because
the setting was so good that it overcame the paper’s deficiencies.  In the newer narrow-margins, the paper no
longer has to be tolerated.  It’s not as
opaque as the paper in the 2002 wide-margin SCR, but that was some of the most
opaque paper I’ve ever seen in a Bible.

Plus, the more opaque paper added bulk, so that the old
wide-margins weighed in at a hefty 3 pounds, 5 ounces.  The newer narrow-margins weigh almost a pound
less than that, and I’m willing to sacrifice opacity to save weight.  As far as I’m concerned, with the better
paper, smaller size, and lighter weight, the newer narrow-margin is the perfect
text block.  I don’t write in Bibles, so
all the white space in the new wide-margins is wasted on me.

Only one thing mars my contentment and delight:  cockling. 
As I noted in the main review, my bulky narrow-margin SCR has moderate
cockling.  I can only describe the
cockling in my newer narrow-margin as severe. 
I don’t know whether this impression is accurate, but it seems to me
that the cockling is the result of the binding thread being pulled too tight
during the manufacturing process.  After
I received the Bible, I went through the breaking-in process about half a dozen
times, which seems to have calmed the cockling down somewhat (by stretching out
the binding thread, maybe?), but it’s still present and still annoying.  However, it’s not enough to keep me from
using the Bible, and it’s entirely possible that other skinny narrow-margins
don’t have cockling issues at all.

Foundation isn’t printing the skinny narrow-margin SCR
anymore, but it’s still available online if you look around (I think evangelicalbible.com still has a few, for instance).  If you don’t write in your Bibles a whole lot
and you’re looking for a new NASB, I’d check it out.
