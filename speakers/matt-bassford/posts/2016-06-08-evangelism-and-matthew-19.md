So far this year, I’ve baptized four outsiders, and I’m studying/working with two more.  With every single one of these people, the issue of marriage, divorce, and remarriage has arisen.  There have been close family members in unscriptural marriages, seekers themselves in second marriages whose scripturality must be verified, and even those who know perfectly well that their marriages aren’t right in the eyes of God and must be dissolved before they can come to Him.


It’s probably still a little bit of a statistical anomaly for me to be encountering these issues with absolutely everybody I study with.  However, as our society grows increasingly messy, questions concerning marriage, divorce, and remarriage grow increasingly common.  In many ways, these situations turn our normal reactions and behaviors on their heads.  Over the past couple of years, I’ve found myself in the bizarre position of actively encouraging people to divorce (albeit to divorce an unlawful spouse) and being actively relieved to learn that someone’s much older children were conceived out of wedlock!  I would never condone a couple living in sin for 10 years, yet the reality is that it’s much easier to clean up the mess from an immoral relationship than to clean up the mess from a failed first marriage.

I don’t enjoy raising Matthew 19:3-9 issues with prospects, at least in part because of the potential for the ensuing conversations to get extremely unpleasant.  There’s nothing that will make your heart sink quite like studying with a couple whom you have been told have the right to be married, only to learn from their own words that they do not!  However, I’m convinced that if I don’t raise the subject, I am not being true to my charge to preach the word, in season and out of season.  I must talk about these things, and so must every evangelist.  With that in mind, I thought it might be useful to others for me to outline the principles that I follow in having these discussions:

**Honor God’s Word.**  I suspect that all of us would rank Matthew 19:9 among the hard sayings of Jesus.  These are the kind of words that can pierce the heart and demand a radical life change.  However, their difficulty does not make them any less a saying of the Lord.  Either we follow His words, or we don’t.

In this, we must beware not only of outright defiance, but of elaborate attempts to wiggle out from under the commandment.  In my studies with people who don’t know the Bible, I’ve learned not only that this is a hard saying, but that it is a simple one.  I’ve studied with people whose reading-comprehension skills aren’t the greatest, but none of them have ever missed the point.  Everybody gets what Jesus is saying, even when the personal implications are profound.

We need to be extremely suspicious, then, of efforts to introduce complexity into a simple text.  I certainly understand what’s behind such attempts.  I’ve been so upset after Matthew 19 studies that I couldn’t get to sleep that night.  We don’t want to put others through that, and we don’t want to go through it ourselves.  However, concealing the truth behind a veil of subtle redefinitions and distorted Scripture does no good for anybody.

Besides, once we surrender the truth on marriage, divorce, and remarriage, we will soon find ourselves surrendering it on the practice of homosexuality, transgender behavior, and a host of other issues.  In my experience, the conversations I have with young people who are struggling with same-sex attraction are no less painful.  In both cases, I can find myself telling somebody that a life of celibacy is the price of discipleship.  If we let our sympathies overcome us in one area, there’s no reason not to allow the same thing in the other.

**Build a Relationship First.**  These days, I almost always prefer to share a meal with prospects before I will study with them.  Even if not a word about the Bible is said during that meal, I still believe that such time is critically important.  It gives me an opportunity to get a read on what kind of people they are, and even more importantly, it allows them the opportunity to see what kind of a man I am.  They must know, understand, and gut-level get that I love them and am sincerely concerned for their good.  Only then, after they’ve had the opportunity to see the love, can they best appreciate my motives for speaking the truth.

**Be Straightforward.**  I had my tendency to procrastinate beaten out of me in law school, but if ever there were a conversation we might want to put off, it is this one!  However, it’s far better to seek and find resolution, whatever that resolution might be, than to allow the unanswered question to overshadow everything else.  How sad it would be for me to baptize a soul into Christ, only to see them lost because of the unscriptural marriage I never warned them about!

In this regard, I can only think of the famous “watchman” passage in Ezekiel 3.  If I warn the sinner, and he does not repent, his blood is on his own head.  However, if I don’t warn him so that he does not repent and is lost, his blood is on mine.  I will be called to account for it.

When the consequences can be so serious for all involved, we dare not delay or beat around the bush.  I invariably ask prospects about their marital status.  If the situation requires it, we go to Matthew 19.  Often, even when the study isn’t necessary, people will want to know why I’m asking, so we go to Matthew 19 anyway.

Even though this is an ugly subject, I believe that my willingness to introduce it has powerfully positive consequences.  People who will become disciples by definition have honest hearts.  They want to know the truth.  They’re not blind to the implications.  I’ve had more than one student observe, “So if my marital status were different, I’d have to put away my wife in order to become a Christian.”  They see that if I’m willing to teach truth on that subject, even with its potential to drive them away from the Lord entirely, then I will teach them the truth on any subject.  They know the honesty in their hearts; now they can see the honesty in my heart too.
