*But Jesus, conscious
that His disciples grumbled at this, said to them, “Does this cause you to
stumble?  What then if you see the Son of
Man ascending to where He was before?”*

*--John 6:61-62*

Same-sex marriage is in the news these days.  Again. 
The Supreme Court is getting set to rule on whether it is
unconstitutional for states to prohibit same-sex marriage within their
borders.  Though the debate about the
practice of homosexuality has a wide-ranging political dimension, I am more
concerned with its religious impact. 
Every few months, another prominent theologian or religious leader comes
out in support of gay marriage.  Many
denominations accept practicing homosexuals into full membership, and the
number of such "tolerant” churches is increasing steadily.

This is particularly strange when the Bible, the
foundational document for any form of Christianity, condemns the practice of
homosexuality in no uncertain terms. 
Many have invested great effort in asserting that Romans 1:26-27 and 1
Corinthians 6:9-10 don’t actually say what they say, but such efforts are invariably
the product of beginning with the desired answer and reasoning backward from
it, rather than beginning with the text and reasoning forward.  If Paul described the practice of
homosexuality as “degrading”, “unnatural”, “indecent”, and “error”, yet didn’t
believe it to be any of those things, then how can we have any confidence in
his ability to communicate any idea?



More straightforwardly, many self-described Christians
simply reject the Pauline condemnation altogether.  Yes, they acknowledge, he said those hurtful
things, but he was the product of a particular place and time, and now that the
human race has become more enlightened, we should no longer cling to the
teachings of such a backward and superstitious era.

Even though this position has an undeniable appeal, it is
far more dangerous than the Scriptural distortion of the first school.  Rather than being an assault on a particular
text, it is actually an assault on the fabric of Christianity itself.  If what Paul writes about the practice of
homosexuality is not true without reservation, how can we be certain of the
truth of anything else in the New Testament? 
If Romans 1 and 1 Corinthians 6 fall, the Biblical narrative of the
death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus falls with them.

Indeed, it is more reasonable to accept that God condemns
the practice of homosexuality than it is to accept that Christ rose from the
dead.  After all, plenty of non-Western
societies through the millennia have condemned homosexual activity without
much, if any, reference to the Bible.  If
people can conclude that homosexuality is unnatural, why wouldn’t God?

On the other hand, the resurrection of Jesus is an
extraordinary event.  No living human has
ever seen a dead man rise.  According to the laws of nature, it is impossible.  We believe that Christ has risen only because
of the Biblical proofs of its own authenticity. 
If those proofs are enough to convince us that the Lord is alive, they
are also enough to convince us that homosexuality is morally wrong.  If they are insufficient to convince us of
the immorality of same-sex intimacy, they cannot be enough to convince us that
Jesus rose from the dead.

At this point, many religious advocates of same-sex marriage
single out Paul for special rejection. 
They believe in the gospels, they claim, but not in the moral authority
of that woman-hating, gay-hating zealot! 
Logically speaking, though, Jesus and Paul stand and fall together.

First of all, Luke, who is our main source for biographical
information about the life of Paul, is also one of our main sources for
information about Jesus.  In fact, if
Luke is wrong about anything, he is more likely to be wrong about Jesus than
wrong about Paul.  Luke never claims to
have been an eyewitness to the miracles of Jesus.  He carried out his investigations in Palestine
decades after the ministry of the Lord.

By contrast, Luke was an eyewitness to much of the ministry
of Paul.  When Paul raised Eutychus from
the dead in Troas in Acts 20, Luke was there. 
If Luke is a credible source, one of the first things that we must
accept is that Paul worked miracles, which confirms everything Paul taught.  If we deny the authority of Paul, we must
also deny the credibility of Luke and of everything he writes about Jesus.

Second, the single earliest record we have (and therefore
one of the best pieces of evidence) of the resurrection of Jesus from the dead
is 1 Corinthians 15, which was written by Paul and is part of an epistle that
also condemns homosexuality.  If we can
have no confidence in 1 Corinthians 6, we can have no confidence in 1
Corinthians 15.  If it isn’t reliable, on
what basis do we accept later accounts?



There are certainly many churches that attempt to limp
between two opinions by accepting Bible teaching on the resurrection while
rejecting  Bible teaching on
homosexuality.  However, such efforts are
not logically sustainable.  If we affirm
that Jesus is Lord on the basis of reason, we must also affirm the teaching of
His apostles on the same basis.  If, by
contrast, we try to sustain our faith with half a Bible, we will inevitably end
up with no Bible and no faith.
