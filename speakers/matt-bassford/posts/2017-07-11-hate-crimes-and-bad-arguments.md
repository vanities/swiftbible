Yesterday morning, I encountered another pulse in the
drumbeat of cultural division in America. 
According to [this article](http://www.msn.com/en-us/news/us/the-state-of-hate-in-america/ar-BBE4pA5), the number of hate crimes is surging across the nation.  Though I’m more than a little suspicious of
the Southern Poverty Law Center (which is the source of the data), I’m willing
to accept the claim at face value for the sake of argument.  The article’s author attributes this increase
to the candidacy and presidency of Donald Trump, who, alas, is sometimes given
to making racist/sexist remarks.

I don’t find this argument particularly compelling.  Trump isn’t a driver of conservative opinion;
he’s an expression of it.  People didn’t
vote for Trump because he said things they never thought of before.  They voted for him because he said what they
were already thinking.

However, that only pushes the question back a level.  Why are racial tensions higher now than they
were 10 years ago?  Why does a
significant segment of the population seem to have become less tolerant?



Here too, the pat progressive answer is that the presence of
Barack Obama in the White House got all the racists fired up.  That may be, but if this is an Obama problem,
why has it gotten worse after he left office and was replaced by somebody whom
the racists found much more acceptable?

Honestly, I don’t think this apparent resurgence of racism
is an action.  I think it’s a
reaction.  In particular, I think the
liberal extension of identity politics to ever more bizarre sexual practices
has increasingly led conservatives to reject the entire identity-politics
edifice as illegitimate.

The basic problem here is the way that the gay-rights
movement has co-opted the language and moral authority of the civil-rights
movement.  The argument goes that the two
are morally equivalent.  “Good people”
support unfettered sexual autonomy just as they support racial equality.  If you don’t support unfettered sexual
autonomy, then you’re a bigot just as somebody who doesn’t support racial
equality is a racist.

From my perspective, though, those two movements are not the
same thing at all.  Certainly, our
society puts all sorts of complex constructions on race, but when you get right
down to it, race is something that you are. 
People have African DNA or Native-American DNA or Asian DNA.  They are indisputably “born this way”.

According to the New Testament, race is a morally neutral
attribute.  The equality of Jew and
Gentile is one of the great themes of the writing of the apostle Paul, and to a
lesser extent, even of the preaching of Jesus. 
Certainly, racists in years past cherry-picked Scriptures to justify
unbiblical practices like race-based slavery and the Jim Crow laws, but that
proves nothing more than that people who are of a mind to do so can twist the
Scriptures to their own destruction.  As
a result, I am emphatically pro-racial equality and accepting of racial
difference in others.  Jesus calls me to
be.

By contrast, sexual autonomy is about what you do, not what
you are.  It’s certainly true that the
origins of various kinds of sexual attraction are murky.  Personally, I suspect that people are
attracted to their own gender for a wide variety of reasons, some of which may
well have a genetic component.  I don’t
know, and the issue isn’t terribly relevant to the analysis anyway.

After all, neither the Bible nor secular law concern
themselves with sexual attraction.  They
concern themselves with sexual practice, with those who want to be intimate
with those of the same sex, get married to somebody of the same sex, and so on.  Somebody who experiences same-sex attraction
and doesn’t act on it gives rise to neither moral nor legal consequences.  Only those who act on their attractions do.

The gay-rights movement, then, is about choice.  It is plainly evident in [this GLAAD-sponsored survey](http://www.nbcnews.com/feature/nbc-out/survey-20-percent-millennials-identify-lgbtq-n740791) that came out a few months back (again, GLAAD isn’t the most unbiased
organization out there, but if they’re willing to say it, I’m willing to repeat
it).  The survey found that as attitudes
toward various sexual practices have liberalized, the frequency of those
practices has increased greatly.

If sexual minority = racial minority, this shouldn’t
happen.  After all, there wasn’t some
great upsurge of people declaring that they were black after the Civil Rights
Act.  The group of African Americans was
the same in 1965 as it had been in 1963. 
It just got more rights.  Rachel
Dolezal aside, people don’t choose to be black.

However, apparently it is true that the more socially acceptable
a given sexual practice becomes, the more people will engage in it.  This is hardly surprising to students of the
Bible.  In fact, Romans 1 predicts
it.  This is a narrative about choice,
not about people who are innately some way that they can’t change.

Furthermore, in stark
contrast to its discussion of race, the Bible unequivocally condemns these
various sexual choices as sinful.  This
isn’t merely a matter of Romans 1:26-27 and 1 Corinthians 6:9-10.  Instead, it derives from the Bible’s basic
perspective on sexual ethics.  The core
principle of godly sexuality appears in Genesis 2:24, and it receives further
elaboration from Jesus in Matthew 19:3-9 (accompanied by a discussion of those
who “have made themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven” in
19:10-12).  God’s rule is simple:  one man, one wife, for life.  Anything beyond that, whatever it might be,
is of evil.

However, despite these differences, the gay-rights movement
insists that conservatives must accept and welcome choices that they believe
are morally illegitimate.  If necessary,
the coercive power of the law will be deployed to guarantee this welcome, like
it or not.  Is it any wonder, then, when
conservatives who feel under threat push back at the polls, even if that
requires them to vote for a candidate whom they believe is flawed?

What’s more, people who a) believe the sexual-rights
movement is illegitimate and b) accept the progressive linkage between the
sexual-rights movement and the civil-rights movement are likely to conclude
that the civil-rights movement itself is also illegitimate.  It’s the same group of people telling them
what to do and think.  If they’re so
badly wrong about homosexuality, maybe they’re equally badly wrong when it
comes to different races!

Bad arguments are bad, and when you make them, they tend to
lead others to suspect that your other arguments might be bad too.  In this case, I think the results are
tragic.  I hate to imagine that our
country is actually growing less tolerant, and I preach with some regularity on
racial issues.  However, unless race is
de-coupled from sexual autonomy, pushback on the latter will inevitably translate
into pushback on the former.
