Our congregation is devoted to following the pattern of the
New Testament.  This pattern restricts
our behavior in many ways.  Less
obviously, though, within that pattern, God gives us a great deal of freedom,
and He expects us to use that freedom to best glorify Him.

This morning, then, we’ll be using our Christian liberty to
try something different.  Rather than
keeping the Lord’s Supper and the sermon separate, we’re going to use the
sermon to focus our attention on the Lord’s Supper.  That way, instead of only having five minutes
or so to prepare ourselves to partake, we’re going to have half an hour.

During this process, we’re going to use Psalm 22.  This psalm is ideally suited to provoke our
reflections for two reasons.  First, it
gives us more insight into Jesus’ frame of mind than nearly any other passage
in the Bible.  Second, even though the
psalm was written a thousand years before Jesus’ birth, it prophetically
describes His death in exact detail, thus showing that Jesus truly was the Son
of God.  As we look toward the Lord’s
Supper, then, let’s consider this psalm together.



The psalm begins with a complaint about **A GOD WHO DOESN’T ANSWER.** Let’s
read from Psalm 22:1-5.  These words, “My
God, my God, why have You forsaken Me?” are the first fulfilled prophecy of the
psalm.  They are, of course, one of
Jesus’ dying utterances on the cross. 
Through the years, there has been a great deal of debate about whether
and how God actually forsook Jesus.  I
don’t want to get into that this morning, but whatever else Jesus meant by
that, He certainly meant for everyone to apply Psalm 22 to His suffering and
death.

On the one hand, the psalmist prophetically notes for Jesus
that God is faithful.  Since the
beginning, the fathers of the Jews have trusted in God, and He has rescued
them.  However, as vs. 1-2 note, in
Jesus’ case, that isn’t happening.  Jesus
has been faithful to God.  In fact, He
has been more faithful than anyone else, ever. 
He’s crying out to God continually, and according to everything that has
gone before, God should be rescuing Him too. 
In the case of Jesus, everything that the Old Testament has revealed
about God seems to have been thrown out the window.

We know how the story turns out, but that should not lead us
to underestimate the depth of Jesus’ anguish about this.  Even though God is just, what is happening to
Jesus is fundamentally unjust.  In fact,
it’s the most unjust thing that has ever happened to anybody.  None of us like it when we feel like God is
letting us down.  How much more painful
must that have been for the Lord?

The next segment of the psalm is about being **MOCKED BY MANKIND.** Let’s keep going in Psalm 22:6-10.  Once again, this does not make for easy
reading.  As David points out prophetically
for Jesus, Jesus has belonged to God since the beginning of His life.  Figuratively speaking, He has trusted God
since before He was eating solid food.

Now, though, His reward for all of His devotion and
faithfulness is getting humiliated by everyone who sees Him.  Note just how brutally specific this prophecy
is.  The Romans intentionally crucified
Jesus on a hill next to a road, so that any passer-by who felt like it could
stop and sneer at Him.  What’s more, His
enemies among the chief priests and scribes made sure to come by.  In exactly the words that David predicted
they would use, they said, “If You’re so close to God, let’s see God show up
and rescue You!”

I don’t care how tough-minded you are.  When you’re in the midst of great physical
pain, and you have all of these vicious, cruel people standing around you and
mocking you, that’s enough to break you. 
Jesus’ emotional torment on the cross may well have been as great as His
physical torment.  In fact, speaking for
Him, David says, “I don’t feel like a man. 
I feel like a worm.”

Next, the psalm presents Jesus as **SURROUNDED BY HIS ENEMIES.** Look
at Psalm 22:11-21.  We’ve already seen
how brutal the enemies of Jesus were in their treatment of Him.  Now, we see the psalmist describe them as
wild animals.  They’re bulls.  They’re lions.  They’re wild dogs.  They’ve forfeited any claim to humanity, and
they are downright bestial in their conduct.

Notice, though, what specific actions David attributes to
these wild beasts.  They’re surrounding
him.  They’re dividing up his garments
and casting lots for his clothing. 
They’re piercing his hands and his feet. 
They’re staring at him and gloating over him.  Finally, they’re laying him in the dust of
death.  They’re killing him.

I have no idea what David thought he was saying when he was
writing those things in the first person. 
However, it is clear that they apply precisely to the crucifixion of
Jesus.  In fact, David clearly predicts
that Jesus would be crucified hundreds of years before crucifixion was first
used as a form of execution.  Nobody in
David’s time was crucifying anybody, and yet, here we have him talking about
Jesus’ hands and feet being pierced and His bones being out of joint.  We have that bizarre detail about Jesus’
enemies gambling for His clothes, and the Roman guards do that too!

This passage is one of the reasons I’m a Christian.  I’ve thought about this a lot, and the only
way that I can explain such a close correspondence between Psalm 22 and the
death of Jesus is the supernatural intervention of God.  I don’t think there’s any way to be honest
with the Scriptures and not reach that conclusion.

However, at this point of the psalm, things change
dramatically.  The psalmist is now
anticipating God’s deliverance and the good things that will happen as a
result.  The first of these is that **HE WILL PRAISE GOD.**  Consider Psalm 22:22-26.  Even here, we run into prophecy.  According to Hebrews 2:11-12, this is a
prediction that Jesus would regard the congregation as His brothers and
sisters, which was certainly borne out.

Additionally, though, this psalm anticipates that Jesus will
rise from the dead.  As we saw just a few
verses back, He was laid in the dust of death. 
Now, though, He’s glorifying God. 
There’s no way for that to happen unless God’s faithful servant comes
back to life.

What’s more, this predicts that God’s treatment of Jesus
will vindicate Him and prove His faithfulness. 
Hasn’t that happened too?  Shawn
preached a whole lesson on the faithfulness of God last week.  We still care about Abraham and Sarah and
David and all the rest.

However, the one event that establishes God’s faithfulness
more than any other is the resurrection of Jesus.  It proves for all time that even death can’t
keep God from protecting and glorifying His own.  Back in the beginning of Psalm 22, it looked
like Jesus’ suffering would call God’s promises into question.  Ultimately, though, Jesus’ resurrection
confirmed those promises as strongly as they could be confirmed.

Psalm 22 concludes by predicting that **ALL WILL REMEMBER** what God has done for the psalmist.  Look at Psalm 22:27-31.  Because of God’s deliverance, all the nations
will come to Him.  Everybody who goes
down to the dust—that is, everyone who dies—will bow to Him.  From generation to generation, the memory of
God’s great faithfulness will be handed down.

If you think about it, this is the most audacious prophecy
of them all.  Who could have guessed that
nailing some Jewish peasant to a cross would be an event that would be remembered
for as long as the earth continues?  And
yet, that is exactly what has happened.

This prophecy continues to be fulfilled today.  In fact, we are about to fulfill it.  Aren’t we gathered here to remember both the
suffering and the glory of Jesus, just like David said we would 3000 years
ago?  That, friends, is awe-inspiring!

As we partake of the Lord’s Supper, let’s think about these
things.  Let’s remember the anguish and
suffering of Jesus as He died on the cross. 
Let’s remember the wisdom of God, which predicted these things a
thousand years before they happened. 
We’re not here for a lie, brethren. 
We’re here because Jesus truly was the Son of God, and the prophecies
prove it.  Finally, let’s remember God
raising Jesus from the dead, an action that proves His faithfulness and
glorifies His name for all time.  Let’s
consider all this as we partake.
