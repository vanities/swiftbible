Like most human beings with an Internet connection, I’ve
spent quite a bit of time over the past week reading about the slow-motion
disaster of the Ashley Madison scandal. 
For those of you who have been living under a rock, Ashley Madison is an
Internet dating site sporting the slogan, “Life is short.  Have an affair.”  If you want to find somebody online to cheat
with, you go to Ashley Madison.



However, some enterprising group, for reasons they haven’t
completely disclosed, stole the personal data of millions of Ashley Madison
customers.  After giving the company a
month to shut down, they published the data on the Internet last week.  Since then, nearly every day has brought some
new development in the story, be it some new hypocrite uncovered or some new lawsuit
filed against the infidelity broker.

I admit that when the story first broke, I was as gleeful
about it as anybody.  I thought
immediately of Numbers 32:23.  Yep; even
3500 years after Moses said it, we can still be sure that our sin will find us
out.

However, as I continued to follow the goings-on, I felt a
growing sense of unease.  First of all,
there are absolutely no good guys anywhere in the whole thing.  The Ashley Madison people tried to get rich
by enabling sin.  All the Ashley Madison
clients, even though they may not have succeeded in having an affair (Internet
scuttlebutt has it that about 99 percent of AM users were male), they certainly
wanted to.

Even the nameless hackers are no heroes.  They got all that personal data by stealing
it.  That’s evil and wrong, full
stop.  I hope that the relevant
governmental unit is able to track them down, prosecute them, and punish them.

Out of such a wretched hive of scum and villainy (to borrow
a phrase), is it possible that any good can come?

Worse still, though, is the undisguised satisfaction with
which many Christians have been watching the scandal unfold.  Yeah, yeah; we get to see all of the
villainous wretches get their comeuppance. 
It’s all been no threat to me personally.  I can’t deny that part of me has been
enjoying the spectacle too.  Josh Duggar
uncovered?  Pass the popcorn!

However, in order to enjoy it, I have to forget one
all-important fact.  On my own merits, I
am no better than the worst sinner who ever logged on to the site.  I haven’t sinned in that way, but I have
certainly sinned.  I have no more claim
to my own righteousness than any of them do. 
My only hope is the blood of Jesus Christ, and I live each day only by
His mercy.  In short, who am I to judge?

I’m no adulterer, but I don’t have any trouble understanding
why someone might want to be.  Here’s
some poor slob who’s dissatisfied with his marriage.  He feels bored; he feels trapped.  The devil enters his heart and begins
painting vivid pictures of all the fun he would have if he had an affair.  Deceived, he logs on to the Ashley Madison
website and gets out his credit card.  He
probably never got that affair (although I’m sure that AM billed him either
way), but now he’s outed, and his life is ruined.  Maybe he loses his job.  Maybe his wife divorces him.  Maybe he commits suicide.

Tempted, deceived, ruined. 
I can’t sneer at somebody who looks like that.  He looks too much like me.

And yet, this empathy, this sense of fellow feeling that I
think all of us would experience if the AM clients were people we worshiped
with, is absent from the online drama. 
Instead of reacting with gentleness, looking to ourselves, lest we also
be tempted, we hoot and applaud as each new victim is paraded before us.  From every pore, we exude the sour stench of
self-righteousness.

The Internet is sneaky like that.  For some reason, when we’re online, it’s easy
for us to forget that we’re supposed to be decent people.  This is most obvious when it comes to anger
and contention, but it may be an equally large problem in remembering to be
merciful.  In the AM scandal, all of the
villains are also victims.  Except for
the devil, villains always are.

Rather than applauding, we ought to be mourning, mourning
all the shattered lives the story is strewing in its wake, mourning even the
godless emptiness of soul that makes things like Ashley Madison possible.  This is nothing more than another act in the
long tragedy of mankind as we try to find our hapless way through this
benighted world.

As we have opportunity, we should work to ameliorate the
harm.  Even if we don’t have opportunity,
we still don’t get to stand back and laugh. 
None of us really need more self-righteousness than we’ve already
got.  Just about all of us could stand to
develop a little more compassion.

Now would be a good time to practice.
