*Finally, brethren,
whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is right, whatever is pure,
whatever is lovely, whatever is of good repute, if there is any excellence and
if anything worthy of praise, dwell on these things.*

--Philippians 4:8 (NASB)

In many ways, the impact of the recent election on my
Facebook news feed was like the wake of an invisible ship.  I saw lots of reminders of our calling as
Christians regardless of election results, with which I wholeheartedly
agreed.  No matter the season, we should
proclaim the kingdom of God and do all things without grumbling or
disputing.  I also saw a handful of
complaints about election posts, but I saw very few election posts themselves.



This is typical for me. 
Even though I’m on Facebook quite a bit, I rarely see status updates that disturb
my equilibrium.  However, I know that my
experience is far from typical for others. 
I have friends who are considering doing away with Facebook altogether
because they feel that it drowns them in a torrent of negativity.

I don’t see negative things on Facebook for a very simple
reason.  I quickly unfollow the people
who post them.  To wit:

- If 90 percent of somebody’s Facebook posts contain snarky
political cartoons, I unfollow them.

- If they share articles with titles like “Obama Reveals True Nature by Growing
Hitler Mustache” and “Republicans Eat Minority Children at Fundraisers”, I unfollow
them.

- If they continually complain about other people, whether
directly or in veiled terms, I unfollow them.

- If they habitually begin and participate in
hundred-comment rant threads, I a) send them a private message encouraging them
to wiser behavior, and b) unfollow them if they don’t listen.

As a result, my Facebook news feed is filled with baby
pictures, Scriptural musings, prayer requests, college-football banter, appeals
for hymn critique, and chatter about high-end Bibles.  It makes me smile.  I think it makes me a better person, too,
especially the prayer requests.  They
remind me to pray, and prayer for others is always an ennobling act!

For my part, I do my best to post only that which will
cheer, amuse, encourage, and edify.  I
try to remember that nobody’s mind has ever been changed by an argument on the
Internet.  I am by nature a contentious
man, so this is a work in progress.

I also try not to be judgmental.  I know plenty of decent, good people who post
thoughtlessly online.  Even as I exile
them from my news feed, I try to remember them for their real-life selves,
rather than their Internet self-caricatures. 
How often I have needed grace from others for my own failings!

However, it is true for all of us that what we read has a
dramatic influence on what we think and feel. 
If we struggle with negative thoughts and negative feelings, we must
banish negative influences, from our Facebook feeds and our lives.  It’s good spiritual hygiene, no more and no
less.
