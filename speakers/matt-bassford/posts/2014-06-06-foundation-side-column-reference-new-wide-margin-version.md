In December 2013, Foundation
discontinued the narrow-margin SCR that I have (sniff, sniff) and replaced it
with a wide-margin version.  Actually,
this isn’t so much a replacement as a reversion.  The original SCR had wide margins when it
came out in about 1997.  Ten years ago, I
ruined two of those Bibles myself.  Then,
sometime around 2005, Foundation began to print the narrow-margin
exclusively.  I don’t have one of the new
wide-margins, but I do own one of the old ones, so I can still talk about the
format a little bit.  Note that all the photos herein are of the 2002 version, not the 2013, so don't get excited about the 2013's paper opacity or anything.

The difference between the
narrow-margin and the wide-margin SCR is, unsurprisingly, margins.  The typesetting in both Bibles is exactly the
same—same column size, same font size, same everything, but the wide-margin SCR
is printed on larger paper.  Its trim
size is 6 ½“ x 9 ¼“, as opposed to the straightforward 6” x 9” of the
narrow-margin version.  The result is a
snowy expanse of white space around the borders of the text block.

For me, that’s a nuisance.  I don’t write in Bibles, so all the added
white space meant for me was added size and added weight (the wide-margin I own
weighs in at 3 pounds 5 ounces, half a pound more than the 2 pounds 13 ounces
of my narrow-margin).  I preached out of
a wide-margin SCR early in my career, but I never enjoyed it.  When I one-handed the tome, it always felt
like it was about to flop to the floor.

However, what was a nuisance to
me was a delight to my mother.  She was
one of the most diligent Bible-writers I’ve ever known in my life, and she was
able to jot all kinds of things down in the margins.  Indeed, the heavily annotated wide-margin I
own used to be hers, but it came into my possession with her passing in
2011.  For people like her, people who
want nothing more than a reference NASB with huge margins to write in, the new
SCR will be a joy.  For people who don’t
like ginormous Bibles, the larger trim size might be a bit much.

One final note:  The new wide-margin SCR isn’t exactly like
the legendary wide-margins of days of yore like my mother have.  The trim size is the same, the typesetting is
the same, but it is printed on thinner paper. 
On the credit side, this means that the Bible is thinner than its
brethren from 2002, and thinner even than the more modern narrow-margins.  It weighs less too.

On the debit side, though, the
paper is less opaque than the 2002 paper. 
I would judge it to be somewhere between the 2002 wide-margin and my
narrow-margin in opacity.  This shouldn’t
be a problem for reading.  I’ve never
been bothered by the ghosting in my narrow-margin.  However, Bible-writers who aren’t careful in
their choice of writing utensil may end up with some bleedthrough problems.
