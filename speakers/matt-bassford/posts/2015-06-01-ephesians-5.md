Most of us have heard countless times that in order to be a
Christian, you have to believe in Christ. 
Unless we accept His claim to be the Son of God, we don’t actually
belong to Him.  It is our faith that
saves us and gives us the hope of eternal life.

However, our claim to faith has significant implications in
this life as well.  If Jesus is truly the
Christ, that makes Him both our King and our example.  The first is a matter of clear-cut
authority.  If Jesus is King, then we
need to obey His words and honor His silences.

The example of Jesus, however, is more indirect in its
influence.  Once we understand His
magnificent and self-sacrificing love, once we grasp that He freely laid down
His life to rescue us, we can’t continue living the same way.  We are compelled by the example of His love
to imitate Him.  Every true disciple
spends a lifetime figuring out what that looks like, but sometimes the Bible
spells out more clearly how Christ should influence us.  We see one such text in Ephesians 5.



**Walking in Love**

In the first main section of Ephesians 5, Paul encourages us
to walk in love.  This begins with living
as **IMITATORS OF GOD.** Consider Paul’s words in Ephesians
5:1-2.  When we confront the sacrifice of
Christ, it tells us not only that we are saved but also what the redeemed life
looks like.  We aren’t to walk in
selfishness anymore.  We are to walk in
love.

This points, I think, to a fundamental truth about
Christianity that we often overlook. 
Back in the days of the institutional controversy, many in institutional
churches described non-institutional Christians as “antis”.  In other words, if somebody had an idea, we
were against it.

Sometimes, I worry that even we ourselves can fall into the
trap of thinking of Christianity as an “anti” religion, that serving Jesus
means that we can’t do this, and we can’t do this, and we can’t do that
either.  Our religious life becomes
defined by the thou-shalt-nots.

That’s a terrible misread of the Bible!  In reality, following Jesus isn’t about the
negative.  It’s about the positive.  It’s about loving God and loving our neighbor
so fully that there is no room left in our lives for evil.  It’s sin that is the negative.  It’s sin that points to the absence of
love.  Unless we make our lives about the
positive imitation of our Lord, the devil will surely fill the vacuum.

However, as human beings, we are prone to foolishness and
self-deception.  Sometimes, we have to
have the negatives spelled out so that we don’t mistake them for
positives.  Therefore, Paul next points
to the necessity of **AVOIDING IMPURE
SPEECH.** Let’s pick up here with
Ephesians 5:3-5.  Even here, Paul points
to the positive.  We’re supposed to use
our mouths for giving thanks.  After all,
man was created to give glory to God, and when we honor Him as He deserves, we
literally fulfill our reason for existing. 
That’s what our lips are for.

However, as with any ability or gift that God gives us, we
can easily turn our speech to evil ends. 
There are a number of them identified here in just a few short
verses.  First, Paul warns us against
naming immorality, impurity, or greed. 
By this, he means that we should not express these things in an
approving way.  Our speech should never
be a vehicle for communicating lust.

Second, we have to put off filthiness, silly talk, and
coarse jesting.  Today, we would probably
refer to these things as foul language, innuendo, and dirty jokes.  As Christians, we must not engage in these
things nor show our approval of those who do.

Paul gives two reasons for avoiding this kind of
speech.  First, it’s not proper for
saints.  It’s not proper for God’s holy,
dedicated people.  If we truly belong to
Him, then why in the world are things coming out of our mouths that have nothing
to do with Him?  Second, the people who
practice these evil things aren’t going to heaven, and the more we talk about
them, the more likely we are to engage in them. 
Our speech should help us and those around us get to heaven, not hell.

Of course, being a Christian isn’t just about talking a good
game.  It’s about walking as **CHILDREN OF LIGHT.** Let’s continue with Ephesians 5:6-10.  There are really three threads in this
reading.  First, Paul warns us against
the dangers of deception.  This is
particularly relevant today.  There are
all kinds of voices in our society that insist that you can sleep around, or be
a drunk, or use foul language and still be a good Christian.  We can have our spiritual cake and eat it
too!  This, of course, is a complete lie.  All of those people out there who live
immoral lives, whether they’ve been baptized or not, are inviting the wrath of
God.  They’ll be destroyed unless they
repent.

Second, Paul points us to the importance of spiritual
knowledge.  If we are ignorant of the
will of God, in our ignorance we will inevitably violate it.  Instead, we have to try to learn what pleases
Him.  Indeed, that needs to be one of the
themes of our lives.  We need to study
the Bible privately and be here every time the doors are open, not because
we’re supposed to, but because that’s the only way for us to learn how to make
God happy.

Third, once we learn how, we can walk in the light.  Just as walking in darkness means that we
habitually practice evil, walking as children of light means that we habitually
practice good.  Our lives are filled not
with the absence of evil, but with the presence of good.  We continually bear fruit for the Lord.  We do something every day that we can point
to and say, “I did that because I want to be like Jesus.”  If that’s not true of us, we need to question
whether we are walking in the light at all.

Before the Lord’s Supper, let’s reflect on these
things.  Let’s consider first of all the
astonishing love of Jesus.  He loved us
so much that He surrendered His life to rescue us from the death we
deserved.  Second, though, let’s ask
whether we are walking in the way that He wants.

**Walking With
Understanding**

Next, Paul turns his attention to the subject of walking
with understanding.  Within this, he
first describes the value of **EXPOSING BY
LIGHT.** Let’s read here from
Ephesians 5:11-14.  Back when I was in
journalism school, the professors liked to say, “Sunlight is the best
disinfectant.”  Evil thrives in the
darkness, but the light destroys it.

There are many applications we might make of this passage,
but this morning, I want to focus on one in particular, which, indeed, is the
reason I chose to preach on Ephesians 5 this morning.  I want to talk about the importance of
exposing sexual abuse.  If you have been
abused, or, God forbid, are currently being abused, don’t let that abuse stay
in the dark!  Get help.  Tell somebody.  In this congregation, we have two people who
are professionally trained in helping abuse victims:  Josh Collier and my wife, Lauren.  They’re safe people.  You can trust them, and they will at the very
least get the ball rolling in finding you the people and the resources you
need.

If you come to me, I’m not a trained counselor, but I will
call the DCFS hotline for you, every time, no matter what name you name.  This is true for a couple of different
reasons.  First, I have to.  According to the state law of Illinois, as
the preacher here, I’m a mandated reporter. 
I have to bring any evidence or allegation of abuse to the attention of
the authorities.

Second, I want to.   They are the ones who are trained in assessing
the credibility of abuse claims, not I, and I would rather see this
congregation’s reputation dragged through the mud than allow for even the possibility
of tolerating a predator in our midst.

If, on the other hand, you are an abuser, you have a
responsibility to expose your own misdeeds. 
You need to acknowledge your sin to your victims and their
families.  You also need to confess it to
the congregation.  I’ve often heard it
said that the confession should only be as public as the sin, but I’ve never
found that rule in the Bible, and if you are a sexual predator, every parent in
the congregation needs to know about it.

Second, you need to pay your debt to society.  You need to hire a lawyer, tell him that you
have a confession to make to the police, and follow the legal process to its
conclusion.  Let the chips there fall
where they may.  For darkness to be
exposed, it must be *exposed*.

I know that is a terrifying, even ruinous price to pay, but
it is the only way to receive forgiveness and grace.  Even if this is what you have done, I and the
other Christians here will not abandon you. 
Even the pedophile has a soul that is precious in the sight of God.  I’ve never been an abuser, but I have been a
sinner, and today, I am righteous only by the blood of Jesus.  I understand all too well the power of the
devil, and if you want to seek God, I will never turn you away.

After this, Paul encourages the Ephesians to **WALK CAREFULLY.** Let’s continue here with Ephesians
5:15-17.  In this text also, we see a
clear connection between knowing and doing. 
In the case of the former, Paul exhorts us to understand what the will
of the Lord is.  As always, the Holy
Spirit does not command us to do the impossible.  If Christ expects us to understand His will,
He must have made His will comprehensible. 
This was true for the ordinary Ephesians who were the original audience,
and it is still true for us.  As Peter
says in 2 Peter 3, some things in the Bible are hard to understand.  As Paul says just a chapter earlier, God
gives some as pastors and teachers. 
However, those things do not diminish the responsibility we have to know
the truth for ourselves.

Likewise, it is our individual responsibility to walk
carefully and make the best use of our time in serving God.  Last Monday, I had to load a moving van
essentially on my own.  The most valuable
time I spent wasn’t the time I spent toting boxes and furniture.  It was the time I spent figuring out where I
was going to put everything before I even started to work.

In the same way, friends, we must be careful to plan our
lives generally to ensure that we are redeeming our time for the Lord.  Before we go on vacation, we need to make
sure that we’ll be able to find a church to worship with.  Before we accept a promotion at work, we need
to make sure that it won’t cut into the time we use to serve God.  Before we allow our children to pursue some
activity, we must ask what the spiritual consequences will be.  Unless we show wisdom and forethought, the
devil will be able to entangle us in worldly things to the detriment of our
souls.

Finally, we are responsible for being **FILLED WITH THE SPIRIT.** Let’s
pick up with Ephesians 5:18-21.  What’s
interesting to me about this text is that in it, Paul compares a spiritually
good thing—being filled with the Spirit—to a spiritually bad thing—getting
drunk.  In both cases, the person under
discussion is filled with some foreign element that he has chosen to take in
that makes him behave differently than he would have otherwise.

The wine-filled man is drunk.  He behaves in a debauched, dissipated
way.  He says and does all kinds of evil
things that he would never have done while sober.  In the same way, the Spirit-filled man is
under the influence of the Holy Spirit. 
He does things that the ungodly would never do.  He sings spiritual songs to other
Christians.  He makes a melody of worship
to God in his heart.  He thanks God for
his blessings rather than claiming the credit for those good things for
himself.  Finally, he willingly subjects
himself to others because above all, he fears the Lord.  The NASB obscures the connection here, but in
the Greek, all of these things are direct results of being Spirit-filled.  If we don’t do these things, if we don’t sing
and worship within and give thanks and choose to submit ourselves, we aren’t
actually spiritual people at all.

**Husbands and Wives**

The final section of Ephesians 5 concerns the application of
these things to husbands and wives.  This
might be the most familiar text in the entire book of Ephesians, but I think it
makes much more sense when we consider it in context.  If you recall the discussion of being filled
with the Spirit from a few minutes ago, what God is telling us here is that we
need to have Spirit-filled marriages.  In
particular, we need to have marriages in which each of us is subject to the
other.  The whole rest of the chapter
explains just what this ought to look like in practice.

Paul begins by applying the principle to **WIVES.**Read with me from Ephesians 5:22-24. 
There are few passages in the Bible that arouse as much indignation in
our time as this one does.  One can
almost hear millions of outraged women crying, “What do you mean, I have to be
subject to my husband in everything??? 
I’m just as good as he is!”  On
the basis of their equal worth, they demand an equal voice with their husbands
in everything.  Ties go to the runner, I
guess.

Before we get too enamored with our society’s view of
things, though, we have to ask what kind of results society gets.  In America, about half of marriages end in
divorce, and it’s fair to say that half the problem is right here.  Couples that follow God’s plan for marriage
don’t get divorced.

Yes, of course, women are worth as much as men are.  After all, men and women alike are created in
the image of God.  However, equal worth
does not necessarily demand an equal role. 
In the family, as in any organization, somebody has to be the head, and
God says it has to be the man.

“Well, yes,” some woman might say, “but my husband is an
idiot.”  That may well be.  However, a couple of observations are in order.  First of all, the woman who is married to an
idiot is married to him by her own choice, which tells me that her judgment
might not be the greatest either.

Second, and more seriously, however much damage a
not-so-smart man will do to his family with his bad decisions, a wife who refuses
to submit to him will do still more.  He
can damage the family’s property or the family’s finances, but she will damage
the fabric of the family itself.

By contrast, Paul begins to explore the husband’s submission
to his wife by comparison to **CHRIST AND
THE CHURCH.** Let’s pick up here with
Ephesians 5:25-27.  One can imagine one
of those not-so-smart husbands chuckling to himself as he reads the previous
paragraph, but this is enough to take the wind out of any husband’s sails!  The husband is supposed to love his wife,
naturally, but his example in the expression of that love is Christ’s love for
the church.

I always cringe a little bit inside whenever the Scriptures
tell me to be like Jesus, because being like Jesus is very, very hard.  This is no exception.  Look at what the Lord did for His
church.  First, He literally sacrificed
Himself for her.  He worked hard to
rescue her from her deficiencies.  His
goal was always her beauty and her glory. 
His life wasn’t about Himself.  It
was about her.

From this comparison, Paul distills a few applications for **HUSBANDS.**Let’s finish off the chapter here by reading Ephesians 5:28-33.  Let me remind you once again that this is all
a type of submission.  It works
differently than the wife’s submission to the husband, but it is no less submission.  Rather than submitting his will to hers as
the wife does, the husband is supposed to submit his good to his wife’s
good.

This means, men, that when we got married, we vowed away any
right we had to use our lives for ourselves. 
God always comes first, but after that, our lives belong to our
wives.  We go to work to provide for her
and for her children.  We find our
happiness by making her happy.  Christ
would never diminish the beauty and glory and honor of the church by anything
He said, so we must make sure that nothing we say diminishes the beauty and the
glory and the honor of our wives.

Here, we find the other half of America’s marriage
problems.  Too many men are overgrown
boys and refuse to do any of these things. 
They don’t have any interest in duty and responsibility and
self-sacrifice.  They’d rather play video
games and watch porn until their wives get sick of them and toss them out on
their ears.  In this, though, there is a
terrible irony.  As Paul says, he who
loves his wife loves himself.  When we
are selfish in our marriages, we destroy our hopes for happiness in this
life.  Only through selfless dedication
to our wives can we truly be happy.
