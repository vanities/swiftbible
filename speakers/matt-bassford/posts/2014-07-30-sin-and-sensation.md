*But the Spirit
explicitly says that in later times some will fall away from the faith, paying
attention to deceitful spirits and doctrines of demons, by means of the
hypocrisy of liars seared in their own conscience as with a branding iron. . .*

--1 Timothy 4:1-2 (NASB)

Sin feels good.  This
is no secret to anyone who has actually sinned. 
There’s plenty of bitter down the road, but there’s plenty of sweet
right now.  If sin weren’t enjoyable in
some way, no one would sin.



These pleasures are also much easier to come by, at least
initially, than the subtler joys of righteousness.  For instance, it is beyond clichéd to observe
that marriage takes work.  Intimacy in
marriage takes work too.  If a husband
and wife want a flourishing intimate relationship, both of them must be
committed to building a flourishing marriage. 
For good or ill, the state of their marriage will find its expression in
intimacy.

That’s a lot of work! 
Indeed, it is far easier for husbands and wives to seek fulfillment in
pornography.   It doesn’t take commitment
to read a titillating romance novel.  It
doesn’t take self-sacrifice to watch a streaming video.  The allure of those things is that they
appear to offer all the satisfaction without the hard work.

Of course, as always in our dealings with the devil,
“appear” should be all-caps, boldfaced, italicized, and underlined.  The passing pleasure of porn is not the same
thing as the enduring joy and contentment of a healthy marriage.  It might seem that way at first.  For most people, their first encounter with
porn offers a searing ecstasy that no flesh-and-blood spouse could hope to
match.

The problem with searing ecstasy, though, is that it sears
you.  I must have read 1 Timothy 4 a
hundred times before I realized this last night.  A branding iron applied to our flesh will
sear that flesh.  It will destroy the
skin and the nerves, leaving behind only a deadened pattern of scar tissue.

Sin will sear our spirits. 
As we practice sin, it deadens our souls, leaving us incapable of feeling
either the pangs of conscience or (and this is particularly ironic) the very
pleasure of the sin that led us astray in the first place.  As any drug user will tell you, the second
hit is never as good as the first. 
Instead of feeling satisfied, you come away from sin feeling dead.

Whether with porn, drugs, or any other addictive sin (and
nearly all sin is addictive), this pattern of initial pleasure and subsequent
deadness is at the heart of sin’s power to enslave.  The sinner is still looking for easy
pleasure, but pleasure is no longer easy. 
The usual answer is an increase in intensity.  Twice as many painkillers, twice as many
romance novels, will get us to alllmost the same place as one hit used to.

Sooner or later, though, our initial sin of choice will lose
its ability to please us entirely, no matter the quantity we consume.  We can look at a hundred pictures of naked
women online and fail to be stirred.  We
have seared our spirits so thoroughly that such things have lost their power to
move us.

Then, the search begins for a different drug of choice.  This, I think, explains the current national fascination
with *Fifty Shades of Grey.* 25 years ago, the country wasn’t ready
for sado-masochism to move mainstream. 
Sure, those practices have always been present off in the shadowy
margins of society someplace (some high-class Romans of Paul’s time got their
kicks out of torturing and killing slaves), but for ordinary people, “the two
shall become one flesh” didn’t involve them.

Now, though, that millions have been deadened to less exotic
practices through their own pursuit of sin, the way has been prepared for the
more exotic.  Pain is a negative feeling,
but it is a feeling.  Humiliation is a
negative feeling, but it is a feeling, and for the jaded sinner, any new
feeling is better than no feeling at all. 
However briefly, *Fifty Shades* fills
us with a counterfeit of life.

Of course, this won’t last either.  Soon, *Fifty
Shades* and its legion of imitators will become tired too.  Most Americans will greet bondage and
domination with an accepting shrug, both in fantasy and in real life.  Booorrr-inggg.

After this, I think we can hear echoes from the future in
the words of the muses of pop culture.  “Blurred
Lines” predicts an increasing interest in rape fantasy.  For all its loathsomeness, rape is an
emotionally intense experience, both for criminal and victim.  I think that intensity will begin to draw
burnt-out sinners.  How long before a
movie-length version of “Blurred Lines” (steamy sex, vague or absent consent)
makes its appearance and fascinates millions, at least for a time?  Sure, it’s evil, but it makes us feel!

If this seems far-fetched, consider that both titles, *Fifty Shades of Grey* and “Blurred Lines”, evoke moral ambiguity in an area where there should be none.  That is the nature of descent into sin.  It moves from forbidden to ambiguous to
accepted.

All of these principles can easily be translated from the
sexual realm to any other.  Sin never
satisfies.  Sin always enslaves.  Sin never contents.  Sin always deadens.  Sexual sin won’t make us happy, but neither
will anger, alcoholism, materialism nor the love of money.  The first time we give in is wonderful, but
we will go on paying for that initial burst of pleasure for a long time.  Perhaps forever.
