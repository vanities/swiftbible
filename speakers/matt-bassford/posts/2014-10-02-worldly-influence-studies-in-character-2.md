*She also gave to her
husband with her, and he ate.*

*--* Genesis 3:6b

In her sin in the garden, Eve not only became the first
human being to sin.  She also became the
first human accomplice to Satan.  After
she ate herself, she persuaded Adam to join her in disobeying God.  Sadly, this kind of behavior didn’t die out
with Eve.  Ever since, people have been
luring one another into the snares of the devil, and it is certain that he will
attempt to entrap us in just the same way.



Sometimes, this happens when we are deceived just like the
unwitting instrument of Satan.  At other
times, though, we sin because of our connection to him.  This appears to have been the case with
Adam.  As Paul says in 1 Timothy 2:14,
“Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and became a
transgressor.”  Adam apparently was under
no illusions about what the serpent had promised.  He went along with Eve’s sin only because of
his attachment to her.

This too is familiar today. 
There are any number of people today who have heard Jesus’ teaching in
Matthew 19, but choose to marry or remain married to an unlawful spouse
anyway.  They aren’t deceived about the
consequences of their decision, but their affection for their spouses overrides
their desire to obey God.  Such people
may not be surprised by their eternal destination, but they will fall beneath
the sword of God’s justice nonetheless.

The possibility that we might be led astray by those close
to us lends particular force to Paul’s admonition in 1 Corinthians 15:33.  He writes, “Do not be deceived; evil company
corrupts good habits.”   Many Christians
would rather not believe this.  They
prefer to pretend that they live in a happy never-never land, where influence
only flows one way, and all of their worldly friends will be led by them to
obey the gospel, but those same worldly friends will never tempt them to fall
away.  Such naiveté can only make the
devil smile.

Instead, as we obey the command to be a light to the world,
we must always be conscious that the world is darkness to us.  The same leverage that we use to bring the
lost we love to the Lord also applies to Satan’s efforts to use them to corrupt
us.  Unless we protect ourselves from the
shock by our close association with the Lord and His people, the devil will
surely succeed in toppling us into the abyss. 
Unless we learn from the consequences of Adam’s association with Eve, we
are doomed to repeat his error.
