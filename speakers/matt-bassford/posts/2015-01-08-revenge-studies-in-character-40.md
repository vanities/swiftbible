*But the sons of Jacob
answered Shechem and Hamor his father, and spoke deceitfully, because he had
defiled Dinah their sister.*

--Genesis 34:13

Here, we see the key element of a story that doesn’t usually
make it into our children’s Bible classes: 
the story of the defilement of Dinah. 
Dinah is Jacob’s daughter, and she decided she wanted to visit some of
the local Canaanite women.  The son of the
local lord, a man named Shechem, sees this unattached female wandering around,
grabs her, and rapes her.  Somewhat
surprisingly, he then decides that he wants to marry Dinah, so he asks Jacob
for her hand.

Jacob’s sons are understandably outraged by this, and they
decide that they want to seek revenge. 
However, apparently they’d inherited their father’s penchant for sneaky
dealing, so they don’t just show up at Shechem’s front door with swords.  Instead, they agree to Shechem’s marriage
proposal, but only on the condition that he circumcise all the males of the
city.  Shechem joyfully agrees, all the
men are accordingly circumcised, but when they’re still incapacitated by the
operation, that’s when Jacob’s sons Simeon and Levi attack the city, kill everyone,
and plunder it.

Simeon and Levi present us with a sterling example of how we
should not react to evil done to us. 
There’s no doubt about it; Shechem was a bad guy, and his later
repentance and desire to marry Dinah don’t make him into a good guy.  Simeon and Levi had every right to be
angry.  They had every right to see
Shechem punished for what he had done. 
There were any number of ways they could have done this.  They could have demanded compensation for
Shechem’s evil act.  They could have asked
God to punish him.  They could even have
attacked the city straightforwardly.

However, they didn’t. 
Instead of acting to correct the injustice, they gained their vengeance
through another injustice.  They
deceitfully entered into an agreement with Shechem and his people, and they
killed innocent men when they were in no position to defend themselves.  This kind of conduct was unacceptable even
among the Canaanites.  In fact, after
Jacob learns of it, he fears that the inhabitants of the land are going to
exterminate his people for what they’ve done.

Today, we must never return evil for evil.  Even when someone unquestionably wrongs us,
that does not give us cause to wrong them in return.  We are allowed to pursue the legal remedies
that were unavailable to people of Jacob’s time.  We are allowed to take the problem to the
Lord.  We are allowed to go to the sinner
ourselves and ask him to make things whole. 
However, we cannot act outside the avenues approved by God.  If we do that, just as Simeon and Levi did
evil in the sight of the Canaanites, we do evil in the sight of the world.  No matter what the provocation, the Lord’s
people must play by the rules.
