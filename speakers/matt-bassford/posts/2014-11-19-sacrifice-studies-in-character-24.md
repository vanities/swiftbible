*Then He said,
"Take now your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land
of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains of
which I shall tell you."*

*--Genesis 22:2*

Sometimes, we get to wondering what the big deal was about
Abraham.  In many of the scenes in which
he appears, he doesn’t seem a whole lot better than the rest of us.  He lied repeatedly, he got in trouble with
his wife’s handmaiden, and generally acted like a normal human being with
normal human failings.  But then, we come
to the story of Abraham and the sacrifice of Isaac.



God tells Abraham that He expects him to sacrifice Isaac as
a burnt offering.  This is not just
Abraham’s son.  This is his only son, the
son whom he physically should not have had, the miracle child of promise.  Abraham would gladly have died to spare his
son’s life, but now he is being instructed to kill him.  In response to this outrageous demand,
Abraham rises early the next morning, travels three days to the place that God
had selected, and prepared to sacrifice his own son.

Abraham didn’t do this out of some lack of natural
affection, nor even out of the belief that God was going to stop the
sacrifice.  Instead, the Hebrews writer
tells us in Hebrews 11:17-19 that Abraham’s actions were based on God’s
promises to him.  He knew that God had
promised that through Isaac he would have a multitude of descendants. Therefore,
if God told him to kill Isaac, it must be because God planned to raise Isaac
from the dead so that His promise would be fulfilled.  Abraham was wrong about that, but he was
wrong for all the right reasons.

Thankfully, none of us will ever be called upon to sacrifice
our children, but there will be times in all of our lives when we are asked to
do something against which our reason revolts. 
When times are tough and we feel like we have to work during the times
of the assembly just to make ends meet, how can we believe that if we put His
kingdom first, all these things will be added to us?  When our friends at school begin to practice
every kind of wickedness, where do we find the faith to see that everything we
leave behind for the gospel’s sake will be returned to us a hundredfold?

It’s in moments like these where we show what kind of
Christians we really are.  As we learn
from the life of Jesus, being His disciple isn’t just about making the obvious
calls and the easy choices.  It’s about
making the tough calls and the hard choices. 
It’s about walking by faith rather than by sight, even when sight is
screaming at us that we’re about to walk off a cliff.  That’s the faith of Abraham, and it’s the
faith that we need to lead us to heaven.
