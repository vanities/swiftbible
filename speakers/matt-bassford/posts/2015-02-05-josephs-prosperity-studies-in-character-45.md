*The LORD was with
Joseph, and he was a successful man; and he was in the house of his master the
Egyptian.*

*--Genesis 39:2*

When last we saw Joseph, he was headed forlornly south as a
slave in an Ishmaelite merchant train. 
As a slave, Joseph didn’t have much say in his fate.  He could have been sold to the owners of one
of the Egyptian turquoise mines, which would have brought him a miserable life
and a quick death.  Instead, he ends up
in the household of an Egyptian official named Potiphar, and once there, his
rise is spectacular.  The Lord blesses him
so that everything he does prospers, Potiphar recognizes this, and soon he
promotes Joseph to be over his entire household.



The many stories in Scripture like this lead many people to
get the wrong idea about serving God. 
People like this are often not very innately religious, but they are
greedy.  They’re interested in getting
rich however they can.  These people look
at the story of Joseph, or the story of David, and they think that godliness is
a means of gain.  They are led further
down this path by TV preachers who are themselves greedy, who promise them
wealth if they just send a check to Abundant Suckers Ministries.  God, of course, has no interest in either
those who teach this false doctrine or those who believe it.

However, the false teachers shouldn’t lead us to ignore
Joseph’s story.  Our priority should
always be the things of the spirit, we should always live for the life to come,
but such a spiritual focus will have beneficial effects even in this life.  All other things being equal, Christians
should prosper more than non-Christians.

There are two main reasons for this.  The first is the opportunity that we have to
seek God’s help through prayer.  We learn
from Jesus’ model prayer in Matthew 6:11 that God permits us to pray not only
for spiritual blessings but for physical ones. 
He will not hear the prayers that come from a covetous heart, but if we
seek provision for ourselves and our loved ones, we can be certain that He will
be sympathetic.  As David says in Psalm
37:23-26, the Lord watches out for the righteous and makes sure that their
needs are always supplied.

In addition to God’s blessing in response to prayer, we
benefit from God’s blessing through His word. 
The Bible teaches us that we are to be sober, industrious, wise, and
diligent.  It warns us away from sins
ranging from lying to drunkenness.  Every
one of those Scriptural teachings, and a hundred others besides, benefits us in
the workplace.  They’re not a
get-rich-quick scheme, but they are a time-tested recipe for success, whether
we work for ourselves or someone else. 
Only a fool wouldn’t want an employee who is hard-working, reliable, and
competent, and if we’re an employee like that, we will prosper just like Joseph
did.  It’s not the point of Christianity,
but it sure is a nice side benefit.
