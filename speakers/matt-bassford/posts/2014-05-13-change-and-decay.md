*These things I
remember and I pour out my soul within me.*

*For I used to go along
with the throng and lead them*

*In procession to the house of God,*

*With the voice of joy
and thanksgiving, a multitude keeping festival.*

-- Psalms 42:4 (NASB)

To many Christians, particularly
those who are older, the modern-day United States is becoming an increasingly
strange place.  My father remembers a
time when the people he knew, even if they weren’t members of some church of
Christ, were at least decent, churchgoing types who subscribed to the same
general principles of morality that he did. 
Today, that moral consensus no longer exists.  Increasing numbers of Americans no longer
feel bound by the restrictions of God’s word, and they are loud in their condemnation
of those who still adhere to it.



The new era is also marked by
large-scale technological change.  In
particular, the Internet offers us hitherto-unavailable opportunities to serve
God, but it brings with it a host of dangers too.  Even in other areas, the world we know today
is very different from the world our fathers knew.

In the midst of all this change,
what is a child of God to do? 
Thankfully, even though the world around us may have changed, the word
of God hasn’t.  The Bible wasn’t written
specifically to people who lived in the United States in 1950.  Instead, its original audience comprised men
and women who lived thousands of years ago on the other side of the world from
us, in a setting as alien to our brethren 100 years ago as is to us today.

The genius of the Bible is such
that even though it was revealed to a specific time, it speaks to mankind for
all time.  This is no less true of
us.  Far from being the obsolete relic of
its critics’ imaginations, God’s word speaks with profound insight on the most
modern of topics.  Its truths apply with
equal relevance to our circumstances.  It
is still a lamp to our feet and a light to our way, no less so to the Christian
of the 21st century than to the Christian of the first.
