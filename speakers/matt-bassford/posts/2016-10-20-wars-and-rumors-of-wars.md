In my lifetime, I can’t remember an election that has caused
more angst among Christians than this one. 
Sure, my whole adult life, there’s been considerable rooting for Our Guy
(the conservative one) over Their Guy (the liberal one), but this time around,
there appears to be widespread disagreement over who Our Guy is, or even
whether there is anybody running for office worth designating as Ours.

Pretty much everybody who has an opinion, though, will agree
that these are not the two presidential candidates we wanted.  They remind me of Mark Twain’s comment on the
sultan of the Ottoman Empire in *The
Innocents Abroad*:  “As for the
Sultan, one could set a trap any where and catch a dozen abler men in a
night.”  Strangely enough, these are the
two we caught.



Similarly, most Christians will agree that America is a
nation in decline.  Out-of-control drug
use, the demolition of the traditional family, and the endorsement of every
kind of sexual sin indicate the decadence of our society.  You don’t have to be much of a student of
history to know what happens to nations that become decadent.

However, I don’t see many Christians connecting the two and
arriving at the conclusion that this awful election is itself a symptom of
larger societal problems.  The awful candidates
reflect the awful state of American morality, and that’s equally true for all
sides of the political spectrum.  In 2
Timothy 3:1-5, I think the “last days” Paul is writing about are the last days
of the Jewish state before the Great Revolt of AD 66, yet his description is
strikingly apt because these are the kinds of men that “last days” tend to
throw up.  Can anybody read the following
without being reminded of the two people from whom will come our next president?

“But realize this, that in the last days difficult times will come. For men
will be lovers of self, lovers of money, boastful, arrogant, revilers,
disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unholy, unloving, irreconcilable, malicious
gossips, without self-control, brutal, haters of good, treacherous, reckless,
conceited, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, holding to a form of
godliness, although they have denied its power; Avoid such men as these.”

This is the kind of character that is formed in the
crucibles of an immoral, self-indulgent nation, from leader to follower.  In fact, when God has set His face against a
nation, people like these are even more likely to emerge as leaders.  Consider Isaiah 3:4-5, when the prophet
writes in another set of “last days”, the ones before the Assyrian invasion of
Judah in 701 BC:

“And I will make mere lads their princes,

“And capricious children will rule over them,

“And the people will be oppressed,

“Each one by another, and each one by his neighbor;

“The youth will storm against the elder

“And the inferior against the honorable.”

When God gives you rotten leaders, it’s a sign that He’s not
best pleased with you!

Nor should we expect this to change unless the country
changes first.  Among many of my friends,
I sense the conviction that all we have to do is make it through the election,
and then everything’s going to return to normal.  In 2020, or 2024, we will once again have the
option to choose between men of good will who differ on issues of policy, or at
least between Our Guy and Their Guy.

I’m not at all convinced that’s so.  If this election is not a freak accident, if
indeed it reflects who we have become, further elections will also reflect who
we have become.

In particular, I think the cries of a rigged election bode
ill for the future.  Societies are funny
things.  They function as long as people
accept the rules, but once people start discovering that they can get ahead by
breaking the rules, society collapses. 
One of the fundamental rules of American society is, “The candidate with
the most votes gets to be President,” but once Americans stop accepting that
rule or stop believing that it is being honored, disaster will ensue.  This has happened once before in the history
of our country, and the Civil War was the result.  I fear we will have more to hear of stolen
elections in the years to come.

There are plenty of other examples of states spinning out of
control, and it happens much more quickly than the citizens of those states
expect.  The beginning of the end for the
Roman Republic was the politically motivated murder of the tribune Tiberius
Gracchus by a mob of senators in 133 BC. 
Blood begets more blood, and in 88 BC, Sulla became the first (though by
no means the last) Roman general to march on Rome.  By the time the civil wars ended 50 years
later, Rome had become an empire, not a republic.

I am neither a prophet nor the son of a prophet, but all
signs point to things getting worse before they get better.  I don’t think this is the end of social
convulsion.  I think it’s the
beginning.

I write, though, not to spread gloom about the future, but
to remind Christians of their true citizenship. 
Setting our hopes on an earthly country is always a bad idea, and it may
well be about to become a very bad idea. 
Peace, prosperity, and a continued golden age for our country are by no
means guaranteed.  Someday, somebody will
witness the downfall of the United States. 
That’s what happens to great nations. 
It could come in our children’s time. 
It could come in ours.

However, as the writer observes in Hebrews 12:28, we receive
a kingdom that cannot be shaken.  I would
imagine this was a source of considerable comfort to Christians in the first
century, and it should be a source of considerable comfort to us too.  If we have set our hopes on the right ruler,
even if everything around us is ruined, we won’t be ruined along with it.
