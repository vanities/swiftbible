For those of you who don't know, Robert Lowry was one of the giants of American hymnody.  He wrote "Low in the Grave He Lay" and "Shall We Gather at the River?" along with the tunes to "I Need Thee Every Hour", "All the Way My Savior Leads Me", and a host of others.  If you want to say that Robert Lowry was the greatest hymnist of all time, I won't argue with you.

Anyways, I juuust got back from vacation, and during said vacation, my wife and I visited her Uncle Chuck and Aunt Betty.  Betty, who is a wonderful, sweet, amazing human being, gave me a stack of old hymnals, among which was *Royal Diadem*, Lowry's 1873 hymnal.  As I was paging through it, I noticed this gem.  I think it proves the point that a good hymn will work outside of its own time.

I've lightly altered the lyrics.  The original first line was "Wake every tuneful string", which didn't exactly mesh with my views on instrumental music in worship.  I also changed "spake" to "spoke" in 3.1.  Other than that, it's the Lowry original.

The tune is unchanged.  I lack the chutzpah necessary to edit a Robert Lowry tune.  If he didn't get something right, what makes me think I will?

The whole thing has long since passed into the public domain.  Enjoy it!
