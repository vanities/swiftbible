*So Lot went out and
spoke to his sons-in-law, who had married his daughters, and said, "Get
up, get out of this place; for the LORD will destroy this city!" But to
his sons-in-law he seemed to be joking.*

--Genesis 19:14

The story of the destruction of Sodom is probably one of the
grimmest in all of Scripture.  Throughout
its length, we see all sorts of people sin in all sorts of foolish and
self-destructive ways, and almost all of them pay the penalty for it.  The participants in this tragedy, though,
don’t realize that they’re in a tragedy, and nowhere is this more obvious than
in the case of Lot’s sons-in-law.  Lot
tells them the sober truth, that their city and everything they own is going to
be destroyed in the morning, and they need to flee RIGHT NOW.  They think that’s the funniest thing they’ve
ever heard.  As a result, come the
morning, the sons-in-law are vaporized along with everybody else in Sodom,
because they didn’t listen to the Lord.



The first lesson that we need to draw from this is that no
matter how eloquently we present God’s word, no matter how deep our
convictions, no matter how obvious the logic of our position, there are going
to be people who brush us off.  Just as
Paul warns in 2 Corinthians 4:4, Satan loves nothing more than to blind the
minds of the unbelieving.  He is a master
at plucking out the seed of the gospel before it can sprout.  Through a million subtle tricks, he can
convince men that they don’t have to believe what they don’t want to believe,
and so they dismiss the word that is their only hope of salvation.  They fail the most important test of their
lives because they don’t take it seriously.

Nor is this something that can only happen to people outside
the church.  “Repent, and be baptized” is
not the last command that we are required to obey.  On the day of judgment, I fear that we will
all be appalled at how many Christians will be lost because they defiantly
refused to give up their pet sins.  There
are no optional commands in the Bible, yet many brethren persist in treating
the commands they find uncongenial as somehow less important than the ones they
like.  Lust is no less deadly than
homosexuality.  Covetousness is no less
destructive than murder.  God forgives
His children when they fight against those things and fail, but cannot forgive
them when they treat the commandment lightly.

Just as outsiders can be destroyed by this, just as faceless
generic Christians can be destroyed by this, so can we ourselves.  Sometimes, we reach the point where we become
hardened to the word.  We listen to
sermon after sermon, we give mental assent to everything that is said from the
pulpit, but we don’t go home and change our lives.  Salvation is not about hearing.  Salvation is about doing, and only if we flee
from destruction can we hope to be saved.
