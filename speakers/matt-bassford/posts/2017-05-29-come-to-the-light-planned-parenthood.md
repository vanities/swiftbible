In 2015, an organization called the Center for Medical Progress released a video of conversations with abortion providers that CMP members had recorded.  These conversations at least arguably (and no, I don’t want to have the argument) showed Planned Parenthood execs selling body parts from aborted babies.  This made headlines and got CMP in serious legal trouble.  Courts ruled that the videos had to be taken down.

Last week, CMP was at it again.  This time, [it released recordings](http://www.nationalreview.com/article/447939/undercover-video-center-medical-progress-exposes-gruesome-abortion-practices) taken at the 2014 and 2015 National Abortion Federation conventions.  These recordings are filled with all kinds of charming sentiments, as when Lisa Harris, director of Planned Parenthood of Michigan, said, “Given that we actually see the fetus the same way, and given that we might actually both agree that there’s violence in here. . . . Let’s just give them all the violence, it’s a person, it’s killing, let’s just give them all that.”  Yeah; I run an organization that kills babies for money.  What of it?

I would think that such a shocking statement made in any context would be newsworthy, but the story has been roundly ignored by the mainstream media.  The courts, however, have not ignored it.  As [this article](http://www.nationalreview.com/corner/448030/center-medical-progress-undercover-video-planned-parenthood-removed-youtube-judge) details, because CMP posted more footage, there are more lawsuits, contempt proceedings, etc., headed their way.

I’m not going to write about how outrageous I find the beliefs expressed by Planned Parenthood muckety-mucks.  I’ve written plenty already about abortion (like [this post](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2015/10/bill-nye-whiffs-on-abortion-part-2.html)).  I suspect that 99 percent of my readers agree with me about it, and for the other 1 percent, if I haven’t changed their minds already, I’m probably not gonna.

Instead, I want to focus on the dramatic efforts that pro-choice forces are making to sweep all of these recordings under the rug.  So far as I know, nobody has denied that the recordings are legit.  These are real-life abortionist higher-ups, freely speaking their minds about their profession.  Nobody handed them a script and put a gun to their heads to make them read it.  This is what they think if they’re being candid.

However, these recordings of what people actually said are soooo offensive that they must never be allowed to see the light of day.   Never mind that the Internet is awash with videos containing every kind of depravity and filth.  Never mind that the same folks who think these recordings are an absurd breach of privacy rejoiced when Mitt Romney was recorded without his consent at a fundraiser during the 2012 election, which was the origin of the infamous “47 percent” quote.  They crowed about what a hypocrite Romney was.  Here’s the truth!  See???

However, these videos, of fully clothed people freely revealing what is in their hearts, these videos the world must *not* see.

I’m no fan of abortion, to say the least.  However, let’s put the specific issue aside.  I would have vast misgivings about supporting any movement whose public position depends on concealing the truth about what its leaders actually believe.

I know “depends” is a strong argument, but I think it’s justified here.  “The decision to terminate a pregnancy should be a private matter between a woman and her doctor,” is something that a significant portion of the American public can get behind (note the language that is simultaneously vague and clinical).  And while there’s also a significant portion of the American public that would agree that “The decision to terminate a pregnancy kills a baby,” those folks are all pro-life (except for the abortionists, apparently).  We’ve fallen far as a nation, but we haven’t fallen far enough yet to accept infanticide without lying to ourselves about it first.

I think that if you aren’t willing to own in public the fundamental convictions that you own in private (to the extent that speeches made at a convention are “private”), you’ve got a hypocrisy problem that way outweighs Mitt Romney’s.

I’m perfectly willing to be judged by that standard myself.  In the course of my work as a preacher, I say plenty of things in public (though admittedly, a worship service and a convention probably offer about the same level of privacy).  I also say plenty of things in private.  I have one-on-one conversations with people about my work all the time.

If you want to have a Bible study with me, record it, and put it up on the Internet, go ahead!  If you want to have a conversation with me at Truth Lectures about some topic and record it and put it up on the Internet, go ahead!  It’s possible that a specific conversation might cause me some embarrassment (and probably point out a place where my words weren’t in line with Scripture), but the gospel that I preach in public is the same gospel that I teach and discuss in private.  There will be no significant differences (unlike, for instance, saying, “We abort fetuses,” in public and “We kill babies,” in private).  I am not a double-tongued, deceitful, hypocritical person.

I cannot help but be reminded here of the Lord’s profound words in John 3:19-21, “And this is the judgment: the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil. For everyone who does wicked things hates the light and does not come to the light, lest his works should be exposed. But whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God.”

Come to the light, Planned Parenthood.  Come to the light, abortion providers.  If what you’re doing is so important and good, be open with your convictions about it.  Say the same thing in public that you say in private.

On the other hand, if you aren’t willing to do that, if you love the darkness rather than the light, that reveals a lot about what you’re doing too.
