Last Friday, I posted what I intended as a deeply personal
reflection on my own desire to accumulate Bibles.  As a result of those reflections, I
determined that I would spend the next year using only one Bible (when I wasn’t
reviewing other Bibles, of course).  Much
to my surprise, “One Bible, One Year” became one of my most widely read posts
in months.  Apparently, quite a few other
Bible geeks have wrestled with the same kinds of issues.

I thought, then, that it would be worth revisiting the
subject.  Particularly, I think it’s
worth revisiting what I said about the flesh and the spirit, which although
accurate, was incomplete.  The flesh
isn’t fleshly only because it cannot be satisfied.  It is also fleshly because it continues to
cling to the things that cannot satisfy it. 
The rich young ruler didn’t fail because he was rich.  He failed because he was unwilling to give
his riches to the poor.



This raises an obvious question, then, for those of us who
accumulate Bibles.  What do we do with
the Bibles we accumulate?  On the one
hand, it could be that we hang on to them even though we do not and cannot use
them all.  Maybe we take them out of
their blue boxes twice a year to oil them (keeps that goatskin nice and
supple!), but that’s about it.  On the
other hand, it could be that we give them away about as eagerly as we collect
them, and in my opinion, short of using it myself, giving a Bible away is about
the best thing I can do with it.

For myself, I try to limit my collection to the Bible I’m
using, one Bible in each of the major translations (KJV, NKJV, ASV, NASB, ESV,
NIV, NLT, and REB, with hopefully an HCSB arriving soon), Bibles with family
significance (such as my mother’s 2002 SCR), and some miscellaneous odds and
ends (several copies of the hip-pocket ESV New Testament I prefer, an NASB I’ve
written in too much to give away, etc.).

Other than that, it’s likely that a given Bible will see
more use in someone else’s hands, and I’ve tried to steer them there.  It’s made me particularly happy when I’ve
been able to give away Bibles to members of the young-adult class I’ve been
teaching.  Is there a better way to say,
“I love you,” than with a Bible, especially a Bible that we ourselves love?  I’m not much for mysticism, but it strikes me
anyway that a Bible given in love is a potent spiritual talisman.

If we aren’t willing to do that, perhaps it’s time to ask
ourselves why.  Some of the answers may
be legitimate.  An acquaintance of mine
collects antique Bibles, none of which would stand up well to the rigors of
actual use.  I assume that he instead
chooses to be generous in other ways.  I
know others who keep a stack of cheaper but still decently constructed giveaway
Bibles, so that if they encounter somebody who needs one, they can solve the
problem quickly.

However, if we have a number of Bibles that we don’t use,
that someone else could, but none of our Bibles ever actually find their way to
any someone elses. . .  Why not?  It is at the least worth considering.
