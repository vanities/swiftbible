As always, the LPCR rounded out its time with me with two
weeks on the go.  During that time, I
took it appointment preaching and, I believe, to one study.  Here’s what 
I thought of it:

**SIZE.** Here is an astonishing thing:  a compact Bible, designed specifically for
portability, PASSED the glove-box test! 
Can you believe it?!  Of course,
even Bibles that don’t compromise nearly as much, all the thinlines of the
world, usually fare pretty well here. 
The LPCR fared wonderfully well. 
The glove compartment of our Rondo is smaller than that of our Corolla,
but when I tossed the LPCR in it, the Bible just kind of rattled around.  The massive amount of leftover room made me
wonder, briefly, if someone had cleaned out the glove box.  Of course, in reality, no one ever cleans out
our glove box.  The LPCR is just a small
Bible.  It bears about the same
proportion to a thinline that a thinline does to a full-size Bible such as the
Crossway Legacy.



Small as it is, though, the LPCR is not a hip-pocketable
Bible.  For the sake of science, I
tried.  Nope.  I couldn’t even fit the end in.  If I want to bring a Bible when I take the
kids to the park, it’s still my old-school ESV New Testament for me.

This gives rise to an interesting question:  why have complete compact Bibles?  Thinlines, which are generally accounted more
functional than compacts, will fit in a glove box, the side pocket of a bag,
etc.  I don’t really carry anything that
is too small to hold a Thinline, though women enamored of itty-bitty purses may
beg to differ.  Conversely, full-Bible
compacts are too big to fill the role of a billfold-sized compact New
Testament.  Even when we discount the
impact of smartphone Bible apps, I struggle to see the utility of Bibles like
the LPCR.  People buy them, apparently,
for reasons that seem good and sufficient to them.  I’m just not entirely sure why.

**REFERENCES.** Even though I’ve been in some fairly
ferocious personal studies recently, I don’t remember using the references in
the LPCR for anything.  I’m not entirely
sure how much good they would have done me if I had needed to use them.  2K was able to get away with a relatively
clean layout in part because the Bible doesn’t contain all that many references,
generally only a couple per paragraph. 
As a “reference Bible”, I would put the LPCR and its bigger brothers
about midway between a workhorse reference Bible like the Foundation SCR and a
text Bible with Old-Testament citations like a Crossway Thinline.  That may be a sweet spot for some people, but
generally, those who like reference Bibles want legions of references marching
in column down the page.

**CONCLUSION.** Not surprisingly, the LPCR puts in its
best performance in the travel role. 
Even though I still bear a grudge against the Bible because of its glued
binding, that didn’t really pose a problem for me when I used it as a carry
Bible.  However, I can’t hip-pocket it,
which is the compact ideal for me. 
Accordingly, I rate it here at 4.5/5. 
Overall, the Bible has received a 12.5/20.
