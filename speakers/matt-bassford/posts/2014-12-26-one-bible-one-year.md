Like many Bible geeks, I have a vague sense of guilt over my
desire to accumulate Bibles.  As I’ve
said before, I think it’s a good thing for us to have Bibles with a physical
form that makes us happy.  God made us to
love finely made, beautiful things, and there is no better object for that love
than a finely made Bible.

Problems arise, though, when we begin emphasizing the form
of the Scripture over its content, when slick layout and good paper and premium
leather become more important to us than the word of truth.  In my own life, I’ve noticed that I can tell
the difference between the call of the flesh and the call of the spirit because
the latter can be satisfied, but the former cannot be.



I’ve never felt the need to replace the Bible with the Qur’an,
the Tao Te Ching, or any other holy book. 
I’ve never wanted another Lord besides Christ.  However, since I became interested in Bibles,
I cannot claim the same loyalty to any particular physical edition of the Good
Book.  There are Bibles that I was really
excited to get less than a year ago, Bibles that I thought would be my one and
only, that today sit on my shelf unused. 
Instead, my attention has become fixed on some new dream Bible.  That doesn’t sound like a spiritual
impulse.  It sounds like a fleshly
one.  It sounds like the desire to
accumulate more and more, to find satisfaction in some physical thing when only
Christ can satisfy.

Of course, this point too can be oversold.  Gluttony is a problem, but the solution to
gluttony is not to stop eating altogether. 
The solution is to regulate the impulses of the flesh with the spirit,
to say no to that second helping unless you’re a farmhand, even if it’s your
wife’s chili and it’s really good.

I still think it’s OK for me to love Bibles.  It’s not OK, though, for me to love them
immoderately, to place more weight on them than they are able to bear.  It’s time, then, for me to bow out, at least
temporarily, from the quest for the One Perfect Bible.  I’ve decided that I need to commit to one
Bible and use that Bible for everything, come what may (unless, of course, it
is somehow destroyed or lost), for one year.

There is a caveat to this. 
I’m not about to stop reviewing Bibles and blogging about it, and my
Bible-review process necessarily involves using said Bible for several
weeks.  That’s going to continue.  However, once the review periods for those
Bibles are over, I’m going to bid them an affectionate adieu and return to the
one Bible to which I have committed.

The Bible I’ve chosen (and this was far from an easy
decision) is a calf-split Cambridge Pitt Minion NASB that my children gave me
for Christmas.  I am a small-Bible guy, I
can still read the thing, and both the setting and the execution of the NASB
Pitt are excellent.  I very nearly went
with my rebound Nelson ASV, but I noticed that over the last month, as I’ve
been using the ASV, some of the pages have been accumulating tiny tears around
the edges.  I don’t think that old paper
can stand up to the kind of heavy daily use I put on a Bible.

Thus, the NASB Pitt it is.  We’ll see how this goes.  If the Lord wills, I'll tell all in December 2015.
