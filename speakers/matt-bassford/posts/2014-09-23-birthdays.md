So. . . I’m 36, I guess. 
Many thanks to the electronic well-wishers who thronged around me on
Facebook yesterday.  It is good to be
remembered, even though birthday celebrations make no sense to me.  If you ask me some Bible question, you get a
novel in response.  If you ask me what I
want for my birthday, you get a deer-in-the-headlights look.  I don’t know, a spare idea for a great hymn,
maybe, if you’ve got one of those lying around? 
In this, as in many other areas, I am the despair of my wife.

The problem (if it may be called that) is that I already
have the  things I want and don’t see the
need for anything else.  I have a
wonderful wife and two wonderful children (Marky’s contribution this
morning:  “We’re lions!  RAWR!”). 
I have my health.  I have the
opportunity to provide for my family doing work that I love.  I live in a land that has not felt the tread
of contending armies since the Civil War (and oh! what a rare and precious blessing
that is!).



Apart from those things, I have what Paul calls “every
spiritual blessing in the heavenly places”. 
I have been rescued from my sins, and adopted as a child of God.  I have the ability to discover Him in His creation
and His word.  I can bring whatever I
want to Him in prayer, assured that He will care even if no one else seems
to.  I belong to His people, who enrich
my life in a thousand different ways, of which the expression of birthday good
wishes is only one.

In short, I already have all the things that wisdom teaches
are the foundation of the good life.  I
don’t have them because I deserve them (indeed, I do not!), but because God is
extravagantly gracious.  Every day, I am
astonished by the greatness of His blessings, and September 22nd was
no different.  With humility and
bemusement then, I can only say “Thank You” to Him, as to the rest of my
friends, and pray that the next year may offer, as the last has surely done,
new opportunities to glorify Him.
