I’ve been using the Foundation SCR for my work at the church
building for about six months.  During
that time, I’ve gotten a pretty good handle on how the Bible functions as a
preacher’s primary Bible.  My
observations are as follows:

Generally, I like the size of the SCR.  My copy, which is of the narrow-margin
variety, has a trim size of 9” x 6”. 
That’s right up against the limits of comfort for me, but it’s still
within them.  I can hold the Bible in one
hand and read out of it without feeling like I’m in the middle of a balancing
act or about to drop the Bible.  It fills
my hand, but it doesn’t overwhelm it. 
Also, the Bible is certainly big enough to make a dignified
notes-holder.

I’m not as big a fan of the size of the SCR when I’m
teaching a Bible class, though.  It
weighs in at about 2 pounds, 13 ounces, which makes it among the larger Bibles
that I own, and it’s heavy enough that I have to think about holding it.  I’m not going to spend a 45-minute class
waving it around like a flyswatter.

Indeed, the weight is enough that I will unconsciously
abandon it on the lectern when I’m not thinking about it.  On one memorable occasion, a student was
reading a passage, I was drifting to the right to make eye contact with her
once she was done, and I realized in mid-drift that I was drifting without my
Bible!  I hurriedly retreated to the
lectern and picked up the text that I was supposed to be reading before anyone
noticed.  I haven’t done that since, but
I do spend Bible classes thinking stern “Hold on to your Bible!” thoughts at
myself, which distracts me ever so slightly from teaching.

The size of the SCR yields mixed results, but its
readability is an asset everywhere. 
Sadly, the paper on my narrow-margin SCR isn’t nearly as good as the
paper on the wide-margin SCR’s from 10-15 years ago, but I find that it doesn’t
matter.  The text is in 11-point type,
which is poster-sized as far as I’m concerned, and text that size laughs at
poorer-quality paper and show-through. 
It’s one of the most readable Bibles I own, and I suspect that if I held
it up for them, the people in the pews could probably read it too!

Even with the giant type, the SCR still has room between its
covers for a sizable number of cross-references.  They’re in font that’s larger than the
primary font of some Bibles I’ve owned. 
I don’t get much mileage out of them in my preaching or study (though
sometimes in the Synoptics I will use them to quickly figure out where the
parallel texts are), but they can certainly be useful in a Bible-class
format.  Sometimes, I’ll be able to find
a particular verse by tracking down a similar verse and following the
cross-references from it.

The layout of the SCR is ideal for everything I do within
the confines of the church building.  It
is verse-by-verse, and the verse numbers are in 11-point type too, right on the
left edge of the column.  When I’m
looking for a passage in the pulpit, that’s equivalent to a neon sign that
says, “Here I am!  Read me!”  Likewise, when I’m writing a sermon or a
workbook, I really have to work at it to miss a Scripture citation.  In addition, the SCR is single-column rather
than double-column, so I only have one row of 11-point numbers to scan, not
two.  Passage-location couldn’t be
easier.

Overall, the only issues I’ve experienced with the SCR in my
preaching, teaching, and study have to do with its size when I am teaching a
Bible class.  As far as I know, there is
no such thing as a smaller single-column, verse-by-verse, cross-referenced
NASB, so this text block is the gold standard in the pulpit.  I give it a 5/5.
