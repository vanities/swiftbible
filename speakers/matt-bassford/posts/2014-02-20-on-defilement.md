*To the pure, all
things are pure; but to those who are defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure,
but both their mind and their conscience are defiled.*

-- Titus 1:15 (NASB)

It seems like every cultural movement has a standard-bearer,
someone who both epitomizes its values and shows the direction it is
headed.  Enlightenment France had
Voltaire.  Victorian England had Charles
Dickens.  In modern America, we have
Miley Cyrus.  As we stumble together
toward depravity, she appears to be the wretch who is leading the whole
miserable parade, at least for the moment.

Poor Miley.  I don’t
mean this sarcastically; indeed, I am quite sincere.  I pity her for any number of reasons, but
perhaps most of all, I pity her for the impossibility of the task she has set
for herself.  She has made it her goal to
shock the American public on a consistent basis.  This was easier several years ago, when she
had not yet completed her metamorphosis from sweet, virginal Hannah Montana to
tawdry tabloid fodder.  Hannah Montana
could do shocking things.  Miley Cyrus
cannot, even as her attempts to do so grow ever more frantic.  Pitiable? 
Yes.  Repellent?  Absolutely. 
Shocking?  Certainly not.

Sadly, this same frantic search for anything that will
shock, anything that will stir genuine emotion, is not limited to Miley
Cyrus.  It is present in her audience as
well.  Poor Americans!  In this too, I am sincere.  Never before has it been so easy for us to
gratify the lusts of the flesh.  Sins
that used to huddle in dark corners now strut in the spotlight.  The Internet will bring any immoral act we
can imagine (and many more that we cannot) to the screens of our computers and
smartphones, streaming, in high definition, probably for free.

However, this Garden of Earthly Delights does not
satisfy.  Indeed, the opposite is
true.  It dulls our palates.  As Miley is driven, so we too are driven to
seek out ever more exotic displays, ever more exotic pleasures that still
retain some vestige of freshness.  Take
the *Fifty Shades of Gray*
phenomenon.  There is nothing new about
bondage, domination, or sadomasochism. 
If you want to read a book about a man who drew sexual pleasure from the
suffering of others, pick up a biography of the Roman emperor Nero.  The genius of *Fifty Shades*, though, is that it has moved those practices from the
fringes to the mainstream, dressed up in an appealing package, to titillate
half the soccer moms in suburbia.  Oh,
my!  He really did that?  Shocking!

Or at least it was, back in 2011.  Now, *Fifty
Shades* has spawned an entire genre of copycat novels, many of which
reportedly make the original look tame by comparison.  Shocking? 
Not anymore.  The *Fifty Shades* movie is apparently pretty
strong stuff, explicit enough to drive even some modern actors and actresses
off the set (There are things THEY won’t do on camera?  Who knew?). 
It’ll shock when it comes out, for a little while, but eventually it
will lose its savor too.  Five years from
now, there will probably be a bondage sitcom on NBC, and nobody will be much
stirred by any of it.

It is true that the wages of sin is death.  Though less obvious, it is equally true that
the wages of sin is deadness, a deadness of spirit in which the sinner becomes
so jaded that he has lost the ability to feel. 
All sin does this.  The alcoholic
doesn’t feel alive the 500th time he gets drunk.  He only feels slightly less dead.

By contrast, this is not true of the delight that God
gives.  I don’t grow weary of snuggling
with my children on the couch watching a Disney movie.  I don’t have to watch two Disney movies with them
next time to feel the same pleasure (thankfully).  Instead, the experience cheers and ennobles
me.  It scrapes some of the accumulated gunk
off the surface of my heart.  And yet,
the devil has amazing success with fooling us, in getting us to believe that
joy can be found instead in the things that degrade and blind.

We know where Miley Cyrus is headed.  We’ve seen it all before.  10 years from now, she’ll look about 30 years
older, gaunt-cheeked, dead-eyed, used up like an aging prostitute by hard
living and exploitation.  She won’t have
to pretend to be ratchet anymore.  She’ll
be there.  We’ll have some new
standard-bearer in her place, fresh-faced, foolish, willing to be used, willing
to help everybody else feel a little bit for a little bit as we simultaneously
bemoan and revel in her corruption.
