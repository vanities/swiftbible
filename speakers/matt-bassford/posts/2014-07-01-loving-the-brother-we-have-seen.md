*If someone says, "I love God," and hates his brother, he is a
liar; for the one who does not love his brother whom he has seen, cannot love
God whom he has not seen.*

-- 1 John 4:20 (NASB)

We all know that the greatest
commandment is to love God, so obedience to this commandment should be the focus of our spiritual lives.  Sadly, all of us face a tremendous obstacle in our pursuit of this love.  It’s
easy for us to reach a point where we can say, “I love
God” with complete sincerity, yet have ill feelings toward some brother whom we
think has wronged us.



However, if we claim to love God whom we have not seen, while not loving the
brother whom we have seen, it's likely that something else is going on.  We probably are able to
say we love God precisely because we *haven’t*
seen Him yet.  If we
have not yet learned to love our brethren, and God were to reveal Himself to us, it is nearly certain that our selfish hearts would lead us to be upset at or indifferent to Him too.  If we doubt this, we must remember that when God truly *did*become flesh and dwell among us, most people found so much to dislike
in Him that they killed Him.

Clearly, we all need to
work on loving our brother whom we do see. 
This love is not merely a warm fuzzy feeling, though it certainly involves
that.  It is the love that transforms
our attitudes and our actions so that we are constantly seeking the other’s
good.  Sometimes, it’s easy to think and
behave in a loving way.  There are some aspects of love that come naturally to us.  At other times, love takes work, and then, we must determine to put in the work required.  After all, if
we haven’t yet learned to love our brother fully, we haven’t yet learned
to love God fully either.
