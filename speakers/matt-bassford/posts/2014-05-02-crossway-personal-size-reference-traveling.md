I
like small Bibles in a lot of settings, but they are never more useful than
when I’m on the go.  They’re more
convenient to tote around, and they’re less vulnerable to a host of car-borne
disasters.  Unsurprisingly, then, I think
the stubby Crossway PSR makes a fine travel Bible.

Certainly,
the PSR doesn’t have the petite dimensions of, say, a Cambridge Pitt Minion or
some other compact Bible, but it still shows an ability to get out of harm’s
way.  First, it passes the all-important
glove-box test.  It fits easily in the
glove compartment of either my Toyota Corolla or Kia Rondo (Yes, I know; I
drive boring cars.  What were you
expecting from a Bible blogger?).



Second,
it tucks neatly into the side pocket of my laptop case.  It’s true that I can squeeze a much larger
Bible into that pocket, as I did for a couple of years when I used a wide-margin
Foundation side-column reference NASB as my primary Bible.  However, that makes the laptop case look
pregnant, and it’s awkward to carry.  The
PSR doesn’t give me any grief in that regard.

It’s
true that the PSR won’t fit in my pocket, but that’s not a big deal to me.  For starters, I’m built like a stick, so it
may well be that there is no such thing as a complete Bible that will ride
easily in the hip pocket of my jeans. 
Second, if I’m going to be on my feet so much that I need a Bible I can
carry with me, I’ll most likely also have a backpack or laptop case that’ll
tote a larger edition.  Third, I’m just
not that keen about squinting at 5-point font, so most compact Bibles are too
small even for me.

The
PSR, then, will travel with me anywhere I want to go.  What’s more, it’ll do the job once it gets
there.  The font of the PSR is large
enough that I can share the Bible with my wife or whoever else happens to be
sitting next to me in a Wednesday-night Bible class.  The PSR is a cross-referenced Bible, so it
offers me some protection against ambushes in a personal study.  Finally, as I’ve described elsewhere, the PSR
performs competently as a preaching Bible and a reading Bible, and it’ll do
both of those things regardless of location.

Sometimes,
in fact, its high functionality surprises me. 
For instance, one Sunday evening, I was doing some appointment preaching
for a nearby congregation, but when I reached their building, I realized that I
had neglected to put my PowerPoint presentation on a jump drive and bring it
with me.  I wasn’t going to be able to
read my passages from the screen, which is what I customarily do during a
topical sermon.  Instead, I was going to
have to locate and read them from the Bible, just like the apostle Paul used to
do from his King James Bible (I kid, I kid!).

This
was a stern test for my PSR, but it performed capably despite the
circumstances.  I do recall one instance
in which I had any trouble locating a Scripture, but that had more to do with
my fumbly fingers than any fault in the PSR. 
I still don’t consider a paragraphed Bible ideal for pulpit work, but
the PSR will preach just fine on the road if pressed into service.

The
only negative I can identify about the PSR as a travel Bible is its
polyurethane cover.  Heat and direct
sunlight aren’t going to be kind to any cover, but they are the particular
enemies of poly Bibles.  All I have to do
is leave the PSR on my dashboard in the sun once, and I might as well drive a
stake through its heart, stuff garlic in its mouth, and bury it at a
crossroads.

That
notwithstanding, this is a Bible I bought for $30 retail.  You can locate it or its PRB descendant
online for $15.  Good luck finding a new
genuine-leather Bible at that price point! 
As a travel Bible, the PSR is an excellent value.  I give it a rating of 4.5/5.



**OVERALL RATING: 
16.5/20**
