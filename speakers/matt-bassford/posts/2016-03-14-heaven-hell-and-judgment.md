The other day, I was over visiting James Hensley, and he
told me that there were four questions he would like me to answer in a
sermon.  The fourth question was, “Did
God create Satan?”, and that one’s not much of a sermon-starter.  I don’t know whether God created Satan
because the Bible doesn’t say one way or the other, and it doesn’t take very
long to say that.

However, James also wanted to know about three other
matters, subjects to which the Scripture does speak.  I don’t know whether James intended his
questions to be thematic, but to my eye, they were.  As a result, they fit neatly under the
umbrella of a single sermon.  This
morning, then, we’re going to be considering the three awe-inspiring topics of
heaven, hell, and judgment.



**Is Heaven Real?**

James first asked, “Is heaven real?”  As questions go, this is an important
one!  After all, if heaven isn’t real,
none of us have any hope, and we’re pretty much wasting our time here this
morning.  Thankfully, the best Biblical
answer is that it is **MORE REAL THAN
EARTH IS.** Consider Paul’s
perspective on it in a passage we read together a couple weeks back, 2
Corinthians 5:1-4.  Really, what Paul is
contrasting here is earthly existence with heavenly existence.  He compares our earthly bodies to a
tent.  Paul was a tentmaker, and he
probably knew better than just about anybody how frail tents were.  Even now, if you’ve gone camping in a tent,
and it’s late at night, and you hear something growling and snuffling around
outside the tent, that’s not the most comforting feeling in the world!  Paul wants us to understand that our earthly
lives are like that.  They’re frail,
fragile, and can be completely destroyed in a moment.

However, he compares our heavenly existence to living in a
house.  Especially when we compare them
to tents, houses are strong and secure. 
You don’t pack them up and carry them around.  They stay in one place.  That’s what our heavenly lives will be
like.  Rather than being filled with uncertainty,
fear, and danger, they will be filled with stability, safety, and peace.  We can’t see that heavenly reality, but
compared to it, this life is only an illusion.

It is also true that heaven is **WHERE OUR CITIZENSHIP IS.** 
Paul advances this idea in Philippians 3:20-21.  2000 years ago, this was a significant point
to make.  Back then, the nature of your
citizenship was a big deal.  If you were
a Roman citizen, as Paul was, you had all kinds of rights and privileges in the
Roman Empire that mere provincial citizens didn’t have.  There are several places in Acts where Paul’s
Roman citizenship changes the story dramatically.

Both then and today, Christians are citizens of heaven.  The New Jerusalem is the city where we
belong.  As was true of being a Roman
citizen, or a United States citizen, for that matter, we have all kinds of
special rights and privileges because of that citizenship.  Not least, it means that we have the right to
pass through the gates of heaven one day.

What’s more, it makes heaven the subject of our
allegiance.  We belong to that country
first and to our earthly country second. 
If we spend our lives anticipating that great day when we will enter
God’s heavenly kingdom, we will live those lives differently.  We won’t be so concerned with the political
tumults of today.  Instead, we will be
concerned with the city not made with hands, with doing everything we can to
maintain our citizenship and extend that citizenship to others.

Third, heaven is **WHERE
OUR INHERITANCE IS.** We see Peter’s
take on this in 1 Peter 1:3-4.  On earth,
we tend to think of inheritances as something of a mixed blessing.  It’s good that we get all of that money and
stuff and whatever, but it’s bad that somebody we love had to die for us to get
it.  Generally, we’d rather have the
loved one than the money!  Peter
describes the blessings of heaven as our inheritance to emphasize that we only
have those blessings because Jesus died so that we could have them.  They are His bequest to us.

On earth, there may well be a number of hoops that we have
to jump through to receive an inheritance. 
The will might have to go through probate, we might have to turn 25
before the trust terminates, and so on. 
When it comes to our heavenly inheritance, the condition that we must
fulfill is lifelong faithfulness.  The
guy who never bothers to probate the will never gets the inheritance, even if
his rich uncle left him everything.  In
the same way, if we aren’t devoted to Christ here on earth, the fact that we
could have received the heavenly inheritance will be meaningless.  We won’t have fulfilled the conditions
necessary to gain it.

Finally, heaven is **WHERE
THE LORD IS.** Here, let’s read
together from John 14:1-3.  It’s funny
how there are some passages that you associate with certain people, and this is
a passage I will always associate with Jesse Fugett.  If you were putting together a song service
and you invited him to read from a passage of his choice, he would always pick
the first part of John 14.

I think he loved this text for good reason.  It’s one of the most beautiful passages in
the entire Bible, not merely because of the simple poetry with which it is
written, but because of the thought it contains.  No matter how humble a Christian we may be,
Jesus knows who we are, and right now, He is preparing a place for each one of
us to live with Him forever.

I think that’s a promise that only a disciple can
understand.  People who are indifferent
to Jesus in this life are also going to be indifferent to the thought of
eternity with Jesus.  Even if they got to
heaven, they wouldn’t enjoy it there. 
However, for those of us who have loved Him and studied Him and sought
Him, there can be no greater reward than to be in His presence eternally.

Before the Lord’s Supper, then, let’s focus on this great
hope of being forever with the Lord in heaven. 
Jesus died so that we could receive this precious inheritance, but the
inheritance itself is that we get to be alive together with Him.  This is the joy that is set before us.  Either it matters more to us than anything
else does, or it doesn’t matter at all. 
What do our lives say about where our hearts are?  Let’s examine ourselves in this as we
partake.

**Is Hell Real?**

As many of you have probably already concluded, James’ next
question was “Is Hell Real?”  It might
seem like this question is answered by the answer to the first.  After all, the Bible never talks about a
third possible eternal destination. 
However, according to a 2015 Pew Research Center survey, 72 percent of
Americans believe in heaven, but only 58 percent believe in hell.  I guess that 14 percent in between either
believes that everybody goes to heaven, or they’re just confused.

Regardless, the Bible certainly teaches that hell is real,
and much to the surprise of people who don’t know the Bible very well, the
primary Biblical authority on hell is Jesus. 
This is at odds with the popular image of Jesus as a milquetoast hippie
in a bathrobe, but it fits very well with the gospels’ presentation of a stern
prophet who came to call His nation to repentance.

The first thing Jesus teaches us about hell is that it was **PREPARED FOR THE DEVIL AND HIS ANGELS.** Look at Matthew 25:41.  This phrase, which isn’t even really part of
the main argument of the context, offers us an interesting glimpse into the
mind of God.  There is a sense in which
mankind is like the rebellious angels. 
After all, like them, we were offered a choice between God and the devil
and chose the devil.  However, the
similarities end there.  The Hebrews
writer tells us that God gives no help to angels, only to the descendant of
Abraham.  Jesus didn’t die to save the
souls of angels.  He died to save the
souls of men, and His blood is powerful enough that if the whole world turned
to Him, it would be sufficient for their salvation.

For us, then, God prepared a Redeemer, but for the fallen
angels, He prepared a place of eternal torment. 
To us, He offers mercy, but to them, He will only mete out justice.  God’s will and God’s plan is that every
single human being should accept His gift of grace.  Hell was not prepared for any of the children
of Adam.  However, if we reject that
grace and express our love for the devil instead, we leave God no other choice
but to consign us to hell along with the devil and his angels.

Even if hell is not intended for people, that doesn’t make
an eternal stay there any more pleasant. 
The Scripture depicts it as **A
PLACE OF TORMENT.** Most notably,
consider Mark 9:47-48.  This brings a
couple of awful images together.  The
first is that being in hell feels like being in an unquenchable fire.  This reminds me of a Valentine’s Day
experience I had back when Lauren and I were still living in Beaumont.  I’d decided that I was going to bake her a
pizza from scratch for V-Day, and at the time, we had a pizza stone on a metal
rack.  I took the pizza crust out of the
oven to put sauce, cheese, and toppings on it, and, without thinking, grabbed
the metal rack bare-handed to adjust the pizza. 
As a result, Lauren got to finish her own Valentine’s Day dinner while I
sat miserably with my hand in a bowl of ice water.  I have a pretty high pain tolerance, but that
hurt!

Likewise, we’ve all seen what happens to an animal carcass
in the summertime after it’s been sitting out for a few days.  You look closely at it, and it is seething
with maggots.  Jesus wants us to imagine
that it is our bodies being eaten up by worms, forever.  He wants us to imagine that it is all of our
skin that is being seared, forever.  I
don’t think there is literal fire in hell or literal worms in hell, but I know
that I don’t want to spend any time in a place the Lord depicts like that!

A different, but no more pleasant, image is that Jesus
describes hell as **A PLACE OF
DARKNESS.** Here, consider Matthew
25:29-30.  Here too, I think there’s
benefit to considering the precise words that our Lord used.  He doesn’t describe hell as merely
darkness.  He describes it as the “outer
darkness”.  This isn’t something that
resonates with us as much because we live in a much safer time than Jesus
did.  Back then, a village or a city
wasn’t merely a place to shelter from the elements.  It was a place where you banded together for
safety from all of the wild beasts and bad men outside who wanted to sneak up
on you and kill you.  If you are cast out
of the master’s house and into the outer darkness, then, you have been denied
any security.  This is why belonging to a
group back then was such a huge big deal. 
Exiles were dead meat.

Jesus, then, wants us to understand hell not only as a place
of torment, but as a place of continual fear and danger.  It’s a place not only of bodily suffering,
but of mental and spiritual suffering.  The
best comparison that I can think of is when, in a horror movie, the killer is
sneaking up on some hapless teenage girl, and she’s fussing around with
something, but at the last moment, she hears a footstep, turns around, sees the
killer, and screams as she is killed. 
That scream, that moment of pure terror, is short-lived in the
movies.  In hell, it goes on and on,
forever.

Finally, Jesus portrays hell as **A PLACE OF SEPARATION.** Look
at Luke 13:26-28.  Here, the Lord is
returns to our theme of exile.  This
time, however, the issue isn’t exile from what. 
It’s exile from whom.  Earlier, we
noted that the central defining characteristic of heaven is that it is the
place where God is.  Conversely, the
central defining characteristic of hell is that it is the place where God
isn’t.  Hell is the absence of God.

The absence of God is nothing less than a disaster, even for
people who spent their entire lives showing not a scrap of interest in
Him.  After all, God in His mercy blesses
even those who reject Him.  The rain
falls on the just and the unjust.  God is
behind every good thing that even the atheist enjoys.  In hell, however, the blessing ceases.  No more kindness, no more mercy,
forever.

In heaven, the followers of Christ get what they spent their
whole lives working toward.  In hell, the
enemies of Christ get what they spent their whole lives working toward.  They don’t want God; now, they don’t get
God.  In tragic irony, they will only
learn their mistake too late.

**Will God Condemn All
Unbelievers?**

James’ third question for me was, “Will God condemn all
unbelievers?”  I didn’t ask for an
explanation of the question, but here’s what I think is behind it.  Every once in a while, somebody will float a
hypothetical about some tribesman in the remotest Amazon jungle who is born,
lives, and dies without ever hearing the gospel.  Will God condemn somebody like that to hell
when they never really had a chance to be saved?

The Biblical answer is **AN
UNEQUIVOCAL “YES”.** Consider what
Paul writes in Romans 1:18-20.  This, of
course, is part of Paul’s great argument that all men are under sin and so need
salvation by grace through faith in Jesus. 
In particular, Paul is talking here about the Gentiles.  When he mentions those who “suppress the
truth in unrighteousness”, he’s not talking about people who reject the
Scriptures.  As he goes on to explain,
he’s talking about people who reject the physical evidence of God in creation,
the evidence that is part of the experience of every human being.  When they look up at the stars or out at the
ocean, yet choose not to glorify God or seek out His will, they have failed in
their duty to their Creator.  For that
reason, Paul says, God will pour out His wrath on them and be justified in
doing so.  Their failure to learn from
nature is inexcusable.

To put things another way, it is not rejection of the gospel
that causes people to be lost.  It is
rejection of God and his will, and the Bible is only one of the ways that God
reveals Himself.  It’s just as sinful for
a tribesman in the Amazon to refuse to honor God as it is for me to refuse.

Of course, not everybody who sees **GOD IN THE NATURAL WORLD** rejects Him.  Some people learn from nature and seek Him,
as Paul points out in Acts 17:26-27. 
Once you realize there is a God, it’s logical to inquire into what God
might expect of you, and God has always wanted all people to do that.  This reminds me of one of the elders of the
Dowlen Road church when I was in the preacher-training program there, a brother
named Victor Campbell, who has since passed on.

Vic was an interesting guy. 
He was an Englishman and was raised as an atheist.  Like many Englishmen do, when he reached
adulthood, he went away to sea.  One day,
he was standing at the railing of his ship, surveying the majesty of the ocean,
when he had a thought he had never had before. 
He said to himself, “There must be a God who is responsible for all of
this.”

That realization changed his life, and after many further
adventures, he found his way into the Lord’s church, settled down in southeast
Texas, and eventually attained the position of elder.  In the words of Paul, he groped for God and
found Him, because He was never very far away.

Indeed, we have **GOD’S
PROMISE** that people who truly want to find Him will succeed.  Consider the words of the Lord in Matthew
7:7-8.  This is tremendously
encouraging!  Every once in a while, I’ll
bump into somebody who shows that God keeps this promise.  One of them is our brother Randy Lee, who
used to be a member here before he moved to New Mexico.  Randy was raised in a denominational church,
but once he became an adult, he started reading his Bible, and he realized that
what he was reading about wasn’t what his church was actually doing.  He moved on to another church, with the same
result, then another, then another, and so on.

Eventually, he found his way to the congregation at Cherry
Hill, but he concluded that they couldn’t justify their institutional practices
from Scripture.  Finally, he came to us
and said, “Yes!  This is what I’ve been
looking for!”  I admit that men with
hearts as honest as Randy’s are rare, but that’s their fault, not God’s
fault.  For people who do have that
honest heart, sooner or later, they’ll find the truth.

I think this promise holds true for our hypothetical Amazon
tribesman too.  Providence is an amazing
thing.  I am confident that if a man is
seeking the truth, somehow God will find a way to get him the truth, even if it
seems impossible to us.  God will condemn
the sinner if He must, but His desire is for everyone to repent and come to a
knowledge of the truth.  He’s not going
to turn away anyone who truly wants to find Him.
