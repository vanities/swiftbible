This will be a short review section.  I could talk about how the GPR laughed at the
glove-box test, but I won’t.  I could
talk about how when open, the GPR covered half the table at Panera, but I
won’t.  Instead, a single incident will
suffice to reveal how I feel about the GPR as a travel Bible.

One Saturday, my wife and I decided to take the family to
Orland Park.  She wanted to go
running-shoe shopping, but we knew that making the Dynamic Duo hang out for an
hour in a shoe store would be unfair to them and destructive to the store.  We decided, then, that she would drop off the
kids and me at Chick-Fil-A, she would shop in our absence, and when she was
done, we would eat lunch together there.



I knew, then, that I would have an hour of hanging out near
the Chick-Fil-A PlayPlace, waiting for wails of dismay from within.  I figured I might as well use the time to do
some Bible reading.  For various reasons,
most of my Bibles were out of the house, so I was left with two choices:  the hulking GPR I was supposed to be reading,
or the pink Zondervan Thinline that we hope to give to our daughter once she
reads well enough to need a Bible.  With
little hesitation, I grabbed the Thinline and headed for the door.

It is therefore literally true that I would rather sit in a
crowded restaurant reading out of a pink Bible than carry along the GPR.  The GPR is good at some things.  This isn't one of them.  1/5.
