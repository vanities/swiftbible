Every once in a while, the Pew Research Center will offer up
another report on religion in America.  I
anticipate these reports with great eagerness. 
They’re impeccably researched, and they often cast useful light on our
religious experiences.

A couple days ago, Pew released a report on changing
churches and church attendance generally. 
You can read the full report [here](http://www.pewforum.org/2016/08/23/choosing-a-new-church-or-house-of-worship/).  There are all kinds of interesting data
therein, but as I was reading through it, one set of results in particular
caught my eye.  The researches asked
respondents a series of questions about regularity of attendance.  This revealed that about 22 percent of
Americans now attend services much less frequently than they used to.



As any church leader knows, declining attendance is a
disastrous sign for the spiritual health of any member.  Sure, showing up every Sunday is not proof
that everything is well with a particular Christian, but no longer showing up *is* proof that everything is not
well!  Practically speaking,
non-attendees are no longer receiving regular instruction about the Bible, nor
are they benefiting from regular interaction with other Christians.  It’s a recipe for spiritual sickness and
death.  I can’t think of a single time
when I saw a brother or sister begin to attend less and was happy about it.

So.  Lots of people
attend less than they used to, and at least for those who are attending
churches that teach the truth, decreased attendance is a terribly bad
sign.  It’s the devil driving a wedge
between us and the Lord.

What’s the wedge?  What
is it that, more than anything else, Satan uses to separate sheep from the
flock?  Porn?  Affairs? 
Evolution?  The influence of
secular humanism?

Nope, nope, nope, and nope. 
According to Pew’s statistics, the single reason most cited for
decreased church attendance is “Too busy”. 
Fully one out of every five reduced attendees became so because their
schedules didn’t have room for faith anymore.

The devil’s most likely appearances in our lives, then,
don’t look like seductive strangers or university professors.  They look like the boss who asks us to work
Wednesday evenings, or even Sunday mornings if he can get away with it.  They look like the school administrators who
schedule open houses and graduations with no concern for times of religious
assembly.  They look like the coaches who
insist on commitment to the team before anything else.  They look like all the other people who are so
intent on cramming our schedules full of stuff so that even if we are
technically able to attend services, we’re too worn out to make it.

These are the spiritual killers.  These are the people who will (obliviously)
destroy our faith and our families’ faith if we let them.  They will inexorably squeeze God out of our
lives, five minutes at a time.  In fact,
if we aren’t paying attention, they are almost certainly doing it to us right
now.

In Matthew 4:10, Jesus rejects the devil’s offer of all the
kingdoms of the world by saying, “You shall worship the Lord your God, and
serve Him only.”  We understand of
course, that putting God first of everything means putting Him first in
everything.  God doesn’t only want us to
be good worshipers.  He wants us to be
good workers, good parents, and good friends.

However, when any of those other things (or, indeed, all of
them) begin to nibble away at the time we devote to the Lord, we must recognize
that we are being called on to change gods. 
A God who isn’t first in our schedules really isn’t first anywhere.
