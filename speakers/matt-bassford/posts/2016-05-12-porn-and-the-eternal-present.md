Every once in a while, the Internet will present you with a
novel argument.  I found one in [this article.](http://www.firstthings.com/blogs/firstthoughts/2016/03/sex-trumps-history)  The author is perhaps too enamored of the
phrase “sempiternal orgiast”, but sempiternal orgiasts aside, he makes a great
point about pornography.  In addition to
all the other evils and harms that accompany porn use, it focuses our mind on
the present at the expense of the past and the future.



As all married people know, sex God’s way is about
relationship.  I’ve always found it
appropriate that the preferred Biblical euphemism for sex is “knowing”, because
that’s really what sex is about.  In
marriage, you know (sexually) the person you know (comprehensively) better than
you know anybody else.

The longer a marriage continues, the more experiences, both
good and bad, you bring to the marriage bed with you.  Sex in marriage isn’t like a vase of cut
roses.  It’s like a lilac bush in your garden.  You see the past in the lilac bush (maybe
pruning that branch wasn’t such a great idea), but you work now, to mitigate
mistakes and repair neglect, so that the plant will flourish and bloom in the
future.  God intends for this to be a
lifelong work, so that even after the charms of youth have faded, the
relationship that a couple has built together will sustain their intimacy.  Gray hair and wrinkles and stretch marks have
a charm all their own when you know where they came from.

That’s God’s way. 
Porn takes God’s way and dynamites it. 
Sex in marriage is about relationship; porn is about the absence of
relationship.  Marriage is about
familiarity.  Porn is about novelty.  I doubt there’s a porn user on the planet who
only looks at the same picture or video over and over again.  Instead, the engine that powers the porn
industry is the search for variety, for that which is more explicit, more
intense, more bewitching than anything the poor soul at the screen has ever seen.  Forget what’s gone before.  Now is what matters.

The eternal now, though, is wretched soil for a soul to
thrive in.  Now does not concern itself
with the past.  It has no interest in the
relationships that have been twisted by the porn-saturated mind.  It has no concern for the promises to spouses
that are not being kept.  For that
matter, it is unconcerned with the promises to God that are not being kept.

Likewise, now doesn’t care about the future.  It has no thought for the corrosive nature of
porn use, for what it will do to the mind and the soul and the heart.  It doesn’t care about the damage being done
to the husband or wife of one’s youth. 
It refuses to see the barriers to intimacy that are rising between the
user and a future spouse.  It ignores the
diminishing returns that accompany porn use, as they do any sin.  Tomorrow is not going to be better.  It is going to be worse, with greater
enslavement and degradation and less and less pleasure.  Finally, of course, now does not acknowledge
the chasm that the sinner is digging between himself and his Creator.

This way of thinking will not remain only in the sexual
realm.  The author of the original
article traces all kinds of social ills to this way of thinking (Lady Gaga was
in there somewhere).  I’m not willing to
blame pop culture on porn use, but it cannot help but corrupt the mind
generally.  If now is what matters, now
is what matters.  It’s tough to save
prudently for retirement or work effectively in your church if your whole life
is focused on the five minutes right in front of you.  I would not be surprised if, as porn use
reshapes our minds in other ways, it reshapes our minds to think that way too.

We have a name for beings that live only in the now.  We call them “animals”.  This, of course, is the devil’s desire for
all of us.  He would like nothing better
than to see each one of us become slaves of our flesh, miserable brutes whose
search for passing pleasure brings them no lasting joy.  No porn addict is happy.  Otherwise, they wouldn’t be addicts.

For those of you who find yourselves enslaved to
pornography, you have my sympathy and my pity. 
Even though it might not seem like it to you right now, there are ways
to escape the pit you’re in.  I would
recommend three of them to you:

* First, resolve that you are no longer going to leave any
  room in your heart or your life for lust. 
  To put it in Pauline terms, make no provision for the flesh.  Make that promise to yourself and God, and
  keep it.
* Second, when you are tempted, pray.  The devil may return at a more opportune
  time, but he will always flee from prayer.
* Third, when you are tempted, count the cost.  Measure the cost not in terms of the pleasure
  of the moment but in terms of what sin has done to your past and will continue
  to do to your future.  Once we reckon up
  the total, we will see that none of us can afford to pay the devil’s prices.

This is no easy battle to win, but we are all more than
conquerors through Him who loved us.  In
Christ, we can do this.  We must do
it.  None of us can dare to forfeit the
future for the sake of an eternal present.
