We live in a world that increasingly takes its cue from
Pilate’s cynical question in John 18:38, “What is truth?”  Even though people have strong beliefs about
good and bad behavior, they simultaneously deny that there is any such thing as
objective morality.  My truth is just as
good as your truth, and in practice, the only way to decide between them is to
argue until one truth claim is drowned out.

Our Lord, however, didn’t operate that way.  Today, we tend to want to separate intellect
from emotion, but Jesus and His contemporaries understood that when it comes to
moral reasoning, the intellect and the emotions must work together.  We can have these intuitions about the right
thing to do, but unless we check them against some ideal of absolute right and
wrong, we can never be sure that they’re valid. 
How many of us have felt strongly that we were right to be angry at
somebody, only to learn later that we weren’t right?

The same holds true with warmer emotions.  Jesus was extraordinarily compassionate, but
His compassion was true to His ideals.  If
we want to be His disciples, we have to understand His mind in these
things.  Let’s consider, then, Jesus as a
man of mercy.



When we consider what the gospels have to say about the
mercy of the Lord, it’s obvious what **HIS
STARTING POINT** was.  This is probably
never better stated than in Luke 15:1-7. 
To me, this is one of the most beautiful thoughts in all of
Scripture.  It teaches us that every
single human being under heaven is valuable and precious in the eyes of
God.  Every one of us is a being of
intrinsic, and indeed infinite, worth.

This is not the way that the people of any time naturally
think.  In Jesus’ time, no less than our
own, wealthy and powerful people mattered, and poor and powerless people
didn’t.  As a result, Jesus’ contention
is no less radical and shocking in our time than it was in His.

True mercy can only be based on this conviction.  Colloquially, the difference between justice
and mercy is that justice is giving somebody what they deserve, but mercy is
giving them something they don’t deserve. 
Why do that?  Why trouble yourself
to be gracious to somebody who isn’t entitled to it, and who, in fact, may even
have offended you?

Jesus’ answer is, “Because everybody matters.”  Fundamentally, we feel compassion and show
mercy because everyone is created in the image of God, and our regard for them
is inseparable from our regard for Him. 
This is true for the people the world in general looks down on:  single moms, alcoholics, and so on.  It’s true for the people who have done things
to offend us personally.  We are merciful
to everyone as our Father is merciful, not merely because we feel, but because
we know.

Throughout His life, Jesus was faithful to His
principles.  First of all, He was
merciful **TO THE IGNORANT.** Look at Mark 6:34.  In context, Jesus’ compassion is even more
impressive.  This comes at a difficult
time in His ministry.  John the Baptist
has recently been beheaded, and Jesus Himself has been harassed to the point of
exhaustion by people who want Him to heal their sick.  Jesus wants nothing more than the opportunity
to spend some time regrouping with His apostles.

However, the crowds figure out His destination, and when He
shows up, they’re waiting.  It’s almost
like those videos you see of the paparazzi waiting for some movie star to come
out a door.  Rather than brushing through
the crowd, though, an exhausted Jesus stops and teaches them.  In the words of Matthew 9, He recognizes that
they are sheep without a shepherd.

I think probably all of us have been in that position, where
we’re absolutely exhausted, but we get handed another job.  Was Jesus thrilled to do this?  Did He even want to do it?  Probably not. 
However, those people mattered so much to Him that He was willing to
push on through His weariness to help them.

This suggests, then, that our evangelism problems might be
mercy problems.  Unlike Jesus, we don’t
look at the lost around us and see people who are harassed and helpless.  Rather than turning aside to the need, which
does indeed take effort, we push through the crowd.  Let’s learn from Him, then, to truly see the
situations that people are in and to share with them the gospel that will help
them.

Jesus’ mercy **FOR THE
NEEDY** is equally obvious.  Look at
the story of Matthew 15:32-36.  This is
the story of the feeding of the four thousand. 
We often talk about Jesus working miracles to confirm His preaching, and
He certainly did that, but here, at least part of His motivation was simple
compassion.  These people were hungry, it
was in His power to help them, so He did. 
Because every person mattered to Jesus, He was willing to attend to their
physical needs too.

This too is a valuable lesson about the way that mercy should
manifest itself in our lives.  In the
Scriptures, I don’t see any justification for using money from the church
treasury to care for the world’s poor. 
However, I see individual Christians repeatedly commanded to tend to the
needy themselves.  We must not think that
the church’s lack of responsibility in this area means that we aren’t
responsible either.

There are innumerable ways that we might fulfill this
commandment, just as there are innumerable needs and even innumerable kinds of
needs.  In our day, it may even be true
that there are more people who are hungry for social and emotional nourishment
than for physical food.  However, we all
ought to be people who are caring for somebody outside of our families
somehow.

This process can often present us with dilemmas.  We’re not sure whether somebody actually does
need help or if they’re merely looking to exploit us.  Certainly, we should not become enablers.  However, if we genuinely aren’t sure what we
should do, if anything, we ought to err on the side of mercy.  Isn’t that what we want God to do with us?

Next, we see Jesus extending mercy **TO THE GRIEVING.** Look at the
story of the widow’s son in Luke 7:11-15. 
Admittedly, there’s an economic side to this story too.  Because the dead man was the only son of the
widow, he was also her only means of support. 
However, I suspect that she’s probably more concerned about the death of
her son than about her grim financial future. 
Jesus sees her tears, tells her not to weep, and solves the problem in
the most dramatic way possible.

None of us have the power to raise the dead, but we are
still surrounded by the grieving.  Our
support is important when children are called on to bury their parents, but I
think it becomes crucial when parents must bury their children.  Lauren and I are among those who have lost
children, and looking back, other than the grace of God, there were two main
reasons why we came through Macy’s death as well as we did.  First, we didn’t isolate ourselves.  Second, we got a massive amount of help from
our church family in Joliet.

In circumstances like that, we’re tempted to avoid the
grieving because we don’t know what to say. 
Frankly, we shouldn’t be worried about that.  We can’t come in thinking that we’re going to
have the magic words that will fix things. 
That’s what Job’s friends thought. 
Instead, the most important things are time, attention, and love.  All of us can offer those, so all of us can
comfort those who mourn.

Finally, Jesus’ mercy extends **TO US.** Look at Hebrews 4:14-16.  I said earlier that the thought that every
human being matters is one of the most beautiful thoughts in the Bible.  This is another.  Jesus was tempted as we are.  Unlike us, He never sinned.  However, rather than teaching Him pride, it
taught Him compassion.  He doesn’t look
at us in all of our vile sins and say, “Look at those disgusting people!”  He looks at us and says, “I never sinned, but
I get why you did.  I will help
you.”  Friends, every one of us is the
object of the compassion of the Son of God. 
We’re insects next to Him, but He cares about each one of us deeply and
personally.  We can be sure that every
problem we’ve got, He’s right there with us.

In fact, every problem that Jesus solved during His
ministry, He will solve for us.  He will
have mercy on us in teaching us and leading us to heaven.  He will have mercy on us in seeing that our
earthly needs will always be provided for. 
He will have mercy on us in comforting us right in the middle of our
deepest griefs.  We can count on Him to
show mercy to us because every one of us matters to Him, and He is faithful to
His convictions.

Jesus is merciful and full of compassion, and this changes
the landscape of human existence. 
Without Him, our lives are grim, hopeless, and bound for disaster.  With Him, every burden we bear will be
lessened if not lifted, and we are assured of a future in which all burdens
will vanish forever.  How blessed are all
who trust in Him!
