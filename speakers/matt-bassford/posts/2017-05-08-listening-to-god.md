As you may well have noticed, a couple weeks back, a brother
named Ben Lee visited our assembly.  In
addition to being a close friend of mine, Ben works with the Dowlen Road church
in Beaumont, TX.  In my estimation, he’s
one of the most gifted and skilled preachers in the brotherhood.  I wanted to have him take the pulpit in my
place that Sunday, but there wasn’t enough time to set things up.  Instead, I took the next best opportunity,
from my perspective, at least.  I asked
him to critique my sermon so that he could help me improve my pulpit work.

We had a valuable conversation over coffee that afternoon
that gave me a lot to consider, but one of the main things that struck him
wasn’t related to my overall sermon quality at all.  My main sermon that morning was on learning
to listen in our human relationships, but he observed that everything I said
applied equally well to our relationship with God.  We decided, in fact, that there was a whole
sermon there, so this morning, I’m preaching on listening to God.



**The Problems With Not
Listening**

There are certainly problems associated with not listening
to one another, but there are *real* problems
associated with not listening to God! 
The first of these, once again, is **FAILURE
TO LEARN.** James highlights the
problems with hearing the word but not learning from it in James 1:22-25.  I have to admit; I’m not someone who’s
terribly concerned about my appearance. 
I’m happily married, so my looks, such as they are, have done the job I
wanted them to do.

However, neither do I
want to look like a slob when I go out in public, so after I’m done shaving, I
check myself in the mirror.  Have I got a
giant glob of shaving cream behind my ear? 
Is there a patch of stubble under my chin that I missed?  However, looking into the mirror like that is
only useful if I act on what I’ve seen. 
If I see that giant glob of shaving cream, forget about it, and come to
the assembly with it still sitting there, my having seen it won’t make me look any
less ridiculous!

So too with our encounters with the word.  If we hear a sermon on listening and then go
home and ignore our spouses, has that sermon helped us?  Does a Bible class on leadership do us any
good if it doesn’t inspire us to step up and lead in the church?  The Bible is an intensely practical book,
brethren, and if we hear it but don’t put it into practice, we have robbed it
of its power.

Second, not listening to God through His word leads to **LACK OF UNDERSTANDING.** Consider the sad story of Jeremiah 7:21-24.  Just as we are, God’s people thousands of
years ago were invited into a relationship with Him.  Both then and now, God’s word gives the
priceless gift of insight into His perfect nature.  Only by taking advantage of this and coming
to know God can we ever hope to please Him.

In fact, so many of the people around us don’t obey God
because they don’t know Him.  They say
things like, “God just wants me to be happy,” by which they mean, “God just
wants me to taste the passing pleasures of sin.”  Those claims have nothing to do with the real
God of heaven; they’re the inventions of people who know the concept of God but
do not understand Him.

How about us, though? 
Do we continually strive to know God better by looking into His word, or
do we assume we’ve got God all figured out and binge-watch a new show on
Netflix instead?  Brethren, every one of
us who is married knows the importance of continuing to listen to our spouses,
yet we’re only going to be married for a short human lifetime.  How much more should we invest ourselves in
understanding God, when we want our relationship with Him to last forever!

Third, not listening can lead to **HASTY ANSWERING** when it comes to the word too.  This happened to the Word of God Himself in
John 9:13-16.  When the Pharisees heard
that Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, they’d heard enough. Healing on the Sabbath
violated their traditions, so Jesus was a bad guy, period.  They never bothered to consider the evidence
of the actual miracle.

In the same way, it’s all too easy for human beings to jump
to a hasty conclusion in some spiritual matter without careful consideration of
the word.  They have some religious
tradition, they believe it’s true, and so they dismiss a challenge to that
tradition before studying the issue. 
There are all kinds of people out there who believe the tradition that
baptism isn’t necessary for salvation and refuse to open their minds to the
passages that teach that it clearly is.

As always, though, this “them” problem can easily be an “us”
problem.  We claim that the Bible is our only
guide, but we must constantly make sure that some human tradition hasn’t
sneaked into our understanding.  We
believe that some practice is right, but can we point to a passage that proves
that it is right?  We think some other
behavior is a sin, but can we go to book, chapter, and verse that show it is a
sin?  If not, we too are answering
hastily without considering the evidence.

Finally, not listening to God can only lead to **QUARRELING** with Him.  Look at the way that the people of Judah
alienate God in Jeremiah 25:4-7.  He
tells them what to do, He warns them that they are going to provoke Him if they
don’t do it, and they rebelliously go ahead and do it anyway.  As God observes, this can only work for their
harm.  It’s never a good idea to pick a
fight with a being who can squash you like a cockroach!

I think all of us would acknowledge that, yes, this is a bad
idea.  That’s an easy thing to say.  It’s much harder to be honest about whether
we are in the middle of picking a fight with God right now.  If we know what the gospel says and that it
applies to us, yet refuse to obey it, we’re picking a fight with God.  If we come to church faithfully yet engage in
the practice of secret sin, anything from porn use to bad treatment of our
spouses, we’re picking a fight with God. 
If we claim to put Him first but in reality put our jobs, our families,
or our free time first, we’re picking a fight with God.  Brethren, this can only work for our harm
too.  Rather than dismissing His word, we
must listen to it and make all the changes that it requires us to make.  Only then can we hope to live.

**How to Listen**

Failure to listen to God can only end in disaster.  Indeed, our very souls depend on our learning
how to listen to Him.  In this, we must
first **DESIRE UNDERSTANDING.** We see what is simultaneously one of the
most joyful and sobering commandments in the Bible in Ephesians 5:15-17.  It’s fair to say that our whole religious
practice is based on this one passage. 
We do what we do because we believe that we don’t have to rely on some
clergy class to tell us what to do. 
Instead, any Christian who is literate can understand the will of the
Lord for themselves.  How marvelous it is
that God has placed this priceless opportunity in our hands!

However, friends, if we’re individually capable of
understanding the will of the Lord, we are also individually responsible for
understanding the will of the Lord.  I do
the best work I know how preaching, and we have a number of dedicated
Bible-class teachers here.  However, it
is not their job or even my job to understand the Bible for you.  Any Christian who relies on others for his
understanding will never understand.

In our obedience to this commandment—and make no mistake; it
is a commandment—there is no substitute for personal study of the word.  Like the Bereans, we must examine the
Scriptures constantly to see whether these things are so.  If we want to find, we must seek.

Second, we must **SEEK
PEACE.** Consider Peter’s aside in his
sermon in Cornelius’ house in Acts 10:34-37. 
In addition to all of its other attributes, the gospel is a gospel of
peace.  It tells us that despite our sin,
we can be reconciled to God through Jesus. 
Once we have been cleansed by the blood of His Son, God doesn’t require
perfection from us, but He does require faithfulness.  Every one of us, then, is responsible for
abiding by the terms of our covenant with God.

Lots and lots of people in the wider religious world will
shout to the heavens how much they looove God. 
Do they really?  Do we,
really?  At a fundamental level, we don’t
show our love for God by talking, any more than we show our love for our
spouses by talking.  We show it by
listening.  We show it by, for instance,
reading through the Old-Testament prophets, not because there are a whole bunch
of spiritual how-to’s there, but because God shows us so much of Himself
there.  We show our love by our hunger
for teaching about Jesus, because the life of Christ is a revelation of God in
human form.  We show our love by taking
every opportunity we can to study His word with others.  This is how we get to know Him and learn to
live at peace with Him.

Finally, we listen to God by being **SLOW TO SPEAK.** Here, I think
Job’s reply to God in Job 40:1-5 is instructive.  After a whole book of flowery, poetic
speeches, when he is in the presence of God, Job realizes that he shouldn’t
have said anything at all.

How much different would the religious landscape around us
be if so-called believers were quick to listen to God and slow to speak for themselves?  How much different would our own lives
be?  I suspect that every one of us knows
what it is to argue with God, to justify our disobedience with all sorts of
excuses and special pleadings.  The
preacher starts in on evangelism, and we’ve thought up 10 reasons why we don’t
talk about Jesus with our friends before he’s even got out of the
introduction!

Brethren, like Job, we need to clap our metaphorical hands
over our mouths and listen to what the Lord has to say.  If some preacher, me or anybody else, says to
do something and doesn’t back it up with Scripture, feel free to ignore
him.  However, if the book, chapter, and
verse is there, and it is with evangelism, we have to quit with the excusing
and start with the doing.  Wouldn’t we
feel like a bunch of idiots if we went to church all our lives and ended up in
hell because we dug in our heels on the Great Commission and wouldn’t
listen!  Our job is not to argue.  It’s to obey.
