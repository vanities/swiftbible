Not much to say here. 
I put the TRB through the usual gantlet: 
two weeks of being used as my daily reading Bible.  Well, two weeks minus the couple of days
during which it was in the van in the garage, and I was too lazy to get out of
my desk chair to get it.  I doubt that
changed my impressions much, though, and here they are:

**SIZE.** This is another place where the TRB’s
just-a-Bible-ness shines through.  It’s
both small and light enough for me to one-hand.  It’s not a featherweight like my reader of
choice, the Zondervan Thinline NASB, but it’s not far off either.  I notice its size a little bit now that I’m
hefting it as I write, but I didn’t notice it when I was using it to read.  Not much to complain about here.



**LAYOUT.** The designers of the TRB made a number of
choices that impact the way I read it, both for good and ill.  The most interesting one of them to me,
though, is the choice I didn’t notice. 
As I mentioned during my review of the TRB as a preaching Bible, it is a
paragraphed Bible with inline instead of superscript verse numbers.  Paragraphing is generally good in a reader,
but the inline numbers look like they would be a disaster.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEisud1LXX0m6IqJljj7BcMVAwAum_neQJ4CEypdCfkCvV8jrL8-JwM5UjuwmgIq0mfJxD24MQZrP7nLXwJJAEkKGel3-hEE3ckqWR9QcbWQBQn0poNWagDzGe2ptYDJ8moBZvqHxZzG1g/s1600/R2.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEisud1LXX0m6IqJljj7BcMVAwAum_neQJ4CEypdCfkCvV8jrL8-JwM5UjuwmgIq0mfJxD24MQZrP7nLXwJJAEkKGel3-hEE3ckqWR9QcbWQBQn0poNWagDzGe2ptYDJ8moBZvqHxZzG1g/s1600/R2.jpg)

In practice, however, they prove to be otherwise.  Not once in two weeks did I notice them or
find myself bothered by them.  Frankly,
this bewilders me.  How can I possibly
not notice eight-point numbers, sprinkled through the text?  I can only assume that I’m so used to
ignoring superscript numbers that inline numbers aren’t much harder to
ignore.   The inline numbers, then, make
the TRB better for preaching, and no worse for reading.  It’s an old-fashioned design choice, but at
least for me, it works.

However, there are aspects of the TRB’s layout that I didn’t
love as well for reading.  Like nearly
every other center-column-reference Bible in existence, the TRB is betrayed by
those references.  They take away
valuable line length that the two text columns desperately need.  Now, the line length of the TRB isn’t terrible
even with the references.  I would
estimate that it probably sports about 37 characters per line.  However, most double-column text Bibles have
about 42 cpl, and at times, that makes a huge difference.

As always, the problems don’t arise in prose.  I had no trouble with my readings from
Acts.  However, at the moment, I find
myself in the middle of reading Psalms too, and that is a problem.  It particularly struck me this morning as I
was reading Psalm 49.  For whatever
reason, it seems that different psalms have different average line lengths, and
the line length of Psalm 49 is just about exactly wrong for the TRB.  In 20 verses, I count 15 one-word (or
fractional-word) widows.  Additionally,
there are eight two-word lines.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhaYxfkhCf1gbX9Set5H02zO90D8o7I3o1OHDVdFpsLJnKNz_CERnux__-p5ENXpsNHEACbw-id8xD5-5hODbEUKAZ_Zu9WpvmEeG2x52q0up-WrC-81UD-kHeclL6dLYAGM-T8JLCHaA/s1600/R4.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhaYxfkhCf1gbX9Set5H02zO90D8o7I3o1OHDVdFpsLJnKNz_CERnux__-p5ENXpsNHEACbw-id8xD5-5hODbEUKAZ_Zu9WpvmEeG2x52q0up-WrC-81UD-kHeclL6dLYAGM-T8JLCHaA/s1600/R4.jpg)

By contrast, my text-only Thinline NASB has 12 one-word (and
no fractional-word) lines, and only four two-word lines.  I don’t have a problem reading poetry in the
Thinline, but the greater frequency of widows and near-widows in the TRB is
enough to take me out of the text.  What
a difference five characters per line makes!

**READABILITY.** The print in the TRB is eight-point Times
New Roman.  It doesn’t give me any
trouble reading it, though I did get up to turn the light on instead of
squinting at it by the light of my computer screen.  However, if your eyes aren’t good for close
work, this probably isn’t the Bible for you.

**CONCLUSION.** As readers go, the TRB isn’t an excellent
reader, but it isn’t a terrible one either. 
Its main flaw is characters per line in the poetry of the Old
Testament.  I think it deserves about a
3.5/5.
