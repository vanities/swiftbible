*For what does the
Scripture say? "Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for
righteousness."*

--Romans 4:3

There are few passages in the Old Testament that are as
important to us today as this one.  It
originally appears in Genesis 15:6, but it is quoted in three different New
Testament contexts, twice by Paul, once by James.  The passage is so critically important
because it tells us how we as imperfect people can approach a perfect God.  It is not our genetic lineage, nor our
rituals and ceremonies, nor our meritorious lives, that endear us to the
Creator.  It is our faith.



Men have fought wars over what this verse means and how
important it is, but we need to be concerned not with the theological and
historical, but with the personal and practical.  Abraham was not a perfect man.  Among other things, we know that he lied on
two separate occasions to protect himself from imagined harm.  However, despite these and whatever other
failings Genesis does not record, God considered Abraham a righteous man, fully
justified in His sight.  Why?  Because even though Abraham was an old man
and his wife was past childbearing age, when God told him that he would have
descendants as numerous as the stars, Abraham believed him.  His faith and his confidence in the power of
God were such that when God told him that He was going to do the impossible, he
accepted it without blinking.

In the final analysis, this is the only way that we can be
righteous too.  We are no more perfect
people than Abraham was.  Nonetheless, we
can be perfected through our faith in the promises of God.  If these promises were made by anybody else
but God, we would laugh at them.  Our
sins paid for by the blood of a Jew who died 2000 years ago?  Ridiculous! 
Resurrection from the dead, when we are nothing more than dust and
worms?  Absurd!  Nonetheless, those things are what God has
promised us, and what He has promised, He is sure to perform if we believe in
Him.

However, the faith of Abraham, and indeed the faith of the
Bible, is more than mere mental consent. 
Faith leads to action.  If I truly
believe that terrorists are going to nuke Joliet tomorrow, at the very least,
I’m going to take my wife to visit my parents for a few days.  Likewise, if we truly believe God, we are
going to act in accordance with that belief. 
We are going to do the things that the word tells us are necessary for
our salvation, up to and including baptism for the forgiveness of our
sins.  We are not saved by getting dipped
in magic water; instead, God sees our obedient faith and reckons it to us as
righteousness.  He does the same when He
sees our feeble efforts to obey His word in our lives.  Those things have no merit on their own, but
they are expressions of our faith, the faith of Abraham.
