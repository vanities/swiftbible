Religion has been in the news a great deal of late.  A few weeks ago, we had the Supreme Court
issue its ruling in the *Obergefell* case,
which legalized homosexual marriage in all 50 states.  However, I want to cast my gaze back in time
for several more weeks.

At that time, the Pew Research Center came out with another
one of its massive studies on religion in the United States.  Their headline finding was that both the
number and representation of self-described Christians in the United States had
declined markedly since 2007.  By
contrast, the number of Americans without a religious affiliation had increased
even more markedly.

Given these data, a number of commentators were quick to
jump to conclusions.  Christianity is on
its way out, they proclaimed.  Our
country will soon become as secular as the nations of Western Europe.  They were equally quick to argue that the
reason for Christianity’s decline is its judgmentalism and lack of
tolerance.  If churches want to be
relevant to Americans today, they’d better give up on their opposition to gay
marriage, sexual immorality, and so on.

Is this true?  Are
these the choices that are before us?  Must
we choose between abandoning the Bible on the one hand and empty pews and dying
churches on the other?  Those are
critically important questions, and I think the Pew report will help us get a
handle on them.  Let’s consider, then,
both research and the Bible as we ask, “Is Christianity doomed?”

**The Pew Center’s
Report**

This morning, the most reasonable place for us to begin is
by looking at the Pew Center’s report itself. 
It is entitled, “America’s Changing Religious Landscape”, and you can read
it for yourself [here](http://www.pewforum.org/2015/05/12/americas-changing-religious-landscape).  Whenever we see a think-tank come out with a
study that appears critical of Christianity, there’s a temptation to dismiss
the think-tank out of hand, but I don’t think that’s fair in the case of the
Pew Center.  They’re non-partisan and
generally well respected.

In any case, I went to the Pew site and read through the
whole report myself.  I suspect some of
the journalists who wrote those articles about it didn’t get through the front
page, and certainly none of them were looking at things from my perspective,
but the fact remains that I got something very different out of it than all of
them did.

The first thing to bear in mind is that when any research institute
says “Christian”, they do not mean the same thing that we do.  They are not talking about New Testament
Christianity.  They’re talking about
anyone who identifies himself or herself as a Christian.

According to their definition, then, “Christian” is composed
of three main flavors:  Catholic,
mainline Protestant, like most Methodists, Lutherans, Episcopalians, and so
forth, and evangelical Protestant.  This
latter group, according to them, includes folks like the Southern Baptists,
most Pentecostals, and us.  Outside of
those, there are some less significant Christian groups that I didn’t think
were worth putting on the slide, which is why the total number of Christians is
going to be greater than the sum of Catholics, mainline Protestants, and evangelical
Protestants.

The Pew research reveals that the number of Christians,
according to their definition, declined from around 178.1 million in 2007 to
172.8 million in 2014.  Of the three main
subgroups we mentioned, the number of Catholics dropped from about 54.3 million
to 50.9 million, despite the large number of Catholic immigrants from Latin
America.  Likewise, membership in the
mainline Protestant denominations declined from around 41.1 million in 2007 to
36.0 million in 2014.  However, the
number of evangelical Protestants increased from about 59.8 million in 2007 to
62.2 million in 2014.  In other words,
the so-called decline of Christianity is the result of the decline of
Catholicism and mainline Protestantism, not of the decline of evangelical Protestantism.

It’s possible, though, to delve even deeper into the Pew
Center’s data than that.  In the
appendices, they give more specific data for what they describe as Protestant
denominations, although these data are always expressed in percentage terms
rather than raw numbers.  To be honest,
I’m not quite sure where they put us. 
There are two categories where we might have ended up:  the “Non-Denominational” category, and the
“Church of Christ” category.  We deny
that we belong to any “Church of Christ” denomination, but they might have put
us there anyway.

In any event, Pew gives us data for both groups.  According to their research, the percentage
of Americans who identify as non-denominational evangelicals actually went up
over the survey period, from 1.2 to 2.0 percent.  The percentage of Americans who say that they
belong to the Church of Christ held steady at 1.5 percent, which represents a
substantial increase in raw numbers. 
Rather than being doomed, we and the churches most like us are actually
doing quite well!

The message here is pretty clear, and it is that all of
those so-smart secular-liberal Internet commentators are exactly wrong.  The churches that are shedding members like
dandruff aren’t the hardline, conservative churches.  People aren’t turned off by the high
expectations and restrictive morality. 
Instead, the churches that Americans are leaving are the ones that
compromise the most with secular culture and demand the least from their
members.

The poster children here are the mainline Protestant
churches.  They’re doing everything that
the secular commentators say they ought to be doing.  They’re allowing practicing homosexuals to
become members and even to become clergy. 
They’re downplaying all the icky sin and repentance and judgment stuff
and focusing on niceness and soup kitchens. 
The result, however, is not prosperity but disaster.  Those denominations are dying precisely
because they’ve become so much like the world that their members see no reason
not to leave them for the world.

That is the kind of Christianity that is doomed—the kind
that we wouldn’t even identify as true Christianity.  By contrast, the more a church relies on the
Bible, the healthier it seems to be.

**Our Applications**

Now that we’ve seen what the modern religious landscape
actually looks like, there are several applications that we ought to draw.  The first is that we ought to **IGNORE WORLDLY WISDOM.** We see Paul point out the problems with
this kind of wisdom in 1 Corinthians 1:20-21. 
The wisdom of the world doesn’t lead people to God; it leads them away
from Him, just as it always has.  If we
follow it, it will have the same effect on us, and God will expose us as fools
too.

This is obvious in the abstract, but it gets a lot less
obvious once we get down to cases.  The
wisdom of the world is particularly compelling when it comes to our desire to
make disciples.  The wisdom of the world
says that individual Christians and churches aren’t doing enough to spread the
gospel on their own, so we need to cooperate with other churches in reaching
the lost, even though no churches in the first century did such a thing.  The wisdom of the world says that outsiders
who come to our assembly will be bored by our singing, so we need to bring in
musical instruments to spice our worship up a little bit.  The wisdom of the world says that young
people are turned off by the Bible’s restrictive stance on sexual immorality,
so we need to soft-pedal the judgmental stuff and talk in vague, glowing terms
about kindness and love.  They’ll like
that!

The problem is that once we abandon the Bible for the wisdom
of the world, instead of converting the world, we have allowed them to convert
us.  Sure, those strategies may be
successful for a time.  As the Lord
Himself said, the world will love its own. 
However, it doesn’t matter how many people pack into a church building
on Sunday morning if Jesus isn’t in their midst.

Also, as the Pew Center report reveals, in the long run,
these strategies aren’t successful.  The
tastes of the world change, and even if we succeed in becoming the big thing
right now, in 20 years the world will abandon us for the next big thing.  Remember Robert Schuller and the Hour of
Power broadcasts from the Crystal Cathedral in Orange County, California?  That was big stuff for decades, but eventually
the magic ran out, they went bankrupt, and had to sell the Crystal Cathedral
itself.  Exactly the same thing will
happen to the megachurches and rock-star preachers of today.  If we imitate them, we may not share their
success, but we will share their fate.

Instead, we must **FOLLOW
JESUS**, even at the cost of offending and alienating the world.  Here, consider what the Hebrews writer has to
say in Hebrews 13:12-14.  This reminds
us, I think, of what actually lies behind compromise with the world.  We compromise because we don’t want to be
seen as different.  We compromise because
we don’t want to make worldly people uncomfortable.  We compromise because the straight truth
might mean alienating them.

The problem is, though, that following Jesus means being
different.  It means making worldly
people uncomfortable.  It means
alienating the wicked.  Jesus wasn’t like
them, and we can’t be like them either. 
Instead, just as He was led out of the city gate to be crucified, we
have to leave the world behind us, bearing its scorn and contempt like Jesus
bore the cross.  We have to declare that
our citizenship is not here.  Our
citizenship is in heaven.

Here too, worldly wisdom pipes up and says, “If you take such
a stark, uncompromising position, nobody will want to be like that!”  Of course, the world has never understood why
anybody would want to be like Jesus.  And
yet, people do!  For 2000 years, men and
women have quietly taken up their crosses and followed Him.  That is where our appeal must lie:  not in praise bands, not in a soft-soap
Christianity that has been stripped of all that might offend.  Our appeal is Christ, and Him crucified.  There is no point in trying to make a
Christian out of somebody who doesn’t want to be like Jesus.

As we seek to make disciples, though, this must begin with **MEN STEPPING UP** as the spiritual
leaders of their families.  As much as
the Pew Center report gave me reason for hope, there is one statistic in it
that I didn’t like.  It has to do with
our success in keeping our children. 
According to the Pew Center, only 47 percent of those raised in a
non-denominational Protestant church remained with that church in
adulthood.  Of those raised in a
Restorationist church, such as the Church of Christ—whatever that is—only 45 percent
continued with that church as adults.

Those are ugly numbers, and the key to solving the problem
is our fathers.  Consider Ephesians
6:4.  Much of the time, we read this as
referring to parents generally, but I think Paul specifically meant
fathers.  Some of you may know Mark
Broyles; he’s a preacher in the Kansas City area who has a particular interest
in marriage and family issues.  He
reports that of children who are raised in the church, but only have a mother
who is a faithful Christian, 15 percent of them will be faithful themselves in
adulthood.  If only the father is a
faithful Christian, children will stay faithful 50 percent of the time.  If both parents are faithful, 80 percent of
their children will be obedient.

Now, these are statistics, not prophecies.  Obviously, families can buck the trend in
either direction.  However, they do
indicate a general truth.  The parent
with the greatest spiritual influence on children isn’t the mother.  It’s the father.  If we want faithful kids, we must have
faithful dads.

This means that for you boys and men who aren’t married yet,
now is the time to resolve that you are going to be a spiritual leader for your
future family.  For girls and young
women, if you marry at all, you need to find a man who will be a leader for
your family.  For those of us who are
married and raising children, bringing those children up in the discipline and
instruction of the Lord is one of the most important things we will ever do.  Let’s make sure we get it right.

**Unchangeable Things**

In stark contrast to what the world says about
Christianity’s need to change with the times, what should really matter to us
is staying true to the unchangeable things of the spiritual realm.  The first of these is **THE WORD OF GOD** itself. 
Here, consider the testimony of Peter in 1 Peter 1:24-25.  There are churches out there that rely on
what’s called continuing revelation. 
They believe that they still have apostles or prophets who sometimes
receive a new and different word from God. 
Not so for us.  We believe what
the Bible says about itself, that the true faith has once for all been
delivered to the saints.  If it’s not in
the Bible, it is not from God, and the Bible isn’t collecting any appendices.  As a result, the Bible can be our moral
polestar.  It does not and will not
change.

In turn, this means that God’s will for His people does not
change.  We can be sure that what pleased
Him 2000 years ago will still please Him today, and that what displeased Him
then will displease Him now.  He has told
us what He wants us to do; it’s up to us to do it.

God’s word does not change, but our understanding of it
may.  Of course, this is not an excuse
for rewriting the Bible to suit the values of our wicked society, as so many
around us do.  To be honest, I don’t know
why they even bother, except that their “new understanding” may salve a guilty
conscience.  Instead, our goal must not
be to impose our meaning on the text, but to discover what meaning God wants to
impose on us.  Among the Lord’s people,
the only desirable change is backward: 
back to the Bible, to the first-century church, and to the will of
Christ.

Indeed, the second of these unchangeable things is **JESUS CHRIST** Himself.  Here, consider the witness of the Hebrews
writer in Hebrews 13:8.  When it comes to
Jesus, nothing changes, not His character, not His authority, and not His will
for us.

This has any number of significant consequences.  If we want to know what Jesus would say to us
and do for us, we need only consider His words and actions during His ministry
on earth.  When we see Him have
compassion on the blind and the sick and the lame, and show mercy to them by
healing them, we may be certain that He regards us with every bit as much
compassion, and that if we come to Him in faith, He will heal all the
sicknesses of our spirits.

When we see Him call sinners to repentance throughout all
four gospels, it leaves us with no doubt what His message for the world today
would be.  Just as He spoke with
authority then, He speaks with authority today, and that authority gives Him
the right to dictate how we should worship and how we should live.  Two millennia ago, He was the only Way to the
Father, and He is still the only Way today. 
Either we come to the Father through Him or not at all.

This leads us logically to a third unchangeable thing:  **OUR
SALVATION.** The Hebrews writer
reaffirms its eternal nature in Hebrews 10:11-14.  Thousands of years of Levitical priests
offering sacrifices daily could not remove the guilt of even a single sin.  However, when Jesus offered Himself on the
cross, the power of His sacrifice was such that through it, the sins of
everyone for all time could be forgiven. 
Now, everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved, and
they will be saved to the uttermost.  Because
Jesus is sure, His salvation is sure.

This means, friends, that we can contemplate the
difficulties of this life with detachment. 
We don’t know how things are going to go for us on earth, though it is
nearly certain that some hard times are going to be involved.  When our time here is over, though, if we
have been faithful to the Lord, we do know what He will award to us.  Through Him, we can have eternal life.

The greatness of our salvation brings us to the fourth of
our unchangeable things:  **OUR NEED TO HONOR HIM.** Here, let’s consider the words of Paul in
1 Corinthians 11:23-26.  This passage
describes a rite that is very familiar to most of us:  our sharing in the Lord’s Supper.  This is something that we do on every first
day of the week, because that’s when we see Christians in the first century
doing it.  This text tells us how long
we’re supposed to go on doing it, and that is until the Lord comes again.  So long as there are still Christians on it,
God expects this.

In all honesty, this is the least we could do.  The gospels clearly reveal Jesus as a man,
but also as more than a man, one who was truly the Son of God Himself.  When He died on the cross, that wasn’t a mere
man dying.  That was God dying, which had
never happened before and will never happen again.  That was God dying for us.  When Jesus asks us to remember Him, we really
have no alternative but to obey.  Let’s
remember the inexhaustible power of His sacrifice now.
