*“Because lawlessness
is increased, most people's love will grow cold. But the one who endures to the
end, he will be saved.”*

- Matthew 24:12-13

When Jesus said this, He was talking about the chaotic
lead-up to the destruction of Jerusalem by the Romans in 70 A.D.  However, He might as well have been talking
about current events in the United States. 
It’s been a rough couple of days in the news.



First of all, we have the release of the FBI report on
Hillary Clinton’s private e-mail server. 
I have no political affiliation, but I believe that everyone should be
accountable under the law, and I am disturbed by the report’s conclusions.  It seems pretty clear to me that she set up
the server because she didn’t want anybody else reading her mail (despite the
open-records laws designed to make government officials accountable) and didn’t
much care whether, in violation of federal law, she exposed classified
information to foreign scrutiny or not. 
She considered herself above the law, and in the end, she turned out to
be right.  The weak and friendless get
clobbered; the powerful skate.

Speaking of the weak and friendless, I am also greatly
dismayed by the recent epidemic of shootings of black men by police.  The past few days have added fatalities in
Minnesota and Louisiana to the grim toll. 
I believe that the protections as well as the standards of the law
should apply to everyone, but they seem not to. 
I am horrified first of all that part of the definition of white privilege
these days seems to be that I don’t have to worry about being shot dead at a
traffic stop.  Why, in our country,
should anyone have to fear that?

Second, I am worried by the ominous increase in police power
and the apparent willingness of police officers to use deadly force.  I understand that this is probably a
fear-based reaction to the Orlando shootings, but fear is a terrible reason to
cede more power to the state and its servants. 
Many Christians are enthusiastic proponents of law and order, and I
understand that to a point, but we must also remember that we live in a society
that is growing increasingly hostile to conservative Christians too.  The erosion of civil liberties may be much
closer to affecting us than we would like to think.

The times, they are a-changing, and not for the better.  However, we must be wary lest the increase in
lawlessness cause our love to grow cold. 
It would be easy for Christians to respond to these things by retreating
into their bunkers (metaphorical and even literal) and regarding the outside
world with fear, suspicion, and contempt.

However, hiding from an uncertain future is not what we are
called to do.  Jesus began His ministry
in an uncertain age too.  Forty years
later, the entire world He knew would come crashing down in fire and
blood.  However, His gospel was a gospel
not of fear, but of love.  When He says
that those who endure to the end will be saved, He doesn’t mean that we are to
endure to the end in grim affirmation of His deity.  He means that we are to endure to the end in
being loving.

We’re still supposed to be salt.  We’re still supposed to be light.  We’re still supposed to reach out to the
needy and friendless and suffering around us, both inside and outside of the
church, and enfold them in the compassion of Christ.  That may not leave us feeling safe and
secure, but danger has always been part of discipleship.  Ask any Christian mentioned in the New Testament!

We may feel powerless to combat the social ills bedeviling
our country, but the course of nations is above our pay grade anyway.  That’s God’s business, not ours.  The work that He has given us is the work of changing
hearts and lives, one soul at a time. 
The impending doom of Jewish society didn’t make the gospel any less meaningful
in the first century.  It made it more
meaningful, and the same will be true for us. 
In such a turbulent time, the world’s need for the Christ who is the
same yesterday, today, and forever can only be heightened.  Let’s be sure that by our words and by our
actions, we reveal Him to those who need Him.
