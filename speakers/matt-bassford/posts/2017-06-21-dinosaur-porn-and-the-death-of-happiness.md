Every once in a while, I will have one of those
down-the-rabbit-hole moments that reminds me that our society is growing
increasingly bizarre.  The most
recent came as my wife was filling out our children’s entries for the local
library’s summer-reading program.  She
didn’t have the books on hand, so she was asking them what they had read and
typing the book title into Amazon to get a page-number count.  One of the books my son had read was entitled
*Dinosaur Boy,* but when she began to enter that into Amazon’s search bar, the first result that autocomplete
suggested was “dinosaur erotica”.

Appalled and astounded, she hit Enter and was presented with
560 results.  There is an entire genre of
books out there about unnatural encounters with extinct reptiles.

At this, most people in our society would probably chuckle
and shake their heads.  Sure, that’s
pretty weird, but at least the purchasers of such things aren’t out there
molesting children or something.  A
victimless crime shouldn’t be a crime at all, right?

I think, though, that it’s pretty obvious who the victims
are.  Typically, people don’t start out
with “ceratopsid” as a sexual orientation. 
Dinosaur porn isn’t where you begin. 
It’s where you end up after more mainstream pleasures have lost their
allure.  The same is doubtless true of
innumerable other sad, squalid corners of Amazon and the Internet generally.  Their inhabitants have become desensitized to
more normal forms of pornography, so they have come to the depraved in the hope
of feeling *something*.

C.S. Lewis captures this problem exactly in *The Screwtape Letters*.  At one point in the book, the wily old devil
Wormwood advises his nephew, Screwtape, “All we can do is to encourage the
humans to take the pleasures which our Enemy has produced, at times, or in
ways, or in degrees, which He has forbidden. Hence we always try to work away
from the natural condition of any pleasure to that in which it is least
natural, least redolent of its Maker, and least pleasurable. An ever increasing
craving for an ever diminishing pleasure is the formula. It is more certain;
and it's better style. To get the man's soul and give him nothing in
return—that is what really gladdens our Father's heart.” (of course, throughout *The Screwtape Letters*, "our Enemy" is God and "our Father" is the devil)

I’m quite sure that even though C.S. Lewis would have been
saddened by the modern porn epidemic, he would not have been at all
surprised.  With porn, the devil has
started with one of life’s great joys, the intimacy between husband and
wife.  However, as with God’s other
blessings, marital intimacy is both fulfilling and demanding.  You have to get married in the first place,
and then you have to spend a lifetime working on deepening and enriching that
marriage.

Porn, though, is a shortcut. 
Porn is a cheat.  It promises the
fulfillment without the work, and at first, it seems to be everything that it
has promised.  However, that initial
experience is no more representative of the reality of porn use than the first
free hit from the crack dealer is of the reality of drug addiction.  As time goes on and the sinner is drawn
deeper into the devil’s web, the devil demands more and more in exchange for
less and less.

Finally, the unhappy porn addict reaches the point where
there is literally nothing, no matter how explicit and shocking, that can still
bring him joy.  They greet even the
dinosaurs with a shrug.  From there, they
may branch out into sexual adventures in real life (and eventually reach the
same point with those, except with additional miseries), or simply remain where
they are, enslaved to joyless sin.  All
sins, not merely porn use, eventually lead to this point.  There is no fulfillment in evil.

In fact, the presence or absence of fulfillment can be a
pretty good guide for distinguishing between good and evil.  I don’t get tired of singing praises to God
because I’ve done it all my life.  I
don’t get tired of snuggling my children because it’s something I do every
day.  Instead, the more I do those
things, or express love in any other way, the more it satisfies me and the more
I enjoy it.  These are the good gifts
that our Father gives us.

Sin, by contrast, can never satisfy.  It can only blight.  Covetousness, lust, and bitterness do not reward
us for savoring them and expressing them. 
Instead, the punishment is contained in the crime.  Solomon got one of everything for himself
(with respect to covetousness and lust both) yet found that he ended up with
nothing.  The more of a voice we give to
our anger, the more it will consume us.  Even
if we pursue our anger to the uttermost ruin of the one who has offended us,
the result will fill us not with joy, but with emptiness.

This is the endgame, always. 
This is where the devil wants to take us.  No matter what it is, anything we put before
God will always fail us and leave us with nothing.  When we anticipate the end from the
beginning, the allurements of sin become a whole lot less alluring.
