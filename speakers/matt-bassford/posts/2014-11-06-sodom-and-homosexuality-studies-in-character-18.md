*Now before they lay
down, the men of the city, the men of Sodom, both old and young, all the people
from every quarter, surrounded the house. And they called to Lot and said to
him, "Where are the men who came to you tonight? Bring them out to us that
we may know them carnally."*

--Genesis 19:4-5

There are few Biblical issues that have the same capacity
for creating debate and hostility as the issue of homosexuality.  On the one hand, we are currently nearing the
end of a multi-decade effort by several powerful elements of American society
to see homosexuality normalized and accepted. 
On the other hand, we see elements of the so-called “religious right”
who have reacted to this environment of tolerance for homosexuality by
depicting it as even worse than other sins and indeed making it a focus of
their preaching and their existence generally.



When we are dealing with a subject that is so emotionally
charged, it is all the more important for us to return to the word of God, see
what it has to say about homosexuality, and refine our attitudes
accordingly.  First, we must acknowledge
that God does not put homosexuality on a different level than He puts murder,
adultery, or, for that matter, lying. 
All sin brings forth death, and no sinner is worse off than any
other.  Our adherence to Scripture does
not allow us to condone homosexuality, but neither does it give us license to
speak or behave toward homosexuals in a hateful or contemptuous way.   As disciples of Jesus, it is always our
responsibility to treat every sinner we encounter with kindness, respect, and
love.

However, our love for the sinner should not blind us to the
evil of his sin.  From the time of Sodom
forward, the Bible never speaks well of homosexual practices.  In recent years, all the Biblical passages
dealing with homosexuality have faced a great deal of “reinterpreteation” that
has much more to do with the reinterpreters’ endorsement of it than what the
passage actually says.  For instance, the
modern line about the men of Sodom is that their sin was not homosexuality, but
rather their failure to extend hospitality to the angels as they should.

Of course, that’s not the Bible’s line.  Jude 1:7 tells us that the men of Sodom were
wrong in “having given themselves over to sexual immorality and gone after
strange flesh”.  They were certainly
inhospitable, but that wasn’t their only or even their biggest problem.  The truth is plain:  God expects man to avoid unnatural sexual
practices, and those who disobey will be punished.  It’s not our responsibility to carry out this
punishment.  That’s God’s role.  However, we are responsible for telling the
truth about God’s will, and the truth is that the unrepentant homosexual will
never be pleasing in His sight.
