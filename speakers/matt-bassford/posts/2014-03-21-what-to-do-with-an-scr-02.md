After last week’s post about my ideal preaching Bible, I got
a question on the Facebook page for Bible Design Blog, where I had linked to
it.  The questioner said he had just
received a 2002 Foundation Side-Column Reference in pristine condition and
solicited input on what he should do with it: 
keep it pristine or wear it out.

For the non-Bible dorks among you, the ’02 SCR is considered
something of a prize among Bible aficionados. 
It is a wide-margin version of Foundation’s classic SCR setting, which
somehow manages to combine a single-column layout, verse-by-verse format, large
type, and robust cross-references in a non-huge Bible.  I consider it the best preaching Bible ever
made.  What sets the ’02 edition of this
Bible apart is the paper.  It is
unusually opaque for a mass-market Bible, which makes the ’02 SCR excellent for
note-taking or even reading (for people who struggle with ghosting in Bibles).



Sadly, they quite literally don’t make ‘em like they used
to.  After about a ten-year hiatus,
Foundation began printing the SCR in wide-margin format again in late 2013, but
the text block is quite different.  The
paper is much thinner and less opaque than in the old SCR’s.  Internet rumor has it that the old SCR paper
is no longer commercially available, perhaps because you’d have to violate a
dozen EPA regulations to mill the stuff. 
On the one hand, this means that the new wide-margin SCR’s are lighter
and handier than the old ones.  On the
other, the notes you write are much more likely to bleed through the paper.

So then, some fortunate soul on Bible Design Blog has found
himself in possession of one of the most wonderful note-taking Bibles in
existence.  What should he do with it?

Let me preface my answer by saying that I am a Bible user,
not a Bible collector.  I don’t have a
problem with people who ARE Bible collectors, mind.  In a day in which millions of people
accumulate jaw-dropping collections of pornography, I’m not about to waste my
indignation on a man who accumulates Bibles!

However, though I respect collectors, I have no desire to
join their ranks.  I don’t collect Bibles
any more than I collect saws or shovels, and for the same reason.  To me, all of those things are tools.  I try to take care of my tools, but I buy
them to use them, not to set them on a shelf and admire them periodically.  Over time, any tool that is used is going to
wear out, and that’s OK with me.

I would say, then, that I think the questioner should use
the SCR if it suits his needs.  I have an
’02 SCR that I don’t use.  It belonged to
my mother and passed to me after her death in 2011.  It is filled with her notes, and I treasure
it for that reason, but it’s not the right Bible for me.  I’m not a Bible-writer, and the wide-margin
SCR’s are too large for me to be comfortable with them in the pulpit.  I wouldn’t use an ’02 SCR because everybody
else said it was wonderful.  I would use
it because it worked for me as a Bible.

If it didn’t work, though, I would pass it on to somebody
for whom it was better suited.  Even if I
don’t benefit from wide margins and super-opaque paper, there are plenty of
Christians with active pens and failing eyesight who would.

In my case, my plan for my mother’s ’02 SCR is
different.  I have a three-year-old
daughter who is very like my mother in temperament.  It is my hope and my prayer for her that she
will also be like my mother in devotion to the Lord.  If that proves to be the case, when she (Lord
willing) graduates high school, I intend to give my mother’s Bible to her, not
to keep on a shelf, but to use, to add to all those notes and underlinings with
notes and underlinings of her own.  It
wouldn’t necessarily be a Bible that she would need physically, but I believe
that her grandmother’s Bible would be to her a source of great spiritual help
and comfort.

The word of God is powerful all by itself, and a
well-constructed edition is like a finely balanced sword.  However, I think that a cherished Bible given
in love may be even more powerful. 
Perhaps the ultimate answer for the ’02 SCR is this:  Use it if it works for you, love it, but be
alert for an opportunity to give it to somebody who needs it more than you
do.  With such sacrifices God is well
pleased.
