My wife originally purchased this Bible in 2010 as a gift
for one of the young people in our congregation, who had recently graduated
high school.  Somehow, we never actually
managed to give her the gift, so it sat, still in its wrapping paper, on our
upstairs bookshelf for four years.  This
stasis was only recently interrupted by my two-year-old son, who tore the paper
off (for reasons known only to him) and revealed that I was now the proud owner
of a Crossway Premium Gift Bible, which I had been considering purchasing for
review anyway.  Perhaps I’ll give it to a
different high-school student at some point.

Before writing this review, I put the Thinline through the
usual tests.  I spent two weeks using it
as my preaching/study Bible, my reading Bible, and my travel Bible
respectively.  The linked posts represent
my impressions.

**VITAL STATISTICS**

Translation:  English
Standard Version (2007)

Cover:  Polyurethane

Binding:  Sewn

Trim Size:  5 ⅜” x 8⅜”

Text Block Width:  ¾”

Weight:  1 pound, 3
ounces

Column:  Double

Layout:  Paragraphed

Font Size:  8

Words of Christ: 
Black

Cross-References:  No



Ribbon Marker:  0

**SIZE.**  It’s funny what a difference an incremental
increase in size makes.  During my review
of the Zondervan Thinline NASB, I noted that it fit only uneasily in the glove
box of my family’s minivan when we went on vacation (fun fact:  the glove box of a Kia Rondo is smaller than
the glove box of a Toyota Corolla).  The Premium Gift Bible is only half an inch longer than the Zondervan Thinline, and
it’s maybe a quarter inch wider.

However, that’s apparently the point where size starts
mattering.  I didn’t have any trouble
putting it in the Corolla glove box, but the door on the Rondo glove box would
not shut.  I had to move a bunch of stuff
around and take out my daughter’s carsickness medicine entirely (poor thing
loses her last meal on any car trip of an hour or more) before I could get the
latch to catch.

That sounds like a small thing, but it makes a big
difference.  In order for a travel Bible
to be useful, it has to stow conveniently. 
If we’re on the road and meeting some friends for lunch after services,
I’m not going to want to spend a minute moving stuff in the glove compartment
around before we get going.  Instead, I’m
going to give up on putting my Bible someplace safe and put it someplace
convenient instead.  It’s going to go on
the dashboard (to melt) or on the floorboards (to get trampled and spilled
on).  At that point, why take a Bible
that I like on the road at all?

Of course, there are other ways in which the Premium Gift Bible was no less convenient than the Thinline NASB. 
It weighs about the same.  It
wasn’t any harder to carry to studies during the week.  However, the fact remains that every Bible is
the product of a series of trade-offs, and the balance struck has consequences
for the utility of the Bible.

It’s not like Crossway’s designers added half an inch of
meaningless white space at the bottom of the Bible.  Instead, they used the extra size to make the
font larger (8-point versus the Thinline NASB’s 7.8, which is such an odd
number that it makes me wonder whether Zondervan’s designers kicked the type
size down so that their Bible would be less than eight inches long).  The Crossway designers also added much more
generous leading than is present in the Zondervan product, which allowed them
to do useful things with their superscript verse numbers.

Those are both good things, but they did push the size of
the Premium Gift Bible (and indeed of all of Crossway’s thinlines) beyond the size
of a true grab-and-go Bible like a hip-pocket New Testament or a Pitt
Minion.  At that point, I start wondering
whether Crossway might as well put better paper in the Bible, give up on the
thinline label, and make it a just-a-Bible text Bible.

**BINDING.** The Premium Gift Bible has a polyurethane
cover, marketed, as all Crossway poly Bibles are, as TruTone.  Crossway calls this particular cover Chestnut
Filigree, which I suppose is as appropriate a name as any.  In color, it’s somewhere between brown and
dark red, and it hints at being a girl’s burgundy Bible.  I like the leatherlike texture of the cover,
but the pattern on it, which is reminiscent of a wrought-iron railing, is a
little too girly for me.  Then again, I
suspect I’m not its target audience.

One thing that I like about the cover is that it is all of a
piece, without any of the sewn seams that often seem like weak spots in poly
Bible covers.  As I flex the cover with
my fingertips, it feels like it would possess both flexibility and
durability.  After enough use, the cover
will hang as limply as any goatskin binding.

Also, as many Crossway poly covers do, this cover feels
thicker and sturdier than do the poly covers put out by other Bible
manufacturers.  As I’ve mentioned before,
I got five years of good service from a poly-bound Crossway Bible despite using
it nearly every day.  A Christian who
doesn’t use a Bible as heavily as a preacher does (i.e., someone who does something
else with his time besides study the Bible) could well get 10 or 15 years of
use out of a Bible like this.

The caveat, of course, is that if you leave this Bible on
the dashboard of your car on a hot July day, be prepared for spectacularly
horrible results.  If poly Bibles are
Superman, sunlight is kryptonite.

Inside, the endpapers
are about what I would expect from a Bible at this price point:  paper, and not particularly sturdy paper at
that.  After only a small amount of use,
it’s begun to crack where the cover has flexed. 
I don’t anticipate that it would fall apart or disintegrate or anything,
but after a few years, it would probably get to looking ratty.

**TEXT BLOCK.** As one might guess from its name, the Premium Gift Bible is one of the cheapest offerings in the Crossway lineup (gift Bibles are generally about the cheapesst Bible any publisher offers).  At the moment, it retails for $16.99 and is
available for considerably less online.

I’ve certainly paid that much for a Bible before and ended
up with a glue-bound stinker, so I assumed that the Premium Gift Bible also would
be a Bible meant to be seen but not actually read.  Not so! 
Wonder of wonders, it has a sewn binding.  Crossway markets it as such, and accurately
so.  Doubting Thomas that I am, I have
peeled back the headband and seen the signatures for myself, and I have pried
into the gutter and seen bindery thread for myself.

This impresses me. 
I’ve talked before about people who shop for Bibles solely on the basis
of translation and price.  Normally, they
too end up with glue-bound stinkers. 
However, someone who buys the cheapest non-compact in the Crossway
lineup is going to get a sewn Bible that will stand up to years of study.  I can’t imagine that Crossway makes a lot of
money selling that quality of Bible that cheaply, but it speaks highly of their
organization that they want people who don’t have a lot of money (or don’t want
to spend it) to have decent Bibles too. 
Good for them!  There are more
important things in life than profit margins.

The text block in the Premium Gift Bible is basically the same
as the block in the normal Crossway Thinline, which retails for $30.  The Premium Gift Bible forfeits some extras, like
a ribbon, a map section, and the words of Christ in red, but 1 Corinthians 14
is going to start on p. 960, just like it does in the normal Thinline.  The paper looks like it might not be as good
as in the normal Thinline, but that may merely be the result of the Bible
having sat on the shelf and yellowed for four years.

The Premium Gift Bible, then, leaves me in a strange place as a
reviewer.  It’s not a Bible I would buy
for myself (though part of me wants to use it for a rebind because it is 40
pages slimmer than the normal Thinline), but I am wholeheartedly glad that it
exists.  I find within myself an
unexpectedly large amount of emotional commitment to the proposition that every
Christian should have a well-made Bible. 
Even a little kid who is going to leave the thing on the church-house
steps half a dozen times deserves to have a Bible that won’t shed pages like
dandruff.

**CONCLUSION.** It’s hard to teach somebody to love a
piece of garbage.  With the Premium Gift Bible, Crossway has rescued untold thousands of parents from having to try.  This Bible, then, though it is the humblest
of the humble, has earned a 9/10 from me for its physical form.
