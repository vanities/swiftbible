[This slide show](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl) urges us to believe that it’s amazing, at least.  Of course, for any Christian, passages like 1
Corinthians 7:10-11 mean that divorce (except in the case of sexual immorality)
isn’t an option.  However, it’s not like
divorce is some wonderful thing that mean ol’ God is keeping us from.  Generally speaking, if we remain committed to
our marriages, we will benefit not only in the life to come, but also in this
life.  A look at the arguments in the
slide show will reveal why.



[**Your Kids Are Better Off**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=1)

There’s more spin than a clothes dryer here.  It certainly doesn't help that the author uses [this article](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/is-divorce-bad-for-children/) to support her contention.  Both authors want us to believe that the kids will be all right, but sometimes,
the truth sneaks out.  For instance, in
support of the contention that divorce *is
not harmful*, the author of the *Scientific
American* article offers this astonishing paragraph:

“For example, in a 2002 book, For Better or For Worse: Divorce Reconsidered,
Hetherington and her co-author, journalist John Kelly, describe a 25-year study
in which Hetherington followed children of divorce and children of parents who
stayed together. She found that 25 percent of the adults whose parents had
divorced experienced serious social, emotional or psychological troubles
compared with 10 percent of those whose parents remained together. These
findings suggest that only 15 percent of adult children of divorce experience
problems over and above those from stable families.”

Words fail me.  Well,
not quite, but close.  The fact that
children of divorcees are *two and a half
times* more likelyto experience
serious problems in adulthood does NOT prove that divorce has an insignificant
effect!  Indeed, it is an extremely
strong proof of the opposite!  The
science here lines up with my anecdotal experience.  Divorce is really hard on kids, both in the
short and the long term.

[**You're a Better Role Model**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=2)

Granted, no man should ever raise a hand to his wife.  However, there are plenty of other ways for
an abused woman to show her children that such behavior is unacceptable.  Spousal abuse is a sin everywhere, but in our
country, it’s also a crime.  I believe
that Christians have both the right and the obligation (under Romans 13) to
report crimes to the authorities so that they can take appropriate action.  That’s part of God’s plan for the protection
of the weak and helpless.

Note, by the way, that the old tag “Once an abuser, always
an abuser” is untrue.  As [this 2015 report from the state of Maine](http://courts.maine.gov/reports_pubs/reports/pdf/Domestic%20Violence%20Docket%20Process%20and%20Recidivism%20Report.pdf) reveals, only about 12 percent of males convicted of domestic violence are
re-arrested for domestic violence within the next two years, and various court
intervention programs reduce the recidivism rate still further.  It’s a false
dichotomy to say that an abused woman’s only two choices are “divorce” and “do
nothing”, and given the numerous negative consequences of divorce, she should
look for a way forward that will work better for everyone.

[**You're in Control**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=3)

Because really, life is about getting our way and having
things precisely as we want them.  That’s
exactly the kind of behavior that helps us to have rich, fulfilling
relationships and the happiness they bring!

Or, instead, perhaps we should try to develop ethically and
spiritually, so that we help and bless others instead of putting ourselves first in
everything.  Just a thought.

[**Divorce Can Pay Off Later**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=4)

Yes, it is true that divorced women will have more earnings
than women who remain married.  However,
that’s not because it’s financially a better decision to get divorced.  Instead, many married women choose not to
work (because they don’t have to), which leads to lower earnings, while
divorced women generally must work, which leads to higher earnings.

In this case, though, we must be careful not to confuse
higher earnings with a higher standard of living.  As [this article](http://today.uconn.edu/2011/09/divorce-is-costly-for-women/) points out, marriage is financially beneficial for women, and divorce is
financially disastrous.  As a rule, even
divorcees with careers never regain the financial stability they had while
married.  Sure, your income may rise
post-divorce, but your new expenses rise even more.

[**You Have More Time to Find Yourself**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=5)

The author notes that those weekends where your ex has the
kids are great for personal exploration. 
Is there some rule that I’m not aware of that says that married women
can never spend time by themselves or work on self-improvement?   That’s certainly not the way things work in
my marriage.  Sometimes, I take the kids
while Lauren goes out shopping by herself for a couple hours.  Sometimes, she spends several days away at a
preachers’ wives’ retreat or a hymn-recording session.  I spent years watching the kids while she
went to class to get a master’s degree.

I think things like that should happen in any marriage, and
to act like they can’t happen unless you aren’t married anymore is to
demonstrate a lack of comprehension about how marriage is supposed to work.

[**You'll Be Healthier**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=6)

Maybe.  Here too, the
author cites [a study](http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0022146514556893) that confirms my anecdotal impression: 
bad marriages often have significant health effects.  What’s absent from this study, though, is any
comparison between the health effects of being married and the health effects
of being divorced.  It simply doesn’t
prove the point the author wants to make.

[**You're More Realistic About Relationships**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=7)

In proof of this point, the author cites a British study
that shows that divorce rates are lower for second marriages than for first
marriages.  Similar data on U.S.
marriages are hard to find (that statistic about 60 percent of second marriages
ending in divorce is apparently made up), but [this article](http://www.wsj.com/articles/SB10001424053111904106704576580652976268350)from the *Wall Street Journal*says that so far as anyone can tell, divorce rates in the U.S. remain constant between first and second marriages.  I don’t see much of a learning curve here.

Even more fundamentally, though, I simply can’t see that
walking out on a marriage shows a realistic understanding of how relationships
work.  Instead, I think it shows the
opposite.  You’re committed to a
storybook vision of marriage, and when real life doesn’t follow the script,
you’re gone.  By contrast, I expected
marriage to be rewarding (which it is) and also to require effort (which it
has).  More modest expectations are more
likely to produce contentment.

[**You'll Hold Your Loved Ones a Little Tighter**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=8)

Except for the part where there is now an ex-spouse whom you
used to love whom you no longer hold, tightly or otherwise.  Divorce is about breaking up relationships,
not strengthening them, and the best way to show gratitude for your children is
by remaining married to their other parent.
