Will man rob God?  No,
but that doesn’t keep him from trying.

Take, for instance, the burgeoning heroin epidemic in
today’s America.  More and more, I’ve
been reading articles like [this one](http://www.msn.com/en-us/news/crime/crime-spike-in-st-louis-traced-to-cheap-heroin-and-mexican-cartels/ar-BBrgLTN) from all over the country.  Strange
though it might seem to nearly-fortysomethings like me, the hot new drug right
now is heroin, often appearing at unheard-of levels of purity.  I think it’s going to dominate the news for
the next decade the way that the crack epidemic owned the headlines in the
1980’s.

However, the users this time around are different.  This isn’t about the people whom white
Americans dismiss with a variety of ugly isms (racism, classism, etc.).  This isn’t about Them.  This is about Us.  This is about middle-class white suburbanites
who drive their late-model SUV’s to the hood to pick up the next hit of their
drug of choice.



**Inside the Epidemic**

I’ve got a good friend who sees a lot of the war on drugs
professionally, and he says that the heroin epidemic is spillover from another
middle-class epidemic that we don’t like to talk about either:  the epidemic of prescription-drug abuse.  According to him, here’s the life cycle of
the typical heroin addict:

Middle-Class White Dude has surgery, or has a back problem,
so his doctor prescribes painkillers. 
However, MCWD finds that he really likes the way that painkillers make
him feel.  He likes them so much, in
fact, that he doesn’t want to contemplate life without them.  He tells his doctor whatever he can think of
to keep the doc prescribing more happy pills. 
He may start seeing multiple doctors with the same manufactured
complaint to get more prescriptions. 
However, sooner or later, the doctor gets (or doctors get) wise to his
addiction and cut him off.

Generally, the next step for MCWD is the prescription-pill
black market (though health-care workers with access to narcotics will start
self-prescribing at this point). 
However, that’s *expensive*!  One pill will cost you four or five
bucks.  That’s not going to go very far
for somebody who has built up a tolerance for painkillers, and the more pills
you pop, the less likely you are to remain productively in the work force.  $5 per pill is long-term unsustainable.

Economics, then, drive MCWD to heroin.  He can get a hit of heroin for a fraction of
the price of a hit of oxycodone, and it’s just as good or maybe even
better.  At this point, his life becomes
about coming up with money for heroin, and he’ll do whatever he has to do to
get it.

Nor is there much of a light at the end of the tunnel.  As my friend says, “There are two kinds of
heroin addicts:  those who have
overdosed, and those who will.”  I’ve
known people who have won the battle with Demon Alcohol, but apparently, once
heroin gets its claws in you, it’s nearly impossible for you to defeat it.  This dark road is one that our friends,
neighbors, and even family members are walking in unprecedented numbers.  Even for those of us with college degrees and
stable jobs, junkiehood is not so far away as we would like to think.

**Where Robbing God
Shows Up**

The heroin addiction, however, is not where the God-robbing
shows up.  Instead, it’s part of the
price that we as a society are paying for an increasing determination to try to
rob God.  After all, at first glance,
there’s no apparent reason why this should be happening now.  Life Magazine was doing photo essays about
heroin addicts back in the 1950’s. 
Heroin’s been around for a while. 
So have prescription painkillers.

However, compared to the society of 60 years ago, our society
today is much less connected, and, especially, much less religious.  The secular elites who have been leading the
charge away from God have been very noisy about the perceived benefits.  Freedom from puritanical sexuality!  Personal autonomy!  Tolerance for all!

This is where Malachi 3:8 shows up, not in robbing God of
the tithes that are due Him, but in robbing Him of the obedience that is due
Him.  However, of all the rhetorical
questions in Malachi, this one has perhaps the most incredulous tone.  Robbing God never works, because whatever you
think you’re going to gain by it, you’re going to lose much more.

Unsurprisingly, the secular elites aren’t nearly as noisy
about the costs of irreligiosity and immorality.  Even the most introverted among us are still
social animals, and churches have been at the heart of American community for
at least the past 200 years.  I’ve heard
it said that without religious community, many Americans live the lives of
superheroes.  They sally forth from their
suburban Bat Caves every morning, they do their work, they return to the Bat
Cave in the evening, but nobody ever finds out who they really are.  The only friends they have are the ones on
Facebook, the kind of “friend” who is still writing “Happy Birthday!” on your
Facebook wall two years after you died.

**Meaningless Religion**

The cost of sin is similar, though not in the obvious,
direct way.  When I was in college, one
of my religious-studies professors defined religion as an exchange.  You get out of it what you give to it.  The defiant and disobedient are obviously
never going to share in the consolations of faith, but neither are many of the
self-described “religious”.  They are
participants in and victims of the hollowing out of American religious
observance and practice.

For many of them, religion consists of listening to a
religious rock concert for an hour or two every morning, interspersed with a
fifteen-minute lecture from a self-esteem expert.  Then, they go home and do whatever they want
all week, not particularly concerned with adhering to any standard for right
conduct.  After all, God is a God of
love, not a God of wrath, right? He doesn’t so much forgive our sins as indulge
them.  Then, next Sunday, it’s back to
the rock concert (though with lyrics about Jesus instead of significant others)
and the self-esteem pep talk.  Come as
you are; leave as you were!

However, religion that demands nothing also offers
nothing.  Disciples who conform
themselves to the world find that they are defenseless against the problems of the
world.  No matter what the self-esteem
gurus may say, disappointment, sickness, suffering, loss and death are our lot
in life.  When the storms come, either
you have built your house on the Rock or you haven’t.

**When the Dam Bursts**

The consequences for us are as predictable as they are
disastrous.  Without community and
without God, Americans are being destroyed from the inside out.  Citizens of the most prosperous, secure
nation of all time suffer from depression at staggering rates.  Increasingly, our votes go to radical
politicians who promise to overturn the established order because we’re
miserable under the established order, and we don’t know why.  Sadly, it’s the society that’s broken, not
the government.

We even turn to really, really bad answers like prescription-drug
abuse and heroin addiction, hoping that a really, really bad answer will prove
better than no answer.  Nonetheless, God
is not robbed, nor is He mocked.  Sooner
or later, the scales will be balanced with our pain.

Recently, I’ve been reading *Soldat*, the memoir of a German army officer who fought in World War
II.  The opening chapters of the book
describe the final, hopeless battle against the armies of the Soviet Union
before the gates of Berlin in April 1945. 
The Germans fought desperately because they knew what atrocities the
Russians would commit against the civilian population of the city (not that the
German army had clean hands either, mind). 
However, the defenders were absurdly outnumbered.  Despite all they could do, the Russians
overwhelmed their lines and won the battle and the war.

I cannot help but wonder if we are seeing something similar,
if we are living in the time when the lines that define our civil society are
being overwhelmed and will soon fail. 
The danger is not in one particular area; it is in all areas at once—the
early stages of a slow-motion catastrophe. 
Our enemies do not appear in the form of an invading army.  They appear in the form of the consequences
of our thoughtless, reckless, self-worshiping behavior.

Maybe it’s not too late. 
Maybe there is still time to shore up the dam before it bursts.  Or, perhaps, the time to stop the cascade of
failure was 20 years ago, and we missed the opportunity.  Regardless, it is sure that any solution must
involve a return to God.  Treating any of
the symptoms, even if they are as dire as heroin addiction, cannot solve the
problem.  As David observed 3000 years
ago, “If the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do?”
