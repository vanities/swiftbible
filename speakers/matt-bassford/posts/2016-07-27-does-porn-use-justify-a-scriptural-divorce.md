A few days ago, I re-encountered an argument I’d seen before
and hadn’t liked.  Here is its form:

* Pornography use involves lust.
* Lust is adultery in the heart (Matthew 7:28-29).
* Married people who put away their spouses for immorality are
  free to remarry (Matthew 19:9).
* Therefore, Christians who divorce their spouse over
  pornography use are free to remarry.



As appealing as this is, I have two main problems with the
argument.  The first is that metaphorical
adultery in the heart simply isn’t the same thing as literal adultery in the
flesh.  Paul describes the unique nature
of sexual immorality in 1 Corinthians 6:15-20:



*(15) Do you not know
that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take away the members of
Christ and make them members of a prostitute? May it never be! (16) Or do you
not know that the one who joins himself to a prostitute is one body with her?
For He says, "The two shall become one flesh." (17) But the one who
joins himself to the Lord is one spirit with Him. (18) Flee immorality. Every
other sin that a man commits is outside the body, but the immoral man sins
against his own body. (19) Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the
Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and that you are not your
own? (20) For you have been bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your
body.* (NASB)

Here, Paul begins by observing that the fleshly joining of
sexual immorality has spiritual implications. 
When a Christian becomes one flesh with a prostitute, he is uniting one
of the members of the body of Christ with that prostitute.  When we engage in sexual sin, we involve
Christ Himself.  It is a perversion of
the spiritual unity we are supposed to share with Him.

Second, Paul points to the uniquely corrupting nature of
sexual immorality.  Our bodies are
supposed to be a holy temple of God. 
However, sexual sin, because of its one-flesh nature, defiles our bodies
in a way that no other sin does or can. 
This is a singular affront to God. 
We take the body that belongs to Him and through sinful intimacy degrade
it as utterly as it can be degraded.

The applications of this text to marriage, divorce, and
remarriage are obvious.  First of all,
one-flesh adultery is as much of a perversion of the marriage bond as it is a
perversion of our relationship with Christ. 
We are one flesh with our spouses, and when we engage in sexual
immorality with a prostitute, we join them with that prostitute too.  We are dragging them into a sinful union to
which they have not consented.  We cannot
make them sin, but we can treat our intimate and holy relationship with them
like an unclean thing.  This, I think, is
the reason that the immorality exception in Matthew 19 exists.  Adultery in a marriage is a soul-deep
outrage.

Second, because adultery is a sexual act, it uniquely
defiles the body as other sexual sins do. 
All other sins, lust included, are outside the body.  The lustful sinner may want to immerse his
body in sin by unlawfully joining it to another, but he hasn’t actually managed
it.  He desires to practice sexual
immorality, but he hasn’t actually practiced it.  He hasn’t involved anybody but himself, so
his sin remains outside the body.

As a result, it simply doesn’t make sense to equate adultery
in the heart with adultery in fact.  Make
no mistake about it; the former is sinful and evil and wrong, but it is not a
corruption of the mystery of sexual union. 
Certainly, the lustful partner in a marriage needs to repent to the
wronged spouse.  Certainly, he needs to
give over his sinful ways.  However, his
bad behavior has not given that wronged spouse the right to divorce him and
remarry like adultery in fact would. 
Lust and adultery are too different.

Even beyond the doctrinal argument against equating lust and
adultery, there’s a practical problem. 
If we accept lust/pornography use as grounds for Scriptural divorce, the
exception will swallow the Matthew 19 rule.

I don’t talk about sex and sexual issues with Christian
women (it seems like handing the devil an engraved invitation), but I will
discuss the subject with Christian men from time to time.  I may be mistaken, but my sense is that lust
is a nearly universal problem among my brothers in Christ.  If there are men of God who are willing to
stand up and say, “I have never lusted after another woman since I got
married,” I salute them, but I fear the number of such men is vanishingly
small.

As a result, nearly any Christian woman who wants a
Scriptural divorce is going to be able to use lust as a pretext.  As long as you can get your husband to
acknowledge that his mind has wandered where it shouldn’t, you can divorce
him!  Catch him using porn?  If you want to, you can make him pack his
bags, yet get remarried yourself.

Of course, in the era of *Fifty
Shades of Grey* and mommy porn, it may well be that lust problems are
equally widespread among sisters in Christ. 
It’s entirely possible that men who want to get out of an unsatisfying
marriage can ask a few pointed questions about reading habits, and then they’re
free to remarry too.  At that point, the
number of people affected by the warning of Matthew 19:9 becomes so small that
I start wondering why Jesus even bothered saying it.  Instead of being one of the hard sayings of
Jesus (and a frequent challenge to evangelism), it becomes an irrelevant
Scriptural footnote.

In fact, this reading would leave Christians more ready to
divorce one another than people in the world. 
Outsiders generally regard spousal porn use as a non-issue or something
to be worked through, not a marital nuke like an affair would be.  They acknowledge the difference.  How strange it would be if we, who claim to
serve a God who hates divorce, are actually less reluctant to divorce than they are!

Once again, I do not mean to minimize the evil or the harm
of lust and pornography use in marriage. 
This can cause terrible trouble for both the sinners and their
spouses.  However, there are plenty of
other sins in a marriage context that aren’t any picnic to deal with
either.  It's extremely difficult to be married to an
alcoholic, but alcoholism doesn’t give grounds for a Scriptural divorce.  When Jesus limits the Matthew 19:9 exception
to immorality, we must be careful to do the same.
