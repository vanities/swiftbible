One of the odder friendships in my life is the one I share
with Jason Engel.  I first met him on
some Bible-geek Facebook group, and we hit it off, even though (because?) our
views on a number of social/religious issues are very different.  I enjoy talking with Jason (online, and
sometimes in real life) because I like seeing how things look from the opposite
side of the political spectrum.  Though I
often disagree with him, I can deny neither his sincerity nor his passion.  Besides, though I find myself in a position
where it’s usually unwise to vent, I get a kick out of reading a good rant now
and again.



Recently, Jason has been particularly bothered by what he
perceives as the hard-heartedness of many self-described Christians toward the
Syrian refugees.  As a result, last week,
he linked to [this blog post](http://johnpavlovitz.com/2015/11/20/but-what-if-it-is-not-well-with-my-soul/).  I decided it would be worthwhile to reach out
to him with my thoughts on the matter, as follows:

*I think it's important to think about how big a circle
around yourself you're drawing. The problem with mass media and the Internet is
that they expose us to situations we can't change and people we can't affect. I
sympathize with your frustration about the refugee issue, but this is really
just another version of the cartoon, "Somebody on the Internet is
wrong." Of course they are. They always will be. You might give yourself a
stroke worrying about it, but you can't change it.*

*Instead, I think it's much better to focus on the people we
can change: family, close friends, people who go to church with us, etc.
Face-to-face is a much more powerful contact than a keyboard. In the group of
people I can affect, I \_do\_ see change for the good.*

*For instance, one of the sisters here at church just
finished up with her own individual coat drive for needy children. At last
count, she had rounded up 58 coats. 10 years ago, she wasn't doing that. I
can't claim that I'm responsible for the change, but I know Christ is. I can
think of plenty of other examples like that. 
Even if the larger world is discouraging, the people to whom I am
closest give me hope, for my work, and more importantly, for God's work.*

*Just my $.02, though I give you fair warning that I may make
a blog post out of it.*

To say the same thing in a slightly different way, I suspect that all of us have much to be thankful for.  The key is looking in the right place.  Happy Thanksgiving!
