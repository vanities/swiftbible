How long 11 years is depends on what you’re doing.  The Bible tells us that the seven years Jacob
spent serving Laban for Rachel only seemed to him like a few days.  I doubt his perspective would have been much
different had he spent 11 years in service. 
That turned out to be just as well, really.



On the other hand, some 11-year spans are long indeed.  I suspect that the state of Illinois only
issues prison terms in multiples of five, but if I got sent down for something
(exceeding the word limit on the Internet?) for 11 years, I’d feel every minute
of that 11 years dragging by.

There’s a third category, though, the category of spans of
time that can be measured on the calendar but never in the heart.  I can remember getting married.  It was cold outside when we were taking the
wedding pictures, endless wedding pictures. 
The wedding march was the hymn tune THAXTED.  Lauren put my wedding ring on the wrong hand
during the ceremony.  We sang “Great Is
Thy Faithfulness” during the reception.

I can remember getting married, not so long ago as calendars
measure time, yet separated from today by a gulf incomprehensibly wide.  How can you measure out 11 years when you
have spent them with the woman who is sunlight, who is rain early and late, who
is sweet air to breathe, who is good black earth to grow in?  Grown I have, invariably in ways I did not
expect, so much so that the man who said the vows in 2005 has become almost a
stranger whose memories I share.

11 years have passed. 
Some years were harder than others: 
the year my mother died; the year our daughter died.  A lot of marriages founder when children die;
ours didn’t.  When Lauren Richardson
said, “Till death do us part,” she meant it.

Not all the memories are good, but most are.  My wife has given me great joy through these
11 years, but especially, she has given me what I needed.  From the beginning, I have loved her for her
mind:  analytical, penetrating, quick and
decisive in exactly the way my own mind isn’t. 
For 11 years, she’s been willing and able to be my sounding board,
talking with me for hours at a time as I slog my way to understanding.

I have loved her for her charm.  Men in my family have a knack for making
enemies, but she’s never had an enemy in her life.  With wisdom and compassion, she has helped me
thread my way through all the relationship pitfalls that await the unwary
preacher (The Lord has done quite a bit of work here too).  In every way, she has been a Priscilla to my
Aquila, thriving in a role that most women would hate.

I have loved her for so many things:  for her beauty, for her unfailing optimism,
for her willingness to bake me a birthday cake when she’s on a diet and can’t
eat any of it.  I have loved her for our
two amazing children, who get all of their good traits from her and all of
their bad traits from me.  I have loved
her, and I will continue to love her through all the days of my fleeting life,
whether they be many or few.

Happy 11th anniversary, my darling!  May the coming year bless you with a husband
who is at least closer to the husband you deserve.
