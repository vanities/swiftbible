Greetings, all!  The
witching hour is upon us!  Well, it’s
kind of a witching hour without witches, although I have wondered whether
hymnists, like witches, gather in covens. 
What is the collective noun for “hymnist”, anyway?  “Circle”? 
“Tribe”?  “Exaltation”, like
larks?  Or “unkindness”, like
ravens?  (Feel free to leave your
suggestion in the comments below.)

Regardless, what I mean to say is that the time has come to
register for the 2015 Hymninar.  If
you’re never heard of a Hymninar before, don’t be surprised.  Unlike “exaltation of larks” and “unkindness
of ravens”, we made it up.  A Hymninar is
a weeklong gathering of those who want to learn about and improve in the
writing of congregational, a-cappella hymns.



This year, the Hymninar will be held once again in Columbia,
MO, from August 2-7.  The cost will be
something like $90 a head.  If this seems
low for a weeklong workshop of any sort, it is. 
This is true for a couple of reasons. 
First of all, there are a number of truly wonderful, hospitable brethren
in Columbia who are willing to put up (with?) the attendees for free all week
long.  Of course, if you would rather
purchase hospitality from a Holiday Inn or similar, you’re welcome to do that
too.  You are, however, on your own for meals.

Second, nobody is getting paid for this.  The entirety of the fee goes toward classroom
rental, parking passes, and purchase of materials.  Just think; you’re getting a week of instruction
from experienced, albeit slightly deranged, writers, composers, and editors FOR
ABSOLUTELY FREE!  What a deal, eh?

This instruction will consist of two main sections.  The first part of the week will be devoted to
working through *A Good Hymn*, a how-to
notebook that spells out the basics of hymnwriting.  This usually takes around two days.  It’s kind of like an English class with weird
humor.

After we’ve finished the notebook, each student is
responsible for writing one four-line verse in accordance with the principles
of good writing.  “How could that
possibly take Three Whole Days?” one might ask. 
Trust us.  It does.  Also, returning writers will have the
opportunity to submit any hymns they’ve been working on through the year for
review.  I leave veterans of the process
to determine whether the collectivity of their reviewers seemed more like an
exaltation or an unkindness.

So.  Wanna come?  If you do, or if you’d just like more
information before deciding, get in touch with me via Facebook (wherein I am
Matthew William Bassford), email at mandlbassford@gmail.com, or even phone or
text at (815)931-1740.  I’m terrible at
getting back with people, so harass me mercilessly until you finally get a
reply.

In order to register, get me your name and contact information.  If you’ve got one of those unisex names,
specify gender too.  We need to know for
housing assignments.  I will pass all
that information along to my wife, who will enter it into a nice neat
spreadsheet instead of writing it on the back of a junk-mail envelope like I
would do.  A few months hence, you will
get a nicely official letter from Craig Roberts (the dude who actually runs
this thing) further explaining relevant details.  July 6th is the deadline for
registration.
