*And Pharaoh said to
Joseph, "See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt."*

*--Genesis 41:41*

Here, finally, we see Joseph’s fortunes begin to turn
around.  For two years after the
cupbearer’s restoration, he has continued to rot in the pits of Pharaoh’s
dungeon.  Then, Pharaoh happened to have
two dreams.  One of them was about seven
thin cows devouring seven fat cows.  The
other was about seven withered ears of grain devouring seven full, ripe
ears.  Unsurprisingly, these dreams
mystify even the wisest men of Pharaoh’s court.



However, Pharaoh’s cupbearer remembers his stint in prison
and the young Hebrew who had correctly interpreted his dream there.  At this point, Pharaoh’s willing to try
anything, so they bring Joseph up from the pit, hose him off, and present him
to Pharaoh.  Joseph hears the dream and
explains that both dreams mean the same thing. 
The whole world is going to have seven years of good harvests, then
seven years of famine.  He goes on to
advise Pharaoh that now is the time to store up enough grain to preserve his
nation through the time of famine.

Pharaoh accepts Joseph’s words.  In fact, he accepts them so completely and is
so impressed with Joseph himself that he immediately elevates the recent
prisoner to the position of second most powerful man in Egypt.  Joseph’s entire life has undergone a stunning
reversal.  Where before nothing went
right, now everything is going right, and it’s going to continue that way for
the rest of Joseph’s life.

When we go through hard times, we need to remember Joseph’s
afflictions and Joseph’s perseverance. 
God was simply using all of the trials that Joseph underwent to prepare
him for eventual success.  It would have
been easy for Joseph to follow the famous advice of Job’s wife and curse God
and die.  However, despite the despair
that Joseph must have felt in the pits of Pharaoh, he never lost heart and
never ceased trusting in God.  That’s why
he eventually saw the blessing.

The same may well be true in our lives.  Every Christian on the planet can probably
testify that being a child of God isn’t all sunshine and roses.  God does not protect us from all affliction
and heartache.  It may well be that at
times we must endure years of misery.

None of that, however, should lead us to question God.  His purpose is not our immediate good, but
our ultimate good.  We cannot expect Him
to shield us from all harm, but we can rest assured that He will never allow us
to suffer without purpose.  The lessons
that we learn from suffering are often the most spiritually important lessons
we will ever learn.  As was true in
Joseph’s case, that suffering will often lead us to joys and opportunities we
would never even have seen otherwise. 
That may be true in this life, but it is certainly true in the life to
come.  All we must do to see it is trust
and persevere.
