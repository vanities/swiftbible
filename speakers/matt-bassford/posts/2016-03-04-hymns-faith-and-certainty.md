If the theme of the 2008 election was “Hope and change”, the
theme of the 2016 edition is fear.  Few
Americans seem to believe that the country is headed in the right
direction.  Instead, they are giving
their allegiance to candidates who promise to reverse the country’s fortunes,
or even to candidates who promise to protect them from other candidates!

My political views are well summed up by Psalm 146:3, but I
can certainly sympathize with those who fear an uncertain future.  So far as I can see, the only certainties
under the sun are all bad.



It is certain, for instance, that someday the United States
will be destroyed.  I like to read
history, so I know that for thousands of years, today’s empires have become
tomorrow’s ruins.  I don’t see any reason
to believe that our nation, great as it is, will be exempt from the rule.  Whether the end comes now or hundreds of
years from now, it will surely come, on the Day of Judgment if not before.

It is similarly certain that all of the earthly
relationships that are dearest to me will not endure.  Some of them may end because I choose to end
them; others may end because my friends and loved ones so choose.  Failing that, all such relationships will end
when friends and loved ones die.

Likewise, it is certain that I cannot even rely on
myself.  For all of my determination to
do right, there have been many times when I have failed to live up to my
ideals.  I am sure that despite my best
intentions and efforts, there will be many such times in the future too.

Nor can I rely on myself in a physical sense.  At 37, gray hair, wrinkles, and a less
capable body all tell me that I’m getting older, and I anticipate that the
process will only accelerate.  I’ve spent
enough time in hospitals and nursing homes to know how it all ends, too.  No one’s earthly life continues indefinitely.

This is a grim picture. 
If life under the sun is all there is, it’s an invitation to
nihilism.  None of us can rely on good
things in this life, only on bad ones.

All my adult life, I have taken refuge from this unpleasant earthly
reality in the certainties of faith.  This
is perhaps most obvious in the hymns that I’ve written, particularly over the
past five years.  You can’t hide who you
are in a hymn and still expect it to be good. 
You can only write from your own resources, from the truth in your
heart.  I’ve been writing hymns with
titles like these:

You Do Not Change

Unwavering Christ

Yesterday, Today, Forever

O Christ, Our Hope in Grief

I Will Answer

O Christ, Our Resurrection

There’s more to my work than that, but it’s certainly an
important, even central part. 
Fundamentally, I am a Christian because I believe I can rely on Jesus to
protect me:  from political upheaval,
from time and chance, from sickness and death, and even from myself.  If Christ is powerless against the evils of
this life, why bother following Him?

My decision to rely on Him begins with His resurrection from
the dead.  In the words of Paul in Romans
1:4, the resurrection declares Him the Son of God with power.  If Christ arose, God reigns, and I can rely
on His promises.  I can be assured that:

* He will not forget me.
* He will not forsake me.
* He will provide for me if I seek His kingdom first.
* He will help me when I am tempted.
* He will forgive me when I repent.
* He will listen when I pray.
* He will continue to rule over the nations, even when evil
  seems to prevail.
* He will judge the world in righteousness.
* He will raise me if I die first.

If those things are taken care of, then so am I!  God has never promised His people earthly
security or stability, much less earthly riches.  Like the prophet Jeremiah, it may be our fate
to face great trial because of the wickedness around us.  We may suffer as Jeremiah suffered.

However, even in the life of Jeremiah, there is hope.  One of my favorite verses in the book appears
in the context of Jeremiah 30-31.  There,
God reveals to Jeremiah that even though Jerusalem will be destroyed by the
king of Babylon, it won’t be a permanent destruction.  Even though things seem hopeless to Jeremiah,
God knows that the day will come when the ruined city will be rebuilt and the
prosperity of her people restored.

In Jeremiah 31:26, Jeremiah’s response is both wordless and
eloquent.  It reads, “At this I awoke and
looked, and my sleep was pleasant to me.” (NASB)  Perhaps for the first time in years, Jeremiah
could rest easy, because now he understood that God was working out all things
for good.

Today, maybe we don’t get to understand where it’s all headed.  Elections and relationships and jobs and
health remain uncertain.  However, we can
be certain that somewhere underneath it all, God is still working out His will,
and if we seek Him, it will be well with us. 
In that, all of us can rest easy too.
