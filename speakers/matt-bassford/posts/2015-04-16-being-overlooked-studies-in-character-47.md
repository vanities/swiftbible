*Yet the chief butler
did not remember Joseph, but forgot him.*

*-- Genesis 40:23*

Through much of Joseph’s career, two things remain
constant:  God is always with him, and
yet the poor guy can’t seem to catch a break. 
Due to the false accusations of Potiphar’s wife, Joseph is put into
prison.  However, even there, he
flourishes, due to the Lord’s blessing. 
The warden soon recognizes Joseph’s excellence, and, just as has
happened everywhere else Joseph has been, soon he ends up running the place.



Then, it finally seems that Joseph is going to be given the
opportunity he deserves.  He is given
charge over two of Pharaoh’s officials who have been imprisoned for offending
Pharaoh.  One of these high officials is
Pharaoh’s chief cupbearer.  One morning,
he has a perplexing dream about squeezing three clusters of grapes into
Pharaoh’s cup.  Joseph, who has the
divine gift of dream interpretation, is able to explain to him that this means
he will be restored to his position in three days.  All he asks in return for this interpretation
is that the cupbearer remember him and rescue him from his unjust
imprisonment.  Everything happens just as
Joseph has predicted, but once he is restored to power, the cupbearer forgets
all about Joseph and his dreams, and Joseph continues rotting in Pharaoh’s
dungeon for another two years.

Sometimes, we get to feeling like we can’t catch a break
either.  We do a bang-up job at work, but
when the time comes to hand out raises and promotions, we get overlooked.  We really invest ourselves in making our
marriages better, but our spouses continue on as they have always done,
oblivious to our efforts.  We constantly
do good to others, but no one seems to care or want to reciprocate.

In moments like these, we need to remember that indifference
to our good works is often the way of the world.  When the quarterback successfully completes a
pass, nobody praises the offensive linemen. 
People only notice the offensive linemen when the quarterback gets
sacked.  Likewise, if we’re toiling away
as we should, most people aren’t going to notice and praise us.  If we do what is right in the hope of
immediate earthly reward, we’re often going to be disappointed.

However, that popular indifference to good works should not
discourage us from practicing what is right. 
There’s no indication in the text that Joseph got disgusted with being
overlooked and spent the next two years slacking off in his prison
responsibilities.  In fact, from what the
Scripture says about the character of Joseph, it’s likely that he was every bit
as industrious after his snub as before. 
We should be the same way.  We
must persevere in doing what is right, not because we hope to prosper thereby,
but simply because it is right.

Finally, as Genesis 41 reveals, the cupbearer forgot Joseph
only temporarily, and it may well be that in our lives, the blessings we hope
to see are only delayed, not denied.  God
works on His timetable, not ours.  We
must accept that patience is often necessary to gain the good things that He
has in store for us.
