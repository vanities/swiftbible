A couple days ago, Disney announced that for the first time,
it would be including an openly gay character in one of its children’s
movies:  LeFou in the upcoming remake of *Beauty and the Beast*.  For me, as for many Christians, this is
distressing news.  My wife and I have
looked to Disney films for years as movies that we can comfortably watch with
our children.  We were planning to see *Beauty and the Beast* once it hit
theaters.  Now, the future of that
particular outing is very much in doubt (to say the least).

However, at least online, many Christians have been
struggling to express why they’re so upset. 
After all, plenty of other Disney films have portrayed sin before.  For that matter, the Bible itself portrays
plenty of sin.  What’s the big deal here?



I think the issue lies not so much in content but in
framing.  To pick a recent example, Flynn
Rider, the hero of *Tangled*, is a
thief.  However, Disney didn’t put out
any press releases in advance of *Tangled* to
announce that there would be thieving scenes in the movie.  The message of the film is not that children
should become thieves or accept theft.  Indeed,
it’s quite the opposite.  By the end of
the movie, Flynn reforms and abandons his thieving ways.

This is different. 
For one thing, we do have a press release.  Disney, like other corporate titans, doesn’t
make statements unless it wants to. . . make a statement.  This is not some organic part of the story of
*Beauty and the Beast* (which, after
all, has been homosexuality-free from the Middle Ages until now).  This is something that has been added in so
that everybody knows where Disney stands, and so that everybody knows where
Disney thinks they ought to stand. 
Disney has, for the moment, stepped out of the storytelling business and
gone into the advancing-a-moral-agenda business.

We all know what the moral of this story is going to be,
too.  LeFou’s gay dilemma is going to be
presented in a heartwarming, sympathetic light, so that the audience will
respond positively to the character and to the practice of homosexuality in
general.  The end of *Tangled* saw Flynn become a reformed thief; the end of this version
of *Beauty and the Beast* will
certainly not see LeFou become a reformed homosexual!

Ironically, even though I’m a Paleolithic conservative who
believes that the Bible is the inspired, inerrant word of God, the thing that
really bugs me about the progressive agenda is the preachy moralism with which
it is advanced.  It’s all the more
obnoxious because it’s a moralism that’s built on sand.  I’m no fan of Islam, but at least Muslims can
point to the Qur’an and say, “It is written.” 
They have a standard.

However, there is no objective basis for the argument that
the practice of homosexuality is right and must be accepted.  It’s not that its proponents aren’t right;
it’s that they can’t be right.  There’s
no possible truth claim here, only feelings.

However, the gay-rights crowd doesn’t let that stop
them.  Rather than acting like their
point of view is one of many possible, equally valid points of view (which is
all the logical basis of their argument allows them to maintain), they insist
that it is one that everyone else must adopt too, and they’re advancing their
convictions by including them in movies aimed at children.

Personally, I’m fed up with getting preached at.  No; I am not somehow morally inferior because
I believe that 1 Corinthians 6 is part of the divine revelation too!  I’m perfectly willing to live and let live, but
it is becoming increasingly clear to me that rather than reciprocating with
tolerance, the folks on the other side aren’t going to be satisfied with
anything less from me and mine than enthusiastic endorsement.  As a result, a movie that should be a neutral
zone for everyone to enjoy, liberal or conservative, has become an ideological
battleground.

I see which way the wind is blowing on this.  In our increasingly secular society, most
Americans get their moral instruction from TV and movies (how far Hollywood has
brought us over the past 25 years, and not in a good direction!).  They’re fine with *Beauty and the Beast* telling them and their children what to think,
and all the progressive sermons are going to bear fruit.  LeFou will open the door for a lesbian Disney
princess, or possibly a Disney prince who wants to become a Disney princess,
and people will buy that too.  Maybe that
puts me, as Barack Obama would say, on the wrong side of history.  However, I’m afraid that I know where it also
puts the United States.
