Last week, I wrote a post about whether baptism was a work
that earned salvation or not.  I argued
that there is no Biblical reason for suggesting such a thing, which led me to
the conclusion that baptism was a necessary part of justification by faith.

However, one of my friends on Facebook raised a common
counter-argument.  He observed that the
thief on the cross appears to have been saved by Jesus’ promise alone, without
having been baptized.  Hence, he
concluded that baptism is not necessary for salvation.



For those who haven’t read it before, the story is found in
Luke 23:39-43.  The crucified thief begs
Jesus to remember Him, and Jesus in return promises him that he will be with
Him in paradise.  This is all we know
about the thief.  For all we know, he may
have been baptized before he took up his career in banditry.  However, for purposes of this discussion, I’m
going to stipulate that the thief is unbaptized, and that he was saved by the
promise of Jesus alone.

Even given, that, though, the story is proof of a very
different principle:  that the Son of Man
had authority on earth to forgive sins. 
In fact, during His ministry, Jesus went to great lengths to establish
that He could forgive sins by His word alone.

This is shown most prominently in the story of the paralytic
in Mark 2:1-12.  Most of us with a
religious upbringing will remember learning as children the story of the
paralytic being lowered through the roof, but that’s not really the point.  Instead, Jesus makes His point by first telling
the paralytic that his sins are forgiven (v. 6).  This elicits the same reaction from the
scribes in attendance that a similar claim would elicit from me today.  Who does this guy think he is, going around
forgiving sins with a word???

In reply, Jesus correctly points out that it’s more
difficult to forgive sins than to heal a paralyzed man.  However, either one is impossible without the
power of God.  He then proves the invisible
miracle of forgiveness by the visible miracle of healing.  The point is plain:  Jesus on earth has authority to verbally forgive sins.

There’s a problem, though, with reasoning from that fact to
the conclusion that baptism is unnecessary for salvation.  I’m not about to deny that if Christ in the
flesh tells you that you have been saved from your sins, you’ve been saved from
your sins.  He’s the Lamb of God who
takes away the sins of the world; He can save however He wants to!

However, it’s been 2000 years since anybody heard “Your sins
are forgiven” from the lips of the Lord. 
10 days after His ascension, Peter proclaimed, “Let every one of you be
baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins.”  Here, as it commonly does in Scripture,
“name” carries the meaning of “authority”. 
During His ministry, He had authority to forgive sins with a word.  Today, we appeal to His authority to forgive
when we are baptized.
