Recently, Lauren and I have been watching an Amazon Video
miniseries called *The Man in the High
Castle.* So far, so good.  Aesthetically speaking, the show is very well
done, and I don’t feel like I have to compromise with Biblical morality to keep
watching it.  Its premise is that the
U.S. lost World War II and was divided into Nazi and Japanese occupied
zones.  Historically, this is suspect
(Germany mayyybee could have won the war; the best Japan could hope for was to
delay losing), but it’s an interesting counterfactual nonetheless.



It does, however, point to a grim truth that is explicitly
historical.  Sooner or later, every great
earthly nation falls.  Great Chinese
dynasties disappeared in the chaos of civil war.  Over the course of the twentieth century, the
British went from being the empire on which the sun never set to being a tiny
island nation once again.  This is to say
nothing, of course, of all the great kingdoms of Bible times, from the
Egyptians to the Romans, that were destroyed as completely as they once
dominated.  Time brings everybody down,
and in time, the United States will be brought down too.  American exceptionalism can’t make us an
exception from that.

Whole libraries might be filled with volumes about the
decline and fall of this empire or that empire, but one of the most striking
explanations of national decline is offered by the Bible.  Historians might explain the destruction of
the kingdoms of Israel and Judah in terms of sociopolitical forces, but the
Bible looks at internals, not externals. 
In particular, 2 Kings 17 sets out the author’s take on the collapse of
the northern half of the divided kingdom. 
A section which the NASB helpfully labels “Why Israel Fell” links
national decline to moral and spiritual decline.  It wasn’t the Assyrians, not really.  It was the idols.

I believe that someday, thinkers will also look to internal
causes to explain the demise of the United States.  It’s hard to imagine some earthly foe being
able to overpower a United States that remains strong.  Instead, it’s almost necessary that we
succumb to inward decay first, before somebody else supplies the final
push.  I have no ability to predict the
future, but here are the trends that I see that most concern me.

**The Destruction of
the Family.** In case anybody missed
the point in Genesis 2, Jesus in Matthew 19:3-9 sets out God’s blueprint for
the family:  One husband, one wife, one
flesh, for life.  In modern America,
every line in that blueprint is under attack. 
The past year has seen enshrined in our laws the notion that two
husbands or two wives is just as good.

The real problems, though, have arisen elsewhere.  Today, the vast majority of Americans believe
that it’s acceptable to become one flesh with somebody outside of
marriage.  This change began 50 years
ago, so we’ve already had the opportunity to see the fruits:  sexually transmitted disease, children
without present fathers, rampant sexual abuse of minors, and poverty are all
part of the bumper crop of social ills.

“For life” is equally under attack.  More than half of American marriages now end
in divorce, with devastating mental, spiritual, and financial consequences for
all involved.  Divorce was insinuated
into American society with the claim that the children would really be better
off if Mom and Dad went their separate ways. 
Today, we know that’s certainly untrue, but divorce has become such an
ingrained part of American society that it is quietly accepted despite its
destructive power.

All of these forces together have begun a cascade of failure
in the family.  I was blessed to have
been raised in a stable two-parent family, and the same was true for both of my
parents.  Everything that I am, I owe to
my mother and my father.  I’m the
beneficiary of a precious set of values that has been handed down through the
generations.  It only takes one
generation to squander that moral inheritance, and that’s happened to tens of
millions of Americans.  I grieve for
every boy that has to figure out how to be a man without a father, and for
every girl who has to figure out what to look for in a man without a strong
paternal example.  Bad decisions usually
beget more bad decisions.  The family is
falling apart, and once it finishes crumbling, American society will be without
its foundation.

**Selfishness.** 2 Kings 17:17-18 reads, “Then they made
their sons and daughters pass through the fire, and practiced divinations and
enchantments, and sold themselves to do evil in the sight of the Lord,
provoking Him.  So the Lord was very
angry with Israel and removed them from His sight.”  Of all the things that the Israelites did
that God hated, it seems to me that He hated most their sacrificing of their
sons and daughters to Molech.  That made
God not merely angry, but very angry.

It makes my blood run cold to think of all the innocent
blood that has been shed in my country since 1973.  How many sons and daughters have been killed
because they weren’t convenient?  This
can only lead, and indeed has led, to a society-wide devaluing of human
life.  It should surprise no one when
violent crime is rampant in a country where murdering the unborn is legal.

Soon, even the flimsy in-utero/ex-utero distinction will be
discarded.  California’s recent decision
to legalize euthanasia shows which way the wind is blowing.  For now, terminally ill patients have the
right to seek medically assisted suicide. 
Experience in other countries has shown that soon, terminally patients
will be more and more strongly encouraged to exercise that right.

For that matter, the consent of the dying itself will become
less and less important.  Don’t they
realize that they’re dying, after all? 
They ought to be wise enough to avoid prolonging the process.  If they aren’t, clearly they need our wisdom
to help them go gentle into that good night. 
Besides, caring for the dying is expensive and inconvenient.  Ain’t nobody got time for that!

This same selfishness and indifference to others reveals
itself in the increasing reluctance of Americans to join voluntary
organizations.  Much has been made of the
decline of church membership.  Membership
has been declining in PTA’s, bowling leagues, and Lions Clubs too.  Modern Americans are such rugged
individualists that they don’t have much interest in or concern for
others.  TV shows and Facebook friends
will do.

The problems with this are twofold.  First, a nation without strong community
associations is like a brick wall without mortar.  The pieces are there, but they have nothing
holding them together.  An edifice that
should be strong is instead vulnerable. 
Second, our indifference toward human life can only make God very
angry.  Even the strongest nation cannot
resist His judgments.  What, then, will
happen to the nation that is no longer strong?

**Greed.** Gordon Gekko to the
contrary, greed is not good.  It’s
selfishness thinly disguised as virtue.  Sadly,
the notion that money and things are most important is enshrined at every level
of American society.  The underlying
message in every TV ad is that if you have enough stuff or the right kind of
stuff, it will make you happy.  Americans
work longer and longer hours to “get ahead”, even though getting ahead only
means getting more stuff.  It’s more
important, we think, to earn lots of money to buy a big house, even if that
means that you don’t spend much time with the family living in your big house.

At higher levels, the legal notion that the corporation
exists to maximize shareholder value excuses all sorts of immoral behavior on
the part of the corporation.  It’s OK to
lay off employees, squeeze the rest, pollute the environment, and engage in
sundry other evils so long as your company is helping somebody make a
buck.  Nor is there much of a sense of *noblesse oblige* among America’s wealthy
today.  Instead, the politically active
rich appear most interested in reducing taxes, regulations, and government aid
to the poor so that they can make more money and keep more after they’ve made
it.  For some reason, many Christians
appear convinced that all this is morally praiseworthy behavior.

The Bible does have a lot to say about greed and its
effects, true, but none of it is positive. 
In particular, I’m reminded of Amos’s words in Amos 2:6-7, “Thus says
the Lord, ‘For three transgressions of Israel and for four, I will not revoke
its punishment, because they sell the righteous for money and the needy for a pair
of sandals.  These who pant after the
very dust of the earth on the head of the helpless also turn aside the way of
the humble.’”  Oppressing the poor makes
God angry and incurs His judgment too!

In conclusion, I can do no better than quote the apostle
Paul in Romans 1:28-32:

*And just as they did
not see fit to acknowledge God any longer, God gave them over to a depraved
mind, to do those things which are not proper, being filled with all
unrighteousness, wickedness, greed, evil; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit,
malice; they are gossips, slanderers, haters of God, insolent, arrogant,
boastful, inventors of evil, disobedient to parents, without understanding,
untrustworthy, unloving, unmerciful; and although they know the ordinance of
God, that those who practice such things are worthy of death, they not only do
the same, but also give hearty approval to those who practice them.*

I don’t know when or how, but I’m afraid I don’t have much
doubt about what.
