*For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and shall
be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.**This mystery is great; but I am speaking with
reference to Christ and the church.*

-- Ephesians 5:31-32 (NASB)

As a whole, one of the greatest strengths of the Lord’s
church is its commitment to the word of God. 
How should we worship?  Look to
the Bible.  How should we organize the
church?  Look to the Bible.  What must we do to be saved?  Look to the Bible.  Indeed, whenever any spiritual argument is
advanced, we look for Scriptural support, and when a preacher or teacher seems
more interested in his think-sos than in book, chapter, and verse, we are
likely to become impatient with him.



However, one area where this is strikingly not true is
our teaching on marriage and the family. 
When a preacher is invited to preach a meeting on the subject, it is all
too common for him to nod in the direction of the Bible by citing one or two passages,
and then spend the rest of his time recounting modern thinking on the subject,
with plentiful reference to psychologists and marriage experts of every stripe.

Certainly, some of those psychologists and marriage
experts have useful things to say. 
However, the framework that we use to consider the subject of marriage
must not be the wisdom of men, but the word of God.  In addition to the several texts that deal
explicitly with marriage, nearly everything that the New Testament says about
individual relationships also applies to the subject.  Spouses are people too.  Bible teaching on our neighbor applies with
equal or greater force to them.  When we
put those Scriptural precepts into practice, our marriages cannot help but
please God.
