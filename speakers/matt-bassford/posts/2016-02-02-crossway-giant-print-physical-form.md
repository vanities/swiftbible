**OVERVIEW.** This Bible is a brick.  The cover isn’t the greatest genuine leather
I’ve seen (though it’s not the worst, either). 
However, the text block is well manufactured, and the print is large
enough that just about anybody who isn’t legally blind can read it.

As with most of the Bibles I review these days, this one is
a review copy, courtesy of Crossway.  As
always, no conditions were placed on my review.

**VITAL STATISTICS**

**Translation:**  English Standard Version (2011)

**Cover:**  Genuine leather

**Binding:**  Sewn

**Trim Size:**  6 ½” x 9 ¼”

**Text Block Width:**  1 ⅝”

**Weight:**  3 pounds, 7 ounces

**Column:**  Double

**Layout:**  Paragraphed

**Font:**  14/15 Lexicon No. 1

**Words of Christ:** Black

**Cross-References:**  No

**Ribbon Marker:**  1

**SIZE.** I believe this Bible is literally the
largest I have ever used.  I’ve never
owned a Bible with a trim size greater than 6 ½” x 9 ¼”, and it outweighs even
my 2002 Foundation SCR by a couple of ounces. 
There’s no point in trying to put this beast in a glove compartment, and
if I were looking for a grab-and-go Bible, I own a couple of dozen better
options.

Once again, we find ourselves face-to-face with the
compromises underlying Bible design.  If
you want to do one thing really well, you have to sacrifice everything else to
get it.  The designers of the GP wanted.
. . giant print, and to get it, they gave up wide margins, cross-references,
and small size.  If you care more about
any of those other things, there’s no reason even to try using the GP as a carry
Bible.  However, if you can’t even read
most other Bibles, here’s one designed for you.

**COVER.** I admit; I came into this review with low
expectations.  My least favorite
genuine-leather cover ever came on a Crossway Legacy.  It started out as stiff as Al Gore in a
casket, and it stayed that way the whole time I owned it.  In my experience, even though a paste-down
genuine-leather cover will be much less flexible out of the box than an
edge-lined goatskin cover, over time, the former will break in and the
difference between the two will become much less pronounced.  Not so with the Legacy.  No matter how much I flexed and oiled, I
couldn’t make the cover soften up.

I was expecting more of the same from the GP, but at the end
of the review period, I can only count myself pleasantly surprised.  Make no mistake; this is still a relatively
thin, hard, inflexible cover.  I would be
disappointed if I encountered this cover on a Foundation Bible.  However, it at least offers hope for the
future.  Even after two weeks of use, it
shows more flexibility than it did out of the box, and I would imagine that the
hopeful trend will continue once the Bible moves on to its new owner.  It’s not the most lovable cover I’ve ever
encountered (that would be the cover on my Leonard’s-rebind black-calf ASV),
but I like it well enough.  The gilt line
around the inside edge of the text block (usually a feature found only in
premium Bibles) also makes me more favorably disposed to the Bible.

Its country of origin may have something to do with
this.  All the Legacies I’ve ever seen
are printed in Italy, but the GP is a China-print Bible.  Different printers have produced a different
cover.

In terms of durability, the cover itself shouldn’t be the
problem.  Genuine leather is genuine
leather.  I’ve never seen a
genuine-leather cover fray and fall off at the spine.

If anything, trouble will come from the endpapers, even
though they’re quality stuff.  Unlike the
thick paper appearing in the endpapers of smaller Crossway Bibles, the GP
features leatherette, which is as nice as endpapers come.  Also, the GP offers a massively reinforced
hinge, which is probably a necessity with a text block as hefty as the one in
this Bible.  However, I suspect that the
hinge is still the vulnerable point. 
Hinges in Bibles this big are prone to splitting, especially if a few
church bulletins and Bible-class lessons are stuffed inside the cover (never do
that!).  I don’t see anything else
Crossway could have done; there’s simply a lot more stress on the cover and
binding of a big Bible than on the cover and binding of a small Bible.

**BINDING.** I suspect that just as endpapers are more
prone to fail in large Bibles, so too are glued bindings.  Thankfully, that’s not a problem that the GP
has.  This is a sewn Bible; underneath
the headband, the signatures are plain to see. 
This is a Bible that will last you more than six months.

Within, the text block of the GP is a testament to the way
that Chinese printers have stepped up their game in the past 10 years.  The paper is nice and heavy (which is why the
Bible is so heavy), there’s no cockling in the gutter, and I haven’t seen any
page curling either.  The Chinese text
blocks that appeared in the first years of this century were pretty
dreadful.  This one is anything but.  In fact, it’s higher-quality than the most
recent US-print Bible I’ve seen.  That
might be bad news for domestic printers, but it’s good news for Bible geeks.

**VALUE.** Generally, this is a well constructed
Bible, but you’ll pay for the privilege. 
Currently, it retails for $90. 
There’s a sense in which you can’t hope for much better than this.  After all, this is a great big Bible, and
great big Bibles require more materials and more work to manufacture.  However, one of the nearest competitors to
the GP, the Foundation Giant-Print Reference, retails for $62.95 in genuine
leather.  One of my friends owns the
Foundation Bible, and when I compared his to the GP, I couldn’t see much
difference in quality (though there are differences in type, setting, etc.,
that might lead one to prefer the GP).  At
$65 retail, the GP would be a fine value. 
At $90 retail, it strikes me as a little overpriced.



**CONCLUSION.** There’s a sense in which rating the GP as
I rate other Bibles isn’t the most logical thing to do.  This is a Bible that gets rid of everything
else to acquire that pearl of great price, extremely easy-to-read print.  That’s a benefit that my rating system
doesn’t take into account at all because I don’t need a large-print Bible.  However, because I am certainly a creature of
habit, I’ll rate it anyway.  At its size
and high cost, I think the GP deserves a 7/10.
