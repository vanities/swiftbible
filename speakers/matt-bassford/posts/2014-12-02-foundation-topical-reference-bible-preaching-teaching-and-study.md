I have never approached a Bible review period with more
apprehension than I approached the review of the TRB.  As I mentioned earlier, this was not a Bible
I specifically asked for.  In fact, it
was not a Bible I would have asked for. 
The text block of the TRB is basically the text block of Foundation’s
Ultrathin Reference, except with 250 pages added to the front, and I had
already looked at and decided not to buy the Ultrathin Reference.

There were several reasons for this.  I like pretty layouts, and the layout of the
Ultrathin isn’t pretty.  I like
double-column text Bibles with wide columns, but the Ultrathin has robust
center-column references that take a big chunk of column width right out of the
middle.  Most of all, the Ultrathin is
one of the most strangely formatted paragraphed Bibles on the market.  Instead of putting its verse numbers in
superscript, the Ultrathin has them in line with the text, like the old Nelson
ASV I own, so that the text reads like, “As He passed by, He saw a man blind
from birth.  2 And His disciples asked
Him. . .”  For me, that was a definite
no-sale point.



And here, I found myself having to review a Bible with
exactly the same formatting issues.  I
was sure that I was going to end up writing a nasty review of this Bible that
Foundation was kind enough to send me for free. . .

. . . but instead, I really enjoyed using it.  Here’s why:

**PREACHING.** In terms of size, the TRB is right in the
sweet spot for me.  As I observed, it’s
essentially a bulked-up thinline, so at5 ½” x 8 ½” trim size, it’s exactly the
right size to hide a half-sheet of paper, but it’s not so large that I feel
like I need a dolly to cart the thing up to the pulpit.  Both of the sermons I used the TRB to present
were expository (one on Luke 11, one on basically the entire book of Malachi),
so I had the Bible in my hand a great deal, but I never noticed the
weight.  As a representative of the just-a-Bible
category, the TRB is neither too large nor too small for the pulpit.

When I look at the TRB/Ultrathin text block, it’s obvious
that it was laid out by the same designer who was responsible for Foundation’s
Side-Column Reference.  It has the same
typefaces throughout, the same style of cross-references, the same book titles,
and so on.  As I’ve said before, the SCR
is the A-10 Warthog of the Bible world. 
It’s ugly, but boy! is it ever effective.  Much the same thing is true of the TRB.

This begins with those strange-looking inline verse
numbers.  They look like they would be a
stumbling block for reading comprehension (and maybe they will prove to be so
once I use the TRB for a daily reader), but they’re awfully useful in the
pulpit.  One of my biggest gripes about
preaching out of paragraphed Bibles is that the verse numbers are hard to spot,
so I always end up missing my starting verse or my ending verse.  By contrast, the verse numbers of the TRB
aren’t hard to spot at all.  Even in
navigating through nearly four chapters of Scripture, I didn’t miss a
citation.  I like a pretty layout, but
more than that, I like a layout that works, and for preaching, the layout of
the TRB works.

**TEACHING.** During the review period, I was still
teaching our middle-school class, and for whatever reason, I didn’t feel much
need to rise from the table and roam around at the time.  In consequence, the TRB just sat on the
table, where its size was irrelevant.  I
suspect, though, that in a setting where I did more pacing while teaching, I
might have found the TRB a touch heavy. 
After all, 250 pages of the approximately 1400 in the Bible composed the
topical reference that I (dirty secret forthcoming) didn’t use.  As far as I was concerned, the topical
reference section might as well have been filled with blank pages.  If I have a choice between a heavier Bible
(the TRB) and a lighter Bible with exactly the same functionality (the
Ultrathin Reference), I know which one I’m going to pick every time.

My relationship with cross-references is an odd one.  When they aren’t there, I don’t miss them,
but when they are, I use them.  The TRB
has the same robust suite of cross-references as my old friend the SCR—all
95,000 of them—and I found myself glancing at them as I was teaching.

This is only the second center-column reference Bible I’ve
used since I started caring about Bibles. 
The other was a Pitt Minion ESV that I gave away before I had an
opportunity to review it.  Of the two
reference systems, I prefer the Pitt’s. 
The center column of the TRB has essentially the same format as the side
column of the SCR, except that it’s shifted into the center.  There is no way to tell by looking whether a
particular reference belongs to the left column or the right column.  References are equally spaced throughout the
center column.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhRCRJzFi0VXsu8yPxtns2SXNr-4lPz1VrMjFURHSifeMi7i1G40n1UGDrWyjOZ3tYY73bVGHw_PIFVb49qgBhKbiAcl-ZDONLR1d5OnCTWNlaKVSSwbZCVwvNDWMR5ugdyRgtU8ypmYQ/s1600/PTS3.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhRCRJzFi0VXsu8yPxtns2SXNr-4lPz1VrMjFURHSifeMi7i1G40n1UGDrWyjOZ3tYY73bVGHw_PIFVb49qgBhKbiAcl-ZDONLR1d5OnCTWNlaKVSSwbZCVwvNDWMR5ugdyRgtU8ypmYQ/s1600/PTS3.jpg)

By contrast, the Pitt has the beginning of the left-hand
column references at the top of the center column, and the end of the
right-hand column references at the bottom. 
Then, the two sets of references fill in toward each other.  This often results in a blank space in the
middle of the column that separates the two sets of references and makes a
given reference easier to locate.

Without that visual cue, I often had to hunt for references
in the TRB.  It never took me more than a
few seconds to locate the reference I was looking for, but the delay was
noticeable and annoying.  Good design is
never obtrusive.

**STUDY.** Here is where we come to the feature that
lends the TRB its name—those 250 pages and 20,000 topics’ worth of topical references.  At first glance, it’s not immediately
apparent how the topical references differ from the concordance in the
back.  As far as I can tell, the primary
difference is that the verses cited in the Topical Reference section don’t
necessarily have to contain the word under which they are sorted.  For instance, there is a topic for Abortion,
a word which does not appear in the Bible at all, though it is certainly a
subject of great spiritual interest to many. 
Likewise, 1 John 2:1-2 is listed under the heading of Spiritual Warfare,
even though those words do not appear in the passage.

As I said, I didn’t use the Topical References section, nor
the concordance at the other end, for that matter.  I primarily write and preach expository
rather than topical sermons these days, and if I do turn to the study of some
topic, I find that the study aids in Bible software are much more powerful and
capable of much more sophisticated searches. 
In short, I like to read and study from Bibles, but I like to use computers
to research.  Having said that, the TRB’s
topical index looks like a respectable representative of its kind.  Those who prefer a handy reference in their
physical Bibles so they don’t have to sit the desk chair up to do a computer
search (I know I’m not the only preacher who’s lazy enough to think about such
things) would likely get a fair amount of use out of it.

As far as its other characteristics, the print in the TRB is
only 8-point.  My close-in vision is
fine, so it never gave me any trouble.  Those
who need larger-print Bibles for prolonged use, though, would be well advised
to stay away.  Similarly, I usually don’t
care for studying out of paragraphed Bibles, but the same inline verse numbers
that made locating Scriptures in the pulpit so easy also aided me in correct
citations for my sermon texts.  To my
knowledge, I didn’t miss a citation the whole time I used the Bible.

**CONCLUSION.** For a church-building Bible, I could do a
lot worse than the TRB.  I didn’t care
for the reference system, and it might have been a touch on the heavy side for
using in an adult Bible class, but it helped me to correctly cite passages,
which is what I need a preaching Bible to do. 
For this role, I rate it at 4.5/5.
