**OVERVIEW.** I’m a double-column Bible kind of
guy.  Generally, I don’t like
single-column Bibles like the Clarion. 
However, even if I didn’t like reading prose from the Clarion quite as
well as from a double-column text Bible, I still have to acknowledge that it
did respectably in prose and shone in poetry. 
From me, that’s a big deal.

As is generally the case, I used the Clarion as my daily
reader for two weeks.  Here’s how I liked
it:

**SIZE.** To this very day, there are few
experiences that I enjoy so much as opening a big fat science-fiction or
fantasy paperback and losing myself in an imaginary world for a few hours.  In the hand, the Clarion feels like a big fat
science-fiction paperback, except with an edge-lined goatskin cover.  It is a delight to hold and read.

**LAYOUT.** The Clarion came into my hands as the
result of an e-mail exchange with Cambridge’s Chris Wright.  He was gracious enough to review a draft of
the article about print size in Bibles that I posted earlier this year, and at
the end of our correspondence, he offered me the opportunity to review the
Clarion.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhY3ROxY5ArCAQMM5ozzlTV7OmFWz7pAbBz8oMSvu4jVEJQQBhyphenhyphenyx79VpG2jxoX55glkov5Qt2drLeBH2YBzWrYh0kjZQ4UVl4fX51SybOxMIkeznEJHBB55aoFidDDzyA-KiP5IisN-g/s640/R+double+column.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhY3ROxY5ArCAQMM5ozzlTV7OmFWz7pAbBz8oMSvu4jVEJQQBhyphenhyphenyx79VpG2jxoX55glkov5Qt2drLeBH2YBzWrYh0kjZQ4UVl4fX51SybOxMIkeznEJHBB55aoFidDDzyA-KiP5IisN-g/s1600/R+double+column.jpg)



As grateful as I was for the opportunity, I have to admit
that I approached it with some trepidation. 
If there’s anything that I’ve learned in the course of reviewing a
couple of dozen Bibles, it’s that I’m just not a big fan of single-column
Bibles.  Yes, I know, the layout looks
more like the layout of books that we read for fun, but I find the line length
of a double-column Bible to be much easier to navigate.  The Clarion is, of course, a single-column
Bible par excellence, and I was worried that I would have to respond to Chris’s
generosity with a churlish review!

However, that proved not to be necessary.  I didn’t love the Clarion in prose, but I
liked it.  Sometimes, the 65-cpl line
length would nudge me a little bit out of my train of thought, but it never
dragged me out into the real world like the Crossway Legacy did.  Here too, 8 cpl is a big deal.  I don’t think I would prefer to use a Clarion
for a whole-year reader, but I wouldn’t hate it.

The poetry, though, is a whole ‘nother kettle of fish.  Poetry in the Clarion is oh-mazing.  It so happened that when I was reviewing the
Clarion, my reading schedule had led me to the book of Job, and Job in the
Clarion is something else.  I loved
reading it so much that I decided I needed to preach on it, and if a Bible
layout does such a good job of presenting the text that you have to tell
somebody about it, it’s done its job.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjtJj0x96oMXpt52fld4G_qguQ_E8EZZYyppCyEp-5Yzz8agQgNWUDDoBKIJFZXORqsl4PEV9IuxQJeZSNcWPGvRgCXRtBgkamUoXxmASc64sKV1MCvBjMqH7zEYZaRQckM9ohFMC8DdA/s640/R+something.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjtJj0x96oMXpt52fld4G_qguQ_E8EZZYyppCyEp-5Yzz8agQgNWUDDoBKIJFZXORqsl4PEV9IuxQJeZSNcWPGvRgCXRtBgkamUoXxmASc64sKV1MCvBjMqH7zEYZaRQckM9ohFMC8DdA/s1600/R+something.jpg)



The gutter on the Clarion, on the other hand, is okay rather
than oh-mazing.  As I’ve mentioned
before, the Clarion’s a chunky Bible, and the thicker a Bible is, the more
gutter problems it’s going to have.  I
wouldn’t say that the Clarion has a bad gutter, like the Cambridge Standard
Text REB does, but it’s not a great gutter either.  Especially in the middle of the volume, the
text is starting to slide down into it a little bit.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjOwgatc2Zl3gU_vR1NxEz_gO_gypZUHLOtMHzim6TbTMcWF4e_gCyAr_jZdJU3z-0Es-bkTP-NaQaccjJkpFyTwasdAKb92hv45ionVlaYsJNvJXYzE0YfOdPpcYu7dQeCPqA6xRTqDg/s640/R+gutter.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjOwgatc2Zl3gU_vR1NxEz_gO_gypZUHLOtMHzim6TbTMcWF4e_gCyAr_jZdJU3z-0Es-bkTP-NaQaccjJkpFyTwasdAKb92hv45ionVlaYsJNvJXYzE0YfOdPpcYu7dQeCPqA6xRTqDg/s1600/R+gutter.jpg)



By contrast, I have no complaints about Cambridge’s decision
to make the Clarion black-letter.  I wish
my Cambridge NASB Wide-Margin were black-letter, but they don’t make such a
thing.  Black-letter Bibles are easier to
print correctly (sometimes, the red ink looks more like orange or pink, and
even Jongbloed doesn’t get the black text and the red text lined up right all
the time), and the red text in a red-letter Bible is distracting while reading.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhxGCtN0ccs7KPvDJSFWAT0XcRTg2i_54-_bT46ZEc4I6GE4kMfL8uNPibaxfF_mstfZBsZ4F2FQkcvWr7nFr4BAyzUdFbyLjWCByoWA2EAN4kM1ZSKEAybrx62_3HeOQDoD_a4EGgRuA/s640/R+redletter.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhxGCtN0ccs7KPvDJSFWAT0XcRTg2i_54-_bT46ZEc4I6GE4kMfL8uNPibaxfF_mstfZBsZ4F2FQkcvWr7nFr4BAyzUdFbyLjWCByoWA2EAN4kM1ZSKEAybrx62_3HeOQDoD_a4EGgRuA/s1600/R+redletter.jpg)

It also helps the Clarion’s cause as a reader that it is
line-matched.  When dealing with thin,
ghosting-prone Bible paper, this is a big deal. 
I’m not sure that the Clarion would be a good reader at all if it
weren’t line-matched, but line-matching makes the Clarion concept come to life.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhITnWP4FVFdYqZBN1VxmXdhZ5knsbJsm8HvuAFBUaOGZ5qeQhseh6haPJXvYrTQlQEWQ_XT_rfGHoDTdcrCiHYeHBB2VQ-N7NBq3nPGWKQvox_UGHITPj-gEEchvS3jEvLVyHrehuNEA/s640/R+text+size.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhITnWP4FVFdYqZBN1VxmXdhZ5knsbJsm8HvuAFBUaOGZ5qeQhseh6haPJXvYrTQlQEWQ_XT_rfGHoDTdcrCiHYeHBB2VQ-N7NBq3nPGWKQvox_UGHITPj-gEEchvS3jEvLVyHrehuNEA/s1600/R+text+size.jpg)



Finally, the Clarion has text that is larger than the
minimum size I prefer.  8.75-point
Lexicon No. 1 is plenty big enough for me, and it should be big enough for
anyone who doesn’t need reading glasses. 
In fact, the generous leading of the Clarion means that in terms of
readability, it may well compare to Bibles in the 10-point range.

**PRODUCTION
QUALITY.** There are few things that I
like better in a Bible than a Jongbloed-printed text block.  I’m not a deep-pocketed Bible collector, so I
haven’t seen any of the truly wonderful Bibles from days of yore.  In my experience, though, the only Bibles
that compare to Jongbloed in print crispness and quality are the Foundation
Bibles from 1999-2002.  They aren’t
making any of those anymore, so Jongbloed-printed Bibles are the current gold
standard.

The Clarion is a Jongbloed Bible, and you can tell.  The text is so dark and crisp that it reads
even larger than the font size and leading would suggest it should.  The laser-like serifs in the small print of
the marginal references are things of beauty. 
In my opinion, the difference in print quality is the single biggest selling
point for premium-printed Bibles.

As for the paper in the Clarion, well. . .  All Bible design is the product of
compromise, and in the case of the Clarion, compromise meant that paper
thickness and opacity was going to have to suffer.  Otherwise, the Bible was going to be two
inches thick.  At least the paper is a
nice cream color, which isn’t so hard on the eyes as bright white.

[![](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEir7CVHzX7Z4HFPEEjAw64BsXHr7SPh-8WNoexWnmgjqBcs2AeBFhsqQtTyq8ZMJy_PIMAcxfexsUqMzy3EJ59v_9BYwDIyeoKwc2hCD36iX4LXc899KB9MBUGQbpHd3eXA8GWuVG0OmQ/s640/R+paper.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEir7CVHzX7Z4HFPEEjAw64BsXHr7SPh-8WNoexWnmgjqBcs2AeBFhsqQtTyq8ZMJy_PIMAcxfexsUqMzy3EJ59v_9BYwDIyeoKwc2hCD36iX4LXc899KB9MBUGQbpHd3eXA8GWuVG0OmQ/s1600/R+paper.jpg)



**CONCLUSION.** When I began my trial period with the
Clarion, I was a diehard fan of double-column Bibles for reading.  Now that I’ve finished with it, I’m still a
double-column fan, but my convictions been shaken a little bit.  I don’t think that the Clarion reads quite as
well as a double-column Bible in prose, but it doesn’t give me problems
either.  The poetry in the Clarion,
though, is magnificent.  If you’re on the
fence between single-column and double-column, and you can afford it, you
should probably go with the Clarion.  I
give it an 8/10 as a reader.

Overall, the Clarion notches a 24.5/30.  That’s probably about as good as a
narrow-margin, single-column Bible is going to get from me at this point.
