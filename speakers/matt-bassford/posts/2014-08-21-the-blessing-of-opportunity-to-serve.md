*Gimel. Deal bountifully with Your servant,*
*That I may live and keep Your word.*
-- Psalms 119:17 (NASB)

This was part of my Bible reading for the morning, and I was struck by it.  The verse has a three-part structure.  The first part is an appeal for God to do something--to bless His servant.  The second and final thirds spell out the consequences of this action.  If God blesses the psalmist, this will allow him to a) live and b) keep God's word.

There's not much unusual about the first of these results.  We commonly pray for life and health today.  The second, though, is fascinating.  The psalmist is so determined to keep the word that he regards the opportunity for obedience as a blessing.

Too often, our attitude is the opposite.  We regard opportunities for obedience as an obligation.  When it's Sunday morning, we say, "Well, I guess I'd better roll out of bed and go to church."  When the church has a work day, we say, "Well, I guess I'd better show up."  When we learn of a brother or sister in need, we say, "Well, I guess I'd better open the checkbook."

This kind of grudging submission reflects a heart problem.  It shows that we regard our lives as belonging to ourselves, so that when we do something for God, we're actually doing Him a favor.  God is like that annoying cousin who always wants to borrow your pickup truck for something.  You help him, 'cause he's family, but you really wish he'd show some decency and leave you alone for once!

Of course, our lives do not actually belong to us.  They belong to the One who paid in blood for them.  Christ does not compel us by putting a spiritual gun to our heads and driving us to obedience.  He compels us by the awesome example of His love.  If Christ loved me so much and did so much for me, how can I possibly refuse to do anything for Him?  How can I possibly have a bad attitude about giving a little of myself, when He has given me everything?

When we see our lives in this light, we regard opportunities for obedience as actual opportunities.  When God grants us a door for service, we should be delighted to see it, and, indeed, eagerly hopeful for more such blessings.  Yes, there are always other things we could be doing with our time or our money, but none of those things are an expression of our love for Jesus.  We can never pay back the debt we owe Him, but we can show Him, at least today, that He did not die in vain.
