Most of us, I think, have thought about the time of our
deaths and wondered whether we would want to know that time beforehand.  On the one hand, it might change the way we
live.  If I knew I was going to die in a
car wreck next week, I’d probably spend that week very differently than if I
knew I was going to pass away peacefully at the age of 95.  On the other hand, particularly as that time
of death drew closer, the fear of death might become downright debilitating.

Of course, it’s not given to us to know how much longer we
have on earth, but our Lord did know. 
The Bible doesn’t tell us whether He had that knowledge from the moment
of His birth, but it’s certainly true that throughout His ministry, Jesus knew
all the details about how He was going to die. 
Not surprisingly, His emotions on the subject were rather mixed.  Before the Lord’s Supper this morning, then,
let’s spend some time studying how Jesus felt as the hour approached.



First, Jesus experienced **DISTRESS** at the thought of His crucifixion.  This is well captured in John 12:27.  This takes place only a few days before His
betrayal and arrest, and it clearly reveals His inward torment at the thought
of what was about to happen.

Here we see how different Jesus’ experience was from what
our experience would be even if we knew we were about to die.  We are powerless to prevent our own deaths,
but at the same time, neither has God specifically planned for us to die, to
the point where the plan will fail if we survive.

In Jesus’ case, both of those things are reversed.  He could have escaped the cross.  He could have summoned 12 legions of angels
to defend Him while He was being arrested, or He could have skipped town before
the high priests’ goon squad showed up. 
Either one would have worked.

However, Jesus knew that His whole ministry, and indeed His
whole life, had been directed toward His atoning death on the cross.  Jesus was human.  He wasn’t any happier about hanging around to
be humiliated, beaten, tortured, and killed than we would have been.  However, acting to save His own life was the
very last thing He could do, because He would wreck God’s eternal purpose if He
did.  What a bitter thing for Him to
face!  The next time we get to feeling
sorry for ourselves because what God is asking us to do is too, too hard, let’s
remember Jesus.

Second, as great as Jesus’ distress was, His **DETERMINATION** was even greater.  This shows up most clearly a little earlier,
in Luke 13:31-33.  The awe-inspiring
thing about Jesus and His death is that He didn’t stand there passively and
wait to be killed.  Instead, He actively
sought His own death out.  He went to the
right city at the right time of year and did the right things to make the right
people mad enough to have Him killed then and there.

This, friends, is something I can’t fathom doing.  I’m already older than Jesus was then, but I
can’t imagine calculatingly arranging my own death like that.  What’s more, Jesus knew a whole lot more about
death than I do.  When He was in heaven,
He had already seen the minutest detail of thousands of people dying by
crucifixion.  He knew what they thought.  He knew what they felt.  For Him to set His face toward that, knowing
precisely what He was going to undergo, is astonishing.  In fact, this display of willpower makes
Jesus’ sinlessness make more sense.  If
you’ve got enough self-control to spend years driving yourself toward the
cross, you can control whatever else you want to as well.

Finally, of course, Jesus was motivated by **LOVE.**Consider John 15:13-14.  This
is a beautiful thought even outside of a religious context.  Imagine the soldier who throws himself on a
grenade to save the lives of his squadmates. 
Even if the guy never did anything in his life before to merit our
attention, that’s the kind of thing that we respect, honor, and remember.

It’s remarkable enough, then, that Jesus was willing to die
for His friends.  What’s truly
extraordinary, though, is that He was willing to define human beings as His
friends.  The powerful people alive in
the world today aren’t my friends.  They
don’t know me, they don’t have any interest in associating with me, and they
certainly aren’t going to die for me.

Jesus, though, was and is incomprehensibly more than some
world leader or billionaire.  He is
God.  God should have no interest in
grasshoppers like us, and yet Jesus knows us, longs to associate with us, and
was willing to die for us, because He loves us, because we’re His friends.
