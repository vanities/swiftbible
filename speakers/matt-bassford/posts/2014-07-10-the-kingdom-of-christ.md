*For He rescued us from
the domain of darkness, and transferred us to the kingdom of His beloved Son. .
.*

-- Colossians 1:13 (NASB)

In the Bible, there are several words that we struggle to
understand, not because the words themselves are particularly difficult, but
because they have a different meaning in Scripture than in our everyday
speech.  One such word is “kingdom”.  In the world today, we think of “kingdom” in
an institutional sense, like the kingdom of England or the kingdom of
France.  Such a kingdom is greater than
its king, and it continues to exist even in the midst of a civil war, when
there are multiple claimants to the throne.



This is not the case with the kingdom of God.  Biblically speaking, this kingdom does not
consist of an institution, but of the scope of the personal authority of God
and (in these last days) of His Son.  The
kingdom of England can continue even without a king, but the kingdom of heaven
has no separate existence apart from its ruler. 
All of creation is part of this heavenly dominion, for Christ created
it, controls it, and daily upholds it by the word of His power.

Similarly, the kingdom of Christ is apparent in the hearts
of those who have pledged their loyalty to Him. 
By this pledge, we come under His personal authority.  He protects us from our enemies, but in turn,
we are personally responsible for honoring and serving Him.  An institution that purports to be under His
dominion but does not obey Him is not part of His kingdom at all.  If we belong to His kingdom, then, we will:

* Do all that He requires of us,
* Abstain from all that He does not permit, and
* Consider what He permits to determine what best
  serves His purposes.



Not everything that Christ allows us to do is useful in advancing
His kingdom.  It is entirely Scriptural
to assemble on the first day of the week at 3 o’clock in the morning.  However, in the absence of exceptional
circumstances (such as those of Acts 20:7-12), it is not prudent.  We are responsible, then, for using our
liberty in Christ with prudence.  We
should do that which promotes His dominion but avoid that which is useless or
worse at glorifying Him.

Our King does not condemn bad judgment (for He is mindful of
our frame), but He will severely punish disobedience.  Those who marvel that a loving God would cast
sinners into hell do not understand the nature of the kingdom.  Sin is not a violation of a bureaucratic IRS
regulation.  We can’t sin and say to
Christ, “It’s nothing personal,” because rebellion against the personal
authority of the King *is* personal.  It is betrayal.  It is treason, and this treason is especially
rank when it comes from those who claim to be His servants.

Christ cannot allow such an insult to His person to
stand.  It would irreparably damage the
dignity of His kingdom.  A king who
allows rebels to defy Him is no king at all. 
Conversely, the severity of the king’s punishments reflects the
greatness of the king.  When the enemies
of Christ are utterly ruined, even that ruin will proclaim His glory.

It does not lie in us, then, to determine whether we will
glorify the King or not.  Willingly or
unwillingly, every knee will bow.  We can
only choose whether we will receive the rewards of His favor or the just
deserts of our failure.
