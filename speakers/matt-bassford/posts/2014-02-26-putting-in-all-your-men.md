*So he answered, "Do not fear, for those who are with us are more than those who are with them." Then Elisha prayed and said, "O LORD, I pray, open his eyes that he may see." And the LORD opened the servant's eyes and he saw; and behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha.*
-- 2 Kings 6:16-17 (NASB)

In April 1863, when General Hooker took command of the Army of the Potomac before the battle of Chancellorsville, President Lincoln advised him, “In your next fight, put in all your men.”  This seems like a strange thing for a civilian politician to say to a military professional.  If you were going to fight a battle, surely you’d employ all your men in an attempt to achieve victory, wouldn’t you?

However, that had not been the habit of Hooker’s predecessors in command.  Generals McClellan and Burnside had failed in different ways, but one thing was true in all the battles they fought.  In every one, they allowed the commander of the Army of Northern Virginia, General Lee, to oppose a portion of their army with the entirety of his.  They had the numerical superiority necessary to succeed, but because they didn’t use it, they either lost outright or won a lesser victory than they should have.

Sadly, Hooker didn’t listen either.  He lost the battle of Chancellorsville for exactly the same reason.  He didn’t put in all his men.

The same holds true for us.  As the prophet Elisha’s words make clear, on the battlefields of the spirit, we have the numerical advantage.  The resources available to us are far more than we need to sweep away the forces of the devil.  For our own equipment, we have the word of God.  We have other Christians we can turn to for help.  Most of all, we have God and His infinite power, which He has promised to employ for our salvation whenever we ask for it.

And yet, all of us encounter spiritual defeat.  Tragically, far too many brethren meet with final spiritual defeat.  They fall away from the Lord and lose their souls.  How can this be?  How can there be such a mismatch between our magnificent resources and our miserable outcomes?

Simple.  We don’t study the Bible.  We don’t assemble with and associate with the saints.  Most of all, we don’t pray.  In short, we don’t put in all of our men.  Just like the Union generals, we allow Satan to hit us with everything at his disposal while we don’t fight back with everything at ours.

If we want to go to heaven and take as many others with us as possible, this has to stop.  As we prepare for spiritual combat today, let’s take advantage of our spiritual blessings in the heavenly places.  Let’s put in all our men.
