Once again, the calendar has flipped on all of us.  I can remember that back when I was a kid,
1987 seemed to last forever, but these days, part of me is still expecting it
to be March 2015.

Regardless of whether we are ready for it or not, though,
2016 is upon us, and with it comes the season for New Year’s resolutions.  As a rule, I approve of New Year’s
resolutions.  Rarely do people resolve to
give themselves over to laziness and vice, so just about every resolution out
there involves self-discipline and self-control, at least to some extent.  Self-control is like a muscle; the more we
exercise it, the stronger it gets.  As a
result, I think there is some spiritual value even to resolutions to lose
weight, exercise more, and so on.

However, I think it’s even better if we use that
self-discipline on a goal that is explicitly spiritual.  As Paul says in 1 Timothy 4, bodily
discipline has little profit, but godliness is profitable for all things.  If you’re going to sink your effort into
something, you’ll get the greatest return from achieving a spiritual goal.  With this in mind, let’s ponder 11
resolutions for a godlier New Year.



**Ourselves**

The first set of resolutions that we’re going to consider
this morning is directed toward improving ourselves.  For many, this should start with **GIVING UP SECRET SIN.** Consider what David says about hidden sin
in Psalm 32:3-5.  There are many, many
sins that people, especially Christians, try to keep secret.  This could be anything from porn use to
alcoholism.  Usually, we hide sin because
of shame.  We fear the consequences of
anybody else finding out what we’re up to.

However, as David points out, the most serious consequences
of secret sin are the ones we can’t duck. 
No matter what we do, we can’t hide from God, and secret sin destroys
our relationship with Him.  What’s more,
we can’t hide from the corrupting effect of sin either.  Sin is sin because sin is evil, and whatever
sin we practice makes us less like Jesus and more like the devil.

If we truly want to get rid of secret sin in our lives, the
best way to do that is to expose it.  We
need to own it to God and ask His help, and we also need to confess our sins to
one another.  There is great power in
acknowledging our sin to our spouse or a trusted brother or sister.  Sin flourishes in the darkness, but only
righteousness can thrive in the light.

Second, let’s resolve to **READ MORE.** Once again, the
Psalms outline the benefits, this time in Psalm 119:103-104.  There’s an old saying that says, “Garbage in,
garbage out,” and I think there’s a lot of truth to that.  If we spend all our time listening to secular
music and watching trashy TV shows, why are we surprised when we have trouble
making spiritual progress?

The reality is that time spent with God is the engine of
spiritual growth.  The time we spend in
the assembly simply isn’t enough to get the job done.  We need to spend time with God daily, and
that time should begin with reading His word by ourselves.

This does not require a vast amount of time.  On my blog recently, I posted a reading plan
for getting through the whole Bible in 90 days, and I admit that it takes a
pretty big commitment to do that. 
However, most Bible-reading plans don’t require you to plow through 15
chapters at a time.  In fact, if we want
to read through the New Testament in a year, all we have to do is read one
chapter a day.  That’s five minutes or
less, even for slow readers.  We can do
it with our morning coffee.  Don’t open
Facebook; open the word of God instead. 
If you spill coffee on your Bible, I regard that as an honorable battle
scar!  This really is a small commitment
that can replace something that’s not very important anyway, but it will do no
less than transform our spiritual lives.

Of course, the counterpart to reading more is **PRAYING MORE.** Look at the prayer pattern described in
Psalm 55:16-17.  When we read the word,
we’re listening to God, but He wants us to talk to Him too.  I think there are a couple of ways that we
can usefully make a habit of prayer.  The
first is to combine it with our morning Scripture reading.  After we’ve gotten through that single
chapter of the New Testament, we can bow quietly to address God about what’s on
our minds.  We can praise Him.  We can thank Him for all He does for us.  We can ask Him for physical and spiritual
help for ourselves.  We can intercede on
behalf of others.  The list goes on and
on.

Likewise, I think it’s valuable to train ourselves to pray
whenever we encounter something that is worthy of prayer.  Whenever something good happens in our day,
we should pause to be briefly thankful. 
Whenever we run into a problem, we should take a moment to turn it over
to the Father.  When we’re in serious
trouble, we should return repeatedly to the courts of heaven.  Like reading, none of this takes a lot of
time, but it too offers the promise of spiritual transformation.

Finally, we will benefit greatly from resolving to **BE THANKFUL** for what God has done for
us.  Look at how eloquently the psalmist
puts this in Psalm 34:8-10.  We recently
saw how thankfulness should appear in our prayers.  Now, let’s consider how it should appear in
our lives.

I don’t know about the rest of you, but it’s easy for me to
fall into the trap of feeling entitled and angry.  I start thinking about all the things that I
deserve and how I want to be treated, and all the ways people let me down by
not living up to my expectations, and pretty soon I get to feeling sorry for
myself and bitter and resentful about everybody else!  Once we open the door for those evil
emotions, they can consume our lives so that we become nothing but bitter,
angry people.

Not only is that attitude sinful, but it’s also unfair.  Ultimately, whether we realize it or not, that
sense of grievance is directed against God, and God hasn’t done anything to
deserve it.  In fact, the opposite is
true.  Just consider, friends.  Rather than spending the new year in our
warm, dry homes, we could be spending the new year in a Syrian refugee
camp.  When we focus on our complaints
rather than acknowledging all the physical and spiritual blessings that God has
piled on us, we’re being ungrateful to Him. 
Let’s be filled with appreciation and thankfulness instead.

In fact, let’s start that this morning as we look toward the
Lord’s Supper.  I don’t want to be in a
Syrian refugee camp, but there are worse places to be than that, and hell is
one of them.  That, of course, is where
every one of us was headed, not because of the sin of some ancestor of ours,
but because of our own sin.  We deserved
to die, but Jesus died instead.  Even if
that were the only thing that God ever gives us (which it isn’t), we should be
thankful for that for the rest of our days and for eternity.  Let’s remember this extraordinary sacrifice
as we partake.

**The Church**

In the second portion of our time together, we’re going to
ask what we can do to better fulfill our obligations toward the church.  Most basically, we can take a step in the
right direction by **ASSOCIATING
MORE.** In this regard, let’s consider
the thumbnail sketch of the very early church in Acts 2:46.  As this text makes clear, the time that the
Jerusalem Christians spent with each other wasn’t limited to their assemblies
on the temple grounds.  Instead, they
associated with each other daily.  The
results of this are evident in the way Luke describes the church.  They’re “of one mind”.  They’re “together”.  They’re filled “with gladness and sincerity
of heart.”

Our congregation isn’t a social club, but our association
with one another is a matter of great spiritual importance.  If we can improve in this area anywhere in
our lives, we should.  If we come to
Sunday worship once or twice a month, let’s work on being here for worship
every week.  If we are here for worship
all the time, let’s start coming to Sunday Bible class.  If we’re doing that, let’s add
Wednesday-night Bible class to our schedules. 
If we’re here every time the doors are opened, let’s work on being
present at all the singings and potlucks and other things that happen outside
of the assembly.  These things connect us
to one another and to God.  Let’s make
that connection strong.

Second, we should look to **HELP MORE** with the work of the church.  Here, let’s ponder Paul’s words in Romans
12:4-6.  Here, he compares the church to
the human body.  It has many members with
different functions, but they all work together toward a common goal.  It follows, then, that just as our bodies are
less effective unless every body part is healthy and functioning, the church
will be less effective if any of us aren’t doing our part.

This is no small thing. 
Ever had a pulled muscle or a sprained ankle or something like
that?  It’s funny that you don’t realize
how much you use all your muscles until one of them isn’t working.  You wouldn’t think that a strained muscle in
your back would have much to do with walking, but then it hurts with every
step!  That’s how it is with the church
too.  Our spiritual lives aren’t our
own.  If we aren’t doing our share of the
work, we’re holding back the whole congregation.

The simplest way to solve the problem is to find something
to do and do it.  We don’t turn away
willing men from serving in the assembly. 
There’s always room for somebody who wants to teach a class downstairs.  People who want to help prepare the Lord’s
Supper, visit shut-ins, or even show up for a work day—all of them are welcome
to help out!  There’s room for all of us
to serve.  Let’s be sure, then, that
we’re taking advantage of the opportunities we have.

Third, let’s make an effort to **GIVE MORE.** Paul addresses
this subject in 2 Corinthians 9:6-7. 
There are a couple of points worth exploring in his words here.  First, he tells us that we are supposed to be
purposeful givers.  There’s an old saying
in the personal-finance world that goes, “Pay yourself first.”  In other words, make sure you’re saving money
before you start spending money.  I think
there’s wisdom in that, but I think there would be even more wisdom in saying,
“Pay the Lord first.”  It’s not our
retirement accounts and emergency funds that need to be at the top of our
budget.  Instead, giving to the Lord’s
work needs to be there.  If He’s truly
the most important thing in our lives, we have to plan to make Him the most
important thing in our finances.

Second, note that Paul couches this not in terms of need,
but in terms of generosity and cheerfulness. 
As I’ve said before, I’m delighted that for the past year or so, we’ve
been bringing in more than enough money than we need to meet our obligations.  However, we shouldn’t be looking at that
budget number on the board up front as a ceiling.  We should be thinking of it as a floor.  There are all kinds of worthy preachers out
there who need our support, and the more we bring in, the more of them we can
help.  What matters is not that we reach
some kind of congregational target.  What
matters is that each one of us is giving what we know is right.

Finally, we can all work on **ENCOURAGING MORE.** Consider
the wise words of Solomon in Ecclesiastes 4:9-10.  The reality is that from time to time, just
about all of us need a hand up spiritually. 
This is true of every Christian, regardless of spiritual maturity
level.  An elder friend of mine recently
asked me to pray that he might continue to be faithful to the Lord.  If we get that helping hand, we can get up
and keep going.  If we don’t, we might be
in serious trouble.

Every one of us, then, needs to be willing to offer help to
brethren who need it.  This is
particularly important in a congregation that doesn’t have elders.  The Scripture tells us that elders are
supposed to be the shepherds of the sheep, and in the absence of shepherds,
it’s awfully easy for the sheep here to wander off or get left behind.  Our lack of elders is the most serious
problem we have as a congregation, no doubt about it, and the only real
solution to it is to appoint qualified men to the office.  Until that time comes, though, every one of
the sheep here needs to be vigilant.  We
need to do our best to be looking out for one another, to respond with a card
or a call or a text when somebody seems to be in spiritual trouble.

**The World**

Finally, let’s look at some resolutions that will improve
our conduct with respect to the outside world. 
First, let’s resolve to **PRIORITIZE.** Jesus Himself gives us a crucial reminder
of what our priorities should be in Matthew 6:33-34.  I suspect that this has been a problem since
the very beginning of the church, but it’s certainly a challenge for Christians
today.  We live in a very busy, high-achievement,
high-stress world, and it’s easy for us to internalize the world’s values and
arrange our lives according to those values rather than the Lord’s values.  Part of the problem is that many of these
worldly values are good things in and of themselves.  It’s good for us to work hard at our
jobs.  It’s good for us to want to
provide every opportunity for our children.

However, if we want to succeed in those things to the extent
that the world does, the only way we can do that is by losing our souls over
it.  Think about it.  People in the world don’t have God in their lives.  They can devote 100 percent of their time to
their work, their kids, and similarly important goals.  They can stay late at the office on Wednesday
night.  They can drive little Riley
around to umpteen million sports and activities.  If we want to match them, we can only do it
by devoting 100 percent of our lives to those things, and that means God gets
crowded out.

If that’s not the outcome we want for ourselves, we’re going
to have to consciously make different choices than the world does.  We have to be willing not to invest our whole
lives in our jobs.  We have to be willing
not to provide every advancement opportunity for our children.  Only then will we have the room in our lives
to seek the kingdom first.

Second, we ought to resolve to **LET OUR LIGHT SHINE.** Once
again, let’s look at the Sermon on the Mount here, this time in Matthew
5:14-16.  This is an extremely rich
concept, and it has a great many aspects to it. 
Letting our light shine means that we approach each day with a different
attitude than the world does.  Rather
than being whiny and self-centered, we are contented and gracious.  It means that rather than snapping at the
checkout clerk because we’ve been in line for half an hour, we smile and thank
her.  It means that we are quick to help
anybody who needs help, whether they need money or a strong back to help them
move in.  Basically, it means that we
live like nobody in the world does, because we have a Master to follow and they
don’t.

Jesus tells us that if we let our light shine like this,
others will see it and glorify God.  The
first part is certainly true.  Nothing
stands out in our increasingly dark world like a genuine, committed disciple of
Jesus.  The second is true too.  Even people who don’t appreciate the Bible
will appreciate courtesy, kindness, and help in time of need.  They might not follow God because of us, but
they will acknowledge that we do, and through us, they’ll learn more about Him.

Finally, we should resolve this year to do a better job of **SHARING THE GOSPEL.** This is one of the reasons why we are
here, as Peter describes in 1 Peter 2:9. 
There is probably nothing else I’ve said in the sermon that generates as
much angst as this one point.  I think
that at least part of the problem is that we overthink evangelism.  It reminds me of back when I was young,
single, and miserable.  If there was some
girl I was interested in, suddenly every word, every look, every chance
encounter was freighted with meaning.  It
was a big deal to say three words to her after class.  Asking her out was some dramatic,
life-altering event that either offered dramatic bliss or terrible
tragedy.  Now, of course, I look back and
realize that my biggest problem was that I couldn’t get over myself.  It was just dinner and a movie!

Similarly, I think that even those of us who are old enough
to know better make way more out of inviting somebody to church or asking them
if they want to study the Bible than we should. 
It’s a simple request.  Nobody’s
going to set our house on fire because we asked.  If they get mad and stomp off, they probably
weren’t a friend we were going to be able to keep long-term anyway.  Let’s not worry so much, then, about all the
world-altering stuff.  Let’s ask and see
what happens.
