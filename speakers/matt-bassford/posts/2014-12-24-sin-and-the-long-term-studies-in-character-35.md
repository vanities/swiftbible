*And Laban said,
"Good!  Let it be as you have
said!" But he removed that day the male goats that were speckled and
spotted, all the female goats that were speckled and spotted, every one that
had some white in it, and all the brown ones among the lambs, and gave them
into the hand of his sons.*

--Genesis 30:34-35

Despite all the turmoil that his unwanted wife Leah has
brought into his life, Jacob remains with his uncle Laban.  Throughout this time, even though he has
married both Leah and Rachel by now, he continues to work for Laban for
nothing.  Finally, when Jacob decides
it’s time to return to Palestine, he asks Laban for a share of the prosperity
that God has given him during Jacob’s sojourn. 
All he wants are the striped, speckled, and spotted animals from Laban’s
flocks.



Decades have passed, but Laban remains the same.  He’s still so crooked that he has to scratch
his back with a corkscrew.  He agrees to
Jacob’s terms, then removes all the livestock that would go to Jacob and sends
them away to be pastured by his sons. 
However, because God is with Jacob, Jacob is able to use miraculous
means to increase the striped, speckled, and spotted in the flocks anyway, so
that in the end he prospers and Laban is diminished.

Even in more ordinary circumstances, even when God does not
directly intervene in the affairs of men, dishonest men often see the same
outcome.  The con man may be able to use
his smooth tongue to defraud a few, but sooner or later, he stumbles and the
law catches up with him.  The dishonest
businessman may be able to secure a short-term advantage by lying and cheating
his suppliers and customers, but sooner or later, he finds himself in a
situation where no one will do business with him.  Ironically enough, men who try to get ahead
through deceit ultimately find themselves worse off than if they had relied on
the truth instead.

This points us to an important truth not just about honesty,
but about righteousness generally.  It is
often true that sin is the most appealing choice in the short term.  When Satan tempts us, he does so by
presenting an quick, easy path to something we think we want.  Commit adultery, and fulfill the desires of
your flesh!  Get drunk, and forget your
troubles!  Lie, and avoid the
consequences of your actions!  Sin is
instant gratification at its finest, and when we embrace sin, there is usually
a sense in which we get what we want, at least for the short term.

Of course, in the long term, the bill comes due.  Our spouse finds out we’ve been cheating, and
our family blows up.  We become
alcoholics, which makes all our problems worse. 
Our lies get uncovered after we become entangled in our own web of
deceit.  God may well nudge the process
along, but even without His intervention, we can be sure that our sin will find
us out.  This is one of the most
important things we can remember when we are tempted:  we can buy now, but we will surely pay later.
