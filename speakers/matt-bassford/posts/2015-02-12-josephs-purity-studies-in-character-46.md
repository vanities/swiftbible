*So it was, as she
spoke to Joseph day by day, that he did not heed her, to lie with her or to be
with her.*

--Genesis 39:10 (NKJV)

Poor Joseph was certainly not among the Bible characters who
had the easiest road to success.  He has
already had to handle the enormous trauma of being sold into slavery by his own
brothers.  Since that time, he’s worked
hard to make a new life for himself in the service of his master Potiphar.  However, Satan places a new stumbling block
in Joseph’s path in the form of Potiphar’s lustful wife.  Not once but continually, she attempts to
induce him to lie with her.



From Joseph’s point of view, there were several advantages
to giving in to her advances.  At this
point, he was still a young man, without any lawful outlet for all of the
passions of youth, so she offered him sexual fulfillment.  Second, by forming an alliance with the
master’s wife, he could ensure his place in the household, as long as he kept
the adultery discreet.  Most young men in
Joseph’s position, without family or godly friends, would surely have
succumbed.

Joseph, however, repeatedly rebuffs her, and he does so on
the basis of principle.  First, he
correctly recognizes that he would betray his master by beginning an affair
with his wife.  Potiphar had done
everything in his power to bless Joseph, and Joseph’s own sense of justice
demanded that he not return evil to the man who had done good to him.  Second, though, Joseph recognized that
adultery with Potiphar’s wife would be an offense against God.  Even though Joseph had been carried away from
his father’s house at an early age, he still remembered enough of the teachings
of the Lord to recognize that what his master’s wife wanted was wrong.

Of course, immorality hasn’t become extinct in the 4000
years since Joseph’s day.  Young or old,
married or unmarried, all of us can be tempted to break the laws that God has
laid down about sexual relations.  In a
culture as sexualized and selfish as ours is, the so-called advantages of this
kind of evil are spelled out in everything from the TV shows we watch to the
worldly friends we associate with. 
Illicit sex is supposedly glamorous and fun, and all of the soap operas
and Top 40 songs gloss over the heartbreak that real-life fornication and
adultery bring.

When we are faced with these temptations, we need to learn
from the example of Joseph.  Like him, we
should be restrained by our sense of justice toward others.  We owe it to our spouses or our future
spouses to be sexually pure and to the members of our church to uphold our
shared reputation through moral behavior. 
More than that, we owe purity to God. 
He has given us so much, and we have given him our promise to be
righteous. When we sin sexually, we betray Him and incur His wrath.  Like Joseph, we must recognize that as
tempting as immorality may appear, it is never worth the price.
