This morning's reflections on the 90-day Caxton read-through: It seems like today's reading was pretty much the Songs of Ascents--the psalms pilgrims would sing on their way to Jerusalem to celebrate the Passover. Through happy chance, we're reading them at about the same time as people thousands of years ago would have been singing them.
This morning, I was particularly struck by the image of pilgrims singing these psalms in the year Jesus was crucified. Over and over again, the Songs of Ascents emphasize God's protection of His people, yet they are climbing toward a Jerusalem filled with Roman occupiers. How fervently they must have longed to see the deliverance of Israel!
This was truly an extraordinary time in history, and not only because of the extraordinary nature of Jesus. The more I read and study, the more its characters and events seem to leap off the page. The cast includes insurrectionists, cynical power brokers, smug religious elites, oppressed commoners, despised collaborators, and Roman officials, all struggling to achieve goals that other groups may or may not share.
The national mood could not be more volatile. In another couple of generations, the whole system will collapse in fire and blood. Everyone has the sense that Something Is About To Happen.
Something does. It is completely unexpected, fulfills the goals of no one (or no one human, at least), yet accomplishes nothing less than transforming human existence forever. As the climbing pilgrims sing of deliverance, the deliverance that is coming, and coming soon, is more profound than any of them could have imagined.
