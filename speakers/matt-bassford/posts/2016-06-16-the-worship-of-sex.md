Yesterday, my friend Steve Wolfgang steered me to [an article](http://www.nationalreview.com/article/436602/its-dangerous-believe-religious-freedom-sexual-revolution) that put together some things that I’ve been struggling toward for a long
time.  The article, written by Mary
Eberstadt for *The National Review*,
argues that the recent culture wars are the product of the rise of a new
religion, one in which sexual autonomy is the highest good.

I think that insight is basically correct.  However, I want to elaborate on it myself,
both because I have some thoughts of my own and because the original article is
written in highfalutin’ *National Review*-ese
that some have found hard to understand.



**Two Approaches to
Different Views**

This idea, that sex has assumed religious significance on
the left, explains a great deal.  For one
thing, it answers the question, “Why do these people care so much?”  I’ve run into this personally on various
college-sports fanboards I belong to, where from time to time social issues are
discussed.  I’m not shy about expressing
my opinions in such venues (always reasonably and politely, of course; I’m
hardly a troll), but I’ve been taken aback by the fervor with which my views
have been contradicted.

There are two ways that we can respond to perspectives that
are different from our own.  The first is
to disagree with the perspective while still acknowledging that those who hold
it are sincere people of good will.  This
is the norm on which democratic pluralism is based.  Both sides argue, but at the end of the day,
each side will agree that the other has a right to exist.

The second path is very different.  Those who take this path both disagree with
the differing opinion and deny the good faith of those who hold it.  The people on the other side aren’t merely
wrong.  They are also evil, bad, morally
deficient.  All good people must agree
with me; if they don’t, it proves that they aren’t actually good.

**Intolerance to the
Left**

The latter is the sound I’ve heard in the culture wars of
late, not coming from the religious right, but from the supposedly irreligious
left.  With few exceptions, the rhetoric
of conservatives has been generally temperate. 
When it is not, it’s as likely to come under attack from other
conservatives as liberals.  Just the
other day, a preacher friend of mine escorted a man out of the church building
because the man wouldn’t refrain from using “the q-word”.  Most Christians don’t believe that the
secular-liberal viewpoint is so evil that it must be eradicated.

The same isn’t true on the other side.  Consider, for instance, [this post](http://thinkprogress.org/lgbt/2016/06/14/3788174/we-cannot-weep-together/) written
in response to the Orlando shootings. 
There have been a few wingnuts (who again have provoked widespread
condemnation), but generally, the conservative response to the carnage early
Sunday morning has been one of horror and sympathy.   Russell Moore, a Southern-Baptist bigwig, took
the opportunity to ask whether people on both sides of the cultural/religious
divide could come together to grieve as a nation.

The author “We Cannot Weep Together” forcefully denies that
this is possible.  He writes of conservatives
who express sympathy while still holding to traditional norms, “Sympathy, then,
serves as their weapon to continue their role as abusers.”  In other words, when I mourn the death of 50
people created in the image of God, yet still insist that 1 Corinthians 6:9-11
is true, I’m being abusive (read: 
evil).  In the author’s eyes,
there is literally no way for me to express the view that the practice of
homosexuality is wrong without behaving in an evil and abusive way.  He spends several paragraphs describing
Moore’s courtesy and consideration for him personally, but as long as Moore
insists on Biblical morality, nope, it’s not good enough.

I’ve seen the same thing from others online.  My conservative religious views make me a bad
person, end of story.  Any opinion that
differs from the secular-liberal consensus is necessarily illegitimate.  “I don’t want to see you here, and I’m even a
little bit sorry that you’re a Mizzou football fan.”  Bakers and photographers need to participate
in gay weddings or be sued out of existence; schools need to guide transgender
students through their transition without informing conservative parents.

Those who trumpet the values of sexual tolerance are, when
it comes to religious liberty, the most intolerant group in American
society.  They will acknowledge
(grudgingly?  for now?) the right of
people to believe and teach what they want in homes and churches, but they seek
to drive conservative views of sexuality out of the public sphere
entirely.  They want to see
Bible-believing Christians treated like racists.  You can think that if you want, but if you
talk about it openly, you’ll get fired or otherwise punished, and everybody
else is going to treat you like a pariah.

**The Religion of
Sexual Autonomy**

Convictions of this strength can only be described as
religious, and they represent the most dangerous kind of religion of all:  the kind that refuses to acknowledge the
right of others to be wrong.  The goal
that it seeks is sexual freedom and sexual self-expression at all costs.  Sex is its god.

This religion began some 75 years ago with the
liberalization of divorce laws, to the detriment of Matthew 19.  Next came the pill and the Sexual Revolution,
then *Roe v. Wade* and abortion on
demand.  After that, it was the emergence
of the gay-rights lobby and gay marriage.

Interestingly, the last has proven to be about social
acceptance rather than adoption of a traditional (and sexually restrictive)
practice.  Marriage has traditionally
been sexually exclusive, with spouses vowing to “forsake all others”.  Not so with same-sex marriage.  As [this post](http://www.slate.com/blogs/xx_factor/2013/06/26/most_gay_couples_aren_t_monogamous_will_straight_couples_go_monogamish.html) observes, gay marriages tend not to be monogamous, but “monogamish”, in which
both spouses are free to stray according to certain pre-determined rules.

If sexual autonomy is the highest good, this makes perfect
sense.  There’s always been a certain
amount of sexual openness in heterosexual marriage, but it’s never been widely
accepted.  The author hopes that gay
attitudes on the subject will begin to shape straight marriages too, leaving
(in my opinion) the institution of marriage in even sadder shape than it
already is.

Of course, the debate these days has shifted to transgender
rights, even vis-à-vis the right, for instance, of teenage girls not to have
anatomical males in their locker rooms. 
No barrier to any practice between consenting adults can be tolerated.  No right is more important than the right to
seek sexual fulfillment down any of these avenues.  Personally, I can’t help but be reminded of
the cry of the kings of the earth in Psalm 2:3.

**Consequences**

This triumphal march has had its share of victims, even
beyond the cake-bakers and T-shirt makers already mentioned.  Divorce on demand has done incalculable harm
to millions of spouses and children.  The
unborn victims of *Roe v. Wade* are
legion.  The epidemic of rape on college
campuses (which I wrote about [here](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2016/01/campus-rape-and-godlessness.html))  clearly has its source in the
intersection of drunkenness and sexual promiscuity.  How about the harms caused by nearly universal
pornography use?  I could easily
continue.  As in “The Monkey’s Paw”, the
worst thing you can get is everything you want.

Of course, the worshipers of sexual autonomy are blind to
these things.  It’s particularly
saddening to listen to the outcry against campus rape by those who don’t
recognize that their worldview is fundamentally responsible for the
problem.  If you follow Biblical morality,
all the problems with blurred lines vanish. 
In the spirit of Proverbs 6:27, they have taken fire to their bosom, and
they’re shocked when it burns them.

The moral of the story is that even if a society rewrites
the rules for proper behavior, it cannot rewrite human nature.  Sexual desire is not a guide to human
happiness; it is nothing more than an urge of the flesh.  There is no higher wisdom here, only an itch that
must be scratched.

Indeed, there’s nothing about sexual desire that’s unique to
humans.  Animals are every bit as capable
of feeling desire and acting on it as we are. 
If we turn an animal impulse into our god, how can we avoid lowering
ourselves to the level of animals?
