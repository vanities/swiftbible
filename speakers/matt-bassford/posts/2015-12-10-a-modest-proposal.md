Normally, my interest in pop culture is about on par with
Genghis Khan’s interest in ballet. 
However, from time to time, some bit of celebrity chatter will impinge
on my consciousness.  So it was that over
the weekend, I stumbled across a controversy provoked by Ayesha Curry, wife of
NBA All-Star Stephen Curry.  Her great
social sin was to say the following on Twitter:

*Everyone's into barely
wearing clothes these days huh? Not my style. I like to keep the good stuff
covered up for the one who matters.*

And again:

*Just looking at the
latest fashion trends. I'll take classy over trendy any day of the week.*



I’m not used to hearing eminent good sense from celebrity
spouses, but from my perspective, Ayesha Curry’s comments qualify.  Yes, there is a place for being sultry and
provocative, but that place is in private with your husband.  In public, wear clothing that decently covers
your body.  Her conception of what is
modest may differ from mine, but at least she acknowledges that modesty is
important.  Hopefully, that’s a message
that my wife and I will be able to convey to our daughter.

Of course, in a world where every kind of perversion is
lauded to the skies, eminent good sense gets attacked.  So many people were so incandescently angry
about Curry’s comment that her Twitter account started showing up on worldwide
“Trending” lists.  Some accused her of
trying to shame women who dressed more revealingly.  Others said things like, “Sounds as if Ayesha
Curry thinks her body/other women’s bodies are like consumer goods marketed
exclusively for use by men, or something.” 
How dare she imply that women are better off not dressing in
barely-there fashion!

This betrays, I think, one of the key blind spots of our
godless society.  It’s the idea that
because actions shouldn’t have consequences for women, they don’t have
consequences for women.  It is true that
no woman should ever be sexually exploited, even if she’s lying passed-out
drunk in a frat-house hallway, even if she’s surrounded by young males (I’m not
going to call them “men”) who have drunk their inhibitions away.

Taking advantage of a woman in such a position is as evil as
can be.  She should be safe.  In real life, she is not safe.  I’ll be making that point to my daughter too.

However, it’s not societally acceptable to point to this
grim fact and say “It’s not wise for young women to go out
binge-drinking.”  That would be
victim-blaming!  Instead, we have to
pretend that there is no connection between the alcohol culture that prevails
on college campuses and the rape culture that also prevails.  Far be it from us to infringe on the right of
women to drink themselves into unconsciousness! 
And so the tragedies continue.

The same thing is true, albeit in a much lesser sense, when
it comes to modesty.  It is true that
every woman should be treated exactly the same, with courtesy and respect,
regardless of what she is wearing.  It is
true that no man has the right to sexually objectify a woman, no matter how
revealing her dress may be.

Once again, there’s a vast difference between “should” and
“is”.  In real life, the more skin a
woman shows, the less respect she gets. 
The more revealingly she dresses, the more sexually objectified she will
be.  Women who don’t want to be treated
accordingly should dress modestly.

This is not the “men are animals” strawman beloved of
feminists everywhere.  This is the
“people are half animals” analysis of the Bible.  Every human being, male and female alike, has
both a spiritual nature and a fleshly nature. 
Some things will wake the flesh up so that the spirit has to slap it
down (or not).  As the millions of porn sites
on the Internet testify, sex is at the very top of the list of things that the
fleshly nature of men finds appealing.  There
are exceptions, but this is the rule.

Immodest dress, then, makes men make a choice.  The more immodest the dress, the harder
making the right choice becomes.  As a
committed Christian, I am strongly convicted that lust is evil and wrong.  I don’t want to objectify any woman,
ever.  However, it’s still a struggle for
me, and the more alluring the outfit, the greater the struggle.  There are millions of men who don’t share my
convictions and so don’t bother struggling at all.

With apologies to Jonathan Swift (I’m more into puns than
satire), my modest proposal is this: 
Women should dress for the way they want to be treated.  If they want to be disrespected and
objectified, they should dress in a way that will make it easy for men to disrespect
and objectify them.  If, conversely, they
want to be respected and honored by men, they should dress in a way that makes
it easy for men to do those things.  For
women who claim to follow Christ, 1 Peter 3:3-4 is pretty clear about whether
they should be emphasizing the outside or the inside.

It’s a free country. 
Women can dress however they want, within the limits of local
ordinances, at least.  However, they’d do
well to listen to Ayesha Curry if they want to be treated as more than the sum
of their parts.
