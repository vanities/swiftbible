Even now that we have found ourselves in a new year, the
sermons from sermon requests continue. 
This time, the request came from the lips of my own daughter.  Zoë asked me to preach a sermon on how to
avoid and defeat sin.

Honestly, I don’t think she has any particular problem with
that one yet, but I do believe it’s a good idea for her to think ahead, and for
the rest of us, who face temptation every single day of our lives, there are
few more pertinent spiritual issues than this one.  This is true not only for new Christians, but
also for those who have been disciples for decades.  If we ourselves remain faithful long enough,
we will witness the shocking spectacle of people we thought were pillars of the
church abandoning the faith and falling away entirely.  As soon as we start thinking, “That can’t
happen to me!” we guarantee that it will. 
This morning, then, let’s consider defeating sin.



**Jesus and Sin**

As we frequently do when considering any spiritual topic,
the first thing we’re going to do this morning is to look to Jesus, our
spiritual example, and ask how He succeeded in His striving against sin.  The way that I see it, it wasn’t so much that
Jesus was a good spiritual tactician. 
It’s that He was a good strategist. 
He didn’t defeat the devil because He had this arsenal of clever little
tricks that he could use whenever He was tempted.  He defeated the devil because He built His
whole life in a way that offered little opportunity for the devil to attack Him.

The first of Jesus’ sin-defeating strategies was His **FOCUS ON GOD’S GLORY**.  Look at how the Lord expresses this in John
17:4.  At first reading, this doesn’t
seem like it has anything to do with sin at all.  This is one of those spiritual mission statements
from the Lord that are so easy to read right through because they’re so
generic.  Jesus lived for God’s
glory.  Yeah, yeah.  We know.

However, Jesus’ words here count for far more than spiritual
boilerplate.  If we *really* live for God’s glory, which Jesus did, that will have a
profound impact on every single day of our lives.  It means that on the job, our first goal will
be to glorify God.  In our family
interactions, the first thing we will seek will be God’s glory.  The same holds true for our friendships and
our free time.

If we are genuinely looking to use each of those things as a
way to honor our Creator, it becomes much harder for the devil to find a
toehold.  It’s not impossible by any
means, as all those preachers who have been led astray by one-on-one Bible
studies with women can testify.  However,
if we order our lives so that God is first of everything and first in
everything, and at every turn we are asking how we can honor Him in this, we
crowd Satan out pretty successfully.

Second, Jesus defeated sin by His **STEADFASTNESS** in serving God. 
This steadfastness is wonderfully on display in Luke 13:31-33.  When Jesus said this, He knew full well that
He was journeying to Jerusalem so that His enemies could arrest and kill Him
there.  However, that knowledge never had
the slightest effect on His actions. 
Going to Jerusalem was the right thing to do, so Jesus was going to go
to Jerusalem, and no other consideration could sway Him.

Every one of us, though, has trouble with imitating His
devotion.  Complete dedication to God
sounds great in theory, but it falls apart awfully easily in practice.  Maybe like Jesus, we see these negative
consequences for perfect obedience rising up in front of us, except that unlike
Jesus, we turn aside to avoid them. 
Maybe it’s simply hard doing the right thing day after day, so that we
reach the point where we persuade ourselves that we need a spiritual vacation.

When we start thinking like that, though, we have made a
crucial mistake.  We’ve started thinking
of our lives as belonging to us, rather than belonging to God.  If they belong to us, sure, we’re entitled to
a vacation now and again, but if they belong to God, we get to rest from our
labors when He says we do, and He says we work until we die.  First-century Christianity is not at all for
the faint of heart.  This is a stern
teaching, but the example of Jesus has never been easy to follow.

Third, Jesus’ practice of constant **PRAYER** made a difference in His fight against evil.  We see one example of many possible examples
in Mark 1:35.  I think all of us have
things in our lives that we love to do and will always manage to fit into our
schedules somehow.  Maybe it’s a favorite
TV show.  Maybe it’s a hobby or time with
a best friend.  Regardless, though, no matter
how busy we are, somehow, that one thing always still happens.

For Jesus, His one thing was prayer.  Sometimes, the gospel accounts read almost
like a schoolboy playing hooky.  Nobody
knows where Jesus has gone, but eventually they find that He has sneaked off to
pray for hours.  There are many
places—every place, really—where our spiritual lives don’t measure up to the
Lord’s, but for many of us, this is one of the most obvious.  Too often, the answer to the question, “Did
you think to pray?” is often a resounding “No!”

The only way to solve the problem is to approach prayer like
Jesus did.  We need to constantly find
ways to make time and attention for it. 
We can put it on the to-do list in our smartphones.  Every time we see somebody’s life event on
Facebook, we can pray about it.  We can
make a point of praying about the day first thing in the morning, before we do
anything else.  We can even pray in the
shower.  One thing’s for certain—the more
we pray, the more we will succeed.

In the life of the Lord, these things and many more besides
led to His ultimate **VICTORY.** The Hebrews writer describes it in
Hebrews 4:14-15.  Of the many
awe-inspiring works of Jesus, this is far from the least!  All of us, to our sorrow, know how easy it is
to sin.  We aren’t focused on God in
everything.  We don’t stay focused, no
matter what.  We don’t use prayer to keep
us close to God.  Then, because of these
and many other failures, we fall into sin.

Jesus never did that. 
Not ever.  Not once.  The devil tried, all right.  Jesus didn’t get any kind of free pass
because He was the Son of God.  However,
never once did Jesus respond to temptation the way that Satan wanted Him too.  He was without sin.

This, of course, is a victory not only for Jesus, but for
all of us.  When we consider His sinless
perfection, we can’t do so as disinterested spectators.  Instead, we do it as people whose lives are
on the line.  We desperately needed Jesus
to do what we couldn’t, because without His perfect adherence to the law of
God, there could be no perfect sacrifice to save us from our sins.  However, Jesus won the victory that He needed
in order to ransom all of us.

**Before Temptation**

If we want to defeat sin in our own lives, we must learn
from Jesus.  For us too, success will come
much more easily if we focus on being good strategists.  We do our most important work before we
encounter temptation.  In this regard, we
must first learn to **SEEK THE THINGS
ABOVE.** Paul points us in this
direction in Colossians 3:1-3.  This is
important not only for its direct effects, but for its indirect effects.  We are most vulnerable to sin when our lives
are empty, but we are least vulnerable when we are working hard for the Lord.

We see this clearly illustrated in the life of David.  David’s spiritual accomplishments—man after
God’s own heart, writer of the Psalms, et cetera—are so well known they’re
almost not worth repeating.  David was a *good* guy.  However, even his righteousness depended on
his keeping busy.  One of David’s jobs as
king was leading the army out to battle against the enemies of Israel every
spring.  However, one spring he stayed
home and let Joab do the leading.  In
that idle time, he looked out his palace window, saw Bathsheba bathing, and the
rest was history, along with any hope of peace for the rest of his reign.  If idle time could wreck a man like David,
how can we expect it not to wreck us, especially when there are so many
low-effort ways to sin?  The more we fill
that idle time with work for the Lord, the more He will be glorified and the
more we will be protected.

Along these lines, one of the works that will help to fill
our lives is **READING THE BIBLE.** The psalmist explicitly links study to
resisting temptation in Psalm 119:11.  The
more we read and study, the more we know. 
The more we know, the less spiritually vulnerable we will be.

As the saying goes, this ain’t rocket surgery.  Every Christian in this room has heard a
preacher tell them they ought to be reading their Bibles more.  Every Christian in this room who has been a
member at Joliet for any length of time has heard me tell them they ought to be
reading their Bibles more.  Conceptually,
it’s an idea that is as simple as it is familiar.  However, if I had to guess, I would guess
that most Christians in the congregation do not read their Bibles regularly
outside of the assembly.  We know this,
we understand it, but somehow, we can’t manage to do it.

In times past, I’ve recommended daily Bible-reading
programs.  Today, though, I’m going to
recommend what I’ve found myself doing recently, and that is carrying out a
quasi-daily Bible-reading program. 
Rather than reading every morning for a fixed time, I’ve been letting
daily readings accumulate in my inbox for two or three days and then knocking
out all those readings in one sitting. 
Often, that gives me the opportunity to appreciate an entire book at
once.  Maybe if the more structured
approach hasn’t been working for you, the less structured approach would.  In this new year, give it a whirl!  I guarantee that if you make a habit of
reading, it will change your life.

Third, we must learn to **BE
WATCHFUL.** Consider, for instance,
Paul’s warning in Ephesians 5:15-17.  The
idea here is not merely of being careful how you walk, but of looking carefully
where you walk.  It reminds me of an
experience I had when I was a boy and my family went camping almost 30 years
ago now.  We were hiking along a trail,
and my sister and I had run on a couple of hundred yards in front, when I
started hearing a strange buzzing sound. 
I didn’t know what it was, so I slowed down and started looking
around.  Then, there in the path in front
of me, I saw a huge rattlesnake.  The
thing must have been six feet or more long, and it was thick, too!  It was coiled up ready to strike, and the
noise I heard was its rattle.  My sister
and I turned around and ran shrieking back up the path, but if I hadn’t been
looking, it could have meant big trouble.

I know that my wife is thinking right now that she will
never go anywhere with me where there is a chance of encountering venomous
reptiles, but the reality is that the dangers of the forest pale in comparison
to the spiritual dangers of our everyday lives. 
That snake didn’t actually want to bite me, but we can rest assured that
the serpent of old, Satan, is eager for the opportunity to kill every one of
us.  We have to keep a careful lookout
for all the situations in our lives where he might be lying in wait, and once
we’ve seen the trap, we need to walk carefully around it.

In this, one of the wisest things we can do is to **AVOID EVIL COMPANIONS.** Solomon takes up this theme in Proverbs
5:7-8.  According to the father character
of Proverbs, the only way that his son can hope to avoid falling for the
adulterous woman is if he never goes anywhere near her.  Of course, this is no less a spiritual
problem today.  There is no Christian so
godly as to be immune to the allure of sexual sin.  If we aren’t careful in guarding our time,
our minds, and our hearts from the devil, we can be certain that he’ll find a
way to use somebody to get us.

We must also recognize the ability of evil companions to
corrupt us more generally.  We can’t
become monks and withdraw entirely from the world, but at the same time, we
must recognize the potential that every worldly relationship has to corrupt
us.  Friendship with the world, after
all, is enmity with God.  If we are not
on our guard, we can be led astray in any number of ways.

**In Temptation**

Of course, all the strategy in the world won’t save us if
our tactics are lousy.  We have to know
how to conduct ourselves when in temptation as well.  Many of the above points have a tactical
application too; for instance, prayer is a great help in temptation.  However, some of our spiritual weapons have
only a tactical application.  Of these,
the first is to **REMEMBER THE WAY OF
ESCAPE.** This is famously mentioned
in 1 Corinthians 10:13.  This passage is
probably a verse that many of us can quote, but knowing it doesn’t do us any
good unless we have the presence of mind to apply it.  This is a truth we have to remember in the
moment, but remembering it can be extremely difficult because temptation often
carries with it a sense of inevitability.

Here we are, on the spot, being tempted.  It may be that we have made a series of bad
choices that have led us to this position. 
Our strategy hasn’t been good, and we know it.  Maybe we’ve been in this position before, and
in almost exactly the same circumstances, we chose to sin.  Satan is fully aware of how powerful our
guilt over these things can be, and he uses the memory of past mistakes to try
to lure us into new ones:  “Well, you’ve
come this far.  You might as well
sin.”  Or, “Who are you to think you can
resist temptation this time?  This sin is
who you are.”

Brethren, those things are lies.  Until we actually sin, we can always choose
not to.  Our failure is not
inevitable.  Finding the way of escape
might be difficult or embarrassing, but it’s always there.  Only when we defeat that sense of inevitable
failure will we be able to find it.

Part of finding the way of escape is **KNOWING WHEN TO FIGHT.** Peter
talks about this in 1 Peter 5:8-9.  Peter
knew a thing or two about confrontation. 
We see him giving into the temptation to deny Jesus before the Lord was
crucified.  However, just a few weeks
later, when he is arrested and summoned before the Sanhedrin, we see him
defying death for the Lord’s sake.

Peter knew very well that it was possible for him and
possible for anyone to resist.  If we
want to imitate him in fighting back, we have to develop the same mindset that
he had.  Peter was willing to defiantly
put even his life on the line because there was literally nothing that was more
important to him than glorifying Jesus. 
If we feel the same way, we will make the same decision.

It can be an expensive decision.  Probably none of us will be faced with the
choice between sin and death, but we can certainly have to choose between God
and our friends, or God and our job, or God and something else that is
extremely important to us.  Sometimes in
the world’s eyes, making the right decision may look like losing the
battle.  Worldly people don’t hold their
scruples higher than continued employment! 
However, only if we resist will we actually win.

Sometimes, though, resistance is the wrong answer.  With all due apologies to Kenny Rogers, there
are times when Christians have to **KNOW
WHEN TO RUN.** Paul makes this point
in 2 Timothy 2:22.  The reality is that
some situations are no-win situations, and the only way to win is to get yourself
out of the situation.  In particular,
Paul mentions youthful lusts as a temptation that Timothy must flee rather than
fight.  I’m not exactly youthful anymore,
but I can remember what that was like. 
It is certainly true that any young Christian who regularly puts
themselves in a position where they have to fight off youthful lusts is sooner
or later going to lose.  They have to be
willing to flee temptation like Joseph did, even if they lose their dignity in
the process.

Of course, youthful lusts are not the only temptation that
can exert this kind of power over us. 
The recovering alcoholic has to flee the temptation to return to the
bottle.  The contentious man has to know
when the time comes to walk away from an argument.  The exact identity of these problem sins will
differ from Christian to Christian, but every one of us has to have the
self-knowledge to recognize the temptations we won’t be able to handle.  When they show up, we have to be wise and
strong enough to head in the opposite direction.
