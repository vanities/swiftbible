*By faith Enoch was
taken away so that he did not see death, "and was not found, because God
had taken him"; for before he was taken he had this testimony, that he
pleased God.*

-- Hebrews 11:5

Our time is in many ways quite different from the time of
Enoch, but in both times, one thing remains constant.  For both Enoch and for us, the biggest
problem is death.  Though the men of
Enoch’s day were much longer-lived than we are, they were still subject to the
curse of Adam, and death almost never misses a stroke.  Unless the Lord returns first, death will
come for each one of us.



In all of recorded history, only two men are presented as
never having to taste death:  Elijah, who
rose to heaven in a whirlwind (2 Kings 2:11),
and Enoch himself.  Elijah is one of the
major characters of the Old Testament, but we know next to nothing about
Enoch.  As the Hebrews writer observes,
the book of Genesis only imparts two spiritually important pieces of
information about him:  he walked with
God, and he was taken away so that he did not see death.

However, from this one-sentence life summary, we can learn
what needs to be at the center of our lives too. First, the motivating force
behind all of this was Enoch’s faith.  We
don’t know what he knew about God, but he knew enough to believe.  We do too. 
God did not intend His word to be the answer to our every idle
speculation, religious or otherwise.  He
intended it to produce belief, and for that, it is sufficient (John 20:30-31).

Because of his faith, Enoch walked with God.  This does not refer to Enoch’s physical
proximity to God, but to his spiritual proximity.  Presumably, Enoch sinned just like any other
man, but from the beginning to the end of his life, he did his best to align
his course with the path God laid out for him. 
Do we, or does our walk only intersect with God’s will at moments of
convenience?  When obedience is
difficult, where can our footprints be found? 
Only those who truly walk with God will be with Him in heaven.

Finally, because of his faith and obedience, Enoch never saw
death.  He was translated into a
spiritual existence without having to pass through the grave first.  Through God, we too can triumph over
Sheol.  God may wake us rather than take
us, but the end is the same, and if we are faithful, we will have a reward not
much different from Enoch’s.
