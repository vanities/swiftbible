Much to no one’s surprise, I had so much to say about the
16-slide slide show I linked to yesterday that I had to break my comments up
into two posts.  Here’s [a link](http://hisexcellentword.blogspot.com/2017/01/the-amazingness-of-divorce-part-1.html) to my
first post, and here are my thoughts on the remaining eight slides:



[**You Don't Have to Feel Alone Anymore**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=9)

If you look closely, there may be some irony here.

[**Bye-Bye, In-Laws!**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=10)

Admittedly, I’m biased here. 
I think my in-laws are wonderful people, and I was friends with them
long before I started seriously dating their daughter.  However, I see at least two problems with
this.  The first is that if you have
kids, it isn’t true.  Guess who one set
of their grandparents will always be!

Second, even if it is true, is ending yet another
relationship something to celebrate? 
Certainly, there are people out there who seem to be entirely bent on
making others’ lives as miserable as possible. 
In my experience, though, most aren’t like that, and even when
relationships fall apart, it’s generally the result of a vicious cycle.  If you believe the worst about somebody, it
will tend to bring out the worst in them too.

I’ve ridden friendships down in flames before, but even now,
I look back on the results with deep regret. 
I obviously don’t have any ex-in-laws to contend with, but I’m still
friends with several parents of ex-girlfriends, and I could have a cordial
conversation with any of those people. 
As a rule, I look to expand the borders of my spheres of affection, not
contract them.  Casually writing others
off strikes me as a needless waste.

[**Tax Credits**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=11)

This is misleading.  Yes,
it is true that a single parent with an income of $75,000 gets a $1000 child
tax credit, and a married couple with an income of $150,000 doesn’t.  However, the government set things up that
way because a married couple with an income of $150,000 doesn’t *need* a $1000 child tax credit.  According to [this calculator](https://dqydj.com/income-percentile-calculator/),
such a couple is in the top 13 percent of American households.  They’re getting by Just Fine on their own,
without government help.

The author’s argument about the Child and Dependent Care Tax
Credit is similarly flawed.  Sure, if
you’re a single parent who earns $30K with a kid in day care, you get more in
tax credit than a married couple that together earns $60K.  However, the author exaggerates the
difference by using per-person numbers. 
In real life, Single Mom only gets $658 more in tax credit than the
married couple.

Let’s look at where that leaves both families.  The married couple with their $60,000 income
has to pay $9400 in child care and gets a $1880 tax break, for a net income of
$52,480.  According to [MIT's living-wage calculator](http://livingwage.mit.edu/counties/17197) excluding child-care
expenses, such a family needs $49,467 to get by in my home county (Will County,
IL).  These people can make it.

By contrast, Single Mom with her $30,000 income pays $9400
in child care and gets a $2538 tax break, for a net of $23,138.  Also according to the MIT living-wage
calculator for Will County, she actually needs $44,454 per year (again
excluding child care), which leaves her with a shortfall of more than $20,000
per year.

Basically, divorcees get more financial assistance from the
government because most government assistance goes to poor people, and getting
divorced is a good way to get poor. 
People, especially women, who don’t want to be poor should rethink
getting divorced.

[**Sweet, Sweet Silence**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=12)

Again, if you absolutely must wake up by yourself some
Saturday morning, go on a weekend trip or send the husband and kids out
camping.  Divorce seems like the nuclear
option here, especially when divorce inevitably makes the rest of your life so
much more frantic.

[**Financial Help Available for Education**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=13)

This is the second verse of the song, “If you get a lot of
financial help from the government, it’s because the government thinks you’re
poor.”  In the case of single mothers,
the government is usually right.  Few
other demographic groups are as financially vulnerable as this one.  If you want to exchange financial security
for the occasional handout, I guess that’s your prerogative.

Also, working full-time AND going to night school AND trying
to raise one or more children singlehandedly strikes me as an ordeal of heroic
proportions.  I’m a pretty strong-willed
guy, but I don’t think I could do it.  If
you ditch the job, you can instead spend the next four years racking up six
figures’ worth of student-loan debt and the 30 years after that paying it
off.  I’m not sure that represents an
actual improvement.  Single motherhood is
a numbers game, and the numbers are brutal.

[**You'll Learn New Skills**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=14)

Or, to put things a slightly different way, you’ll have
twice as many things to do in even less time. 
Congratulations!

[**It Can Be Its Own Antidepressant**](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=15)

This isn’t even what the blurb underneath the slide
says.  The author writes, in boldface no
less, “**[D]ivorce doesn’t solve
depression**.”

Yeah.  What she said.

**[Sexual Freedom](http://www.msn.com/en-us/lifestyle/family-relationships/16-amazing-post-divorce-things-no-one-tells-you/ss-AAlOyEl#image=16)**

Of course, the slide here is the obligatory picture of two
sets of bare feet between the sheets—one of the great Internet clichés of our
time.  To be honest, I don’t understand
this one.  If you’re concerned about
sexual morality, it’s not any more godly to commit fornication than it is to
commit adultery (and according to Matthew 19:9, you may still be committing
adultery even post-divorce).  If you’re
not concerned about sexual morality, then adultery and fornication are matters
of equal indifference.  Of course, it’s
possible for someone not to be concerned with sexual morality but to be
concerned with their marriage vows, but if those vows are important to you, why
are you getting divorced?  A promise
broken in front of a judge is no less broken than one broken in a sleazy
motel.

If you want to be evil, I guess you can be evil.  Divorce doesn’t change the situation.

In sum, the lesson from the slide show is that even in
earthly terms, there are lots of bad reasons to get divorced and not a whole
lot of good ones.  God is far wiser than
we are, and sometimes His reasoning eludes us. 
However, it’s easy to understand why He says in Malachi 2:16, “I hate
divorce, and him who covers his garment with wrong.”  If more Americans paid attention, we’d all be
better off for it.
