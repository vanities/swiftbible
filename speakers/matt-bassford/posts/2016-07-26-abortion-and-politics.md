I believe that abortion is a very great evil.  I’ve preached sermons in which I compare
legalized abortion to the worship of Molech. 
I believe *Roe v. Wade* was
illegitimately decided.  If I could end
abortion, I would do it in a heartbeat.

However, when I hear people arguing that we should vote for
Republican presidential candidate X (Trump or any other) so that they will
install Supreme Court justices who will vote to overturn *Roe v. Wade*, my eyebrow goes up. 
I’m skeptical first of all that ending abortion is as much of a priority
for the typical Republican presidential candidate as it is for me (and that
goes double for Trump).



Second, and even more importantly, the goal of packing the
Court with Republican appointees has been achieved before, and it did not
result in the end of legalized abortion. 
Let’s take a trip in the Wayback Machine to 1992, when *Planned Parenthood v. Casey* came before
the Court, an abortion case that gave the Court the opportunity to overturn *Roe* if it so chose.  At that time, the nine justices on the court
were (with appointing president in parentheses):

William Rehnquist (Nixon)

Byron White (Kennedy)

Harry Blackmun (Nixon)

John Stevens (Ford)

Sandra Day O’Connor (Reagan)

Antonin Scalia (Reagan)

Anthony Kennedy (Reagan)

David Souter (George H. W. Bush)

Clarence Thomas (George H.W. Bush)

Of the nine justices, the only Democratic appointee was Justice
White.  Six of the nine (Stevens,
O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas) were appointed by Republicans after
the 1973 *Roe v. Wade* decision put
abortion at the center of the culture wars. 
Five of the nine (O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas) were
appointed by presidents who campaigned on ending abortion.  Basically, the situation in 1992 was everything
that vote-Republican-for-abortion’s-sake people say they want to achieve.  *Roe v.
Wade* is going down, right?

Wrong.  Instead, *Roe* was upheld in a 5-4 plurality
decision.  Every single one of the
justices in the plurality (O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Stevens, Blackmun) was a
Republican appointee.  White, the only Democratic
appointee, actually voted to overturn it. 
The three primary authors of the plurality opinion (O’Connor, Kennedy,
Souter) were either Reagan or Bush appointees.

Ronald Reagan, icon of modern conservatism, appointed
pro-choice justices with two of his three picks.  And yet, we’re supposed to vote for Donald
Trump, who didn’t even mention abortion during his nomination speech, because
he’s the guy who’s going to bring in the justices who will end abortion?  Even if that is his goal, history suggests
that Supreme-Court justices commonly vote in unpredictable ways once
appointed.

In short, as great an evil as abortion is, there appears to
be little hope of addressing it through the political process.  Republican presidents may not even have the
will to end abortion, and if they do have the will, they appear not to have the
ability.  I would vote Republican if I
believed doing so would lead to *Roe v.
Wade* being overturned.  However, the
evidence leads me to conclude exactly the opposite.

Rather than focusing on the mirage of a political solution
to a moral problem, I would encourage Christians to oppose abortion on an
individual level by (in Romans 12:21 terms) seeking to overcome evil with
good.  We can demonstrate the value we
place on all life by:

- Befriending and helping vulnerable pregnant women.

- Adopting a child.

- Serving as a foster parent.

- Participating in [Sacred Selections](http://www.sacredselections.org/),
which raises money to fund adoptions by Christians.

- Praying for God to change the hearts of Americans

And, most of all:



- Sharing the gospel with a friend.  Our nation has turned away from Biblical
morality, and unless that trend is reversed, its laws will continue to reflect
godlessness.  Every one of us needs to
have a hand in expanding the kingdom of heaven, one soul at a time.  The devil may want us to believe that
politics offers a solution, but the true solution can only be found where it
always has been:  in the Lordship of Jesus
Christ.
