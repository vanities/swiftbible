*And Jacob said to his
household and to all who were with him, "Put away the foreign gods that
are among you, purify yourselves, and change your garments.”*

*--Genesis 35:2*

There are many unfortunate chapter breaks in Scripture, and
the break between Genesis 34 and Genesis 35 is one of them.  If we simply start off reading in Genesis 35,
it seems like God has randomly commanded Jacob to get up and go dwell in
Bethel.  In reality, though, this command
is closely linked to the events of Genesis 34. 
In that text, Simeon and Levi have just made Jacob and his household
odious in the sight of the peoples of the land by butchering the men of Shechem.  Genesis 35 doesn’t say so explicitly, but it
strongly implies that the natives are in the process of banding together to
pursue Jacob and kill him and his family. 
God’s command, then, is a command to flee, to escape the consequences of
Simeon and Levi’s treachery.

It’s in this context that we see Jacob’s words in Genesis
35:2.  As committed as Jacob may have
been to God, his family worshiped idols too. 
Jacob may have winked at these things before, but now he and his family
were in danger, and they were about to travel to a place where God had granted
Jacob a vision of heaven.  In other
words, enough was enough.  Jacob
confiscated his family’s pagan trappings and buried them under a tree near
Shechem.

In this, Jacob provides a valuable lesson for heads of
households and spiritual leaders today. 
We only ever see Jacob worshiping Jehovah.  He neither paid homage to any other god nor
encouraged his family to do so.  Nonetheless,
even without his endorsement, idolatry still crept in.  So it is today.  It’s not enough for Christian men to seek God
on their own and assume that their families will follow their lead.  Although it’s not likely that our wives or
children will set up graven images in the front hall, the modern world still
offers plenty of idols to tempt our families. 
Those idols can be anything from materialism to sports to hobbies.  All of them, though, have the potential to
turn our loved ones’ hearts away from God.

When those idols appear, when family members have become
devoted to something other than God, that’s when the leader needs to lead.  Jacob didn’t offer reduced idol time or a
90-day waiting period.  He told his
family to get rid of the foreign gods immediately, he expected to be obeyed
immediately, and he was obeyed immediately. 
He had the moral authority to lead his household through a drastic
spiritual change.  In the church and in
the home, we need leaders who measure up to Jacob.  Sadly, such men are becoming rarer and rarer.  Too many lack the integrity, vision, and
commitment to demand obedience and get results, and it is one of the worst
problems in the Lord’s people today. 
It’s time for Christian men to stand up and be men, to lead their people
in the uncompromising purity that God requires.
