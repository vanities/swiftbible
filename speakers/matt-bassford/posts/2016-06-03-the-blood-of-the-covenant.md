There are many reasons to believe in the inspiration of the
Bible, and one of the most meaningful to me has always been the unity of
Scripture.  Even though the books of the
Bible were penned by dozens of different men, they all share the same themes
and tell the same story.  This is
particularly clear when we consider the similarities between the Law of Moses
and the law of Christ. Much of the book of Hebrews is devoted to explaining
that the Old Law was nothing more than a setup, so that when Christ came, we
could understand what He was doing. 
Let’s see how this works in Hebrews 9, as the writer there explains what
Moses can teach us about the blood of the covenant.



The first section of this context concerns **OFFERINGS UNDER THE LAW.** Read with me here from Hebrews
9:1-10.  Here, the Hebrews writer focuses
on the tabernacle of the Law of Moses and the rituals of the Israelite Day of
Atonement, the one time a year when the high priest would bring an offering of
blood into the inner room of the tabernacle, the Most Holy Place, which
symbolized the presence of God in heaven.

From this, the Hebrews writer draws two conclusions.  Because anybody who entered the Most Holy
Place at any other time would die, it showed that under the Law, God’s people
did not have direct access to Him. 
Instead, they were prevented from coming into the presence of His
perfect holiness by their sins.  This in
turn showed the failure of the sacrifices for sin under the Law.  Even when offered faithfully, they couldn’t
cleanse the conscience from guilt.

This problem was solved, and could only be solved, by **CHRIST OUR HIGH PRIEST.** Let’s keep going with Hebrews
9:11-14.  The Hebrews writer begins this
section by identifying two ways in which the priestly service of Jesus is
superior to the priests under the Law of Moses. 
First, Christ made His offering in a better tabernacle.  Those priests offered their sacrifices in a
tent that stood for the presence of God.  Jesus, though, appeared in the actual presence
of God in heaven.  Second, the priests
under the Old Law came with the blood of goats and calves, but Jesus brought
His own life’s blood.

Because Jesus presented a superior offering in a superior
location, His sacrifice of Himself had a superior effect.  The rites of the Old Law could bring about a
bodily purification.  The power of the
blood of Jesus, by contrast, was enough to purify not only the body but also
the conscience.

However, that’s not all that the blood of Jesus did for
us.  In addition to purchasing
forgiveness of sins, it inaugurated **A
NEW COVENANT.** Here, consider Hebrews
9:15-24.  In this text, the Hebrews
writer is taking two different meanings of the word “covenant” and showing how
the death of Jesus is meaningful under both of them.  The first thing that a covenant can be is a
testament or will, the document by which someone arranges for his possessions
to be distributed after he dies.  We all
know that simply because we’re mentioned in somebody’s will, we can’t barge
into their house and take whatever we want while they’re still alive.  Instead, the will only comes into effect when
the testator dies.  So too, the inheritance
that Jesus has willed to those who sinned under the first covenant, and indeed
to us, is the gift of eternal life. 
Under the terms of His last will and testament, He had to die before we
could receive the inheritance.

In a second and related sense, “covenant” is used in the
Bible to refer to an agreement between man and God.  Another name for the Law of Moses is the “old
covenant”.  As the Hebrews writer points
out, the old covenant began with blood too. 
At Sinai, Moses sprinkled the lifeblood of animal sacrifices on the book
of the Law, the tabernacle, the vessels used in the service of the tabernacle,
and the people themselves.  From that
point on, their covenant with God was sealed. 
Only death could purify and make an agreement with God possible.

However, just as our covenant and our promises are better
than the covenant and promises of the Israelites, so too the blood that
ratifies our covenant with God has to be better.  Here also, only the blood of Jesus will do.  Jesus didn’t sprinkle His blood on the
earthly stand-ins.  Instead, He
consecrated the heavenly reality.  His
blood didn’t touch our skins.  Instead,
it cleansed our hearts.

Finally, the Hebrews writer emphasizes that Jesus did these
things **ONCE FOR ALL**.  Let’s finish up the chapter by reading
Hebrews 9:25-28.  Here, the Hebrews
writer focuses on one final way in which the covenant of Christ is
superior.  Under the Law of Moses, the
high priest went into the Most Holy Place with blood once a year, every year,
to offer atonement for the people.

This isn’t the way that the sacrifice of Christ works.  As the Hebrews writer observes, Jesus’
offering of Himself isn’t like the high priests’ offering of animal
sacrifices.  Otherwise, Jesus would have
to die on the cross once a year, every year. 
Instead, Jesus offered Himself once for all.

The awe-inspiring power of His sacrifice was enough to
consecrate God’s people in both directions. 
It brought about true forgiveness of sins for all the sins committed
under the old covenant, and it continues to atone for our sins today, two
thousand years later.  This single
selfless act transformed the spiritual reality for the entire human race.  As we partake of the Lord’s Supper
momentarily, let’s remember Him and the awesome thing He has done for us.
