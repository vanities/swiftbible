*So the LORD said to
Cain, "Why are you angry? And why has your countenance fallen? If you do
well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin lies at the
door. And its desire is for you, but you should rule over it."*

-- Genesis 4:6-7

In many ways, it’s unsurprising that the first murder was
committed because of Cain’s envy of and bitterness toward Abel.  From that time forward, envy has been a
plague on humankind.  It has been
responsible for wrongs ranging from theft to warfare, and Abel’s was certainly
not the last murder we can lay at the feet of envy.  According to Mark 15:10, envy was what motivated the Jewish
leaders to hand Jesus over to be crucified.



Envy is perhaps most unpleasant of all, though, when it
appears in the church.  Satan has been
quite successful at convincing many Christians that spiritual success is a
competitive sport.  When we see our walk
with Christ through that competitive lens, it becomes a point of pride for us
when we gain the respect of other Christians and a corresponding point of shame
when we see someone else receive the elevation that we desire.  In the congregational context, few things are
more difficult than appointing elders in a church that has had none before, and
often the root problem in such cases is the envy of unqualified men for those
who are qualified.

If we wrestle with envy in such instances, we’ve forgotten
one of the basic truths of discipleship. 
Walking in the footsteps of Jesus isn’t about seeing how many others we
can impress with our godliness.  It’s
about seeing how many others we can serve. 
Genuine Christianity is always focused on the other, not the self.  Once we learn to think in this way, we no
longer rely on others to recognize us and fuel our sense of
accomplishment.  Instead, even if not
another soul knows what we’ve done, we find our reward in the knowledge that
we’ve helped someone who needed it and glorified God.

Once we’ve reached that point, our envy struggles should
dissipate, because envy can find no foothold in the *agape*-filled heart.  Whether
other people applaud us for our actions or not, who cares?  That wasn’t why we were doing good in the
first place.  Let other people win the
praise of men.  We must be content with
winning the praise from God.  If that’s
the kind of praise we can claim, we can be at peace, knowing that, “Whoever
exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.”
