It’s probably true that I’m a grumpy old man in
training.  The longer I live, the more I
harrumph.  However, even in our strange,
degenerate age, there are some things worth harrumphing about.  Take [this article.](http://www.msn.com/en-us/news/world/atheist-pastor-sparks-debate-by-irritating-the-church-into-the-21st-century/ar-BBsbpNt)

For those who don’t have the time to read it, it’s about a
pastor in the United Church of Canada who is an open, avowed atheist.  During the “worship” services at her
congregation, there is no more talk of silly superstitions like God and Jesus.  Instead, the focus is on compassion, love,
and celebrating members’ achievements.

The United Church of
Canada is a broad-minded lot, but even in it, there are some folks who think
that a pastor ought to believe in God. 
The atheist is now up for disciplinary review.  She is unrepentant.  She thinks (to borrow a phrase) that she’s on
the right side of history, and that soon the entire denomination will end up
where she now stands.



On one level, this is absolutely bonkers.  I’m sure there have been plenty of atheist
religious leaders through the centuries, but there haven’t been too many who
admitted it.  I have no doubt that if I
took the pulpit on Sunday and announced that I no longer believed in God, I
would find myself at the center of many earnest Bible studies, and if I didn’t
recant, I’d find myself looking for a new line of work.  Like “sincere hypocrite”, “atheist pastor”
would seem to be a contradiction in terms. 
Toward what, exactly, are you shepherding the flock?  Death, like in Psalm 49:14?

However, Ms. Atheist Pastor has a point.  She is on the right side of a certain kind of
history.  Her proudly acknowledged
godlessness is the final, pure expression of her church’s rejection of
God.  It is the logical conclusion toward
which the United Church of Canada and many others have been traveling.

All of this started long ago, of course, once men got to
thinking that they were smarter than God is. 
They thought they could improve on the simple pattern of worship and
service recorded in the New Testament. 
Churches got fat and proud and started branching out into things that
were never the church’s business.

Soon, the inspiration of the Bible came under attack.  Learned men began to dismiss stories of Noah
and Jonah as myths while claiming to cling to the “essentials” of the faith.  Soon, skepticism began to attach itself to
those essentials too.  The record of the
gospels became suspect, and religious leaders began to deny that Jesus was
bodily raised from the dead.  Christianity
became a feel-good story rather than the truth.

The rejection of Christ picked up steam with the rejection
of Biblical morality.  No matter that
Scripture has condemned certain practices as evil for thousands of years; now
sexual immorality of every sort is celebrated as an expression of personal
autonomy.  God may have created them male
and female, but our society labors to make the distinction meaningless.

If you don’t honor God in the church, if you don’t honor God
in His word, and if you don’t honor God in your personal life, there’s not
exactly a whole lot of God-honoring left. 
Darwin might say that God has become a vestigial organ (not that people
believe in vestigial organs anymore).  He
is shrunken, atrophied, useless.  His
church has been eaten up from the inside like a spider consumed by a wasp
larva.  The shell of the church might
remain, but it is inhabited by a spirit quite alien to His.

At that point, why not bring in the atheist pastor?  Why not dispense with the pretense that we
care about God anymore?  Once He is
neatly out of the way, stuffed into a storage closet along with the unused
Lord’s Supper serving trays, we can focus on the worship that truly interests
us:  the worship of ourselves.

That’s always been a church with a lot of followers.  People might as well start owning it.
