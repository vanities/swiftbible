*By faith Noah, being
divinely warned of things not yet seen, moved with godly fear, prepared an ark
for the saving of his household, by which he condemned the world and became
heir of the righteousness which is according to faith.*

-- Hebrews 11:7

It’s not hard for Christians in this place and time to get
to feeling lonely.  All around us, the
world seems to be growing more and more wicked every day, those who pay any
attention to God’s will seem to be growing fewer and fewer, and the future
seems to be growing bleaker and bleaker.



Sadly, there’s nothing particularly unusual about our
position.  The true Christians in the
second and third centuries must have felt the same way as the church began its
drift into apostasy.  The righteous in
the last days of Judah were just as appalled by the wickedness of their
neighbors and just as fearful of the coming judgment.  However, of all the lonely men of God since
the beginning of time, none of them were as lonely as Noah.  We at least have a church with which we can
worship, but Noah preached for 100 years and never found anybody outside his
own family who would listen.

Despite this unpromising situation, Noah demonstrated his
faith in his life in several different ways. 
First, he was surrounded by people that were bent on doing evil
continually, yet he himself never ceased to be righteous.  Today, we struggle with this.  The devil is too often able to convince us
that what is popular must also be right, and so we allow the wicked around us
to drag us down to their level.  Let us
instead use the word for our standard, rather than imitating the behavior of
those who have forgotten it.

Second, Noah never forgot the judgment to come.  He believed God’s warning of a great flood
even though he had never seen rain, and he spent 100 years of his life
diligently preparing for it.  We too have
been warned that judgment is coming.  It
may well come upon our nation for its wickedness, but in the last day, it will
certainly come upon the entire earth. 
Like Noah, we must listen to God’s warning and take the steps that will
protect us from His wrath.

Finally, Noah trusted in God’s salvation.  We remember that it rained for 40 days and 40
nights, but we forget that the waters did not begin to subside until the
seventh month, and it was a full 14 months before Noah was able to emerge from
the ark.  In that time, there was plenty
of opportunity to doubt, but there is no indication in the text that Noah’s
faith ever wavered.  Like him, let us not
trust in the wisdom of the world that is so evident about us, but in the
salvation that only the invisible God can provide.
