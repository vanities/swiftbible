I’ve been maintaining *His
Excellent Word* since February 2014, but for about the past six months or
so, something hasn’t seemed right to me. 
For the first three years of the blog’s existence, my pageview trend was
a fairly typical exponential growth curve: 
1887 views in March 2014, 3723 in March 2015, 12,612 views in March
2016, and 28,820 views in March 2017.

However, in mid-2017, the exponential growth curve suddenly
came to a screeching halt.  July 2017 was
the last time I got more than 15,000 views in a month.  Part of that was due to posting less while I
was moving, but now that I’ve returned to blogging regularly, the old
viewership patterns still haven’t returned.



This puzzled and frustrated me.  It seemed like something had changed, but I
wasn’t sure what.  Then, I started
getting comments from various friends. 
They said things like, “I hadn’t seen any of your posts on Facebook for
a while, but then I stumbled across one and realized that there were several
months’ worth of posts I hadn’t read.” 
Since I’ve been no less diligent to share posts on FB recently than I
was a year ago, I found this extremely strange.

In fact, it got me to thinking.  The vast majority of my blog traffic has always come from FB
links.  What if my abrupt decline in
readership is due to something that FB has begun doing?

To test this hypothesis, I decided to compare two of my
recent high-readership posts to two similarly high-readership posts from six
months ago, to see what statistical differences might emerge.  I did this at about 3 pm on March 6th;
obviously the numbers will have changed slightly since.  Consider these two pairs of posts; see if you
come to the same conclusion I did.

Not too hard to spot, is it? 
My *HEW* links today are getting
a fraction (35 percent for the first pair, 27 percent for the second) of the
exposure that similar links (as evaluated by page views, likes, and shares) got
nine months ago.  My readership has
plummeted because FB isn’t showing my posts to nearly as many people, even
though FB use patterns haven’t changed that much overall.  Also, 150 more people (out of 600 total
likes) have liked my blog’s FB page than had in early May 2017, so that should
be generating more views too.

I have trouble seeing the logic to this from a business
perspective.  FB depends on content that
people find interesting.  It should be
featuring posts that get lots of likes and shares because more people will want
to like and share them, increasing user engagement.  I should be no less useful to FB than I was a
year ago.

Admittedly, it could be that FB is punishing me for not
paying to promote my posts.  However, I
doubt this.  Most of the posts on my news
feed aren’t paid.  Just now, I had to
scroll through 30 links and updates before I found one that was sponsored.  Everything before that was ordinary chatter from
my friends.  FB has not reduced the
exposure of unpaid posts by two-thirds, so why would it reduce my exposure that
much simply because I’m not paying either?

I could speculate about other reasons why FB might be doing
this, but I wouldn’t have any basis for such speculation.  It is worth noting, though, that FB has
decided recently that *The Babylon Bee* is a
fake-news site (somebody’s satire detector is broken, I guess) and has begun
including debunking links from Snopes under posts like “CNN has purchased
heavy-duty washing machines to spin their news.”  I feel justified in saying that FB is not as
content-neutral as it used to be, and how that might affect my content is
unclear.

Regardless, it is clear that FB, for whatever reason, has
begun hiding my writing from people who would like to see it.  I don’t appreciate that.  If you don’t appreciate that either, here’s
what you can do.  On the blog’s main
page, there’s a quartet of black buttons on the upper-right-hand side that
slide out when you mouse over them (I cannot, alas, figure out how to make the
black buttons emerge on a phone).  The
top one looks like a radio signal, and if you click on it, it will give you the
opportunity to subscribe via email or a couple of different RSS feeds.  Either do the RSS-feed magic or enter your
e-mail address, and FB can’t keep you from seeing anything I write.

Many thanks, and keep reading!
