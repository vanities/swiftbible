Last week, Charli Couchman posted a picture of the attendees
of the first Hymninar (in Columbia, MO in 1997) on the Sumphonia Facebook
page.  I was one of said attendees, so I
was in it, in all of my scrawny 18-year-old glory.  My first reaction was to reply with a snarky,
“Little did I know what I was getting into!” 
My second reaction was to do some serious thinking about providence.

“Providence”, of course, is not a word that appears in the
Bible, but it identifies a thoroughly Biblical concept:  God intervening in the world in
non-miraculous ways.  So far as I can
tell, the difference between providence and miracle is that the former is uncertain,
but the latter is unquestionable.  Sick
people gradually recover all the time, but demons don’t cast themselves out.

As a result, I can’t be certain that what I’m about to
describe is providential, but I have my suspicions.



Before about May 1997, I had never given a moment’s thought
to writing hymns.  I would not have gone
to the Hymninar if Craig Roberts had not invited me.  However, it is no exaggeration to say that
his invitation transformed my life, in ways even more fundamental than the
immediately obvious.  Let me explain.

If it had not been for the 1997 Hymninar, I would not have
gone to R.J. Stevens’ Oklahoma singing school when the Hymninar was folded into
it.  If not for the connections with
Texan Christians that I made attending the singing school, I would not have
gone to the University of Texas for law school. 
If I had not gone to UT Law, I would not have met and married my wife,
nor would I have met and roomed with David Raif.  If I had not known David, I would not have
followed him in the preacher-training program at the Dowlen Road Church of
Christ.  If I had not been a Dowlen Road
alumnus, I would not have been invited to preach for the church in Joliet (one
of my mentors, David Banning, was the brother-in-law of one of the elders at
Joliet).  In short, the most consequential event of my life is a conversation I don't remember having.

This is to say nothing, of course, of the impact that the
’97 Hymninar had on me as a hymnist. 
I’ve always loved to write, and it may be that I would have turned my
hand to hymns eventually, but my work would not have been the same.  Sans Hymninar, I would not have collaborated
with Charli on “Exalted” in 1999.  I
would not have worked on “Savior and Friend”, “Servant’s Song”, and “The Rock
of My Heart” with Craig, Richard Morrison, and Glenda Schales respectively, in 2000.  With the exception of Craig, I wouldn’t even
have known those Christians had I not gone to the Hymninar.

Nor is it certain that I would even have become a preacher
without that one invitation.  In the
Hymninar-less world, I would likely have followed my brother to law school at
the University of Virginia, with consequences I can’t even imagine.  It is undeniably true, though, that my life
would have been dramatically different.

I can’t say for sure that God did all that, much less how He
did it.  However, I repeat that I have my
suspicions.

None of this was obvious to me at the time.  In fact, I spent a great deal of that time
struggling to take my life in a completely different direction than it
eventually went.  Many of those years
were miserable, as I confronted dead end after dead end like a rat stuck in a
maze.  Only in retrospect is the arc of
the story clear.

Honestly, all of this makes a much stronger case for divine
purpose for the individual than I’m comfortable with.  It grates on me a little bit when people say,
“Everything happens for a reason.”  I still
don’t think that’s categorically true, but there certainly seems to be a
purpose behind a lot of the things that happened to me.

To this day, though, I don’t have any idea what that purpose
was and is.  I don’t think it’s limited
to the hymns that are the most obvious fruit of my Hymninar attendance.  Instead, it looks like my going Joliet was
part of the purpose too, even though I never imagined that I might live in
Joliet (or Illinois at all, for that matter) up until about two weeks before I
got the invitation to try out.

Why here, though?  I
believe that I’ve changed lives in the 10 years I’ve been up here.  Presumably I could say the same if I had
spent 10 years preaching the gospel anywhere. 
That’s what the gospel’s supposed to do, after all.  There are people I can look back on and say,
“If not for me, he. . .”  I don’t know,
though, whether another preacher in my shoes would have been equally important.

Indeed, I wonder if the greatest impact I have had/will have
here is one that I will never recognize. 
I’m sure that Craig had no intention of transforming my life back in
1997.  So far as I know, his thought
process was pretty simple.  He liked
writing hymns, and he figured that because I liked to write, I might like to
write hymns too.  Why not ask?  Marriages and sermons were no part of his
thinking, yet the results in those areas proved even more important to me than
the hymns did.

It could be that I’m here to play Craig’s role for somebody
else.  Who can say whether I will launch
them on a course they never dreamed of by making a single offhand remark?  Who can say whether I already have?

Of course, this has nothing to do with my super-duper
charisma.  It has to do with God, who is
able to use whatever tools are to hand to accomplish His will, even when the
tools have no idea that they’re being used. 
This isn’t about me.  It’s about
all of us.  When God is involved, it can
easily be that the most important thing we ever do is something we don’t notice
doing.  Maybe some Christian janitor who
spends his life sweeping floors will have more effect on the Lord’s people than
all the hymns I ever write.

I can’t say to each of you that you are where you are for a
purpose.  Providence doesn’t allow for
that kind of certainty.  However, I can’t
say that you’re not, either.  In fact, I
can’t do better than the similarly unsure-but-suspicious Mordecai in Esther
4:14, “And who knows whether you have not attained royalty for such a time as
this?”

As with Esther, so with all of us.  God is accomplishing His will, even if His
name is never mentioned.
