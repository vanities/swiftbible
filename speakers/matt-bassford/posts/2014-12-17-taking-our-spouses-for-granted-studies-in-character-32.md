*So Jacob served seven
years for Rachel, and they seemed only a few days to him because of the love he
had for her.*

*--Genesis 29:20*

One of the most two-edged traits that human beings possess
is our ability to get used to anything. 
Sometimes, this can be a tremendous blessing.  Every day, people with disabilities do things
that the able-bodied consider heroic. 
Almost uniformly, though, these people will say that they aren’t heroes,
and they mean it.  They do what they do
without much thought, because they’ve gotten used to their problems.



This same trait, though, can itself be a problem, when we
become used to our blessings instead of our burdens.  We can work for something, pray for something,
wrestle for something, but then once we get it, we forget how we felt before
and start taking it for granted.

Perhaps nowhere is this a bigger problem than in our
relationships with our spouses.  When we
look at Jacob and see how he ended up working 14 years for the right to marry
Rachel, we think it’s impressive, but we don’t think it’s bizarre.  I remember perfectly well what being single
was like.  It was miserable!  I’m sure I bored God to tears with my
continual prayers that He send me a woman to marry, and despite all of those
prayers, a big part of me was convinced I’d never find somebody.  If some man had come along and offered me the
woman of my dreams in exchange for seven years of labor, I may well have taken
him up on the spot.

Now that I’ve met the love of my life, now that I’ve been
married to her for almost ten years, does the way that I treat her line up
with how badly I wanted to find her?  Do
I take time daily to appreciate and praise her? 
Am I careful to make sure that all of her needs are met?  Do I put what she wants to do before what I
want to do?  Or instead, do I just take
her for granted?

For the sake of our marriages and our souls, we all need to
be constantly assessing ourselves on this scale.  We need to ask if we show Jacob’s love for
Rachel to our spouses.  In most
marriages, the truth is that we actually need our partners more after we tie
the knot, not less.  However, we’re
blinded to our need by the fact that we have them.

Some people don’t realize this until it’s too late.  I knew an older Christian in Texas who had
been in what could only be described as a bitter marriage.  He and his wife had quarreled for more than
fifty years. Then she died, and the scales fell from his eyes.  He realized how much he missed her and needed
her.  He even returned to the Lord so
that he could see her again someday. 
Just imagine how much different their marriage would have been if he’d
seen that before she passed instead of afterward!  We have the opportunity he lost.  Let’s not waste it.
