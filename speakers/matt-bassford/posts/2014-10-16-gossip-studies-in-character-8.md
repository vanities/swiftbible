*And Ham, the father of
Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and told his two brothers outside.*

--Genesis 9:22

In many ways, the book of Genesis is a series of dismal
recorded firsts.  In Genesis 9:22, we
come to another first.  As far as the
Scriptural record reveals to us, Ham was the first gossip.  When his father Noah foolishly became drunk,
Ham looked upon him in his undignified nakedness and ran and told his brothers
about it so they could share in the amusement of seeing their father
dishonored.

 When Noah awoke, he realized what Ham had done, became
angry, and cursed his own grandson because of Ham’s wickedness.  Thousands of years later, this curse caused
the descendants of Canaan to be subjugated by the children of Israel in the
conquest of Canaan.  Ham’s gossip had
disastrous and far-reaching consequences.

Gossips today can surely understand what Ham found so
irresistible about betraying his father to his brothers.  Human beings are social creatures.  We are always interested in the doings of our
families, friends, and co-workers.  We
love to talk about them, especially when the events we discuss are shameful or
cast others in a bad light.  And so we
lean in a little closer, lower our voices, and gossip.

When two people gossip together, they’re really forming an
alliance against the subject of their gossip. 
By sharing in the story of the unrighteous thing he’s done, they confirm
their righteousness in their own eyes. 
The subtext of any gossip is, “Have you heard about the awful thing that
Brother So-and-So has done?  It’s a good
thing that you and I are good and not wicked like Brother So-and-So.”

This is why gossip always reveals a malicious and unloving
heart.  If the gossips were truly
concerned about the other who had sinned, they’d be talking privately to him in
an attempt to turn him from the error of his way.  If they were truly concerned about God and
His desires, they’d be doing the same thing.

As it is, though, despite whatever pious horror gossips may
express at someone else’s misbehavior, they’re actually secretly glad that the
misdeed has occurred.  It’s given them an
opportunity to sharpen their claws at someone else’s expense and establish
their own holier-than-thou credentials. 
In fact, deep inside, gossips want the bad behavior to continue.  That way, it will provide fodder for many
gossip sessions to come, with all kinds of displays of pursed lips and shaking
heads.

As righteous and justified as the tale-bearer feels, the
harm he does is immense.  Nothing will
destroy a church faster than just a handful of truly dedicated gossips.  As they criticize the church leadership, make
public others’ private struggles, and repeat any rumor that comes their way,
they sever the bonds of love that tie that church together.  The Lord hates gossips.  Let’s be sure that we are not included in
their number.
