**OVERVIEW.** This is my favorite design for a
wide-margin Bible, period, and its physical construction does it no disservice.

Believe it or not, I actually bought this Bible.  I’ve been after Crossway for years to send me
a copy of their Single-Column Journaling Bible, but a few months back, I
finally gave up and bit the bullet.  As a
result, I’m under no obligation to review the Bible honestly.  However, I don’t think I’ll start lying about
Bibles today anyway.



**VITAL STATISTICS**

**Translation:**  English Standard Version (2011)

**Cover:**  Genuine Leather

**Binding:**  Sewn

**Trim Size:**  6 ¼” x 
8”

**Text Block Width:**  1 ⅜
”

**Weight:**  2 pounds, 3 ounces

**Column:**  Single

**Layout:**  Paragraphed

**Print Size:**  7.5

**Words of Christ:**  Black

**Cross-References:**  Minimal

**Ribbon Marker:**  1

**SIZE.** The SCJB isn’t as small as its sibling,
the Crossway Double-Column Journaling Bible, but it’s still relatively petite
for a Bible with room to write in.  I can
fit it in the glove box of my Corolla without moving things around, though the
cover does get stuck in the dashboard. 
What’s more, at a smidge over two pounds, this is a moderately sized
Bible.  You’d probably need a pretty
hefty purse to hide it in, but a backpack would do the job nicely.  If you want to carry the SCJB off to the
outdoors to journal while meditating on the glories of nature, it will serve
well in such a role.

**COVER.** Because I bought this Bible rather than
requesting it from Crossway, I was able to get it in genuine leather.  If you look at crossway.org, you won’t even
see the genuine-leather edition there (it’s black as opposed to the brown
natural cowhide that is available). 
Instead, it’s available only through christianbook.com.  If you’re planning to put a lot of miles on a
Bible, in my opinion, there’s no reason not to upgrade to one bound in the skin
of a dead animal.

In years past, I’ve spent a lot of time hating on Crossway’s
genuine leather.  These days, I’ve had to
take it all back.  All of the genuine-leather
Crossway Bibles I’ve handled recently have had well executed, lovable
covers.  The SCJB is no exception.  Instead of having a hard, glossy, plasticky
shell, this leather has a matte, almost soft, finish.  You can scratch it with a fingernail if
you’re of a mind.  It smells like leather
instead of chemicals.  Basically, I like
the genuine leather well enough that I wouldn’t feel a need to upgrade to
cowhide, or even calfskin or goatskin if it were offered.  Bravo, Crossway!

As is usually the case with genuine-leather Bibles, the SCJB
has a paste-down binding.  Instead of
being attached to the book block with little tabs of leather, this cover is
attached with leatherette endpapers.  I
was glad to see the leatherette; endpapers made of leatherette are more durable
than those made of thick paper.

Also, the bookboard in the cover seems to be unusually thin
(if it’s there at all; I can’t really tell). 
Combined with the width of the Bible, this makes for a very flexible
paste-down cover.  As a result, it should
be less prone to endpaper splits than paste-down Bibles usually are.

I’m not sure where this Bible would fail.  Most likely, the leatherette on the corners
would start coming up.  However, some
white glue would probably postpone the day of destruction for a good long
time.  Sure; the cover is probably not
going to be the equal of an edge-lined Jongbloed product.  However, the price isn’t equal to that of an
edge-lined Jongbloed product either.  For
what it is, I really like this cover.

**TEXT BLOCK.** Sewn, like nearly every Bible Crossway
produces.  This is always important, but
I think it’s particularly important in a wide-margin Bible.  Nobody wants their carefully annotated text
of 2 Peter to fall out and flutter away on the breeze!  In this case, I think the book block will
prove to be as durable as the cover will.

Interestingly, even though this is a genuine-leather Bible,
the edges of the book block aren’t gilded. 
I suppose Crossway left the edges plain for the sake of journalers who
want to write/draw/paint on them.  I’m
not one of those people, but I don’t mind the look.  There’s a simplicity to a Bible without
gilding that accords well with my quasi-Puritan religious aesthetic.  My religious tradition likes everything as
plain as possible (with the exception of props for vacation Bible school), and
the SCJB fits right in.  Cromwell would
have approved.

**VALUE.** According to Christianbook.com, the Bible
“retails” for $95.  However (at least
when I was writing this review), they’re perfectly willing to sell the Bible to
you for $44, and they’re the only people who can sell the genuine-leather SCJB
at all.  In other words, I’m not sure
that “retail price” means anything when nobody actually sells it at the retail
price.

Regardless, I paid that for the Bible, and I’d happily do it
again, even though my life is generally filled with free Bibles.  I like inexpensive Bibles with polyurethane
covers for a lot of purposes.  However, I
don’t want a poly cover on my wide-margin Bible.  Properly used, a wide-margin will be your
companion for years or decades, and you don’t want the cover falling apart on
you when you’ve got the Bible half-filled with notes.  You never know, but I don’t think the cover
of my SCJB will do that, and if I ever start using the ESV for my primary preaching
translation, this is the Bible I will use.

Small, sturdily constructed, pleasant to touch, and redolent
of leather, all at a reasonable price. 
What more could any Bible geek want out of a Bible?  I think this one is entitled to a 9.5/10,
with the only mark against it being that it’s not Pitt Minion size.
