Life is hard; that’s kinda how it goes.  We live in a world that has been marred forever
by the presence of sin, and the devil has a deep and abiding interest in all of
us.  Put those two things together, and it’s
certain that at some point in our lives, we’re going to encounter difficulty.

However, it is not the presence or absence of trial in our
lives that defines our relationship with God. 
It is how we handle that trial. 
Some Christians shatter whenever they encounter adversity.  It ruins their earthly lives and drives them
from the Lord.  Others, by contrast,
blossom as the result of trial.  They
learn the lessons it has to teach and draw closer to God in consequence.

If trial is inevitable and it can have such dramatic effects
on us, we need to learn how to deal with it. 
With this in mind, then, let’s see what the Scripture says about
overcoming trial.



**Faces of Trial**

Before we start talking about what we ought to do when faced
with trial, though, we have to define what we’re talking about.  The devil, you see, loves to sneak up on us,
to involve us in a fight for our spiritual lives when we don’t even realize
we’re fighting.  That’s why we need to
learn to identify the faces of trial, to recognize when we’re in a situation
where we must take particular care.

The first of these situations is **TEMPTATION.** We see a
familiar grouping of such temptations in 1 John 2:15-16.  Sometimes the devil appeals to us through our
flesh.  He tries to get the human-animal
part of each one of us to take over.  He
wants us to live basically the same way that dogs live, satisfying our desires
for food or sex or rest whenever we feel like it.

At other times, he appeals to us through our eyes.  Dogs don’t care about material possessions,
but we do, and it’s very easy for the devil to convince people to devote their
lives to the heaping up of more stuff. 
Even if we can’t get it, Satan wants us to constantly think about it.

Finally, we come to the pride of life.  Humans are social creatures.  We care about the opinions and the esteem of
others, and the devil can use that to manipulate us into doing evil.  Lying, for instance, is often a pride-based
sin.  The truth would make us look bad,
so we choose deceit instead.  Also, most
types of anger and contentiousness fall into this category too.  At bottom, we all want our way because
getting our way shows how important we are. 
No matter who we are, pride, along with the two kinds of lust, is always
going to be a feature of our spiritual landscape.

Similarly, we often encounter trial as Christians because of
**OPPOSITION.**  Consider
the warning of the Lord in Matthew 10:34-36. 
We can confidently expect that following Jesus will disrupt our
relationships with others in our lives, even those who are closest to us.  Some Christians are blessed to have no one in
their immediate families but other Christians. 
Most of us, however, aren’t in that position, and in spiritual things,
our relationship with unbelieving family can range from a wary truce to
full-blown hostilities.  We have a
responsibility to let them know where they stand according to the Scripture,
and when they choose not to obey the gospel, the Scripture will always stand
between us.  The closer the relationship,
the more painful that becomes.

If this is true even of our family members, how much more
will it be true with those to whom we aren’t related?  Conflict can arise with the godless and
immoral people of the world, with those who claim to follow Jesus but aren’t actually
New-Testament Christians, and even with those who are New-Testament Christians
but disagree with us in some matter.  In
all these cases, the only way we can dodge problems is by concealing the truth
about what we believe, and once we’ve done that, we’ve provoked a conflict with
the Lord Himself!  The true disciple
cannot avoid opposition.

Next, Christians often face trial as the result of **ILL HEALTH.**This happened even to the apostle Paul, as he records in 2
Corinthians 12:7-8.  Even though these are
thorns in the flesh, they can still pose tremendous spiritual problems.  First of all comes the suffering involved
with illness.  Nobody likes hurting all
the time, and as Paul observes in 1 Corinthians 12, when one part of the body
suffers, the whole body suffers with it.

Second, illness, especially chronic illness, is often
accompanied by frustration and depression. 
Unless we’ve suffered from a particular ailment from birth, we can
remember a time when we felt better and were able to do more, and the
realization that we aren’t there anymore is often quite painful.  Once we start dwelling on that, gloom begins
to pervade the entirety of our lives, so that we aren’t able to see even the
things that remain within our capacities. 
We get so used to saying, “I can’t” that it becomes automatic before we
even check to see if we can.

This in turn makes us less useful to the Lord than we ought
to be.  In my time as a Christian, I’ve
seen more than a few elderly people go into spiritual retirement.  They aren’t able to do what they used to, so
rather than looking for ways in which they can still be useful, they leave all
of the work of the church to others.  In
such times, friends, we must remember that even though Jesus doesn’t expect us
to do more than we’re able, He doesn’t tolerate us doing less either.

Finally, we can encounter trial in the form of **THE CARES OF THE WORLD.** Jesus warns us against these as part of
the parable of the sower, in Mark 4:18-19. 
Interestingly enough, these cares are often the result of wanting to do
the right thing.  We want to provide for
ourselves and our families, but work stresses us out.  We want our children to have the best of
everything, but shuttling them to practices and meetings only adds to our to-do
lists.  We want to look after family
members in ill health, but as the tenure of our caregiving stretches on into
weeks, months, or years, the burden of that responsibility slowly becomes
unbearable.

Once again, the problem with these trials is that the devil
will use them to separate us from the Lord. 
Sure, we’re doing all these important things, but we aren’t seeking His
kingdom and His righteousness first. 
Brethren, the word the Lord uses to describe disciples like that is
“unfruitful”.  No matter how good the
excuse, it’s never acceptable for us to put God on the back burner.  If we pretend to ourselves that we’re only
doing so temporarily, we will soon find that the devil has a way of getting
temporarily to stretch on indefinitely. 
This is no less dangerous to our souls than getting caught up in an
adulterous affair would be, and we need to recognize the danger for what it is.

**Jesus and Trial**

As grim as all of this has been, the presence of trial in
our lives is a hopeful thing for Christians, not a hopeless one, and we can
first of all take heart from our Lord Jesus. 
He faced many trials but overcame them all.  The first strategy He employed was **RELIANCE ON SCRIPTURE.** Consider His behavior in Matthew
4:3-4.  Whenever the devil tempted Him,
He didn’t have to go unroll a scroll to find a relevant passage.  He knew them all already.

When we see how useful the word wass in getting Jesus
through trial, it’s hardly surprising that the devil so consistently uses trial
to try to separate us from the word.  He
tries to convince us that we’re too busy to spend even ten minutes with the
Scripture every day.  He tries to heap so
much on our plates that Wednesday and even Sunday services get crowded
out.  He wants to keep us out of the
Bible, and he wants to keep the Bible out of us.

Brethren, I don’t know about y’all, but whenever I see that
the devil wants me to do something, that tells me that it’s a good idea to do
exactly the opposite.  The hard times of
our lives are when we need to spend more time with the Scriptures, not
less!  My experience with daily Bible
reading is that the times when I am lowest are when my heart is most open to
the word.  I pay more attention, I learn
more, and I’m changed more.  Similarly,
if life is difficult, is there a better place to be than here in the presence
of God with all our brethren?  In trial,
let’s turn to the truth.

Next, we see Jesus respond to trial with **DETERMINATION.** Let’s look at Luke’s description of Him
in Luke 9:51.  Jesus knew perfectly well
that going to Jerusalem meant that He was going to be tortured and killed, yet
He set His face toward the city and went there anyway.

Too often, though, we allow the fear of pain to deter us
from what we know we ought to be doing.  It’s
gonna hurt to give up our favorite sin, so we continue to cling to it even
though we know it will eventually destroy us. 
Our friends are going to make our lives miserable if we’re open about
our faith, so we clam up instead.  It’s
easier to do nothing than to stretch the limits of our failing bodies, so we do
nothing.  We’re so worn out from looking
after our families that we simply don’t have the energy to look after the
things of the kingdom too.

Friends, the Bible has a lot to say about people of God who
back away from tests, who choose the easy rather than the hard road, who do
what they can to preserve peace and ease in their lives above all else.  The problem is that none of it’s good.  The Hebrews writer tells us that Christians
who shrink back are shrinking back to destruction.  We’re not going to find salvation by stopping
short in front of the trial.  Our hope
lies in pressing on through it.

Of course, in setting His face toward Jerusalem and indeed
toward the cross, Jesus employed **PRAYER** to
succeed.  Look at the Hebrews writer’s
explanation of this in Hebrews 5:7.  In
the garden of Gethsemane, Jesus was very clearly not OK with what was about to
happen.  Even with all of His awesome
strength and character, the thought of the next 24 hours drove Him to His
knees.  The key, though, is not that the
Lord was driven to His knees.  The key is
what He did when He found Himself there. 
Prayer was more than enough to turn His defeat into victory.

So too for all of us. 
I’ve heard it said that the Lord never gives us more than we can
handle.  That’s not in the Bible, and the
apostle Paul, for one, disagreed with it pretty explicitly.  In 2 Corinthians 1, he speaks of being
burdened beyond his strength so that he would learn to trust not in himself,
but in God.  Sure, there’s always going
to be a way of escape, but sometimes the way of escape is to flee to the throne
of God and ask for His help.

I don’t think I’m overstating the case when I say that
prayer should be a reflex for Christians. 
Whenever we find ourselves in trouble, the very first thing we should
think to do is to pray about it, and every time, it will be the single most
valuable thing we could do.  I guarantee
that on the Day of Judgment, there will not be a single soul regretting that
they prayed too much!

Because Jesus approached His trials in this way, the result
for Him was **VICTORY.** Let’s read here from Hebrews 12:1-3.  There’s so much that I love in this passage,
but one of my favorite parts of it is its description of the attitude of
Jesus.  He was so focused on the joys of
heaven that he was able not only to endure the cross, but to despise its
shame.  He was being publicly tortured to
death in the most humiliating way possible, but figuratively speaking, Jesus
looked at the devil and said, “That all you got?”  As dreadful as the cross was, it was nowhere
near enough to deter the Lord, and because He endured, He received the joys
He’d been anticipating the whole time.

If we keep the mind of Christ, we will see the same
result.  Sure, if we start looking around
everywhere but heaven, there’s no longer any compelling reason to persevere
through trial.  However, if we keep our
eyes fixed on heaven, there is no other choice but to persevere.  Every one of us has access to everything we
need to gain the eternal prize.  The more
we live for that, the more likely we are to succeed.

Momentarily here, we’re going to partake of the Lord’s
Supper.  As we do so, let’s first
remember Jesus.  Let’s remember the
character and faith that carried Him through crucifixion and death to eternal
glory.  Let’s resolve too to make His
character and faith our own.  We too will
overcome, if only we follow in the footsteps of Christ.  Let’s consider this as we partake.

**Further Help**

In the final part of our time together this morning, let’s
examine some more help in trial that the Scripture encourages us to seek.  First, the Bible tells us, we are to learn
from **THE EXAMPLE OF THE PAST.** James raises this point in James
5:10-11.  Particularly, James cites the
example of Job, the single character in Scripture who, more than any other, is
synonymous with trial.  And yet, despite
the great suffering that Job faced, in the end, his story reveals not God’s
failure, but God’s faithfulness.  God is
unfailingly compassionate and merciful toward those who seek Him.

The applications of Job’s life to our lives are both obvious
and profound.  No matter how Job felt in
mid-trial, God never abandoned him and was constantly looking to guide his feet
into a better future.  God promised to be
faithful to Job, and in the end, He kept His promise.  God has promised to be faithful to us too,
and even if, like Job, we are in mid-trial with no end in sight, we can be
certain from Job’s story that God will keep His word to us too.

This, in fact, is one of the greatest lessons of
Scripture.  Contrary to the preaching of
the Joel Osteens of the world, the Bible is not a road map to Easy Street.  I can’t think of a single godly man or woman
in Scripture who avoided trial.  In fact,
most of them faced severe trial. 
However, I also can’t think of a single godly man or woman whom God
betrayed.  As for them, so for us.

Second, James also encourages us to lean on **OUR BRETHREN** in trial.  This appears in James 5:16.  God is certain to help us, and He commonly
extends His help through other Christians.

Satan, of course, is no dummy.  He knows this perfectly well.  In trial, then, we can be sure that the devil
is going to seek to separate us from that help. 
He’s going to tempt us to avoid rather than to seek other
Christians.  The more isolated we are,
the more vulnerable we will be.

And yet, even though there’s no way it’s going to work out
well spiritually, many Christians pull back from their brethren when they’re facing
trial.  They’re too busy to make it to
services.  They don’t have the emotional
energy to maintain those relationships. 
They want to keep up the church front and avoid confessing their
frailties to the people who care about them.

Friends, that’s a terrible mistake.  In fact, it defeats God’s plan for the
church.  It’s always enjoyable to
associate with other disciples, but we don’t really need one another when
things are going smoothly and we’re sailing along to heaven.  Instead, we need one another when we’re
hurting and the path ahead is not clear. 
How foolish, then, when we’re in that situation, to turn away from the
very people whom God gave us to help us! 
Sure, there’s always the risk that other Christians will let us down,
but we must never allow the risks of seeking help to deter us from reaching
out.  The outcome if we open up is not
certain, but the outcome if we keep to ourselves is all too certain!

Finally, we can endure through trial by considering **THE FUTURE.**Peter takes on this topic in 1 Peter 5:10.  It’s important to focus, as Jesus did, on the
joy set before us, but it’s equally important to remember how close that joy
is.  Peter here tells us that God will
bless us after we’ve suffered a little while. 
Similarly, Paul in 2 Corinthians 4 describes the severe trials of the
first-century church as “momentary, light affliction”.

Of course, the devil will try to deceive us about this as
well.  One of the odd characteristics of
trial is the way that time seems to telescope when we’re in the middle of
it.  Maybe only a few days have passed,
but our subjective sense is that we’ve been stuck inside this thing
forever.  If we get too caught up in that
illusion, it’s easy for us to lose hope, and once that happens, he’s got us.

Instead, we have to keep the timeline of trial in
perspective.  Even the most terrible
trial this life has to offer can’t last for more than a little while, and in
severity, it can’t be more than momentary, light affliction.  What matters is not the tiny little trials of
this life.  Instead, it is the lasting
comfort of God and the eternal weight of glory that our endurance through trial
is storing up for us.   The worst that the devil can do is always
outweighed by the best that God has to offer, and every one of us can be
certain that the best is right around the corner.  Hold on, and we’ve got it made!
