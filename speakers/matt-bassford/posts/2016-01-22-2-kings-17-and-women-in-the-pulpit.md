Last Sunday, almost all of the students in my preschool
class were sick, so I sat in on the auditorium Bible class instead.  The subject of the day was 2 Kings 17.  I hesitate to describe 2 Kings 17 as my
favorite chapter in 2 Kings, if only because the subject matter is so grim, but
it is unquestionably one of the most useful. 
Along with Romans 1, it is one of the most complete descriptions of
apostasy in the Bible.

As we were studying, one verse in particular stood out to
me:  2 Kings 17:15.  It reads, “They rejected His statutes and His
covenant which He made with their fathers and His warnings with which He warned
them. And they followed vanity and became vain, and went after the nations
which surrounded them, concerning which the LORD had commanded them not to do
like them.” (NASB, and so throughout) 
This event comes fairly late in the decline that the chapter
chronicles.  By this point in time, the
Israelites no longer sought to add to or evade the law of God.  They flatly rejected it because they wanted
to be like the nations around them.

When I read this text, I cannot help but be reminded of the
trend among churches of Christ today toward inviting women to preach.  I certainly find myself on the more conservative
side of our religious tradition.  I have
many disagreements with my brethren in the Restoration movement about the
restrictive nature of the silence of the Scripture, in areas as diverse as the
use of instrumental music in worship and cooperation among churches.  I believe that we honor the silence of God by
not presuming to act; they disagree.



This is something different. 
This is taking a plain statement in Scripture (in 1 Timothy 2:11-12,
which reads, “A woman must quietly receive instruction with entire
submissiveness. But I do not allow a woman to teach or exercise authority over
a man, but to remain quiet.”) and rejecting it entirely.  This is outright rebellion against the
expressed will of God.

Defenders of women’s right to appear in the pulpit attempt
to justify their position using the Bible. 
In particular, they like to employ the old false-teacher trick of
pitting the Scripture against itself. 
Their passage of choice here is Galatians 3:28, which observes, “There
is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free man, there is neither
male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus.”

I have always understood this text to mean that all
Christians are of equal worth, even if they find themselves in different
positions and have different roles.  They
argue that it means that in Christ, those positions and roles have been
abolished.  A woman can take the pulpit
because, after all, we are all one in Christ Jesus.

I think this argument proves more than its proponents intend
(or maybe not).  After all, if the
distinctions between male and female have been erased in Christ, then no
obstacle remains to Christian men marrying Christian men, or Christian women
marrying Christian women.  “Let the
marriage bed be held in honor among all!” they might well exclaim.  Just as this reading of Galatians 3:28
cancels out 1 Timothy 2:11-12, so too it can cancel out 1 Corinthians
6:9-10.  If, on the other hand, God-given
gender roles remain important in marriage, what basis do we have for denying
that they remain important in the church?

In reality, though, everyone knows that the real motivation
behind women in the pulpit is not Galatians 3:28.  It is “the nations which surround us”.  The secularist elite in America is determined
to erase all distinction between men and women, even in areas where it is
plainly foolish to do so.  Take, for
instance, the recent decision that women in the U.S. military should serve in
combat roles.  It’s biological fact that
women as a whole are physically weaker and less capable then men.  The average woman pitted against the average
man in combat is going to lose.  All
sorts of horrible things are going to happen to women who are placed on the
front lines.  This is as inevitable as
sunrise.  And yet, the leaders of our
society are so bent on following their ideology that they close their eyes to
reality.

I will admit that to my own human wisdom, women in the
pulpit is not as obviously a bad idea. 
My own wife, for instance, says that if she had been born a man, she
would be a preacher.  I believe that if
God allowed her in the pulpit, she’d make a fine preacher indeed.  She’s intelligent.  She’s good with people.  She’s well educated.  In high school and college, she was a
champion debater.  Most of all, she loves
the Lord with all her heart.  Free
Lauren!  Let her preach!  Why not?

Because of 1 Timothy 2:11-12.  That’s why not.

We do not reveal our faithfulness to the wisdom of God in
the areas where His wisdom and ours agree. 
When that’s the case, we have no trouble “obeying” Him, because
obedience coincides with what we wanted to do anyway.  Instead, we show our true colors when we must
choose between following His wisdom and following ours.  If we were really following ourselves all
along, that’s where God’s path and ours will diverge.

It’s not as though God’s refusal to allow women to teach or
exercise authority over a man is irrational. 
1 Timothy 2 lays out the logic quite clearly, and it goes all the way
back to Genesis.  However, it doesn’t
line up with the received wisdom of our time, and to all too many Christians,
the latter is more important.

Every Christian will affirm that Christ is Lord.  That is, after all, one of the requirements
for becoming a Christian in the first place. 
However, we prove His Lordship over us not by what we say, but by what
we do, not only when obedience is easy, but also when it’s hard.  1 Timothy 2:11-12 is part of the upward call
of God in Christ Jesus.  It’s up to us
whether we’re going to listen.
