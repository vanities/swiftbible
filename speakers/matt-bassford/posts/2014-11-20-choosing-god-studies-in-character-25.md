*“And if the woman is
not willing to follow you, then you will be released from this oath; only do
not take my son back there.”*

--Genesis 24:8

When we consider the life of Abraham, we see that he was
called the friend of God not because he was sinless, nor even because he was
particularly wise, but because he pursued the promises of God with steadfast
determination.  We see this in the way he
instructs the servant he sends to find a wife for his son Isaac.  He knows that God has promised him that
through Isaac, he will have numberless descendants, and also that his
descendants will receive the land of Canaan as an inheritance.  He also knows that the current inhabitants of
Canaan are godless and evil.



As a result, he calls one of his most trusted servants to
him and instructs him to return to the land of Abraham’s kinsmen to find a wife
for Isaac among them, because only a woman from his own relatives can be
trusted to maintain the family’s faith in God. 
The servant asks, quite reasonably, what he’s supposed to do if no woman
from Mesopotamia is willing to travel hundreds of miles through lawless country
on the word of a stranger.  Abraham replies
that come what may, Isaac must never leave Canaan.  His family and his faith will be continued,
some way, somehow, but he must remain in the promised land.

Sadly, this same tenacity is far from evident in the lives
of many of God’s people today.  We know
what God says.  We know that if we remain
faithful until death, we will receive the crown of life.  We know that if we seek the kingdom first,
all these things will be added to us. 
The problem is that we don’t always live like it.  We fear poverty, so we allow our jobs to take
us away from full commitment to the Lord. 
We fear unhappiness, so we cling to our pet sins rather than rejecting
them.  We become so distracted by the things
we see that we forget the things we cannot see.

Like Abraham, we must learn that sometimes the best way to
make decisions is to close our eyes. 
Nothing is certain about our situation or the consequences of our
actions.  The life that appears so barren
may hold limitless possibilities for joy; the financial future that seems so
desperate may offer enormous promise right around the corner.  We simply do not know, and we cannot allow
ourselves to be dissuaded by appearances. 
All that we do know is that God will fulfill His promises, even if we
can’t imagine how He could possibly do it.
