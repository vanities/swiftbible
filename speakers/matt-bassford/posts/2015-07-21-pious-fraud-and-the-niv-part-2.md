Last week, I wrote about a post I saw going around on
Facebook.  For clarity’s sake, I’m
repeating it here:

*Hello
beloved saints,*

*I’m
sure you know that NIV was published by Zondervan but is now OWNED by Harper
Collins, who also publishes the Satanic Bible and the Joy of Gay Sex.*



*The
NIV has now removed 64,575 words from the Bible including Jehovah, Calvary,
Holy Ghost and omnipotent to name but a few. . .*

*The
NIV has also now removed 45 complete verses. 
Most of us have the Bible on our devices and phones.*

*Try
and find these scriptures in NIV on your computer, phone or device right now if
you are in doubt:*

*Matthew
17:21, 18:11, 23:14; Mark 7:16, 9:44, 9:46; Luke 17:36, 23:17; John 5:4; Acts
8:37*

*.
. . you will not believe your eyes.*

*Refuse
to be blinded by Satan, and do not act like you just don’t care.  Let’s not forget what the Lord Jesus said in
John 10:10 (King James Version)*

I explained that even though many of the facts in the post
are true, its implication--that the NIV is being corrupted by a group of evil
conspirators who are out to destroy Christianity--is absurd.

However, a couple of my Facebook friends, people who are
neither fools nor Internet flame warriors, got very concerned about this.  I think they fell for it in part because it
didn’t occur to them that it might be part of somebody’s agenda to undermine
confidence in the NIV.

It’s not too hard, though, to identify this group and its
agenda by considering the logical structure of the post.  The 64,575 words that the NIV has “removed”
are words that were present in the KJV, at least, in the 1769 Blayney recension
of the KJV.  The 45 complete verses that
the NIV has removed are verses that were present in the KJV.

It’s the last three words of the post that really give the
game away, though.  John 10:10 isn’t
significantly different in any of the Bibles I own, even in the freer
translations such as the REB and NLT. 
However, it is important to the author of the post that this passage be
read in the KJV, because he believes that only the KJV represents the inspired
word of God.

I don’t have anything against King-James-Version-Only
(henceforth KJVO) believers as such. 
There are a number of arguments that one might make for using the KJV,
and several of them are worth considering. 
For instance, my own father-in-law prefers to use the KJV (though he
isn’t KJVO) because he finds its language to be more beautiful and reverent
than the language of more modern translations.

I can sympathize with that. 
There’s a cadence to the public reading of the KJV that no other
translation can match.  If I chose my
translation on the basis of beauty rather than utility, I would probably choose
the KJV too.  I'd be OK with that because I believe the KJV to be the word of God, just as any honest
translation is.

The Trinitarian Bible Society (not technically KJVO, but
pretty close) argues that because the manuscripts supporting KJV readings are
more numerous, though later, they should be preferred over the fewer, earlier
manuscripts that provide the textual basis for more modern translations.  I don’t find that argument persuasive, but at
least it is an argument made openly and in good faith.

By contrast, the argument quoted above seeks to
deceive.  Its author knows that there are
64,575 words in the KJV that the NIV does not include because he or somebody he
refers to as an authority went through and made a list.  Nobody would miss most of the words on that
list.  Nobody cares that the KJV uses
“unicorn” and the NIV doesn’t.

However, the author/list-maker selected the bare handful of
words whose omission would look the worst—“Holy Ghost”, “Jehovah”,
“Calvary”—and presented them as though they had been removed and not
replaced.  Removing “Holy Ghost” and
replacing it with “Holy Spirit” doesn’t move my needle (nor that of most other
Christians), but removing “Holy Ghost” and replacing it with nothing sure
would!  The author wishes us to believe
that the latter rather than the former has occurred.  He aims to mislead.

Likewise with the missing verses.  If I thought that some set of translators had
started insidiously removing verses from the Bible, I would be extremely
concerned about that.  I would search
until I found an answer.  In the case of
the “missing” verses in the NIV, though, all you need to figure out the truth
is 30 seconds with a hard-copy NIV and the ability to read a footnote.

Either the author of the post didn’t spend that 30 seconds
before spewing on the Internet, which would be extraordinarily reckless, or he
did spend 30 seconds, discovered the truth, and knowingly posted a deception to
advance the KJVO cause.  Because of his
misrepresentation of the “removed” words, I suspect that his presentation of
the “missing” verses is deliberately deceptive too.

I entitled this series of posts “Pious Fraud and the NIV”
not because “pious fraud” is a cool phrase (though it is) but because it is an
actual thing.  Whenever a believer uses a
lie to advance his faith, that’s a pious fraud. 
The classic example of pious fraud is the false prophet who fakes up a
miracle to inspire his followers.  The
post above is also a pious fraud.  The
author considers his goal—persuading others to abandon modern translations for
the KJV—to be so important that he is willing to deceive to attain it.

He would likely insist that everything he said in the post
is true.  In a narrow, Pharisaical sense,
that is accurate.  It does not contain a
single out-and-out lie.  It merely
invites the reader to draw conclusions that the author knows are incorrect.  However, the same Bible that condemns lying
also condemns deceit.  Take, for
instance, 1 Peter 3:10-12:

*For
he that will love life, and see good days, let him refrain his tongue from
evil, and his lips that they speak no guile; let him eschew evil, and do good;
let him seek peace, and ensue it.  For
the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous, and his ears are open unto their
prayers:  but the face of the Lord is
against them that do evil.*

That’s straight out of the KJV. 
Sadly, the author apparently prefers promoting his favorite translation
to obeying it.

the KJVO author pick on the NIV when he hates all modern translations
equally?  Why did he mention looking up
those verses on a phone or mobile device?
