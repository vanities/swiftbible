*Then Laban and Bethuel replied, "The matter comes
from the Lord; so we cannot speak
to you bad or good."*

-- Genesis
24:50 (NASB)

This
intriguing comment is made to a man whose name we do not even know.  He is the servant of Abraham whom Abraham has
sent to Mesopotamia to find a wife from among his own people for his son Isaac.  This servant arrives in the city of Nahor,
asks God for help in finding a suitable woman, and, before he finishes praying
in his heart, is presented with Abraham’s great-niece Rebekah.  He takes her to Laban and Bethuel, who are
her brother and father, explains what has happened, and asks for her as Isaac’s
bride.  Genesis 24:50 contains their
reply.

Laban isn’t
one of the great heroes of faith, but this answer is impressive.  In effect, he is telling Abraham’s servant
that because God has already spoken, the matter is already decided.  Laban may or may not have an opinion, but
that opinion is irrelevant and not even worth bringing up.  Because God wants Rebekah to marry Isaac,
that’s what must happen.

If there is
any tendency that gets mankind into trouble in matters of faith, it is the
tendency to speak where Laban and Bethuel did not.  It is the tendency to say, as it were, “The
matter comes from the Lord, but
let us speak to you bad or good anyway.” 
We presume that even though God has already expressed His will, our
opinions are somehow still relevant and, indeed, are worthy of being considered
over and above the will of God.

Instances of
this presumption are as numberless as the descendants of Abraham.  Every apostasy is the result of human
determination to speak bad or good in a matter from the Lord.  He
has said that homosexuals will not inherit the kingdom of heaven?  Who cares? 
We know some nice homosexuals, so let’s endorse gay marriage anyway!  He has said that a woman should not teach or
exercise authority over a man?  Who
cares?  We know some smart women, so
let’s have female pastors anyway!  He has
said that His worship is to consist of singing? 
Who cares?  We have some lovely
musical instruments, so let’s have instrumental music in our assemblies
anyway!

In all of
these areas, the true problem is the heart. 
If we desire to submit our wills to God’s, the matter will rest with His
expression of that will.  However, if we
want to do what we want to do, we will always be able to come up with a
justification for doing what we want. 
Although these justifications may satisfy us, they will do nothing to
satisfy God on the day of judgment.  Like
Laban and Bethuel, we must have the humility and wisdom to stifle our own
opinions in a matter from the Lord.
