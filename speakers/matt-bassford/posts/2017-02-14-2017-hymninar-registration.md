After a year's hiatus, the Hymninar is back!  For those who have never encountered it before, it is a week-long hymn-writing workshop held in Columbia, MO.  This year, it will run from Monday, June 5th, through Friday, June 9th.  Typically, Monday through Thursday are full-day sessions, and Friday's session is a half-day long.

The first part of the week will be focused on instruction in the art of writing hymn texts.  During that time, the class will study through *A Good Hymn*, a workbook that identifies the essential qualities of a good hymn by looking at hymns that have stood the test of time.  During the second part of the week, students will endeavor to put what they have learned into practice by writing a single four-line verse good enough to be published in a hymnal.  Returning students will also be able to present entire hymns they've written outside of class for critique and suggestions for improvement.  If you're interested in writing hymns, there's simply no better way to do this than to attend.

The cost of attendance will be about $85, which will cover class materials and parking. The cost of meals is not included. Brethren who live in the area have generously offered to host attendees in their own homes, so there are no fees for lodging.  However, those who wish to spend the week staying in a hotel at their own expense are certainly free to do so.

As is usually, the case, my wife and I will be handling registration (read:  my wife will be handling registration, and I will be writing the blog post).  If you would like to attend, please contact me on Facebook (where I am Matthew William Bassford) or by emailing me at MandLbassford (at) gmail (dot) com.  If you don't hear back from us shortly, recall to mind the parable of the unrighteous judge and try again!  Registration will end on May 15th.

All of the above sounds dreadfully dry, but it really isn't.  As a rule, hymnists tend to be creative, weird, witty types who can't resist the opportunity to crack a joke, particularly at the expense of some of the howlingly bad hymns that have been written through the centuries.  If you can contemplate the likes of "Heaven's Airport" and "Blest Is the Man Whose Bowels Move" (both real hymns) with a straight face, the Hymninar probably isn't for you.  If, on the other hand, you find yourself wanting copies of these hymns so you can laugh at them with your friends, you'll probably fit right in.  Hope to see you there!
