In a perverse way, Omar Mateen and the rest of the hateful
tribe of mass shooters have done us one useful service.  They’ve given us another proof of the
existence of God.

Other than a handful who have truly lost their way, we all
know how to describe what happened in the Pulse early Sunday morning.  It was evil, pure and absolute.  I didn’t know anybody involved, but when I
sit and reflect on dozens of human beings slaughtered like cattle, for no better
reason than the malice of one man, I have trouble keeping my dinner down.  There is nothing redeeming about it.  It is utterly, hopelessly evil.  There were people there that night who looked
full in the face of the devil.

But.  Where there is
absolute evil, somewhere, there must also be absolute good.  Logically, one cannot exist without the
other.  The darkness is deep indeed, but God
is light, and in Him there is no darkness at all.



As we are angry, then, as we grieve, we need to remember to
respond by seeking the good.  As it’s possible
to look full in the face of Satan, so too it’s possible, from time to time, to
glimpse the face of God.  Generally,
these glimpses don’t make headlines. 
They’re not visible except to a few at a time, but they are real too.

Yesterday, I baptized a man. 
His name is Chris.  There were no
reporters on hand; the TV cameras weren’t rolling.  Quietly, he was buried with Christ and rose
to walk in newness of life.  He was a
kind, decent man before I ever laid eyes on him, and I hope that now that he is
a disciple of Jesus, his kindness and his decency will define him even more.

That, I think, is what God’s answer looks like.  Evil arrives with a flash and a bang, but the
kingdom of God does not come with signs to be observed.  It grows within us, and because it has grown
within us, it grows around us.

The people of God do not come armed with assault
rifles.  They come armed with the things
that the world never notices:  with a
kind word to someone who’s having a rough day, with hospitality for the
stranger, with thoughtfulness, humility, and grace in an era of brusque
indifference.

Next to the brutal certainty of a body count, these things
seem ephemeral.  Like the wind, you hear
the sound of them, but you don’t know where they have come from or where they
are going.  Shooting somebody is an act
of sight.  Doing good for somebody is an
act of faith.  Sometimes, it bears fruit
immediately, sometimes decades later, and sometimes never.

You don’t know.  You
never know.  All you can know is that
somehow, God is working out His will in these things, and that because He is,
this world with so much awfulness is still a hopeful place.  If we want to answer the nightclub shooting,
if we want to strike back at the enemy behind the terror, this is how we do
it.

Politics and earthly conflict offer only a false hope.  After 9-11, the U.S. answered by invading
Iraq and Afghanistan.  Fifteen years and
untold thousands of deaths later, we’re still fighting in those places, and the
world is even less safe than it was. 
That doesn’t look like a solution. 
It looks like an expansion of the problem.  The kingdom of God does not come with signs
to be observed, nor does it achieve peace through superior firepower.  Instead, it achieves peace through means that
the world dismisses.

On Sunday, the devil called Omar Mateen, and he
answered.  He came armed with an AR-15
and hatred.  For three hours, he
murdered, until police gunfire chopped him down.  If he gets a tombstone, maybe Matthew 26:52
should be on it.

On Sunday, God called Chris, and he answered.  He came in the only suit (only tie?) I’ve
ever seen him wear.  He came, candidly
ignorant of the nuances of discipleship. 
He came with a big heart, earnestly seeking God, wanting to share in the community of believers.  Omar
Mateen has done his work, but Chris’s work is just beginning.

Does one Chris outweigh an Omar Mateen?  Literally, yes; Chris is a big dude!  Spiritually. . . well, that’s up to him, as
it’s up to each one of us.  Our weapons
are not the weapons of earthly warfare, but they have extraordinary power in
the battle we should be fighting.

A mass shooting has to be a call to arms.  Let’s make sure those are the arms we’re
using.  The world has become a darker
place.  Let’s shine more brightly in it.
